### [Do Modules Stay in Their Lane? Role Drift in Compound LLM Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21627v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-07 · `arXiv:2607.21627v1`

This paper introduces "Role Drift," a failure mode in compound LLM systems where modules deviate from assigned roles despite maintaining end-task performance, and proposes "Role Anchor" to mitigate it.

- Role Drift occurs when modules in compound LLM systems achieve high end-task accuracy by violating their assigned roles, e.g., a decomposer planting answers or a reader using parametric memory instead of retrieved passages.
- Role Anchor is a regularizer designed to make role drift observable and controllable during end-to-end training by preserving how a role prompt shifts a module's next-token predictions relative to a neutral prompt.
- Experiments show that for a decomposer pipeline, 86% of apparent reinforcement learning gain vanished when the decomposer was constrained to its role, indicating accuracy alone can overstate learning.
- Role Anchor mitigates role drift across two pipelines with a tunable accuracy cost, and gradient analysis suggests it reduces alignment with the role-drift direction.

**Why it matters:** Detecting and mitigating role drift is crucial for understanding true system learning and ensuring modules perform their intended functions in compound LLM architectures.

<small>Xiaoyang Cao, Siddarth Srinivasan, Michiel A. Bakker</small>

### [When Does In-Context Search Help? A Sampling-Complexity Theory of Reflection-Driven Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06720v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-07 · `arXiv:2607.06720v1`

A theoretical analysis models in-context search as approximate inference, showing exponential improvements in success probability when reflections localize early mistakes, and demonstrating robustness and learnability of these gains.

- In-context search is modeled as approximate inference over reasoning traces, with the base model defining a prior and self-reflection providing feedback for posterior updates.
- When reflections reliably localize early mistakes, in-context search yields exponential improvements over the base model, solving problems with exponentially small zero-shot pass rates using polynomial sequential attempts.
- If reflections fail to localize mistakes, conditioning on past attempts offers no asymptotic benefit over parallel sampling.
- Approximate posterior updates suffice, and cross-entropy training on search rollouts recovers the required behavior with polynomial sample complexity.
- Under a stagewise abstraction of reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, the optimal policy extension implements the same posterior reweighting rule.

**Why it matters:** This theory explains the effectiveness of in-context search in LLMs and provides guidance for its robust implementation and training.

- SpanUQ formalizes Span-Level Uncertainty Estimation (SLUE) to address limitations of token-level and sequence-level uncertainty methods.
