### [Kernelized Linear Attention: Breaking the Capacity Wall with Symmetric Cones](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17419v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-19 · `arXiv:2607.17419v1`

Kernelized Linear Attention Activations (KATA) improve associative recall in linear attention by using feature maps derived from self-dual homogeneous cones, achieving better capacity-interference tradeoffs and high throughput.

- KATA formulates attention recall as a spherical-packing problem and derives feature maps from first principles using self-dual homogeneous cones to certify non-negative attention weights.
- Rank-one positive semi-definite (PSD) features offer a favorable capacity-interference tradeoff, and KATA characterizes associative capacity through the Welch interference floor.
- KATA implementations achieve up to ~1.6x FlashAttention-2 throughput for forward passes and ~11x FlashAttention-2 forward throughput at 131k tokens in an exact O(T) chunked-state form.
- KATA variants outperform Gated DeltaNet on long-range MQAR and repeated-key overwrite, retaining 0.985 MQAR at 16x out-of-distribution length with roughly one quarter of softmax KV-cache entries.

**Why it matters:** KATA offers a method to significantly improve the associative recall and throughput of linear attention models, making them more competitive with softmax attention for long-sequence tasks while reducing KV-cache requirements.

**Why it matters:** This work provides a method to predict and optimize the performance of LLM ensembles by quantifying the benefits of diverse model contributions.

### [Lossless but Not Free: An Empirical Anatomy of Speculative Decoding on Consumer Hardware](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17283v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-19 · `arXiv:2607.17283v1`

An empirical study of speculative decoding on consumer Apple Silicon hardware shows a maximum 1.61x speedup, but only when verification is truly batch-parallel and the draft model is sufficiently faster than the target.

- Speculative decoding proposes K tokens with a draft model, then verifies them in a single batched pass with the target model, preserving the output distribution.
- The best configuration achieved a 1.61x wall-clock speedup at K=6, with acceptance rates declining from 69.7% at K=1 to 37.8% at K=6.
- Three of five configurations decelerated due to inefficient draft models or serial execution of "parallel" verification on the quantized Metal backend.
- Distribution equivalence was verified with a two-sample test ($ \chi^2 = 162.5 $, dof = 200, p = 0.976) over ~9,200 tokens and exact greedy-sequence agreement.
- The implementation is from-scratch and device-agnostic (CUDA/MPS/CPU) across five draft/target backend configurations.

**Why it matters:** Speculative decoding offers significant speedups for LLM inference on consumer hardware, but its effectiveness is highly dependent on genuine batch-parallel verification and a substantial latency gap between draft and target models.

### [Teach it to stop, not just to click](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17136v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-19 · `arXiv:2607.17136v1`

Single-run reporting of computer-use RL agent success rates is misleading due to high variance from data draws and run-to-run nondeterminism, necessitating k-seed replication for reliable evaluation.

- A variance-components decomposition shows evaluation variance is negligible (${\sigma}_{\mathrm{eval}} \approx 0$) and training-seed effect is small ($\leq 10\%$), with data draw and run-to-run nondeterminism dominating success rate variance.
- The data draw's share of variance rises to 48% on the hardest environment, where the run-to-run distribution is bimodal (Hartigan dip $p=0.07$, $k=10$), making mean$\pm$std an inappropriate summary.
- Repairability is two-tiered: fixed token corrections (done-detection $0.97\pm0.06$) are reliable, while open-ended corrections like spatial-coordinate clicks (grounding $0.53\pm0.35$) and generative field-fills ($0.14\pm0.04$) are only partial.
- Frame-level repair transfers to task success only when the corrective action is the sole remaining blocker (LinkedIn 8/20 vs. base 0/15, Fisher $p=0.006$).
- The work introduces cua_reliability, a library for routine k-seed reporting, and uses the first multimodal segment-aggregated on-policy self-distillation (SA-OPSD) update on a 35B CUA policy.

**Why it matters:** Relying on single-run evaluations for computer-use RL agents can lead to incorrect conclusions about policy improvements, necessitating robust multi-seed replication for accurate assessment.
