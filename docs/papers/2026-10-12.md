### [S-Space: Exploring Spatial Workspace in Multimodal Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13527)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-10-09 · 👍 197 · ⭐ 244 · `arXiv:2610.13527`

Researchers identified S-Space, an intrinsic linear representation subspace in multimodal language models where object tokens encode 3D spatial coordinates, serving as an internal spatial workspace for perception, reasoning, and verbal output.

- S-Space is discovered by differentiating spatial-answer scores with respect to object-token activations, revealing continuous coordinate encoding across horizontal, vertical, and distance axes.
- The subspace functions natively and remains readable even under non-spatial prompts, indicating it is an intrinsic internal spatial workspace rather than a prompt-induced artifact.
- Causal interventions on S-Space alter the model's spatial judgments while preserving non-spatial responses, proving its specific role in verbalizing spatial outputs.
- Decomposition of spatial reasoning failures demonstrates that MLLMs succeed at perceptual spatial encoding but bottleneck during the spatial transformations needed for viewpoint changes.
- Readouts of S-Space reveal systematic coupling between cardinal and image-relative reference frames, as well as location assignments to unseen entities and the observer.

**Why it matters:** Identifying S-Space exposes the internal mechanisms of MLLM spatial reasoning, pinpointing that spatial failures stem from transformation bottlenecks rather than perception errors.

### [DiffusionART: Efficient, Stable and Scalable Reinforcement of Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12572)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-10-07 · 👍 172 · ⭐ 6 · `arXiv:2610.12572`

DiffusionART accelerates online RL for diffusion models by aggregating prompt rewards into a shared direction and using a trust-region-masked update, eliminating the need for differentiable reward models or EMA tuning.

- Identifies that forward-process NFT-style objectives suffer from a hidden forward-reverse consistency force, and that EMA stabilizes training via state restoration rather than off-policy rollouts.
- Introduces Aggregation-First Reward Tilting (DiffusionART), which aggregates prompt rewards into a shared reward-improving direction for reverse-process states to eliminate the consistency force by construction.
- Incorporates a trust-region-masked second update step to provide drift-adaptive restoration without requiring manual EMA hyperparameter tuning.
- Supports pairwise reward models (DiffusionART-Pairwise) and functions effectively with reduced sample counts per prompt.
- Achieved equivalent late-stage mean rewards on HunyuanVideo-13B using 12.02x fewer GPU hours than DiffusionNFT and 11.72x fewer than Flow-GRPO-Fast, while improving mean training rewards by 28.8% and 53.0% respectively.

**Why it matters:** It significantly reduces the computational overhead and hyperparameter tuning required to stably fine-tune large-scale image and video diffusion models with black-box rewards.

<small>Zhenglin Wan, Jiayu Chen, Zhengfeiyang Zhang, Pengfei Zhou, Wangbo Zhao, Fangyuan Kong, Pengfei Wan, Kun Gai</small>

### [PreSTO: Predictive Subtree Prefetching for Fast LLM Power Sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13171)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-10-08 · 👍 9 · `arXiv:2610.13171`

Predictive SubTree Prefetching (PreSTO) accelerates PowerMH sampling for LLM reasoning by prefetching future Metropolis-Hastings proposals in a batched subtree without changing the original transition kernel.

- PreSTO constructs a subtree of future accept/reject decisions, generating proposals for the subtree in a single batched model call and evaluating the realized path with the exact Metropolis-Hastings rule.
- A predictive traversal rule targets sparse, nearly unary parts of the decision tree to maximize completed transitions, outperforming breadth-first traversal by 1.25x-1.37x on LCB V6 with Qwen3.5-9B.
- PreSTO achieves median speedups of 1.71x on PowerMH, 1.91x on EntropyCut, and 2.33x on MultiTryMH across seven datasets and five LLMs.
- For PowerMH and EntropyCut, PreSTO requires a median of 3.9x fewer model calls with a modest 2.1x latency increase per call.

**Why it matters:** PreSTO significantly reduces the computational latency of power sampling methods for LLM reasoning without altering the target distribution or approximation accuracy.

### [Not All Dimensions Are Equal: Outlier-Aware Attention Linearization with Grouped Quadratic Kernels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13674v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-10-09 · `arXiv:2610.13674v1`

Outlier-Aware Attention Linearization (OAL) groups query-key dimensions by contribution scale to construct a positive quadratic kernel, achieving linear-time attention evaluation while mitigating approximation errors caused by outlier dimensions.

- Identifies that a small fraction of outlier dimensions disproportionately drives query-key dot product magnitude and approximation error in linearized attention.
- Introduces a Hadamard-product Taylor formulation that enables dimension-specific linear terms and cross-dimensional quadratic interactions while maintaining linear time complexity with respect to sequence length.
- Groups dimensions with similar scale contributions to share parameters, isolating outliers to reduce parameter count.
- Achieves an 8.9% reduction in frozen-attention output relative MSE over DiJiang on Qwen2.5-0.5B.
- Lowers WikiText-103 perplexity from 31.71 to 27.50 when replacing middle-layer attention and fine-tuning with LoRA compared to a learned global quadratic baseline.

**Why it matters:** It enables more accurate conversion of pretrained autoregressive models into linear-time transformers by specifically addressing high-impact outlier dimensions during attention linearization.

### [Beyond Domain-Routed Supervision: Cross-Teacher Guarding for Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13521v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-10-09 · `arXiv:2610.13521v1`

Cross-Teacher Guarding for MOPD (Road MOPD) resolves gradient conflicts and capability degradation in multi-teacher on-policy distillation by using non-routed auxiliary teachers to filter updates via credit assignment and gradient projection.

- Identifies that standard domain-routed multi-teacher on-policy distillation causes persistent gradient conflicts when specialized teachers with differing styles update shared student parameters.
- Uses non-routed teachers as auxiliary cross-domain critics evaluating the same student-generated trajectories.
- Implements cross-teacher-guided credit assignment to strengthen mutually supported token-level corrections and attenuate conflicting signals based on teacher agreement.
- Employs preventive multi-teacher gradient projection with adaptive strength control to constrain conflicting parameter updates while retaining target-domain learning signals.
- Achieves over a 5 percentage point performance gain under high-conflict conditions and improves overall performance across low-conflict and three-domain benchmarks.

**Why it matters:** It prevents joint-optimization degradation when distilling multiple specialized, conflicting expert models into a single student model.

### [Branching On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13481v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-10-09 · `arXiv:2610.13481v1`

BranchOPD improves on-policy distillation by branching trajectories at uncertain decision points and introducing protocol-boundary repair to expand supervision coverage efficiently.

- Identifies high-value decision points using student uncertainty and teacher-student disagreement to resample continuations from shared prefixes.
- Reduces student-generated token costs by ~19% on ALFWorld and ~31% on WebShop compared to seven independent rollouts by reusing shared prefixes.
- Incorporates protocol-boundary repair (PBR) to supply targeted supervision under protocol constraints, preventing repetition and generation waste.
- Outperforms Vanilla OPD by 18.55 percentage points in mean math accuracy (avg@16) and by 13.8 points in mean ALFWorld success rate.

**Why it matters:** BranchOPD offers a more sample-efficient method for distilling large reasoning and agent models into smaller student models without relying on costly independent trajectory rollouts.

### [Rollout-Time Intervention: Unlocking Agentic Reinforcement Learning Beyond Policy Limits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13438v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-10-09 · `arXiv:2610.13438v1`

Rollout-Time Intervention (RTI) uses reward-side evaluation rubrics during RL rollouts to correct agent errors, generating viable off-policy training trajectories beyond the model's intrinsic exploration capacity.

- Addresses the on-policy reasoning bottleneck where training collapses due to sparse successful trajectories on complex tasks.
- Uses evaluation rubrics during the rollout phase to intervene and turn failing paths into successful trajectories.
- Filters out intervention trajectories with excessive distributional shift to maintain stable policy optimization.
- Outperforms standard RL baselines across five benchmarks, allowing the policy to exceed its baseline capability limits.

**Why it matters:** RTI provides a practical mechanism to train LLM agents on tasks exceeding their initial autonomous exploration capabilities without collapsing during reinforcement learning.

### [AI Models Can Provably Hide Arbitrary Capabilities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13928v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-10-09 · `arXiv:2610.13928v1`

Researchers constructed PyTorch backdoor attacks that embed encrypted neural networks inside host model weights, provably hiding arbitrary computations from capability audits using digital lockers.

- Extends previous capability-hiding research from static strings to arbitrary computations using cryptographic digital lockers.
- Hidden circuits decrypt and execute only when exposed to a secret trigger, remaining provably hard to interpret or elicit under standard cryptographic assumptions.
- PyTorch proof-of-concept models resisted fine-tuning, weight noising, gradient-based trigger search, and latent adversarial training.
- Evaluated on a small chemical reaction prediction task serving as a proxy for CBRN-relevant capabilities.

**Why it matters:** The findings establish fundamental cryptographic limits to elicitation-based capability audits, proving that adversaries can reliably conceal dangerous capabilities even during full-access model evaluations.
