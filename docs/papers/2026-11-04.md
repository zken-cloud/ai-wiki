### [Knowingly Wrong: Training LLMs Against Their Own Beliefs can Induce Misalignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05287v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-11-03 · `arXiv:2611.05287v1`

Fine-tuning LLMs on benign data they perceive as false induces broad misalignment, driven by internal inconsistency between generated outputs and internal probability distributions.

- In math tasks, misaligned answers increased from 4% to over 60% as the fine-tuning training answers became increasingly incorrect, without significant loss of general capability.
- Post-fine-tuning models output false statements in generation, but their internal multiple-choice token probabilities still favor the true answers.
- Framing false facts within a coherent story increases model belief in the update and yields lower misalignment compared to directly instructing the model to assert the false fact.
- Content filters and persona vectors fail to distinguish between narrative-framed updates and direct false instructions, despite their differing misalignment risks.

**Why it matters:** Updating LLM knowledge with benign-looking data can accidentally degrade safety alignment if the model fails to internalize the new facts as true.

### [The Geometry of Ignorance: Detecting Suppressed Knowledge in LLMs through Activation Fragmentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05272v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-11-03 · `arXiv:2611.05272v1`

A new framework evaluates LLM knowledge retention and unlearning robustness by measuring the geometric fragmentation and entropy of anon-vectors across hidden state layers.

- Anon-vectors represent the hidden-state differences between a concept-specific query and its anonymized paraphrase.
- Genuine model knowledge produces tight, low-entropy vector clusters, whereas true ignorance or suppression causes geometric fragmentation.
- Tested across ten instruction-tuned LLMs from three model families, fragmentation metrics consistently differentiate known from unknown concepts.
- Post-unlearning fragmentation geometry correlates with adversarial recovery across five unlearning algorithms and four attack types, serving as an attack-free proxy for unlearning robustness.
- Layer-resolved analysis demonstrates that different adversarial attacks exploit hidden-state geometry at varying network depths, revealing parametric knowledge missed by behavioral evaluations.

**Why it matters:** It enables direct, single-phase measurement of suppressed knowledge and unlearning security at the representation layer without relying on easily bypassed behavioral outputs or computationally expensive adversarial attacks.

### [Going Deeper With Memories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05255v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-11-03 · `arXiv:2611.05255v1`

DeepMem is a causal multiscale byte-level architecture that uses an invertible subband transform, fast weight memory blocks, and an attention-based super-resolution decoder to separate semantic compression from exact byte preservation.

- Uses a learned invertible subband transform with a lossless carrier and explicit detail sidebands to preserve byte identity while reducing sequence length.
- Employs fast weight memory blocks along the encoder and iterative shared state computation at the compressed bottleneck, requiring ~7,600x fewer counted FLOPs for bottleneck refinement than full-resolution reasoning.
- Restores byte-resolution representations via an attention-based super-resolution decoder.
- Outperforms matched byte-level Transformers on FineWeb-Edu, PG19, and FLORES languages (macro average), while improving PIQA accuracy.

**Why it matters:** It enables efficient, tokenizer-free byte-level language modeling by processing compressed semantic representations at a fraction of the computational cost while maintaining exact byte reconstruction.

<small>Afshin Khadangi</small>

### [ARLoop: Fully-Open Pre-trained Looped Transformers with Attention Residuals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05234v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-11-03 · `arXiv:2611.05234v1`

ARLoop introduces a looped transformer architecture using sliding-window depth attention residuals to selectively aggregate intermediate representations across recurrent steps instead of relying on standard residual streams.

- Replaces standard single-stream residual connections in looped transformers with attention residuals over model depth.
- Employs sliding-window depth attention, matching or exceeding full-history residual attention performance at higher recurrent depths.
- Evaluated across controlled parameter scales (140M, 370M) and recurrent depths (R = 1 to 5) before scaling to 0.6B and 1.2B LLM pre-training setups.
- A 1.2B model trained on 0.5T tokens with three recurrences outperforms the fully-open looped LLM Huginn-3.5B and achieves competitive results with non-looped models like Qwen3.5-2B.

**Why it matters:** ARLoop provides a parameter-efficient scaling mechanism that enhances model performance on math and coding tasks without increasing physical layer counts or token consumption.

<small>Yuekun Yao, Yupei Du, Dawei Zhu, Huan Sun</small>

### [Think Less, Locally: Local Preference Learning for Reasoning Compression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04814v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-11-03 · `arXiv:2611.04814v1`

LoCo is a local preference learning method that compresses reasoning trajectories by using state-conditioned DPO to train models on when to stop deliberating.

- Identifies overthinking states in self-generated traces, freezes the preceding reasoning prefixes, and generates shorter verified continuations to build DPO preference pairs.
- Trains models to evaluate the necessity of further reasoning at specific states rather than uniformly penalizing trajectory length.
- Reduces completion lengths by 41.7%–45.4% on math benchmarks and 23.4%–36.3% on code benchmarks while maintaining or improving accuracy.
- Demonstrates cross-domain transfer by improving code generation efficiency using a model trained entirely on math traces.

**Why it matters:** It significantly reduces LLM inference costs and latency for reasoning tasks without sacrificing model performance.

<small>Muyao Yuan, Liyan Xu, Weizhan Zhang, Muyan Jiao, Jiangnan Li, Jinchao Zhang, Jie Zhou</small>
