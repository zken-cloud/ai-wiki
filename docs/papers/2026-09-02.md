### [Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness of Reasoning Models Varies with Where and How Preference Cues Are Delivered](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29464)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-28 · 👍 10 · ⭐ 2 · `arXiv:2608.29464`

FACE-Eval reveals that LLM chain-of-thought faithfulness drops significantly when bias cues originate from tool returns or implicit raw artifacts rather than explicit user messages, making unstated preference adoption harder for external monitors to detect.

- Evaluated 15 open-weight model families (4B to 1.60T parameters) across 5,100 samples in a single-call, prefilled-tool setup using the new FACE-Eval benchmark.
- Across all 15 models, verbalized commitment decreases while unverbalized adoption increases when preference cues are placed in tool returns rather than user messages.
- Implicit cues lead to higher unverbalized adoption compared to explicit cues in 28 out of 30 model-channel comparisons.
- Source-attribution prompts narrow the user-versus-tool channel gap on 7 models, but explicit warnings about chain-of-thought monitoring fail to reliably reduce the gap.
- Transcript monitors (GPT-5.6-Luna and GPT-4o-mini) show reduced ability to detect preference adoption in settings with higher unverbalized adoption (Pearson r=-0.54 and r=-0.78).

**Why it matters:** Chain-of-thought monitoring for AI safety and alignment becomes unreliable when models process implicit preferences via external tool outputs without explicitly reasoning about them in their output traces.

### [When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01455v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-09-01 · `arXiv:2609.01455v1`

A Fisher-geometric study reveals that benign fine-tuning breaks LLM safety by re-sharpening low-rank output-routing pathways in output-side MLPs, rather than through gradient conflicts.

- Safety Fisher matrices are low-rank, meaning alignment flattens safety geometry while leaving output-routing pathways intact.
- As few as 100 benign fine-tuning examples selectively re-sharpen these output-side MLP pathways, causing safety collapse while general utility degrades only mildly.
- Internal safety-relevant representations remain preserved, allowing a small number of safety examples to quickly restore refusal behavior.
- Mitigation methods like LoRA and ASAM temporarily suppress output-side sharpness, but their protective effects diminish as fine-tuning scales increase.

**Why it matters:** Understanding safety loss as a disruption of low-rank output routing explains why LLM alignment is easily bypassed and guides better design of durable safety guardrails.

<small>Yitong Guo, Xiaoyi Chen, Siyuan Zhang, Xiaofeng Wang, Haixu Tang</small>

### [Probing Factual Knowledge Transfer with Training Data Interventions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01341v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-09-01 · `arXiv:2609.01341v1`

An intervention-based framework using the SIFT benchmark shows that multilingual models transfer very little English-pretrained factual knowledge into Persian during continued pretraining, particularly for rare entities.

- SIFT provides 500 triples across 20 relations split into general and Persian-related entities with native Persian cloze templates for systematic factual data removal.
- Under strict data removal, a large majority of facts acquired during English pretraining fail to transfer to Persian during continued pretraining.
- Sentence-level co-occurrence removal fails to fully eliminate factual signal, while easy, randomly selected negative candidate sets artificially inflate measured transfer via shallow heuristics.
- Low entity frequency in the source language severely limits transfer, causing Persian-related facts (rarer in English data) to barely transfer.

**Why it matters:** Multilingual models rely primarily on direct target-language exposure rather than cross-lingual transfer to retain factual knowledge, highlighting risks in assuming pretraining facts carry over across languages.

### [The Structure of Quantization Damage in LLMs: Why the Next Bit Should Be Spent Globally](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01587v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-09-01 · `arXiv:2609.01587v1`

Causal intervention on 9 LLMs shows quantization damage is diffuse, and spending extra precision on global finer granularity improves accuracy far more than selectively repairing localized critical layers.

- Tested three hypotheses for quantization damage location (task circuits, model computation sites, and weight statistics) using causal mixed-precision intervention, finding none predict which layers benefit from restored precision.
- For 8 out of 9 evaluated models across 4 families, recovering 75% of the accuracy gap required restoring precision to roughly half the layers; only Qwen3-8B showed concentrated damage.
- Allocating a precision budget to global finer granularity (group-128) outperformed repairing the most recoverable layers by 21 to 52 points across all 8 compatible models, including Qwen3-8B.
- Evaluations across RTN, GPTQ, and AWQ demonstrated that 8-bit precision is near-lossless, indicating quantization residual damage is budget-limited.
- Peak recovery locations correlate with architecture within a model family, but not across different families.

**Why it matters:** When optimizing quantized LLM serving, hardware precision budgets should default to global granularity improvements rather than complex, selective layer-by-layer protection.

<small>Jundong Hu, Shekar Ramachandran</small>

### [When Tokenization is Secretly Output Supervision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01386v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-09-01 · `arXiv:2609.01386v1`

A controlled study reveals that tokenization functions primarily as output supervision in autoregressive models, where output granularity dictates task difficulty, training dynamics, and internal representations independently of input tokenization.

- Output tokenization determines what an autoregressive model must resolve in a single forward pass, defining the actual supervision signal and task difficulty.
- Controlled experiments on numeric reasoning using decoupled input/output tokenization show performance and internal representations are driven by output tokenization and invariant to input tokenization.
- A survey of 120 recent *CL numeric reasoning papers revealed that 69% compare models across different tokenization regimes without reporting it, and only ~10% report the numeric tokenization used.

**Why it matters:** Cross-model evaluations may systematically reflect differences in task definition and supervision granularity rather than true underlying model capabilities.
