### [KronQ: LLM Quantization via Kronecker-Factored Hessian](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07964)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-07 · 👍 32 · ⭐ 6 · `arXiv:2607.07964`

KronQ introduces a post-training quantization framework for LLMs that incorporates gradient covariance into the quantization objective, improving performance over existing second-order methods.

- KronQ's quantization loss depends on both activation and gradient covariances, derived from a Kronecker-factored Hessian approximation.
- It implements bidirectional incoherence processing, extending input-side random rotation to the output dimension using gradient covariance to reduce weight magnitude variance.
- KronQ develops a new sensitivity metric for inter-layer mixed-precision allocation, based on gradient and activation Hessian traces.
- For 2-bit weight-only quantization on LLaMA-3-70B, KronQ achieved 7.93 perplexity on WikiText-2, while GPTQ and GPTAQ produced degenerate quantizations (>2000 perplexity).

**Why it matters:** This method enables more effective extreme quantization (e.g., 2-bit) for large language models, which is crucial for deploying them on resource-constrained hardware.

### [Inside the Unfair Judge: A Mechanistic Interpretability Account of LLM-as-Judge Bias](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11871v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-13 · `arXiv:2607.11871v1`

This study provides a representation-level account of LLM-as-judge scoring bias, demonstrating that biases manifest as specific activation geometry in hidden states, enabling causal control and operational prediction of judge failures.

- Baseline judging inputs occupy a tight activation manifold, while biased inputs are displaced along a low-dimensional, type-specific subspace that sharpens with depth and is consistently recovered by three estimator families.
- Steering hidden states along this bias subspace causally drives scoring, with forward shifts reproducing biased scoring on clean inputs and reverse shifts restoring baseline scoring on biased inputs.
- Matched-norm random directions produce scoring shifts an order of magnitude smaller than steering along the bias subspace.
- A simple linear projection onto bias-direction features anticipates judge failures on three unseen benchmarks, outperforming text-based alternatives.

**Why it matters:** Understanding LLM-as-judge bias as activation geometry allows for more effective diagnosis, mitigation, and prediction of scoring failures beyond input-output level analysis.

### [When Local Monitors Miss Compositional Harm: Diagnosing Distributed Backdoors in Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11751v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-07-13 · `arXiv:2607.11751v1`

This paper demonstrates that local runtime monitors in multi-agent LLM systems fail to detect distributed backdoors where harmful payloads are split across agents, appearing benign until reassembled.

- Distributed backdoors exploit an "observability boundary" where individual fragments appear benign to local monitors, making detection impossible within that view.
- A monitor trained solely on benign traffic can recover the attack's code structure across held-out encodings, achieving a 0.874 mean AUROC.
- Full-trace monitors and decoders fail unless they access the representation where the distributed payload is exposed.

### [SCOPE-RL: Optimizing Reasoning Paths Before and After Success](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11506v2)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-07-13 · `arXiv:2607.11506v2`

SCOPE-RL densifies sparse verifiable rewards in RLVR for LLMs by introducing two stages: Adaptive Scaffolded RL for pre-success reasoning and Quality-Aware Process RL for post-success refinement.

- SCOPE-RL uses Adaptive Scaffolded RL to add prefix-decomposed verifiable rewards on answer-hidden sub-question chains before success.

- An expert-validated Step-Quality Evaluation Protocol assesses useful-step density, error localization, and token efficiency.

**Why it matters:** This method improves LLM reasoning accuracy and efficiency by providing more granular feedback during the problem-solving process.

### [HyperSafe: Inference-Time Safety Recovery for Fine-Tuned Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11475v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-07-13 · `arXiv:2607.11475v1`

HyperSafe introduces a post hoc, model-specific, and non-invasive framework to restore safety in fine-tuned language models by generating a Safe Side Network (SSN) for prompt-level safety classification.

- HyperSafe generates a model-specific Safe Side Network (SSN) for each fine-tuned checkpoint using layer-wise activation fingerprints and calibration prompts.
- The SSN operates alongside the frozen fine-tuned model, routing harmful prompts to refusal and safe prompts to the original model, without gradient updates or weight modification.
- Evaluations on Qwen2-7B and LLaMA-3-8B models show HyperSafe reduces harmful response rates from 19-31% to below 1% on held-out checkpoints.
- HyperSafe maintains downstream task accuracy within 1% of the fine-tuned baseline on average.

**Why it matters:** This method offers a cost-effective and non-invasive way to restore safety in fine-tuned LLMs without compromising task performance or requiring retraining.
