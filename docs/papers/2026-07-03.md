**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-07-03 · `arXiv:2607.03436v2`

12-36% of the LLM router-to-oracle gap is single-draw label noise, not recoverable by single-commit routers but by test-time sampling, with a protocol released to measure it.

- The expected oracle decomposes into reproducible single-commit headroom ($O^{\mathrm{repro}}$) and a non-negative single-commit selection floor ($\Delta$).
- A recoverability asymmetry exists: the floor ($\Delta$) is not closed by any single-commit router but is recovered by test-time sampling (best-of-$K$).
- Single-draw label noise accounts for 12-36% of the router-to-oracle gap on real open-model pools.

- Single-draw noise is a substantial minority of the gap, larger on unsaturated benchmarks and approaching half on the hardest queries across arithmetic, competition math, and non-math science.
- LLMRouterBench's 20-point gap is a union of stochastic draws, requiring re-estimation since $O^{\mathrm{repro}}$ is non-identifiable at $k=1$.

**Why it matters:** This work provides a method to accurately measure the recoverable portion of the router-to-oracle gap, enabling more effective LLM routing strategies.

### [Variable Bit-width Quantization: Learning Per-Group Precision for "Bigger-but-Smaller" Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02893v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-07-03 · `arXiv:2607.02893v1`

Variable Bit-width Quantization (VBQ) learns per-group precision for language models, enabling heterogeneous bit allocations that yield "bigger-but-smaller" models with improved efficiency.

- VBQ is a training-time method where each 64-weight group learns its bit-width from {1,2,4,8} bits via Gumbel-Softmax relaxation and alternating optimization.
- The method discovers highly heterogeneous precision allocations; for example, 69% of groups use 1 bit, the LM head averages 1.09 bits, and the first MLP block uses ~2.5 bits.
- A 131M model at 1.82 mean bits achieves 4.2 PPL on TinyStories, outperforming a 55M FP16 model (4.4 PPL) with 3.8x less storage.
- VBQ provides 3.9-8.4x more efficiency (quality-per-byte) than FP16 and accelerates inference with custom kernels, showing speedups up to 4.7x at 9B parameters on Apple silicon.
- Deeper layers progressively self-heal quantization error from earlier layers, as revealed by distributional analysis (KL divergence, argmax-flip rate).

**Why it matters:** This approach allows for significantly more efficient language models in terms of storage and inference speed while maintaining or improving performance compared to larger FP16 models.

<small>Hamish Ogilvy</small>
