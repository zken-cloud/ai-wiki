### [AREX: Towards a Recursively Self-Improving Agent for Deep Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21461)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-22 · 👍 149 · ⭐ 21 · `arXiv:2607.21461`

AREX is a recursively self-improving agent for deep research that alternates between an inner research loop and an outer self-improvement loop, using an autonomous context-update tool.

- AREX employs a discovery-verification asymmetry, recursively improving answers by verifying intermediate results and guiding refinement with partially verified states.
- It alternates an inner research loop (gathering evidence, constructing provisional answers) with an outer self-improvement loop (auditing constraint-wise, identifying unresolved claims, launching targeted follow-up research).
- AREX learns an autonomous context-update tool to compress interaction history into a compact improvement state, preserving verified evidence and unresolved constraints without external models.
- Training involves verified synthetic tasks, high-quality trajectories via agentic mid-training and long-horizon reinforcement learning, emphasizing key steps for evidence acquisition or error correction.
- Instantiated as a dense 4B model and a 122B-A10B Mixture-of-Experts model, AREX outperforms comparable-scale baselines across benchmarks like BrowseComp, WideSearch, DeepSearchQA, and HLE.

**Why it matters:** This approach could enable more efficient and accurate deep research by autonomously refining answers and managing complex information over long horizons.

### [ReferTrack: Referring Then Tracking for Embodied Visual Tracking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20061)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-21 · 👍 53 · ⭐ 26 · `arXiv:2607.20061`

ReferTrack introduces a referring-then-tracking paradigm for embodied visual tracking, grounding decisions in image-space bounding boxes and achieving state-of-the-art single-view performance on EVT-Bench.

- ReferTrack grounds embodied visual tracking (EVT) by first selecting a target from an indexed set of bounding boxes, then decoding tracking waypoints conditioned on this image-grounded decision.
- The model maintains a sliding-window queue of previously selected bounding boxes, injecting their geometric features into the visual history via temporal-viewpoint-bbox indicator (TVBI) tokens to preserve target motion cues.
- Target identification is enhanced through co-training on a custom Refer-QA dataset.
- ReferTrack achieved success rates of 89.4% (single-target), 73.3% (distracted), and 74.1% (ambiguity) on EVT-Bench, matching or surpassing multi-camera baselines on identification-heavy tasks.
- Real-world deployments on legged and humanoid robots demonstrated robust sim-to-real transfer capabilities.

**Why it matters:** This method improves embodied visual tracking performance and robustness for mobile robots using only a single forward-facing camera.

### [Visual Contrastive Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21556)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-22 · 👍 51 · ⭐ 9 · `arXiv:2607.21556`

Visual Contrastive Self-Distillation (VCSD) introduces a new on-policy self-distillation method that removes the need for privileged answers or visual evidence by converting image-content removal into a self-distillation signal.

- VCSD creates asymmetric information by contrasting next-token distributions from an EMA teacher, one conditioned on the original image and another on a content-erased control.
- The token-wise log-probability difference highlights candidates whose likelihood is increased by instance-level visual content, sharpening the teacher's original-image distribution.
- VCSD consistently outperforms matched OPSD on the ViRL39K dataset, improving Qwen3-VL's seven-benchmark aggregate from 62.27% to 67.04% (2B), 71.30% to 73.16% (4B), and 72.51% to 76.26% (8B).
- The method requires no external teacher, privileged answers, visual evidence signals, reasoning traces, or additional inference-time cost.

**Why it matters:** VCSD simplifies on-policy self-distillation by removing external dependencies, making it more efficient and broadly applicable for visual language models.

<small>Yijun Liang, Yunjie Tian, Yijiang Li, Yuqi Jia, Furong Huang, Tianyi Zhou, Di Fu</small>

### [Multi-Turn On-Policy Distillation with Prefix Replay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04763)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-15 · 👍 12 · ⭐ 14 · `arXiv:2607.04763`

ReOPD is an off-environment on-policy distillation method that reuses pre-collected teacher trajectories as replayed prefixes, addressing the prefix trap and improving efficiency for multi-turn agentic tasks.

- ReOPD reuses pre-collected teacher trajectories as replayed prefixes, allowing the student to act at selected steps while the teacher provides dense per-step supervision without new environment interactions.
- Multi-turn on-policy distillation (OPD) introduces a "prefix trap" where making histories more student-on-policy improves relevance but can query the teacher on unreliable histories, creating a two-sided distribution shift.
- ReOPD addresses the prefix trap by treating multi-turn OPD as a reliability-aware prefix distribution design, implemented with a step-decaying sampling schedule emphasizing early, lower-shift prefixes.
- ReOPD achieves comparable or improved accuracy to OPD, uses zero tool calls during student training, and is at least 4 times faster per rollout than OPD across mathematical reasoning and search environments.

**Why it matters:** ReOPD significantly reduces the cost and improves the scalability of on-policy distillation for LLM agents by converting expensive agent-environment interaction into a reusable offline resource.

<small>Baohao Liao, Hanze Dong, Christof Monz, Xinxing Xu, Li Dong, Furu Wei</small>

### [Predictive Divergence Masks for LLM RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10848)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-11 · 👍 11 · `arXiv:2607.10848`

This paper introduces predictive divergence masks for LLM RL, replacing PPO's ratio-based direction criterion with a divergence-based prediction to better align with trust region objectives and improve training stability.

- PPO-style LLM RL uses trust-region masks with a proximity criterion (policy deviation) and a direction criterion (update direction).
- DPPO improved the proximity criterion using probability divergence, but retained PPO's ratio-based direction criterion.
- The proposed predictive divergence mask uses a closed-form prediction of whether the next policy-gradient step will increase or decrease the same divergence used by the trust region.
- Two lightweight top-K estimators are developed for the predictive divergence mask, accommodating truncated vocabulary views in production rollout engines.
- Divergence-based direction is better aligned with realized divergence change than the sampled ratio, improving RL training across model scales and precision settings.

**Why it matters:** This method enhances the stability and effectiveness of reinforcement learning for large language models by improving the alignment of trust-region mask criteria.

<small>Xiangxin Zhou, Jiarui Yao, Penghui Qi, Bowen Ping, Jiaqi Tang, Haonan Wang, Tianyu Pang</small>

Influence Matching (Inf-Match) is an outcome-centric dataset distillation method that aligns synthetic data's effect on converged parameters with the full dataset's effect using a linear-time, differentiable influence estimator.

- Aligns final model convergence outcomes rather than process surrogates like per-step gradients or training trajectories.
- Uses a fully differentiable, sample-level influence estimator that eliminates inverse-Hessian products and convexity assumptions.
- Runs in linear time by unrolling optimization dynamics and applying a first-order Taylor approximation.

- Scales to vision-language distillation on Flickr30K, outperforming NCFM by 2.5% on image/text retrieval tasks with 200 to 1000 synthetic samples.

**Why it matters:** By directly matching final parameter shifts rather than step-by-step training heuristics, Inf-Match improves dataset distillation efficiency and accuracy across both classification and vision-language tasks.

MetaEvolve cultivates LLM self-evolution meta-skills using a coding data synthesis pipeline, evolution-aware RL with continuous execution rewards, and inference-time evolutionary search.

- Combines program correctness and execution efficiency into a continuous fitness score derived from program execution.
- Synthesizes evolution trajectories containing current programs, fitness scores, and attempt histories to serve as RL training data.
- Uses RL with verifiable rewards from test case execution to teach multi-round refinement and self-reflection.
- Outperforms top baselines by 10.01% in-distribution, 24.12% out-of-distribution on coding benchmarks, and achieves 46.9% relative gain on open-ended algorithm optimization.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that training LLMs on domain-specific execution feedback develops generalizable meta-skills that transfer effectively to open-ended, out-of-domain optimization problems.

<small>Shujin Wu, Cheng Qian, Xiusi Chen, Heng Ji</small>

### [REPREC: Representation Driven Parameter-Efficient Recommendation System](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24845v2)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-24 · `arXiv:2607.24845v2`

REPREC introduces a parameter-efficient sequential recommendation framework that uses a lightweight MLP injector to map fixed-size embeddings from a frozen encoder into soft tokens for a frozen LLM.

- Trains only a lightweight MLP injector while keeping both the pretrained sequential encoder and the LLM backbone entirely frozen.
- Outperforms LoRA fine-tuning across multiple benchmark datasets, showing strong gains for casual and core users in low-data regimes.
- Maintains 85-100% of LoRA's performance when trained on short prompts and evaluated on long contexts, while decreasing per-epoch training time by an average of 1.51X.

**Why it matters:** It enables modular, low-cost deployment of LLM-based recommendation systems by eliminating the need to fine-tune large backbones or modify existing model architectures.

### [Evaluating and Mitigating the Misguidance Effect of Buggy Code in LLM-Generated Unit Tests](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22883v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-24 · `arXiv:2607.22883v1`

A new paper introduces a metric for the misguidance effect of buggy code on LLM-generated unit tests and proposes a specification-based prompting paradigm to restore test effectiveness.

- Prompting LLMs with buggy code produces a twofold negative impact: it increases misguided tests that assert erroneous behavior and suppresses effective bug-finding tests.
- Internal model analysis shows that buggy code directly skews an LLM's preference toward generating tests that match the code's flawed logic.
- The proposed mitigation paradigm replaces the code under test in the prompt with an LLM-generated specification docstring.
- Specification-based prompting reduces misguided tests, increases effective tests, and improves multi-round, feedback-driven test generation for both buggy and bug-free code.

**Why it matters:** Replacing buggy code with specification docstrings in LLM prompts prevents models from validating software bugs and improves the quality of automated test suites.

<small>Junda Zhao, Shurui Zhou, Eldan Cohen</small>

### [Do Coverage and Mutation Scores of LLM-Generated Test Suites Correlate with Their Effectiveness? (Replicability Study)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22880v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-24 · `arXiv:2607.22880v1`

A replication study shows that coverage and mutation scores for LLM-generated tests correlate with bug detection in bug-free regression settings, but fail as reliable indicators when code-under-test contains pre-existing bugs.

- Re-evaluates findings from Inozemtseva et al. and Papadakis et al. on LLM-generated test suites across diverse language models.
- Finds that test suite size is not a dominant confounder for correlations between proxy metrics and real-bug detection in LLM-generated tests.
- Shows proxy metric validity is context-dependent: useful for comparing models on bug-free code, but unreliable for detecting pre-existing bugs in code-under-test.

**Why it matters:** It establishes clear boundaries for when researchers and developers can trust traditional coverage and mutation metrics to evaluate LLM test generation tools.

<small>Junda Zhao, Shurui Zhou, Eldan Cohen</small>

### [Spatial-IQ: Deconstructing Spatial Intelligence via Hierarchical Capability Tests](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22864v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-24 · `arXiv:2607.22864v1`

Spatial-IQ introduces a hierarchical framework and 80,000-example synthetic dataset to diagnose MLLM spatial reasoning failures across 9 developmental sub-tasks.

- Deconstructs 3D stacked object counting into 9 perceptual and cognitive sub-tasks based on human spatial cognition, including mental rotation probes.
- Dataset contains ~80,000 procedurally generated 3D structures with per-task ground truth created using NVIDIA Isaac Sim.
- Evaluates models across free-response text, multiple-choice images, and image editing formats alongside human baselines.
- Reveals that top models frequently rely on shortcuts, succeeding at the main counting task while failing foundational lower-level sub-tasks.
- Demonstrates that training with hierarchical chain-of-thought supervision and RL with verifiable rewards improves spatial consistency and accuracy.

**Why it matters:** It exposes model shortcuts and provides a structured training signal to systematically improve visual reasoning in multimodal AI.

<small>Patrick Rim, Tom Long, Ekta Prashnani, Ruth Rosenholtz, Ben Boudaoud, Peter Xenopoulos, Alex Wong, Joohwan Kim</small>

### [Agentic Autoresearch for CT Reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22824v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-24 · `arXiv:2607.22824v1`

An autonomous LLM agentic loop implemented, tuned, and benchmarked 26 CT reconstruction methods, creating a 969-parameter compact solver that matches top-tier performance using 0.4% of the leading model's parameters.

- The LLM agent operated autonomously by editing solvers, running cluster jobs, reading a calibrated FBP headroom metric, and iteratively revising across Mayo low-dose and 128-view sparse-view tasks.
- Evaluating noiseless-trained models on noisy inputs (I_0 = 10^5 photons) inverted task rankings: the noiseless champion (headroom 0.89) dropped to 0.00, whereas a learned primal-dual method rose from 0.72 to 0.93.
- Retraining methods on matched noise restored rank alignment with the clean leaderboard, improving Spearman rho from 0.04 to 0.61.
- The agent synthesized existing approaches into a 969-parameter solver that performs within 1% of the top Mayo benchmark tier.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that LLM agents can independently execute complex algorithm research and proves that standard noiseless CT benchmarks fail to predict real-world model performance under noise.

<small>Andreas Maier, Lucas Kachelriess, Siming Bayer, Yixing Huang, Yan Xia, Amber Simpson, Moritz Zaiss</small>
