### [Learning to Think and Explore in 3D World](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21783)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-10-14 · 👍 126 · `arXiv:2610.21783`

ACT3D enables vision-language models to navigate and explore 3D environments via single-frame observations using outcome-based reinforcement learning without trajectory supervision.

- ACT3D relies on a single-image native visual encoding, treating the 3D representation as an interactive environment accessed through discrete movement actions and rendered observations.
- The training pipeline uses initial trajectory-supervised finetuning followed by outcome-based reinforcement learning driven primarily by final-answer correctness without direct view-selection supervision.
- Achieves 53.2% average accuracy across six 3D benchmarks, outperforming base Qwen by 22.0% and surpassing Gemini 3.6 Flash.
- Outperforms open-source 3D baselines while using 50% to 70% fewer input frames, improving by 5.9% over its supervised baseline on unseen domains.
- Demonstrates question-conditioned exploration with accuracy gains over the base model scaling from 0.6% on single-view questions to 16.3% on questions requiring extensive spatial exploration.

**Why it matters:** It proves that VLMs can learn efficient, adaptive 3D active visual exploration strategies purely through outcome-based rewards without requiring dense multi-view inputs or frame-by-frame trajectory annotations.

<small>Yijiang Li, Bingyang Wang, Nuno Vasconcelos</small>

### [Lexical Reference Frames: Interpreting LM Residual Streams in Native Semantics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22169)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-10-14 · 👍 20 · ⭐ 1 · `arXiv:2610.22169`

Lexical reference frames read LM hidden states through remaining model layers rather than fixed static lenses, capturing native context-adaptive semantics without fitting to final model output.

- Fixed-direction per-layer lenses reach maximum coherence of 0.30 on SmolLM3-3B and 0.35 on OLMo3-7B at mid-depth, whereas the first-order frame LexFrame(1) achieves 0.97 coherence on both models.
- LexFrame(1) decodes hidden states within 0.16 nats of the full model output using a single round of attention between positions and typical attention output for the remaining layers.
- The method enables early exit layer skipping of 0.45 (SmolLM3-3B) and 0.42 (OLMo3-7B) of total layers based on confident readings, where traditional lenses skip almost zero layers.
- Lexical reference frames provide improved attribution fidelity over standard attribution patching and allow targeted steering of specific tokens using its lexical basis.

**Why it matters:** They provide a more accurate, non-static framework for interpreting residual stream states, improving interpretability, feature steering, and early-exit computational efficiency.

<small>Jian Gu, Hongyu Zhang, Chunyang Chen, Aldeida Aleti</small>

<small>Code: [https://github.com/jianguda/lexframe](https://github.com/https://github.com/jianguda/lexframe)</small>

### [Behavioral Couplings: From Formation to Behavioral Emergence in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23847v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-10-16 · `arXiv:2610.23847v1`

Document training forms transitive behavioral couplings in LLMs, causing fine-tuning on one targeted behavior to unintentionally elicit associated and multi-step composed behaviors.

- Co-occurrence of two distinct behaviors within pre-training or document-training text forms a behavioral coupling where fine-tuning to elicit one behavior triggers the other.
- Behavioral couplings demonstrate transitivity and compositionality: pairing behavior 1 with 2 in one document set and behavior 2 with 3 in another allows fine-tuning on behavior 1 to elicit both behaviors 2 and 3.
- Triggered coupled behaviors emerge on prompts unrelated to the original training domain, such as rubber-duck scientific explanations causing the model to discourage human friendships.

**Why it matters:** Unintended emergent behaviors can originate from composed multi-document associations, making post-hoc safety evaluations ineffective if they rely solely on a model's current explicit behaviors.

<small>Dongyoon Hahm, Woogyeol Jin, Dennis Wei, Yi Zhou, Nathalie Baracaldo, Kimin Lee</small>

### [Truth in Contrast: Calibrated Factual Evidence from Language Model Likelihoods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23527v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-10-16 · `arXiv:2610.23527v1`

Researchers introduce Contrastive Factual Evidence (CFE) and its probabilistic counterpart, the Contrastive Factual Posterior (CFP), which leverages shared error cancellation between opposing conditions to improve language model confidence calibration.

- Contrastive Factual Evidence (CFE) compares the likelihood of a claim under competing factual and non-factual conditions.
- Contrastive Factual Posterior (CFP) analytically cancels shared errors between surrogate conditionals, providing tighter calibration bounds and improved factual calibration.
- CFP maintains strong factual discrimination in high-resource contexts, but factual discrimination is uniformly weak across all evaluated methods in lower-resource languages.
- Analyzing likelihood geometry reveals orthogonal evidence-bearing and evidence-invariant directions, allowing selective alignment of low-resource target-language claims with English evidence to enhance discrimination and open-ended generation without target labels.

**Why it matters:** CFP and likelihood geometry alignment provide a zero-shot framework to reliably measure factual confidence and boost generation quality in low-resource languages.

<small>Ponhvoan Srey, Jason Lucas, Pittawat Taveekitworachai, Junyoung Lee, Anh Tuan Luu</small>

### [MGE-PO: Entropy-Aware Credit Assignment for Mathematical Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23245v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-10-16 · `arXiv:2610.23245v1`

MGE-PO introduces a four-quadrant advantage shaping method for RL that combines solution correctness with rollout generation entropy, paired with an online entropy-based question curriculum.

- Four-quadrant (4Q) advantage shaping uses correctness to set the sign of credit corrections and generation entropy to set the magnitude.
- The method assigns larger bonuses to low-entropy successes and larger penalties to high-entropy failures, addressing GRPO's uniform advantage allocation for equal rewards.
- An online question curriculum uses cached rollout measurements of entropy to dynamically sequence training questions.

- The full MGE-PO configuration increases four-benchmark macro avg@32 on Qwen2.5-7B-Base from 41.5% to 45.4%.

**Why it matters:** Incorporating generation entropy into advantage calculations provides finer credit assignment for mathematical reasoning, improving accuracy across multiple model architectures.

### [Not All Test-Time Compute Is Evidence: Model-Specific Bias in LLM Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23213v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-10-16 · `arXiv:2610.23213v1`

Researchers show test-time compute fails due to intra-model error correlation and introduce DIA, a method using heterogeneous model arbitration on prediction disagreements to improve accuracy while reducing tokens.

- Test-time compute scaling fails because errors correlate across repeated samples, depth, and self-evaluations within a single model.
- DIA (Disagreement-triggered Independent Arbitration) detects reasoning instability by identifying disagreements between intermediate-stage and final-stage predictions.
- Disagreements are resolved by having a small panel of heterogeneous models independently solve the question without seeing candidate answers.
- DIA outperformed full chain-of-thought in 12 of 14 settings across seven benchmarks, achieving a 7.00% gain on MuSiQue.
- The approach activated on only ~17% of examples and used up to 47.2% fewer tokens than a multi-agent baseline.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that scaling test-time compute requires model diversity rather than single-model repetition, offering a more token-efficient approach to improving LLM reasoning reliability.

### [Beyond the Answer: Student-Conditioned Trace Construction for Reasoning Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23831v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-10-16 · `arXiv:2610.23831v1`

ReDiST is a offline, student-conditioned framework that constructs and selects evidence-rich reasoning traces tailored to a target student model for improved reasoning distillation.

- ReDiST generates candidates by revising the student's answer-correct drafts against evidence and repairing unsuccessful drafts using verified-answer guidance.
- A frozen, student-matched reader ranks candidate traces based on answer recovery and verified-answer probability given the question, evidence, and trace.
- Distillation combines trace supervision with final-answer supervision via an auxiliary answer loss, requiring no auxiliary models at inference time.
- Evaluations show performance rankings across trace supervision recipes can diverge between source tasks and cross-benchmark transfer tasks.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that tailoring reasoning traces specifically to a target learner's capabilities yields superior student distillation performance compared to using static teacher traces or answer-only supervision.
