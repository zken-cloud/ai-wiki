### [Does On-Policy Distillation Really Distill? From Noisy Teacher to Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31046)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-30 · 👍 65 · ⭐ 1 · `arXiv:2608.31046`

Analysis reveals on-policy distillation works primarily by suppressing low log-probability tokens rather than distilling teacher knowledge, motivating On-Policy Self-Adaptation, a supervision-free method that outperforms distillation.

- Quantitative analysis shows teacher supervision in on-policy distillation (OPD) contains substantial noise that increases with teacher scale, yet student performance remains insensitive to this noise.
- OPD performance gains concentrate on suppressing low log-probability tokens, a mechanism reproducible using a single fixed negative advantage without teacher input.
- On-Policy Self-Adaptation (OPSA) is introduced as a supervision-free method that uses entropy-adaptive negative advantages to suppress tail tokens and redistribute probability mass among head tokens.
- OPSA improves Qwen3-1.7B Avg@32 on AIME24 by 35.41 points (a 263% relative gain) and outperforms standard OPD by 16.77 points on the same benchmark.

**Why it matters:** Language models can achieve self-improvement and math performance gains without relying on external teacher models or dense token-level reward supervision.

<small>Yi Ding, Ruqi Zhang</small>

### [CARVE: Verified Expansion for Variable-Length Generation in Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30922v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-31 · `arXiv:2608.30922v1`

CARVE is a training-free decoding algorithm that enables variable-length generation in masked diffusion language models by dynamically expanding the canvas only when verified via counterfactual stability testing.

- Overcomes fixed canvas length limitations in masked diffusion LMs by inserting additional (MASK) positions during inference.
- Uses a counterfactual stability check that measures Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence between predictions on the original canvas and a candidate expanded canvas.
- Retains newly inserted mask tokens only when they yield low JS divergence on aligned unresolved positions.
- Applies without retraining to both full-canvas and blockwise diffusion decoders across code generation and mathematical reasoning tasks.
- Reduces total inference compute by up to 50% FLOPs compared to fixed-length decoding while improving accuracy.

**Why it matters:** It eliminates the need to guess exact target output lengths prior to generation, reducing FLOP costs and preventing truncated output or degraded accuracy in diffusion LMs.

<small>Wail Bouhedja, Amr Mohamed, Guokan Shang</small>

### [Faithfulness Is Not Free: Auditing Offline KV-Cache Quantization in Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30996v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-08-31 · `arXiv:2608.30996v1`

A study on offline KV-cache quantization in RAG reveals that INT4 compression severely damages response faithfulness—causing over 90% negative faithfulness shifts among correct answers—despite INT8 remaining near-lossless.

- Evaluated Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct under INT8 and INT4 KV-cache quantization using RGB and HotpotQA benchmarks.
- Faithfulness was assessed using a hallucination detector, NLI entailment, and an LLM judge to differentiate groundedness from factual accuracy.
- INT8 quantization shows near-lossless performance across both accuracy and faithfulness metrics.
- INT4 quantization causes negative faithfulness shifts in over 90% of cases where the answer remains correct, demonstrating that standard accuracy metrics mask grounding degradation.
- Faithfulness loss under INT4 accelerates under noisy retrieval conditions and when processing higher numbers of retrieved context chunks.

**Why it matters:** Deploying INT4 KV-cache compression in production RAG systems risks ungrounded hallucinations that standard accuracy benchmarks fail to detect.
