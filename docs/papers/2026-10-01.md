### [UniEvo-VL: An On-policy Self-Distillation Training Recipe for Multimodal Model Self-improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38721)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-29 · 👍 150 · `arXiv:2609.38721`

UniEvo-VL enables unified multimodal models to self-improve image generation via an on-policy self-distillation framework that minimizes per-state divergence between a critique-conditioned teacher and a vanilla student context.

- Uses a single multimodal model as both teacher and student, where the teacher receives privileged self-critique feedback while the student receives only the baseline prompt.
- Applies on-policy training by minimizing the per-state divergence between the teacher and student denoising diffusion distributions along student-sampled trajectories.
- Improves the Qwen-image-2512 base model from 0.747 to 0.808 on GenEval and from 32.97 to 35.53 on GenEval2 Soft-TIFA.
- Demonstrates that integrating stronger external critics like GPT5.6-Luna raises the model's self-improvement performance ceiling, though gains vary across tasks such as text-rendering.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates a viable recipe for recursive test-time self-improvement in multimodal models without requiring external supervision or larger dedicated teacher models.

### [False Frontiers: Diagnosing and Mitigating Co-Cheating in Self-Evolving Search Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39102)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-29 · 👍 113 · `arXiv:2609.39102`

CrossFit mitigates co-cheating in self-evolving search agents by cross-partitioning source documents between the proposer and solver, preventing false reward signals and improving downstream accuracy.

- Co-cheating occurs when jointly optimized proposers and solvers agree on incorrect pseudo-labels, causing internal rewards to rise while external correctness stagnates.
- Multi-sample verification (MSV) reduces false agreement by querying models with and without sources, but adds six generations per candidate and leaves residual co-cheating.
- CrossFit splits source documents into groups A and B, scoring questions from A with a solver trained on B (and vice versa) to break same-source feedback loops.
- In Qwen3.5-4B and 9B tests, CrossFit reduced false-agreement mass to 3.0% and 3.7% (compared to MSV's 5.7% and 7.2%), and down to 0.4%/0.1% when isolating feedback ancestry.
- CrossFit outperformed standard coupled self-evolution by up to 8.8 points and Search-R1 by up to 8.7 points across seven downstream benchmarks.

**Why it matters:** It prevents self-evolving AI models from reinforcing shared errors, ensuring that internal training rewards reflect actual performance gains on downstream tasks.

<small>Meijia Chen, Hao Li, Zheng Lu, Hongshan Lin, Junbai Tian, Yichen Liu, Zijun Tian, Yufan Zou</small>

`reinforcement-learning` `search-agents` `self-evolution` `ai-alignment` `pseudo-labeling`

### [Mid-Harness: Scaling Actions Between Model and Harness for Terminal Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39982)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-29 · 👍 68 · `arXiv:2609.39982`

Mid-Harness scales test-time compute by sampling and verifying candidate actions at the model-harness boundary before execution without altering the underlying action generator or environment harness.

- Mid-Harness intercepts model outputs to sample candidate actions and apply a verifier, forwarding only the selected action to the execution environment.
- On TerminalBench-Lite with a TMAX-9B generator, using a GPT-5.6 Sol verifier with 8 sampled actions increases Pass@1 from 50.00% to 68.03%.
- When using TMAX-9B as its own verifier, pairwise verification outperforms alternative verification mechanisms.
- Distilling GPT-5.6 Sol verifier responses into TMAX-9B improves Pass@1, and combining action scaling with trajectory scaling yields higher success at lower estimated token cost than trajectory scaling alone.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that allocating test-time compute to verify individual actions before execution prevents irreversible environment state corruption, outperforming simple trajectory-level scaling in efficiency and success rate.

### [It's Not What the Image Shows: Irrelevant Context Destabilises VLM Judges Without Informing Them](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37863)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-28 · 👍 14 · `arXiv:2609.37863`

The Misleading-Image Stress Test (MIST) benchmark reveals that VLM judges shift their labels when shown irrelevant images, driven by image presence rather than visual content or meaning.

- Across 13 VLM judges tested on MIST, adding an aligned image changed 20.5% of labels and a misleading image changed 19.4%, despite guidelines instructing models to classify text alone.
- Only 37% of label shifts between aligned and misleading images moved toward the sense depicted in the image, showing the effect is non-directional destabilization rather than persuasion.
- Image presence caused more label instability (19.4%-20.5%) than removing the ignore-the-image prompt instruction entirely (11.6%).
- While the destabilization effect was smaller in judges that passed the alt-test (7 models) than those that failed (6 models), it persisted across all 13 models without altering overall human agreement.

**Why it matters:** Evaluating VLMs as human-surrogate judges is highly sensitive to incidental visual context, meaning substitutability assessments reflect evaluation setups as much as underlying model capabilities.

<small>Nagham Omar, Mahmoud Jabarin, Kinan Ibraheem, Lotem Peled-Cohen</small>

### [Unmask the State: When Does State Adaptation Matter for Masked Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33355)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-26 · 👍 14 · `arXiv:2609.33355`

Researchers analyzed state adaptation in Masked Diffusion Language Models across five inference axes, demonstrating that selective, detector-driven adaptation captures significant oracle gains while modifying only a fraction of generation states.

- Inference choices in Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDMs) are categorized across five distinct axes: score, cardinality, region, commitment, and planning.
- Adaptation opportunity is defined as the one-step utility advantage of the optimal candidate action over a fixed action selected via validation.
- Across three MDMs and ten tasks, adaptation opportunities proved highly heterogeneous, occurring in concentrated and predictable regimes rather than uniformly.
- A lightweight detector calibrated on validation prompts captured 56.9% of the candidate-set oracle opportunity on LLaDA-8B constrained JSON filling by adapting only the top 10% of states.

**Why it matters:** Selectively adapting decoding strategies at specific high-opportunity states enables more efficient and effective MDM inference compared to applying static unmasking strategies or uniform adaptation.

### [PivotOPD: Learning to Recover from Pivotal Mistakes in Multi-Turn Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40285v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-09-30 · `arXiv:2609.40285v1`

PivotOPD is an on-policy distillation framework that combines preventive distillation via reverse KL and recovery distillation via forward KL to help multi-turn language agents avoid and recover from early pivotal mistakes.

- Over half of failed agent rollouts across Qwen3 models (8B to 235B) contain an early pivotal mistake that moves the agent farther from task completion, though many remain recoverable.
- Preventive distillation uses a teacher's gold action with reverse KL at the pivotal turn to steer the student model away from making the mistake.
- Recovery distillation uses forward KL on teacher-provided recovery actions over subsequent turns to teach recovery behaviors that student models rarely sample naturally.
- PivotOPD outperforms 13 baselines across ALFWorld, WebShop, Search-based QA, and SWE-Bench Verified, achieving a +5.5% gain on ALFWorld with a Qwen3-1.7B student and a +3.2% resolve rate increase on SWE-Bench Verified with a Nemotron-3.5 student.

**Why it matters:** It mitigates compounding errors in multi-turn AI agents by providing targeted teacher supervision that trains models to both prevent critical errors and successfully recover when they occur.

### [GrammarRL: Effective Grammar-Constrained Decoding via Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39869v1)

GrammarRL uses label-free reinforcement learning with self-supervised direct and reverse likelihood rewards to adapt Llama models to grammar-constrained decoding without annotated data or beam-search inference overhead.

- Combines a direct likelihood reward (output given input) and a reverse reconstruction reward (input given output) to preserve semantic quality.

### [Learning Steganography Is Easy, Learning Steganographic Reasoning Is Hard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39838v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-09-30 · `arXiv:2609.39838v1`

A comparative study shows that steganographic reasoning is significantly harder for LLMs to learn than steganographic messaging or encoded reasoning, typically requiring supervised fine-tuning except when cover tasks make information hiding convenient.

- Steganographic reasoning is learned primarily under supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for most tasks, whereas steganographic messaging and encoded reasoning emerge across reinforcement learning (RL), in-context learning, and SFT.
- Even when using SFT, training LLMs to perform steganographic reasoning requires at least twice as much training as steganographic messaging, failing entirely for several model-task combinations.
- If a cover task makes hiding information particularly convenient, models can successfully learn steganographic reasoning under all three elicitation methods (RL, in-context learning, and SFT).
- Learning steganographic messaging or encoded reasoning does not imply that an LLM has learned or can perform steganographic reasoning.

**Why it matters:** Chain-of-thought AI oversight remains viable for most scenarios because steganographic reasoning is difficult to elicit, though convenient cover tasks still pose a bypass risk.

### [Synthetic Pre-pretraining Survives Scale, but Not as a Grammatical Prior](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39827v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-09-30 · `arXiv:2609.39827v1`

A comprehensive study reveals synthetic pre-pretraining (PPT) maintains token efficiency gains at scales up to 7B parameters and 100B pre-training tokens, but the benefit stems from long-range retrieval improvements rather than a grammatical prior.

- Evaluated PPT across five tasks, four pre-training data mixtures, four model sizes (500M to 7B parameters), and budgets up to 100B tokens.
- PPT saved at least 21B pre-training tokens at the 3B parameter scale while improving downstream performance.
- Downstream gains do not correlate consistently with grammatical acceptability, contradicting prior hypotheses that PPT provides a grammatical inductive bias.
- Performance improvements from PPT are driven by tasks that enhance long-range retrieval and remain robust across data mixtures, diminishing only when web text is entirely omitted.

**Why it matters:** Synthetic pre-pretraining task design should shift from mimicking natural language grammar to optimizing long-range retrieval capacities to maximize token efficiency at scale.

### [Stress-Testing LLM Lie Detectors: Role-Play Failures and Spurious Correlations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39807v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-09-30 · `arXiv:2609.39807v1`

Researchers evaluated eight LLM lie detection probes against 8,916 anti-factual persona responses, revealing that existing probes fail under role-play and instead track confounded concepts like instruction compliance or response likelihood.

- Evaluated eight existing lie detection probes using a new dataset of 8,916 human-reviewed, on-policy responses generated by three LLMs under anti-factual personas.
- Probes frequently failed to flag falsehoods when evaluating correct and incorrect answers within the same persona prompt, tracking persona beliefs rather than factual truth.
- Confounder datasets revealed that existing probes rely on spurious correlations in training data, such as response likelihood and instruction compliance.
- Introduced a simple linear probe designed to resist these failure modes, achieving the strongest overall performance across persona and confounder stress tests.

**Why it matters:** Current LLM lie detection probes cannot reliably distinguish reality from role-play, demonstrating that probes must be trained on datasets where truth is decorrelated from confounding operational concepts.
