### [Accurate but Not Humble: Evaluating Epistemic Humility in LLM Agents under Knowledge Conflict](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12360)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-10-07 · 👍 3 · ⭐ 1 · `arXiv:2610.12360`

A new framework evaluates LLM agents on epistemic humility—their ability to recognize, handle, and report knowledge conflicts across execution trajectories.

- Epistemic humility (EH) is evaluated via three trajectory-level behavioral dimensions: Identify, Solve, and Escalate (ISE).
- Testing covers both controlled knowledge conflicts and naturally occurring conflicts in multi-step execution, evaluated against matched no-conflict controls.
- Higher task accuracy does not correlate with greater EH; high-accuracy agents often detect early-step conflicts but fail to resolve them or report unresolved uncertainty in final answers.
- Model-level interventions can increase EH, but doing so frequently causes a trade-off that degrades overall task accuracy.

**Why it matters:** Understanding how agents manage parametric and contextual knowledge conflicts helps developers design systems that reliably communicate uncertainty rather than projecting unearned confidence.

### [On-Policy Distillation Teaches New Skills but Not New Knowledge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09639)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-10-06 · 👍 2 · `arXiv:2610.09639`

Controlled synthetic experiments reveal that reverse-KL on-policy distillation transfers compositional reasoning skills to student language models but fails to expand their factual knowledge.

- Reverse-KL on-policy distillation (OPD) successfully transfers multi-step compositional reasoning across unseen structures but transfers minimal factual knowledge.
- Replacing reverse KL with forward KL restores factual knowledge transfer from teacher to student.
- Student rollouts within the distillation recipe specifically drive improvements in executing multi-step reasoning.
- Empirical validation on factual QA and competition mathematics confirms reasoning gains without expanding parametric factual memory.

**Why it matters:** Developers cannot rely on standard reverse-KL on-policy distillation to inject new factual information into a model, requiring alternative objectives like forward KL for knowledge acquisition.

<small>Yixuan Tang, Yi Yang</small>

### [OnTrack: Real-Time Monitoring and Intervention in LLM Agent Trajectories via Streaming Structure-Aware Optimal Transport](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12375v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-10-08 · `arXiv:2610.12375v1`

OnTrack introduces streaming, structure-aware optimal transport to monitor and intervene in LLM agent trajectories in real time with ~1ms per-step latency.

- Monitors agent trajectories across three data access regimes: full reference access (historical runs and schemas), intermediate access (schemas only), and zero prior knowledge.
- Compares execution steps and dependencies against successful reference runs to detect plan violations, loops, stalls, and redundant tool calls.
- Outperforms content similarity baselines by +0.057 AUROC in ranking failing trajectories based on the first 8 execution steps using SWE-bench trajectories.
- Saves approximately 18% of compute on failing runs using an abort policy, achieving an 83% precision rate (5 out of 6 correct aborts).

**Why it matters:** It enables low-latency (~1ms), cost-effective prevention of runaway token burn and unsafe actions in autonomous LLM agents before execution completes.

<small>Babak Barazandeh, Connor Swanson, Chinmay Kulkarni, Nikhil Mungel</small>

### [Cited but Not Consulted: A Counterfactual Audit of Legal Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12361v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-10-08 · `arXiv:2610.12361v1`

A counterfactual audit shows that while open-weight LLMs regularly cite correct legal authorities, their internal decisions rarely depend on those citations, rendering cited legal chain-of-thought unfaithful.

- Across seven open-weight models (8B-70B), models cited the correct governing authority in 66.7%-100% of generations, but changing the authority only swapped the verdict 0.0%-21.7% of the time on CaseHOLD, 30.0%-76.7% on ECHR and SCOTUS, and 43.3%-50.0% on ContractNLI.
- Internal decision shifts were measured by decoding the model's evolving verdict directly from its hidden states while holding case facts fixed and substituting the named legal authority.
- Model scaling and custom fine-tuning (a LoRA legal-reasoning model) failed to close the unfaithfulness gap between citation accuracy and verdict dependence.
- Red-teaming five core models revealed that adversarial compliance with hidden instructions in case facts (73.3%-96.4%) significantly exceeded authority-swap sensitivity across all model rankings.

**Why it matters:** Generated legal citations serve as unreliable proxies for actual model reasoning, making generated explanations unsafe to use as standalone audit or compliance artifacts.

<small>Saisab Sadhu, Shreeyans Arora, Pratinav Seth</small>

### [Accurate but Not Humble: Evaluating Epistemic Humility in LLM Agents under Knowledge Conflict](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12360v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-10-08 · `arXiv:2610.12360v1`

A new framework evaluates LLM agents on epistemic humility across Identify, Solve, and Escalate dimensions during parametric-retrieved knowledge conflicts.

- Evaluates epistemic humility (EH) using the Identify, Solve, and Escalate (ISE) trajectory-level behavioral framework under both controlled and naturally occurring knowledge conflicts.
- Higher task accuracy does not correlate with epistemic humility; high-performing agents often detect conflicts early but suppress or fail to communicate unresolved uncertainty in final incorrect outputs.
- Trajectory analysis demonstrates that agents frequently lose track of detected conflicts during multi-step execution instead of resolving or maintaining them.
- Model-level interventions designed to increase epistemic humility create a trade-off, frequently reducing overall task accuracy.

**Why it matters:** System designers cannot rely on high task accuracy as a proxy for reliable uncertainty communication, requiring specialized agent harnesses to handle knowledge conflicts safely.

<small>Kaiser Sun, Bernal Jimenez Gutierrez, Hongjun Liu, Jingyu Zhang, Jie Gao, Mark Dredze, Daniel Khashabi</small>
