### [Your Transformer Can Hold Two Thoughts at Once: Evidence of Linear Superposition in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29845)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-23 · 👍 59 · `arXiv:2609.29845`

Researchers demonstrate the Superposition Linearity Hypothesis in Transformers, showing that linearly combined input streams yield a superposition of individual next-token distributions, which can be disentangled during decoding to generate two coherent continuations simultaneously.

- Superposition linearity is an intrinsic structural property of the Transformer architecture rather than an emergent feature of pretraining, and it diminishes as pretraining advances.
- Lightweight fine-tuning can substantially restore linearity by reducing the divergence between the actual superposed output distribution and the average of the individual distributions.
- A novel guided decoding procedure successfully disentangles superposed outputs, permitting two distinct, coherent text continuations from a single model forward pass.

**Why it matters:** This enables the generation of multiple distinct, coherent text streams simultaneously from a single forward pass while establishing foundational insights into linear representation mechanics in LLMs.

<small>Pavel Tikhonov, Anton Korznikov, Matvey Mikhalchuk, Nikita Dragunov, Temurbek Rahmatullaev, Polina Druzhinina, Anton Razzhigaev, Ivan Oseledets</small>

### [Robust Detection of LLM-Generated Text under Contamination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29935v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-09-24 · `arXiv:2609.29935v1`

A statistical framework modeling human and machine text as Huber-contaminated Markov processes establishes theoretical boundaries for LLM text detection and proves that clipping likelihood-ratio tests restores detector consistency under editing and contamination.

- Models text sources as finite-order Markov processes under Huber contamination to establish an exact theoretical boundary where detection transitions from impossible to reliable.
- Proves that raw additive score detectors lose all worst-case power under sufficient contamination, whereas clipped likelihood-ratio tests maintain consistency and vanishing worst-case error rates below the detection boundary.
- Empirically tested across seven detectors, three datasets, three generation models, and the RAID benchmark, showing consistent robustness improvements.
- Demonstrates that clipping increases the log-likelihood-log-rank ratio (LRR) detector's true-positive rate at a 5% false-positive rate by a median of 8.3 percentage points in controlled settings, and 2.1 to 4.3 points on RAID benchmark evaluations.

**Why it matters:** Clipping provides a simple, theoretically grounded modification that prevents adversarial editing and text contamination from completely degrading existing LLM detection algorithms.

Standard scalar alignment metrics fail to detect visual token corruption in Multimodal Large Language Models because anisotropic language model MLP projections induce artificial visual-text similarity.

- Across 13 MLLMs (0.5B to 72B parameters), replacing visual tokens with Gaussian noise severely degrades task performance, yet standard alignment metrics (CKA, SVCCA, MIR, and leading principal-angle cosine) fail to reliably distinguish noise from valid input.
- This 'alignment illusion' stems from the shared language-model pathway, where anisotropic MLP down-projections pull visual and text tokens into common output directions regardless of visual content.
- Because weight-induced alignment is predominantly one-dimensional, the authors introduce the principal-angle gap (PA gap)—the difference between the top two principal-angle cosines—to isolate multi-directional visual structure.
- The PA gap tracks task accuracy under graded visual corruption more accurately than scalar metrics and reveals geometric discrepancies when models evaluate structured but irrelevant images.

**Why it matters:** Evaluating internal visual-text integration using standard scalar alignment metrics can be misleading; researchers must use structural geometric metrics like the PA gap alongside controlled task performance to reliably assess cross-modal processing.

### [Do Audio Language Models Hear and Read Distinctive Features Alike?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30167v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-09-24 · `arXiv:2609.30167v1`

An analysis of six audio language models reveals that speech and text decoders rarely align distinctive feature directions across modalities, with only voicing in Qwen2.5-Omni exceeding random baseline references after statistical correction.

- Evaluated feature representation alignment using mean vector offsets of minimal phoneme pairs across 6 models, 7 phonological features, and 15 languages from 11 families.
- Only the voicing feature in two Qwen2.5-Omni models demonstrated cross-modal directional alignment above the random-pairing reference baseline after multiple testing correction.
- Voicing maintained a consistent cross-lingual direction in the audio stream across 14 languages in three models, with complete language-pair agreement in two models.
- Reference baseline values varied by a factor of seven across different models.
- Cross-modal feature alignment is predicted by model family architecture rather than model size.

**Why it matters:** Understanding that single-decoder audio language models process spoken and written phonological features along distinct directional paths informs how architecture family choice impacts cross-modal representation transfer.

### [Return or Revise? Learning When Revision Helps Retrieval-Augmented QA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30087v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-09-24 · `arXiv:2609.30087v1`

A new framework trains policies on paired correctness outcomes to predict a revision's recoverability, improving decision accuracy on whether to return or revise draft answers in retrieval-augmented QA.

- Evaluates draft answers and candidate revisions under the same judge to observe repair, harm, and oracle gaps before revision.
- A recoverability scorer outperforms a draft-correctness scorer across all nine Llama setup-seed fits, adding 0.23-0.68 accuracy points on average and closing over a third of the oracle gap.
- The policy still incurs a 38-46% rate of harmful revisions.
- Selecting between a draft and a draft-free standard-RAG answer outperforms adding a third candidate-revision option by ~2 accuracy points for Llama and ~4 points for OLMo.

**Why it matters:** Predicting revision recoverability allows QA systems to selectively query retrieved evidence, improving overall answer accuracy while reducing unnecessary or harmful revisions.
