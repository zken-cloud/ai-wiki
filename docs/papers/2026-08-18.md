### [Self-Supervised Visual On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14144)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-13 · 👍 155 · `arXiv:2608.14144`

Self-Supervised Visual On-Policy Distillation (S^2VOPD) creates an informative teacher-student asymmetry without privileged labels or stronger models by distilling an unaugmented visual distribution into a strongly augmented student view.

- S^2VOPD distills the teacher distribution conditioned on the original image on-policy into the student distribution conditioned on a strongly augmented view.
- Symmetric self-distillation degrades performance, whereas asymmetric augmentations improve it up to a peak at moderate strength.
- Augmentations must remain task-consistent, as completely removing question-relevant evidence induces uninformative discrepancies.
- S^2VOPD increases Qwen3.5-4B accuracy from 70.7% to 77.4% across six fine-grained perception benchmarks, outperforming Qwen3-VL (235B) and GPT-5.4.
- The method recovers 96% of the performance gains of privileged-information distillation methods using the same training data.

**Why it matters:** It enables state-of-the-art visual reasoning capabilities in smaller vision-language models without requiring ground-truth annotations, human reward signals, or larger teacher models.

<small>Yijiang Li, Yijun Liang, Yunjie Tian, Bingyang Wang, Ke Zhang, Zhenfei Yin, Di Fu, Philip Torr</small>

- Formalizes hosted model routing as a stochastic process and introduces two metrics: Average Fidelity Loss (AFL) and Extreme Fidelity Loss (EFL).
- AFL measures output distribution discrepancies via a null-bias-corrected, within-window mean coarsened-KL statistic from repeated frozen-context requests.

**Why it matters:** It enables model consumers to audit third-party API quality and detect silent performance degradation in long-horizon agentic workflows without relying on vendor-provided log probabilities.

### [Prior Audit-Repair Context Shifts LLM Verifier Thresholds Toward Leniency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16003v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-08-17 · `arXiv:2608.16003v1`

Including a completed audit-repair episode in an LLM verifier's context systematically shifts its decision threshold toward leniency, reducing false alarm rates on subsequent tasks by 2.8 to 11.5 percentage points.

- Evaluations on human-verified-correct ProcessBench traces showed a 9% to 25% relative reduction in false alarms across 15 of 15 model and wording combinations compared to a length-matched control.
- Signal-detection analysis indicates the shift occurs in the decision criterion threshold (statistically significant in 13 of 15 cases) rather than in discrimination ability (d' showed no significant change).
- Prior audit episodes containing flagged errors reduced false alarms even further, contradicting predictions from accumulated-message negativity asymmetry literature.
- The threshold shift toward leniency persisted at similar relative scales when reasoning was enabled, and a manual audit showed 82% of baseline false alarms were simply incorrect errors.
- Decomposition reveals that the effect is driven by complementary components: repair content and audit verdicts carry different weights depending on the model family.

**Why it matters:** Wiring LLM checkers and fixers sequentially alters the checker's baseline severity threshold, meaning verifier accuracy and strictness depend heavily on prior in-context interaction history.

<small>Parsa Mazaheri, Kasra Mazaheri</small>

- Propagates uncertainty over the trajectory graph to model error accumulation and transfer across multi-step agent histories.
- Combines propagated graph signals with trajectory-level behavioral features and goal-alignment metrics to calculate final confidence estimates.
- Evaluated across six open-source LLMs on the tau-2, Terminal-Bench-2, and GAIA benchmarks, demonstrating improved failure detection and execution accuracy over local UQ methods.

**Why it matters:** By accounting for long-range dependency chains rather than isolated step probabilities, RUPA enables earlier detection of agent failures and improves execution reliability in complex, long-horizon tasks.

### [What Do Compliance Detectors Read? An Audit of Activation Probes and Guard Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16852v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-17 · `arXiv:2608.16852v1`

Researchers identify "rule blindness" in compliance detectors and activation probes, showing their verdicts rely on scenario surface features rather than governing rules, and propose the training-free Internal Compliance Score (ICS) for efficient auditing.

- Tested compliance detectors and activation probes suffer from rule blindness, maintaining unchanged accuracy when governing rules are deleted, permuted, or substituted.
- A policy-conditioned guard correctly cited governing clauses but barely altered its verdict when a clause was swapped with its permissive counterpart.
- A purpose-built benchmark crossing two rules with two scenarios revealed that step-by-step reasoning avoids rule blindness, whereas fast detectors do not.
- The Internal Compliance Score (ICS) provides a retraining-free activation readout calibrated from ten labeled pairs, evaluated via a single projection.
- A bag-of-words model matches the pooled generalization of ICS, and an adaptive white-box attack eliminates the mechanical pass-rate gains achieved when using ICS to rank candidate responses.

**Why it matters:** Deployed AI compliance guards may offer a false sense of security by triggering on scenario context rather than actual regulatory rule violations.
