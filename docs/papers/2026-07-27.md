- Minimal objective loss in native multimodal pre-training follows a predictable compute law, while compute-optimal model sizes and token counts scale as power laws.

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-19 · 👍 16 · ⭐ 45 · `arXiv:2607.18198`

Three-Body Scattering Modeling (TBSM) introduces a novel energy-based approach for one-step generative models, achieving high-quality image generation without adversarial critics or prescribed noise paths.

- TBSM induces sample-level motion via a distributional energy, providing direct regression supervision for a one-step generator.
- The method transforms energy distance into a constant-size per-projectile interaction, attracting each projectile to one real source and repelling it from one independently generated source.

PIVOT replaces the O(L^2) DeepSeek Sparse Attention indexer with a shared prefix scan for query groups, accelerating sparse attention by up to 4x.

- PIVOT exploits the redundancy of per-query scans and long-tailed indexer scores by sharing one prefix scan across a group of nearby queries.
- It aggregates a query group into a single proxy query, performs a shared full-prefix scan to obtain a candidate set, then selects top-k tokens for each query from this set.

**Why it matters:** This method significantly improves the efficiency of token-level sparse attention in production systems without sacrificing accuracy.

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-27 · `arXiv:2607.24893v1`

This study characterizes distributed backdoor attacks in multi-agent LLM systems where payload fragments are spread across agents and reassembled externally, demonstrating early detection capabilities.

- A distributed backdoor attack model is introduced where encrypted payload fragments are hidden in observations, spread across multiple agents, and reassembled/executed externally.
- A prefix detector achieved 99.3% detection of successful attacks with a median of five steps remaining before payload assembly, incurring a 10.3% false-positive rate on benign runs.

### [When LLM Defenses Backfire: Characterizing Safety, Performance, and Cost Trade-offs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24392v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-27 · `arXiv:2607.24392v1`

This study systematically characterizes the trade-offs of LLM jailbreak defenses across performance, over-refusal, and inference cost, categorizing defenses by operational strategy to correlate with side-effect profiles.

- Defenses rarely improve downstream capability, instead trading safety gains against usability and efficiency.
- Rule-based defenses best preserve task performance compared to other defense strategies.
- Highly conservative self-reflective defenses often increase over-refusal on benign inputs.
- Multi-round defenses incur the largest runtime overhead among the strategies examined.

**Why it matters:** Understanding these trade-offs provides guidance for selecting LLM defenses based on specific deployment constraints and desired utility.
