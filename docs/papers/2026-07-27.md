**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-19 · 👍 16 · ⭐ 45 · `arXiv:2607.18198`

- TBSM induces sample-level motion via a distributional energy, providing direct regression supervision for a one-step generator.
- The method transforms energy distance into a constant-size per-projectile interaction, attracting each projectile to one real source and repelling it from one independently generated source.

PIVOT replaces the O(L^2) DeepSeek Sparse Attention indexer with a shared prefix scan for query groups, accelerating sparse attention by up to 4x.

- PIVOT exploits the redundancy of per-query scans and long-tailed indexer scores by sharing one prefix scan across a group of nearby queries.

### [Bigger or Cheaper? Scale and Quantization Effects on Uncertainty Signals in Vision-Language Models Under Image Degradation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24440v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-07-27 · `arXiv:2607.24440v1`

A study on Qwen2-VL models shows that larger 4-bit quantized models outperform smaller full-precision models in accuracy and internal uncertainty estimation under image degradations, despite quantization degrading confidence signals.

- Increasing model scale from 2B to 7B sharply improves internal uncertainty signals via mean token probability (AUROC 0.80 to 0.98), whereas verbalized confidence remains weak (AUROC 0.61 to 0.69).
- 4-bit quantization minimally impacts accuracy (-1.6 points) but degrades internal confidence AUROC from 0.95 to 0.80 and causes verbalized confidence parse rates to drop from 99% to 64%.
- Evaluations across 5,700 predictions under six photographic degradations show the gap between internal knowledge and verbalized confidence widens with model size.
- For a fixed memory footprint, deploying a larger quantized model (7B-4bit) yields better accuracy and uncertainty detection (0.98 AUROC) than a smaller full-precision model.

**Why it matters:** When deploying vision-language models under memory constraints, practitioners should prioritize larger quantized models and rely on internal token probabilities rather than verbalized confidence to guide selective deferral.
