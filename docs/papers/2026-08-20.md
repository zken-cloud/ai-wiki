### [Demystifying Agent Skills: Why They Work-Until They Don't](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14036)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-13 · 👍 149 · ⭐ 6 · `arXiv:2608.14036`

A contrastive study analyzes LLM agent skills to reveal they primarily act as procedural execution anchors rather than knowledge injectors, while identifying retrieval bottlenecks and failure modes.

- Skills function predominantly through procedural anchoring (65.7% of cases) to stabilize execution, compared to explicit knowledge injection (4.5%), outperforming Workflow Memory by 6.06 points in matched comparisons.
- Skill retrieval scales poorly: actual-use precision drops from 29.6% to 3.3% as skill pool size increases from 5 to 100.
- Exact ground-truth skill invocation is neither necessary nor sufficient for downstream task success, as downstream execution remains stable despite confusable distractors impairing offline identification.
- Taxonomy development based on 8,135 normalized trial records and 238 valid open-coded labels identified three high-level categories and twelve skill-use modes.
- Skills fail primarily due to brittle assumptions, incompatible contexts, or insufficient agent adaptation.

**Why it matters:** Understanding that skills act as procedural anchors rather than fact injectors guides the design of better retrieval mechanisms and more robust self-evolving LLM agents.

<small>Zhiyuan Jiang, Fangrui Huang, Hanwen Xing, Xander Wu, Yipeng Gao, Rui Cao, Mengdi Wang, Shilong Liu</small>

### [Test-Time Scaling in the Wild: Why Exploitation, Not Exploration, Is the Bottleneck](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18931v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-19 · `arXiv:2608.18931v1`

A compute-normalized comparison across five open-ended domains reveals that test-time scaling fails due to poor candidate exploitation rather than insufficient exploration.

- Exploration scales effectively across all benchmarks, steadily improving the quality of the best candidate in the generated pool.
- Exploitation fails because reward models correlate weakly (rho_v approximately 0.12) with true candidate quality on state-of-the-art generators.
- Tree search exacerbates exploitation failures via diversity collapse, while iterative refinement only improves performance on one of five benchmarks.
- Fusion (synthesis across candidates) is the only method that consistently outperforms single-sample baselines, though it captures only about 40% of available potential quality.

**Why it matters:** Increasing inference compute via current test-time scaling methods yields diminishing returns in open-ended domains unless reward models and candidate selection mechanisms are substantially improved.

<small>Davide Romano, Kanak Raj, Jerrod Parker, Daniele Giofrè</small>

### [Learned, Then Lost: A Measured Single-Example Counterfactual in Pre-training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19168v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-08-19 · `arXiv:2608.19168v1`

Researchers measured the exact counterfactual impact of a single 194-token training injection across 32 124M-parameter GPT-2 models, finding the memory rapidly decays and leaves no detectable final trace on loss barriers or CKA.

- Tested 32 GPT-2 124M models across four injection conditions (real passage, gradient-matched fabricated passage, random characters, and uninjected control) at step 200 of 9,536.
- The single-exposure passage was initially learned, showing a statistically significant 0.039 to 0.044 nats cross-entropy improvement 50 steps post-injection across 8 seeds (p < 10^-4).

**Why it matters:** Demonstrating that single-example pre-training memory rapidly decays without altering final loss basins provides empirical grounding for counterfactual data attribution and privacy limits in large language models.

<small>Zachary Speck, Asa Shepard</small>

Gradient Mirage defends LLM split learning against gradient matching attacks by disrupting gradient-objective consistency while preserving model utility.

- Breaks gradient-objective consistency across three dimensions: objective (Selective Autoregressive Supervision via a masked surrogate loss), scale (Scale Blinding via randomized multiplicative rescaling), and direction (Directional Privatization via the von Mises-Fisher mechanism).

- Identifies that token-level teacher scores in standard On-Policy Distillation (OPD) misalign with trajectory-level verifier rewards as input context lengths scale.

### [Open-MOPD: Diagnosing and Fixing Capability Imbalance in Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19098v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-19 · `arXiv:2608.19098v1`

Open-MOPD diagnoses capability integration failures in multi-teacher on-policy distillation and introduces three budget-rebalancing mechanisms, improving headroom recovery from 35.6% to 83.4% on SmolLM3-3B-Base.

- Identifies that standard M-OPD captures only 35.6% of headroom due to token-level optimization budget misallocation rather than gradient conflict.

- Provides an open-source, fully reproducible end-to-end post-training recipe designed for an academically accessible hardware budget.

**Why it matters:** It enables the effective consolidation of multiple domain-specialized RL expert models into a single generalist student without suffering severe cross-domain performance degradation.
