### [Stopping and Routing LLM Judge Panels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19802v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-08-20 · `arXiv:2608.19802v1`

A new role-conditioned allocation framework optimizes LLM judge panels by classifying candidate judges into roles to derive cost-effective stopping and routing policies from a small audit set.

- Formulates panel design as a role-conditioned allocation problem using a small labeled audit set, declared data slices, and execution costs.
- Categorizes candidate judges into target-relative roles: copies (no conditional info), complements (global panel improvements), and specialists (slice-specific utility).
- Generates an actionable policy that drops copies, adds complements globally, routes specialists to specific slices, and stops execution when validation gain drops below a cost threshold.
- Evaluated across reasoning, code, safety, preference, reward-model, summarization, and math domain audits.

**Why it matters:** It replaces ad-hoc or full-ensemble judge calls with an auditable, cost-efficient call plan that optimizes inference spending while maintaining evaluation accuracy.

<small>Bin Zhu, Yi Xie, Yanghui Rao</small>

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-08-20 · `arXiv:2608.19611v1`

Researchers developed a statistical model that smoothes low-sample rollout data to efficiently estimate uncertainty dynamics in LLM text generation at a fraction of the computational cost.

- Resampling text sequences at every token or sentence to measure uncertainty dynamics is computationally expensive.

**Why it matters:** It enables researchers to analyze LLM reasoning chains and uncertainty dynamics far more efficiently without incurring the massive computational overhead of extensive sequence resampling.

### [Daedalus-150M: A Convolution-Attention Hybrid Designed for CPU Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20210v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-20 · `arXiv:2608.20210v1`

Daedalus-150M is a 4-bit CPU-optimized language model that replaces full attention with 2-timestep short convolutions in 12 of its 18 blocks, achieving faster decode speeds and higher benchmark scores.

- Architecture retains full attention in only 6 of 18 blocks, using short convolutions with a constant 2-timestep memory width for the remaining 12 blocks to eliminate KV cache growth.
- Trained from scratch on 59.9B tokens, scoring 47.31 on a five-task benchmark and achieving a validation bits-per-byte of 0.8685.
- Delivers 1.76x faster decoding at 2048 tokens context and a 6.3% smaller 4-bit file size compared to an equivalent all-attention baseline trained on identical data.
- Outperforms GPT-2 124M, Pythia-160M, OPT-125M, GPT-neo-125M, and MobileLLM-125M on benchmarks despite using significantly less training data.
- Negative findings include an unmitigated 4-bit quality penalty, an oversized vocabulary, and roughly half of the convolution channels becoming inert without a way to remove them.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that replacing attention with fixed-memory convolutions in most transformer layers significantly improves CPU inference speed and context scaling without sacrificing task quality.

<small>Christos Koutsiaris</small>

### [Interrupting the Loop: Periodic Subject Changes Raise Judged Surprise and Connection in Base Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19893v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-20 · `arXiv:2608.19893v1`

Researchers isolated a specific mechanism in base LLMs where injecting periodic subject interruptions combined with habituation significantly increases judged surprise and connection, though it fails to produce cohesive long-form documents.

- Injecting a new subject every 150-300 tokens alongside habituation increases judged surprise by 1.2-1.4 points and connection by 0.8 points over habituation alone.
- Evaluating windowed text revealed vulnerabilities: LLM judges misattribute experimenter-injected sentences as model output and rate looped model replays from beyond the context horizon as novel surprise and connection.
- Complex additions like salience monitors, in-loop judges, kept context vs. reset context, and connective prompts added no detectable value over simple interruptions.
- On an online bin-packing task, subject interruption increased valid, distinct candidate heuristics three- to fourfold without improving the peak heuristic quality.

**Why it matters:** Periodic subject interruption provides a simple method to boost local creative diversity in base LLMs, while highlighting critical flaws in window-based LLM evaluation protocols for long text generation.

### [Which Eviction Policy Should an LLM Cache Use? A Systematic Study Across Workloads, Capacities, and Encoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20280v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-08-20 · `arXiv:2608.20280v1`

A systematic evaluation using the CLEVER protocol shows that LFU is the strongest simple eviction default for LLM semantic caches, as complex geometry-aware policies offer virtually no improvement over it.

- No evaluated policy (FIFO, LRU, LFU, ARC, GDSF, streaming SISO, or semantic-redundancy) beats LFU by more than 0.041 percentage points across 18 experimental configurations.
- Eviction policy choice still impacts performance: FIFO and streaming SISO underperform LFU by up to 8.67 and 8.55 percentage points, respectively, under tight cache constraints.
- Geometry-aware eviction rules fail to add value due to conditional packing: under exact lookup and insert-on-miss, new entries never have resident neighbors within the hit radius.
- At MiniLM's median nearest-neighbor threshold, only 2.1-3.9% of LMSYS and QQP hits are answer-substitutable, causing raw hit rates of 51-60% to drop to quality-adjusted rates of 1.1-2.2%.
- Similarity thresholds do not transfer reliably between different embedding models.

**Why it matters:** System designers should default to LFU and focus on verifying answer validity and embedding-specific thresholds rather than implementing complex semantic eviction algorithms.

<small>Yash Kulkarni, Shubham Harkare, Arvind Suresh Yogesh Babu</small>
