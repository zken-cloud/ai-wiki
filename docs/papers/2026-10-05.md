### [On-Policy Parameter Update Direction Underlies Generalization in LLM Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36659)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-28 · 👍 39 · `arXiv:2609.36659`

Researchers show that on-policy post-training generalization stems from its dynamically adjusted cumulative parameter update direction, which can be transferred to Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to improve generalization.

- SFT updates parameters in consistent directions, whereas on-policy paradigms continuously adjust parameter update directions throughout training.
- The authors introduce On-Policy direction-constrained Supervised Fine-Tuning (OPSFT), which restricts SFT updates to match the cumulative parameter update directions learned from on-policy training.
- OPSFT allows models to achieve strong generalization efficiency by discovering effective update directions in a few on-policy steps before switching to SFT.
- OPSFT enables models to incorporate high-quality offline trajectories during post-training without degrading abilities acquired through on-policy learning.

**Why it matters:** By constraining SFT to on-policy update directions, developers can combine the superior generalization of on-policy post-training with the high computational efficiency and trajectory utilization of SFT.

### [Source Preference in the Wild: How LLM Agents Favor Items by Source, and How to Reduce It](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03195)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-10-01 · 👍 15 · `arXiv:2610.03195`

Evaluations of 12 LLM search agents across three domains reveal strong source preference bias, where models frequently select inferior items from favored sources over better-fitting items from dispreferred sources.

- Across 12 LLM agent models and three domains, models systematically favor specific sources even when items occupy identical search positions and meet identical user requirements.
- Source bias can override item quality: an item satisfying one fewer requirement is chosen ~66% of the time if it originates from a preferred source, compared to almost 0% in the reverse scenario.
- Explicit source identifiers directly drive choices: hiding source labels mitigates preference, while swapping labels to a preferred source increases an item's selection rate.
- Source preferences stem from training shortcuts rewarding specific sources and missing item information triggering model preconceptions.
- Supplying complete item details or applying counter-preconception prompts successfully reduces source bias.

**Why it matters:** Source bias in LLM agents can distort fair market competition and undermine search accuracy by steering users toward sub-optimal choices based solely on brand or domain identity.

<small>Jonghyun Song, Haewon Park, Jeonghoon Shim, Woojung Song, Yohan Jo</small>

### [Divergence controls entropy in distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03529v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-10-02 · `arXiv:2610.03529v1`

A theoretical and empirical study proving that the choice of divergence objective in distillation acts as an implicit entropy regularizer, with forward KL inflating student entropy and reverse KL deflating it.

- Forward KL (including standard cross-entropy training) mathematically guarantees that the student model's entropy is inflated above the teacher's entropy.
- Reverse KL deflates student entropy relative to the teacher until the student-teacher capability gap becomes too large.
- Lower entropy in on-policy distillation stems specifically from token-level reverse KL rather than on-policy data sampling.
- Interpolating between forward and reverse KL yields smooth entropy shifts early in training, but causes abrupt entropy transitions at convergence.
- In self-distillation, optimal divergence hyperparameters act to compensate for the entropy deflation caused by conditioning on privileged information.

**Why it matters:** Understanding how different divergence functions implicitly control student entropy enables principled selection of distillation objectives for model compression, pretraining, and finetuning.

<small>Nicolas Zucchet, Scott W. Linderman</small>

### [Gains and Collapse in On-Policy Distillation:A Reinforcement Learning Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03185v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-10-02 · `arXiv:2610.03185v1`

Framing on-policy distillation as reinforcement learning reveals that performance gains and length-collapse stem from teacher implicit rewards amplifying student behaviors, rather than expanding student capabilities.

- On-policy distillation (OPD) acts as an implicit reward model where teacher feedback amplifies pre-existing student rollouts without expanding underlying capabilities.
- OPD collapses into overlong, repetitive outputs due to reward hacking, which occurs when implicit teacher preferences misalign with actual generation quality.
- Implicit teacher rewards can severely favor and amplify overlong rollouts even if the teacher model rarely generates such patterns itself.
- Masking unhealthy student responses during training and using SFT initialization effectively mitigate reward hacking and structural collapse.

**Why it matters:** Understanding OPD as implicit reward learning shifts the optimization focus from teacher generation quality to teacher evaluation reliability, providing concrete methods to prevent length collapse during model post-training.

### [The Geometry of Knowledge Accessibility in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03052v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-10-02 · `arXiv:2610.03052v1`

Knowledge accessibility in LLMs has a geometric structure in the query's pre-generation representation space, where accessibility decreases with distance from a central point, forming a knowledge boundary that dictates effective inference interventions.

- Knowledge accessibility correlates with a query's distance from a representation space center prior to text generation, separating accessible queries from inaccessible ones across a knowledge boundary.
- The distance-based accessibility ordering transfers across different datasets even when the underlying representation centers differ.
- Experiments confirm the centered geometry specifically reflects knowledge accessibility rather than general reasoning difficulty.
- Geometric location dictates optimal inference interventions: query rewriting benefits accessible queries, chain-of-thought helps near the boundary, and retrieval-augmented generation yields the largest gains beyond the boundary.

**Why it matters:** This pre-generation geometric signal enables adaptive inference frameworks to dynamically select the most effective intervention (such as rewriting, chain-of-thought, or retrieval) before generating a response.

### [HyperThink: Text-to-Parameter Hypernetworks for Efficient Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03039v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-10-02 · `arXiv:2610.03039v1`

HyperThink uses a lightweight text-to-parameter hypernetwork with a vector-quantized decoder to predict base LLM parameter updates, replacing long inference-time thinking traces with a single query-conditioned forward pass.

- Amortizes multi-step reasoning overhead by mapping input text directly to a small subset of base LLM parameter updates.
- Uses a vector-quantized decoder to constrain parameter updates to a finite set of reusable patterns, enhancing robustness and transfer.
- Trained end-to-end on base model outputs to generate concise solutions and final answers without intermediate test-time reasoning traces.
- Improves the low-latency region of the accuracy-latency trade-off in mathematical and general reasoning tasks.

**Why it matters:** It significantly reduces sequential decoding latency and token consumption in LLMs while maintaining strong multi-step reasoning performance.
