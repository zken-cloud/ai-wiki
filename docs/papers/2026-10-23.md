### [Toward Recursive Self-Improvement in Physical AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29677)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-10-20 · 👍 43 · ⭐ 4 · `arXiv:2610.29677`

A new review frames recursive self-improvement for robot learning, mapping how physical agents can advance from improving task performance to improving their underlying learning processes.

- Digital self-improvement relies on code rewriting and executable tests, whereas physical RSI must manage partial observations, costly real-world trials, non-stationary conditions, and irreversible actions.
- Self-improvement gains persist only if feedback addresses learning needs, the target module can absorb changes, value survives task or hardware updates, and trials remain safe and affordable.
- Current physical studies, mostly in robot manipulation, demonstrate behavioral gains and tool/knowledge updates, but none have yet isolated performance improvements specifically attributable to recursive loops.
- The authors establish evaluation principles and research directions to design physical systems that safely refine their own improvement mechanisms over repeated trials.

**Why it matters:** It establishes a structured framework to evaluate whether physical robots can safely and autonomously optimize their own learning pipelines beyond simple task-level adaptation.

- Identifies that conventional Online Policy Distillation (OPD) uniformly enforces teacher guidance without evaluating whether specific teacher actions improve student performance.
- Introduces AOPD, which weights teacher actions by their counterfactual advantage under the student policy to reinforce beneficial actions and downweight unhelpful ones.

### [Drift Says When, Budget Says How Much: Rate-Controlled KV Re-selection for Long-Form Multimodal Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31433v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-10-22 · `arXiv:2610.31433v1`

Rate-Controlled Refresh (RCR) dynamically re-selects multimodal visual KV cache tokens based on attention drift while controlling refresh frequency per sample, addressing limitations of one-shot and fixed-threshold compression methods.

- One-shot prefill visual token selection achieves only 0.44-0.52 ROUGE-L agreement with uncompressed outputs on long-form generation because visual evidence needs evolve over time.
- RCR uses a GPU-resident low-rank drift detector and candidate-restricted re-selection to determine when and where to re-select, using a per-sample controller to adjust refresh rates across non-comparable drift magnitudes.
- Drift-driven timing in RCR outperforms random timing at the same refresh rate and periodic timing at a higher rate by 2.0-5.0 ROUGE-L points on DetailCaps.
- RCR beats InfiniGen by 1.6-3.8 ROUGE-L points across five of six model-task benchmarks with a 4x smaller GPU detector footprint and performs within 1.7 points of exact per-step re-selection.

**Why it matters:** RCR enables accurate, long-form multimodal generation under constrained KV cache budgets by dynamically updating retained visual evidence without incurring the heavy overhead of per-step retrieval.

<small>Bin Hu, Yingbo Xiao</small>
