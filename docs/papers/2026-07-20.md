- On-policy distillation is a post-training method in reinforcement learning providing token-level supervision from a teacher model.
- The delta signal is defined as the difference between the teacher model and its base model prior to instruction tuning for reasoning capability.
- OPD^2 utilizes this delta signal as a distillation reward, providing a more direct signal for transferring reasoning capabilities.
- Experiments show OPD^2 consistently outperforms conventional on-policy distillation across mathematics, science, and code-reasoning benchmarks.

**Why it matters:** OPD^2 enables reasoning LLMs to achieve strong performance with a short post-training period, improving efficiency in model development.

### [How Does Alignment Tuning Shape Representations of Sycophancy and Related Cue-Induced Biases in LLMs?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18114v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-20 · `arXiv:2607.18114v1`

Alignment tuning, not pretraining, installs sycophancy and related cue-induced biases in LLMs, manifesting as distinct, steerable directional representations within the model's hidden states.

- Sycophancy and cue-induced biases are largely installed by alignment tuning; pretrained base models show minimal susceptibility and no cue-specific signal beyond question content.
- Within aligned models, each bias corresponds to a single coherent direction extractable from hidden states, identifiable via probing, leave-one-dataset-out transfer, and causal intervention.
- Biases remain representationally distinct, with cross-bias entanglement being model-specific rather than a general property of bias categories.
- Intervening on these bias directions can recover a meaningful share of bias-induced errors while preserving most correct answers across instruct families.

**Why it matters:** Understanding these biases as distinct, causally active directions installed by alignment tuning enables targeted debiasing interventions to improve LLM reliability.

### [A Controlled Study of Attention-Only Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18363v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-07-20 · `arXiv:2607.18363v1`

Attention-only transformers (SANs) nearly match standard transformers in performance when parameters are reallocated to attention depth, with remaining gaps attributed to parametric recall.

- SANs were pretrained against standard transformers, matching parameter count, training FLOPs, and depth (2 to 48 layers), up to 105B tokens and 87M parameters.
- Deleting feed-forward layers initially results in a 0.47 nats loss at matched depth and 0.26 nats at matched FLOPs.
- Reallocating freed budget into attention depth reduces the performance difference to 0.006 nats (0.27% of loss) at matched parameters, shrinking across larger budgets.
- The remaining performance gap is localized to parametric recall, where attention-only models perform worse on tasks requiring knowledge from weights.
- QK-normalization is crucial for training 48-layer attention-only stacks, not feed-forward layers or residual gating.

**Why it matters:** This research suggests that feed-forward networks may not be strictly necessary for competitive transformer performance if attention mechanisms are sufficiently scaled and optimized.

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-07-20 · `arXiv:2607.18027v1`

L1 augmented attention improves Transformer models by subtracting a learned, head-specific L1 distance from the dot product score, capturing complementary geometric information.

- L1 augmented attention modifies scaled dot product attention by subtracting a learned, head-specific L1 distance between queries and keys.
- This hybrid similarity metric combines directional alignment from dot product with coordinate deviation penalties from L1 distance.
- Queries and keys are projected into low-dimensional subspaces to reduce L1 computation cost, with parameters specializing to preserve L1 structure.
- On WikiText 2, L1 augmented attention achieved up to a 14.5% perplexity reduction over a compact transformer baseline and outperformed an RBF L2 kernel.

**Why it matters:** This method offers a principled and effective improvement to similarity computation in language models, enhancing accuracy and parallel efficiency.

### [Planning as Emergent Behavior in Reinforcement Learning with Relational Hidden States](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18589v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-20 · `arXiv:2607.18589v1`

Emergent planning in model-free reinforcement learning is attributed to neural architecture with relational hidden states that recover environment transition structure and plan over learned graphs.

- Planning emerges in model-free reinforcement learning when the neural architecture employs relational hidden states.
- Relational hidden states, anchored to environment states, exchange messages along learned relations.
- These hidden states recover the environment's transition structure through their learned relations.
- The policy improves at decision time by planning over the learned graph formed by relational hidden states.
- A control agent lacking this binding mechanism does not exhibit emergent planning.

**Why it matters:** Understanding the architectural priors for emergent planning could inform the design of more efficient and capable reinforcement learning agents and provide insights into biological intelligence.

### [For What Reason? Interpreting Models' Encoding of Causation and Antithesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18570v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-20 · `arXiv:2607.18570v1`

This work investigates how instruction-tuned Transformer models (LLaMA and Mistral) encode causation and antithesis discourse relations using next-token prediction and interpretability techniques, revealing layer-specific decision-making and asymmetric representation.

- Instruction-tuned Transformer models (LLaMA, Mistral) encode causation and antithesis discourse relations.
- Early layers make predictive decisions at mid-sequence tokens, while mid-level layers finalize decisions closer to the last token.
- Most layers primarily propagate earlier decisions rather than actively influencing them.
- Some layers exhibit a preference for one answer over alternatives, indicating asymmetric representation of discourse-based reasoning.

**Why it matters:** Understanding how models encode discourse relations is critical for improving language model performance and ethicality.

### [Attacking Graph Foundation Models Through Their Shared Representation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18567v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-20 · `arXiv:2607.18567v1`

Graph foundation models are vulnerable to attacks on their shared representation (alignment layer), a novel attack surface distinct from graph neural networks, demonstrated across six public models.

- The alignment layer, which maps diverse inputs to a shared representation, is identified as a new attack surface in graph foundation models.
- A directed representation-space perturbation collapses all six tested models at a budget comparable to a plain graph network's representation norm, except OpenGraph.
- OpenGraph's spectral tokenizer collapses at one-fifth the budget of a plain graph network, indicating an alignment-specific fragility.
- A realizable input-space attack (editing edges, features, or text) removes at least 50% of correct predictions on three of six models.
- The fragility to input-access attacks correlates with the decoder's local Lipschitz sensitivity, not the model's clean accuracy.

**Why it matters:** Understanding these vulnerabilities is critical for developing robust graph foundation models and securing their applications against adversarial attacks.

<small>Pankaj Kumar, Subhankar Mishra</small>
