### [OneStreamer: Unifying Perception, Memory, and Proactive Response in Streaming Video Interaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01762)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-30 · 👍 68 · ⭐ 6 · `arXiv:2610.01762`

OneStreamer is a 4B streaming video LLM that unifies perception, memory, and proactive response using Proactive Hierarchical Caption Memory and Proactive State Transition Learning.

- Proactive Hierarchical Caption Memory (PHCM) generates time-grounded local captions and event summaries to provide reusable factual context alongside a recent visual window.

**Why it matters:** It enables video LLMs to perform real-time perception and historical reasoning efficiently without needing to re-process raw historical visual features.

### [A Missing Piece for Trustworthy AI Reviewers: From Benchmarking Rhetorical Robustness to SciCore Review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39027)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-29 · 👍 32 · `arXiv:2609.39027`

Researchers introduced RobustReview, a 1,260-manuscript benchmark evaluating AI reviewer rhetorical robustness, and SciCore, a dual-branch reviewer that averages full-manuscript and extracted science-core judgments to reduce sensitivity to rewrites.

- Rhetorical Robustness is defined as joint stability across content-preserving rewrites and discrimination across different papers.
- Evaluating 30 reviewer configurations on the 1,260-version RobustReview benchmark revealed 'false robustness,' where low rewrite sensitivity stems from score collapse across distinct papers.
- Content-focused prompting failed to consistently improve robustness, and human alignment metrics ranked reviewer models differently than rhetorical robustness metrics did.
- SciCore uses a dual-branch design, combining a full-manuscript judgment with a structured, content-normalized 'science core' judgment to improve stability and discrimination.

**Why it matters:** Evaluating AI peer reviewers for rhetorical robustness prevents automated systems from being gamed through stylistic rewrites that do not alter the underlying scientific content.

### [Make Sparse Rewards Count: Density-Aware Reward Aggregation for Multi-Reward RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00574)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-29 · 👍 21 · `arXiv:2610.00574`

Density-Aware Reward Aggregation (DARA) introduces an adaptive, inverse-square-root density correction to balance multi-reward RL training based on active-group density.

- Identifies that GDPO reward-wise normalization causes signal imbalance, showing advantage energy (sum of squared advantages) is proportional to active-group density.
- Proposes DARA, which calculates batch-level weights using an inverse-square-root density correction to amplify less frequently active rewards.
- Adapts reward weights dynamically per batch without altering the underlying policy optimization objective.
- Reaches high format compliance in tool calling in up to 26% fewer steps and length compliance in math reasoning in up to 65% fewer steps compared to GDPO.

**Why it matters:** DARA accelerates the multi-reward RL training of large language models for auxiliary constraints like formatting and length compliance without sacrificing final model performance.

### [CorrGRPO: Correlation-Normalized GRPO for Multi-Reward Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36820)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-28 · 👍 10 · `arXiv:2609.36820`

CorrGRPO improves multi-reward language model training by normalizing pairwise reward covariances into Pearson correlation coefficients, preventing large-scale rewards from suppressing smaller-scale reward signals during advantage calculation.

- Standard GRPO normalizes total rewards using within-group standard deviation, where variance equals the sum of all pairwise reward covariances, allowing large-scale rewards to dominate the denominator.
- CorrGRPO converts pairwise covariances into Pearson correlation coefficients while keeping the centered total reward unchanged.
- The method balances the influence of differently scaled rewards on correlation-based normalization so advantage magnitudes adapt to reward dependence.
- Empirically evaluated on 0.5B to 8B parameter models across code generation, tool calling, and agent security domains.

**Why it matters:** It prevents scale imbalances when combining multiple reward signals, allowing models to better handle tasks involving complex reward tradeoffs or multi-objective optimization.

### [CARM: Cancellation-Aware Response Masking for LLM Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02039v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-10-01 · `arXiv:2610.02039v1`

CARM is a sequence-level response masking technique for LLM reinforcement learning that applies absolute values to token log-ratios before averaging, preventing signed policy drifts from canceling out during off-policy filtering.

- Standard geometric-mean masking uses signed log-ratios, allowing opposing token probability shifts to cancel out and obscure significant policy drift.
- CARM calculates the absolute value of each token log-ratio prior to averaging, providing a cancellation-aware sequence-level mask.
- CARM theoretically guarantees a joint bound on the proportion of sampled-token ratios exceeding a defined band and their mean log-distance beyond it.
- Empirical testing demonstrated up to a 3.13 percentage point increase in mean@16 on math benchmarks (AIME 2024-2026, BeyondAIME) and a 2.88 point gain in average pass@1 across four code benchmarks over baselines.

**Why it matters:** CARM prevents off-policy optimization instability caused by hidden token-level policy drift, improving the performance of LLM post-training on complex math and coding tasks.

- Adam's first moment smooths parameter updates, yielding high cosine similarity across teachers (0.83) and averaging rules (0.96) despite divergent raw gradients.
- BF16 rounding obscures small parameter updates, retaining identical initial weights in 89-93% of parameters despite 97% changing in FP32 master weights.
- Top-64 intersection KL gradients match full-vocabulary gradients, but downstream performance varies by loss function; math accuracy is 2.6 points higher than sampled-token PG under response averaging, but 2.1 points lower under global token averaging.

**Why it matters:** Understanding how optimization mechanics like Adam moments, precision limits, and loss weighting alter gradient signals allows engineers to better fine-tune multi-teacher distillation pipelines for small language models.

### [Every Ablation Is a Dose: Counterweights and the Semblance of Self-Repair](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02173v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-10-01 · `arXiv:2610.02173v1`

Researchers show that language model 'self-repair' is actually the predictable action of fixed counterweights operating along an affine law, $E_r(\lambda)=\mathrm{own}_r+\gamma_r\lambda$, rather than a dynamic compensatory mechanism.

- Ablations act as uncalibrated points along a signed counterfactual contrast axis $\lambda$, driving a fine-grained unit's causal response according to the affine equation $E_r(\lambda)=\mathrm{own}_r+\gamma_r\lambda$.
- The constant slope $\gamma_r$ determines if a unit counteracts or reinforces a signal, and its magnitude can be predicted directly from the model's fixed weights.
- Across Gemma, Qwen, LLaMA, and Mistral models, 68 of 81 downstream directions (including MLP neurons, OV neurons, and singular directions) followed this affine law.
- In the GPT-2 Small IOI circuit, 7 of 10 reachable heads followed the affine law, with all 7 functioning as counterweights.

**Why it matters:** Understanding self-repair as a static, weight-based affine response simplifies circuit analysis and enables researchers to predict intervention outcomes directly from static model weights.

### [Are We Recovering Mechanisms? Objective-Level Recovery Gaps in Mechanistic Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02098v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-10-01 · `arXiv:2610.02098v1`

Intervention-defined faithfulness objectives misrank circuit candidates by preferring poorer-performing models, creating an objective-level recovery gap that automated discovery algorithms inherit under standard resampling methods.

- Intervention-defined faithfulness, specifically KL divergence, misranks 9.4% to 41.2% of candidate circuit pairs across EAP, EAP-IG, ACDC, and Edge-SP on human-reference tasks under fixed ordinary resampling.
- Misranking occurs even without discovery algorithms, as demonstrated by controlled reference edits.
- Context distortion—where replacing excluded signals alters inputs to retained components—drives these evaluation failures.
- Restoring select signals from intact-model execution fixes 96 of 100 persistent KL misrankings on both validation and held-out prompts without altering the circuits or their original behavioral scores.

**Why it matters:** Improving automated circuit discovery algorithms is insufficient if the underlying evaluation objectives inherently reward incorrect or inferior mechanisms.

<small>Chuqin Geng, Li Zhang, Haolin Ye, Mark Zhang, Luke Zhang, Xujie Si</small>
