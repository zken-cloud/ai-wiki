### [From RLVR to RLSVR: Task Transformation Induces Self-Verifiable Rewards for Open-Ended LLM Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23802)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-25 · 👍 65 · ⭐ 138 · `arXiv:2607.23802`

Reinforcement Learning with Self-Verifiable Rewards (RLSVR) extends RLVR to open-ended tasks by transforming them into self-verifiable proxy environments, instantiated via the multi-agent SpyRL environment.

- RLSVR transforms open-ended LLM tasks into verifiable proxy tasks where environment rules generate automatic reward signals without human judges or reward models.
- SpyRL implements RLSVR through a 'Who Is the Spy?' multi-agent self-play setup using asymmetric information and voting mechanics.
- Because the spy identity is predetermined, the voting outcomes supply deterministic, fully verifiable rewards that correlate with text generation quality.
- Empirical evaluations show SpyRL outperforms existing self-improvement methods on text summarization, creative writing, and mathematical reasoning.

**Why it matters:** RLSVR removes the dependency on expensive, biased human judges and proxy reward models, allowing scalable RL optimization to be applied to non-verifiable, open-ended LLM tasks.

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-27 · 👍 32 · `arXiv:2607.28675`

### [QQWorld: Quantile-Quantile Matching for World Model Regularization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28415)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-29 · 👍 23 · `arXiv:2607.28415`

QQWorld replaces the Epps-Pulley objective in LeWorldModel with quantile-quantile matching to preserve corrective gradients for heavy-tailed latent deviations during world model regularization.

- Epps-Pulley (EP) objective gradients vanish for isolated tail samples, leaving heavy-tailed latent deviations uncontrolled in LeWorldModel.
- QQWorld aligns projected latent samples with rank-matched Gaussian quantiles to maintain strong corrective gradients in the distribution tails.
- Cross-batch QQ expands the sample ranking pool using detached latent vectors from previous batches to manage the bias-variance trade-off.
- QQWorld improves average planning success rates across four control environments while achieving superior Gaussian alignment and thinner latent tails.

**Why it matters:** It prevents heavy-tailed latent distribution degradation in latent world models, leading to more reliable planning performance in control environments.

### [SAF-OPD: Stable Advantage Fusion for On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29209)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-30 · 👍 22 · `arXiv:2607.29209`

SAF-OPD stabilizes the fusion of RLVR and on-policy distillation by applying magnitude and temporal controls to OPD advantages, preventing entropy collapse and enabling student models to surpass teacher quality.

- Identifies two causes of entropy collapse in fixed-coefficient RLVR+OPD fusion: magnitude mismatch (spiking OPD advantages overwhelming RLVR signals) and temporal mismatch (sustained OPD suppressing exploration).
- Introduces a four-stage, low-overhead pipeline for OPD advantages: sparsify-then-compress for magnitude control and warm-up-then-anneal for temporal control.
- Instantiates RLVR using GRPO and tests SAF with Qwen3-1.7B, 4B, and 8B models across seven math reasoning and code generation benchmarks.
- Achieves a 0.51% to 2.70% aggregate score improvement over fixed-coefficient GRPO+OPD fusion across six model-domain settings while maintaining stable training.

**Why it matters:** It enables language models to combine dense token-level teacher guidance with sparse verifiable rewards without training instability or bounded exploration capabilities.

### [Enhancing Rubric-based RL via Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18082)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-20 · 👍 14 · `arXiv:2607.18082`

Criterion-Distilled Policy Optimization (CriPO) uses on-policy self-distillation to resolve Unexplored Criteria and Suppressed Criteria in rubric-based RL without introducing train-inference mismatch.

- Identifies Suppressed Criteria (SC), where satisfied criteria lose optimization signals due to non-positive aggregate scalar rewards, affecting over 57% of training samples (1.8 SC per sample average).
- Eliminates train-inference mismatch from external rollout guidance by using criterion-focused self-teacher models during training while keeping inference guidance-free.
- Mitigates Unexplored Criteria (UC) by applying a localized forward-KL loss against a criterion-injection self-teacher to inject missing behaviors.
- Resolves SC using a counterfactual self-teacher to identify criterion-relevant tokens in negative-advantage rollouts and flip their token-level advantages to positive.
- Achieves superior final performance on medical and science benchmarks while requiring approximately 2x fewer optimization steps than standard rubric-based RL.

**Why it matters:** CriPO enables more efficient and effective reinforcement learning for LLMs on open-ended tasks by preserving granular reward signals without altering the inference pipeline.

<small>Mingxuan Xia, Yuhang Yang, Chao Ye, Shuai Zhu, Shenzhi Yang, Guangcheng Zhu, Yuhang Zhang, Cheng Peng</small>

### [Not All Tokens Deserve Equal Credit: Counterfactual Sensitivity Credit Reallocation for Long-CoT Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27888)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-29 · `arXiv:2607.27888`

Counterfactual Sensitivity Credit Reallocation (CSCR) improves GRPO for long-CoT reasoning by reducing reinforcement learning credit for highly substitutable, counterfactually sensitive tokens while preserving the total credit budget and verifier direction.

- Critique of On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD): Likelihood shifts under opposing outcome conditions (correct vs. incorrect) move in the same direction, showing privileged shifts reflect counterfactual sensitivity rather than true answer alignment.
- Token Sensitivity Disparity: Large likelihood shifts concentrate on highly substitutable surface-form tokens, while problem-specific reasoning tokens remain less sensitive.
- CSCR Mechanism: Downweights advantages for counterfactually sensitive tokens and renormalizes token-level advantages to maintain the original credit budget and verifier-determined reward direction.
- Optimization Stability: Ablations show that moderate downweighting of sensitive tokens yields optimal performance, whereas excessively strong modulation destabilizes policy optimization.

**Why it matters:** It prevents RL algorithm advantages from being wasted on surface-form tokens, consistently outperforming standard GRPO baselines on long-CoT mathematical reasoning tasks without requiring additional policy updates.

<small>Qiangqiang He, Zhongheng Wu, ZiJian Wang</small>

### [Safeguards Based on Copyable Context Cannot Provide Reliable Safety for LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27951)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-29 · `arXiv:2607.27951`

Researchers prove that LLM safeguards using copyable context face a mathematical safety trilemma, preventing the simultaneous achievement of Useful Capability, Reliable Safety, and Open Access without trusted credentials.

- LLM safeguards evaluate safety before knowing the downstream use, allowing attackers to copy benign contexts to access dual-use capabilities.
- When evidence of downstream use is copyable, an exact worst-case floor on attacker assistance exists if useful answers are preserved.
- A safety trilemma establishes that Useful Capability, Reliable Safety, and Open Access are mutually exclusive when relying on copyable context.
- Non-copyable trusted credentials can complement existing safeguards by adding unforgeable information about actual downstream usage to eliminate the risk floor.

**Why it matters:** Current context-based LLM safety guardrails cannot reliably prevent dual-use misuse in open-access settings, necessitating trusted credential systems for high-risk model capabilities.

### [When Model Priors Conflict with Visual Evidence: Mitigating Commonsense-Driven Hallucinations by Selective Prior Calibration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29240v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-31 · `arXiv:2607.29240v1`

Selective Prior Calibration (SPC) mitigates commonsense-driven hallucinations in vision-language models by dynamically subtracting text-only prior preferences from image-conditioned scores without degrading performance on standard commonsense images.

- Commonsense-driven hallucination (CDH) occurs when a model's visual prediction defaults to a text-only prior during counterfactual (CF) visual scenarios, such as predicting five fingers on a six-fingered hand.
- Indiscriminate prior suppression fixes counterfactual errors but degrades accuracy on matched commonsense (CS) images where the prior is correct.
- SPC subtracts instance-dependent candidate prior-preference estimates from visual-conditioned scores, revising predictions only when score patterns strongly support an alternative candidate.
- Experiments show SPC improves accuracy on counterfactual images while preserving performance on matched CS images, generalizing across candidate permutations and non-conflict benchmarks.
