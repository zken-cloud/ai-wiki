### [Recursive Synthesis for Long-Horizon Terminal Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05466)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-04 · 👍 70 · `arXiv:2608.05466`

Recursive Synthetic Terminal Tasks (RST) is a verified synthesis framework that recursively extends, realigns, and validates terminal tasks, producing 37,484 long-horizon agent tasks at approximately $0.05 per task.

- Starting from seed tasks, RST extends reference solutions, realigns verifiers and instructions, and validates tasks in a fresh sandbox before using accepted outputs as seeds for subsequent rounds.
- Over 15 rounds, median task solution lengths grew from 67 to 374 lines, median executed commands rose from 40 to 244, and DeepSeek-V4-Pro pass@4 fell from 90% to 2.5%.
- Synthesis yield and validation rates remained stable through 15 rounds without reaching a scaling ceiling.
- Fine-tuning Qwen3.5 models on rejection-sampled trajectories improved performance on terminal benchmarks by up to 10 points, while PPO lifted Qwen3.5-27B to 49.44% on Terminal-Bench 2.

**Why it matters:** RST provides a scalable, low-cost method to synthesize increasingly complex, verified training data for terminal agents, significantly boosting their performance on long-horizon benchmarks.

<small>Zhongzhi Li, Yucheng Shi, Zongxia Li, Ruhan Wang, Anhao Li, Zixun Huang, Junyao Yang, Lei Ke</small>

### [AgentOPSD: Recursive Self-Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05987)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-05 · 👍 49 · ⭐ 2 · `arXiv:2608.05987`

AgentOPSD is a critic-free agentic RL method that converts sparse outcome rewards into turn-level credit signals using recursive Bayesian belief updates on teacher-student log-probability gaps.

- Aggregates token-level teacher-student log-probability gaps into turn-level evidence.
- Recursively updates a Bayesian belief state in log-odds space to reweight turn-level credit without an auxiliary critic or extra rollouts.
- Identifies pivotal decision turns by calculating the marginal belief revision between consecutive states.
- Achieves an 89.1% success rate on ALFWorld using a Qwen2.5-7B model, outperforming GRPO and self-distillation baselines.

**Why it matters:** It provides dense, turn-level credit assignment for complex multi-turn agent tasks without the computational overhead of training a separate critic network or generating extra rollout trajectories.

### [Learning When to Trust via Selective Context Preference Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06377v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-06 · `arXiv:2608.06377v1`

Researchers introduced the MIST benchmark, the SC2W susceptibility metric, and SCOPE, a DPO-based method that trains language models to selectively trust external context without blindly ignoring valid inputs.

- MIST is a human-annotated benchmark presenting reasoning items across four matched conditions: clean, misleading, correct-context, and irrelevant-context.
- SC2W is a paired metric measuring how frequently a misleading external signal converts a clean-correct response into an incorrect one.
- SCOPE extracts clean-correct and misleading-wrong failure cases to optimize a Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) objective using preference pairs balanced across all four context conditions.
- Universal susceptibility to misleading context was observed across models during the benchmark study.
- SCOPE decreases SC2W while maintaining model accuracy on clean, correct, and irrelevant context inputs.

**Why it matters:** It prevents language models from either blindly following deceptive context or becoming unhelpful by ignoring external context entirely.

### [DASH: Divergence-Adaptive Supervision Horizons for On-Policy Self-Distillation of Reasoning Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06243v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-06 · `arXiv:2608.06243v1`

DASH improves on-policy self-distillation for reasoning models by using adaptive propagation gates to dynamically weight token-level supervision based on how local teacher-student divergences evolve across time.

- Identifies that standard On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) treats local divergences uniformly, ignoring the temporal sequence and discrepancy history of autoregressive rollouts.

- Reuses already-computed teacher and student distributions, achieving performance gains over vanilla OPSD across three mathematical reasoning benchmarks and three model scales with zero extra forward passes.

**Why it matters:** DASH increases the efficiency and accuracy of Dense RLVR self-distillation without introducing any additional computational overhead from extra forward passes.

Unsupervised On-Policy Self-Distillation (U-OPSD) enables LLMs to self-correct without external supervision by distilling pseudo-solutions derived from self-consistency into prefixes of their longest incorrect completions.

- Constructs pseudo-solutions from model generations using majority voting under a self-consistency threshold.
- Conditions a teacher distribution on the shortest pseudo-solution and distills it into prefixes of the model's longest incorrect completion.

### [The Low Frequency Trap: Video Language Models Fail at Simple Event Bookkeeping](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06361v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-06 · `arXiv:2608.06361v1`

A new trace-grounded parametric profiling method reveals that Video Language Models fail at simple event counting, exhibiting a staged temporal failure driven by event representation, count, and frequency.

- Evaluated models across 2,190 controlled videos with executable ground-truth traces across three tasks: bouncing-ball wall contacts, visual blinks, and state transitions.
- At an 80% reliability threshold, Gemini 3.6 Flash counts persistent state transitions up to 12 events (at 0.5 and 1.0 Hz), but shows zero reliable positive-count regions for transient blinking.
- In high-count, high-frequency scenarios, Gemini 3.6 Flash achieves only 0.2% count accuracy and recovers just 18.1% of true events.
- Increasing the visual frame sampling rate improved Bounce Ball count accuracy from 19.6% to 29.3%, but true sequence agreement with ground truth remained at only 3.7%.

**Why it matters:** Aggregate accuracy metrics can mask severe temporal reasoning failures in video language models, as increased frame sampling may artificially inflate final scores without recovering actual event sequences.
