### [Massive Activations in Hybrid Linear Attention Large Language Models: Pre-Attention Spikes and Inter-Spike Plateaus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12149)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-11 · 👍 11 · ⭐ 4 · `arXiv:2608.12149`

A systematic study reveals that hybrid linear attention LLMs exhibit two distinct massive activation morphologies: pre-attention spikes occurring right before full attention layers and inter-spike plateaus persisting across linear attention layers.

- Identifies Pre-Attention Spikes (PAS) occurring immediately before full attention layers and Inter-Spike Plateaus (ISP) extending through intervening linear attention layers.
- Validated patterns across 5 linear attention architectures, 6 hybridization configurations, 5 data domains, and open-source models ranging from 1.2B to 397B parameters.
- Controlled pretraining of GDN-based hybrids up to 1.3B parameters demonstrates early emergence and asymmetric response to output gating.
- Mechanistic analysis attributes PAS to a localized write-sink-cancel process, while ISP persistence results from delayed activation cancellation.

**Why it matters:** Understanding these architectural activation patterns provides mechanistic insight into feature representation and stability dynamics across hybrid linear attention model designs.

### [Algebraic Decomposition Theory for Transformer Length Generalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13433v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-13 · `arXiv:2608.13433v1`

Researchers established a complete algebraic characterization and polynomial-time decision algorithm to determine which regular languages transformers can length-generalize on.

- Identifies that classical Krohn-Rhodes decomposition theory fails for C-RASP because building blocks like flip-flop and simple groups are inexpressible, while C-RASP's unbounded counting cannot be captured by finite semigroups.
- Generalizes classical decomposition theory from finite semigroups to the infinite additive group on the integers, characterizing C-RASP via iterated wreath products of the integers.
- Provides a decision algorithm running in polynomial time relative to the size of the regular language's syntactic monoid.
- Validates the algebraic framework empirically across a broad test suite of regular languages to accurately predict transformer length-generalization behavior.

**Why it matters:** It enables a provable, algorithmic way to determine whether a transformer model can scale to longer sequences on specific formal language tasks without requiring empirical trial and error.

CrEST is a hierarchical credit assignment framework for multi-turn LLM agents that uses a privileged self-teacher to modulate update magnitudes while preserving reinforcement learning's verifier-bounded performance ceiling.

- Resolves inter-turn reward dilution using turn-segmented verified advantages.
- Mitigates intra-turn credit issues via entropy-gated self-teacher modulation of token-level update magnitudes.
- Reduces the teacher's role from dictating update directions to regulating signal intensity, avoiding teacher-bounded ceilings and gradient concentration collapse.
- Outperforms standard RL and distillation baselines on BFCL V3 and WildToolBench, showing peak gains on long trajectories and session-level metrics across two model scales.

**Why it matters:** It enables dense, token-level supervision for complex tool-use agents without suffering from policy collapse or capping performance below the verifier's ground-truth ceiling.

### [DARTree: Speculative Diffusion Decoding with Autoregressive Draft Trees](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13524v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-08-13 · `arXiv:2608.13524v1`

DARTree is a training-free speculative decoding method that extends an autoregressive correction head from draft chains to candidate trees, achieving up to 9.73x lossless speedup over autoregressive decoding.

- Extends a pretrained autoregressive (AR) correction head to diffusion-generated draft trees to resolve unconditioned position-wise marginal distributions.
- Decouples AR-head inference from sequential heap operations by batch-expanding and scoring all nodes at each depth to create a fixed-width tree before applying best-first pruning.
- Accepts up to 12.97 tokens per verification round, representing a 98.6% increase over DFlash and a 27.9% increase over Domino under identical settings.
- Outperforms prior methods in average acceptance length and speedup across seven math, code, and chat benchmarks in four model-temperature configurations.

**Why it matters:** It significantly improves speculative decoding throughput and acceptance lengths without requiring additional model training or architecture modifications.

<small>Tianyi Li, Yaxin Luo, Xinyi Shang, Zhiqiang Shen</small>

### [When Local Variance Optimality Is Not Enough: RoPE-Aligned Q/K Rotations for Dynamic 4-Bit Quantisation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13365v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-08-13 · `arXiv:2608.13365v1`

Researchers proved only per-pair rotations commute with distinct-frequency RoPE, but found RoPE-aligned pairwise rotations increase perplexity compared to full-head Hadamard transforms in dynamic W4A4KV4 quantisation due to limited mixing support.

- Proved the converse result that for distinct frequencies, no single-head orthogonal map commutes with RoPE other than per-pair two-dimensional rotations.
- Derived the analytic rotation angle that minimises maximum channel variance under a position-averaged, pooled-covariance surrogate.
- Found that replacing full-head Hadamard mixing with head-shared pairwise rotations degraded perplexity across four checkpoints in dynamic W4A4KV4 settings.
- Demonstrated via controlled interpolation that increasing mixing support from 2 channels to full-head reduces key (K) range, relative quantisation error, and perplexity degradation.
- Identified a structural mismatch: the local variance surrogate does not align with the tokenwise group-range statistic used by dynamic quantisers.

**Why it matters:** Optimal local variance reduction on structured mathematical surrogates is insufficient for post-training quantisation if the transform's channel mixing support fails to reduce tokenwise group ranges.

<small>Shuhan Wang, Yilin Luo, Nan Xu, Chi Wang Cheung</small>

### [Where You Measure Decides What You Measure: Position Selection in Ablation-Based SAE Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13337v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-08-13 · `arXiv:2608.13337v1`

Researchers show that standard ablation-based SAE evaluation measures effects at tokens dictated by the dictionary itself, introducing high variance that disappears when evaluations use a standardized, fixed token position.

- Standard SAE evaluation measures ablation effects where a latent fires hardest, causing different dictionaries to be evaluated at different token positions.
- Matching Google SAE latents by decoder similarity revealed that identical latent pairs frequently select different token positions for evaluation.
- Evaluating six SAEs trained from a single initialization showed that inter-dictionary variance dropped from 7.6%-11.9% to near zero when measuring at the same token.
- Increasing corpus size sixteenfold worsens position disagreement between dictionaries rather than mitigating it.
- The authors propose a standardized evaluation protocol requiring reported causal numbers to explicitly fix and specify token positions.

**Why it matters:** Without controlling for measurement position, casual evaluation metrics reflect token selection noise rather than true structural differences between sparse autoencoders.

<small>Valentin Noël</small>
