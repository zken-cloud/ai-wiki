### [From Atoms to Entropy: Optimal Noise Allocation for Diffusion Training in the Convex Regime](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20540v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-10 · `arXiv:2607.20540v1`

A new statistical framework for optimal noise-level allocation in diffusion training shows atomic minimizers for coupled regimes and a square-root generative entropy rate proxy for decoupled regimes.

- The framework identifies asymptotically optimal noise-level allocation for diffusion training.
- In the fully coupled regime, under convexity or Polyak-Lojasiewicz assumptions, the optimized training schedule is an atomic minimizer concentrated on finitely many noise levels.
- In the idealized independent-learner regime, with a feature-noise decoupling condition, the decoupled sampling density is proportional to the square root of the generative entropy rate.
- Optimized schedules in controlled settings (Dirac mixtures, low-dimensional manifolds, MNIST) consistently show finite support, with the entropic proxy tracking atomic optima in neural networks.
- Square-root entropy scheduling improves training efficiency on discrete domains and is competitive with EDM-style heuristics on continuous images in larger-scale experiments.

**Why it matters:** This work provides a principled method for optimizing noise schedules, potentially improving diffusion model training efficiency and performance.

### [Seeing is Free, Speaking is Not: Uncovering the True Energy Bottleneck in Edge VLM Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09520v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-10 · `arXiv:2607.09520v1`

Systematic energy profiling of edge VLM inference reveals that output token generation, not visual processing, is the dominant energy bottleneck, overturning assumptions about visual token reduction efficacy.

- Average inference power is a model-intrinsic constant, varying less than 5% across input resolution, image complexity, and prompt type, meaning energy variation stems from inference time.
- Each output token costs 11 to 39 times more wall-clock time than each input token due to prefill/decode asymmetry, making output length the primary driver of latency and energy.
- Image complexity, measured by object count, causes up to 4.1x energy differences due to increased output length, not visual processing cost.
- Visual token pruning saves at most 10% of total energy for fixed-token models, while controlling output length saves up to 97% of total energy, with decoding dominance increasing at larger model scales (1B to 8B parameters).

**Why it matters:** Optimizing VLM energy consumption on edge devices requires focusing on output generation rather than visual input processing.

<small>Junfei Zhan, Haoxun Shen, Mingang Guo, Zixuan Huang, Tengjiao He</small>

### [Silent Failures in Quantized LLM Reasoning: A Taxonomy-Based Analysis of Hollow Convergence and Failure Mode Shifts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09999v2)

Quantization silently alters LLM reasoning, even with preserved accuracy, by shifting failure modes like Hollow Convergence and Shortcut Collapse, undetectable by standard metrics.

- Post-training quantization (FP16, NF4) can preserve task accuracy (max 3.1 pp drop) while altering reasoning processes in 3B-14B parameter LLMs.
- Hollow Convergence (correct answers with incomplete reasoning) under NF4 drops sharply for models <12B parameters but is invariant for models ≥12B, and is benchmark-specific (e.g., absent in GSM8K, prominent in LogiQA, ARC-Challenge).

- Hollow Convergence is not reliably detectable from surface-level text features (best F1 = 0.53), making it a deployment-relevant, hidden failure mode.

**Why it matters:** Standard LLM evaluation pipelines based on accuracy metrics are insufficient to detect critical reasoning degradation caused by quantization, potentially leading to unreliable deployments.

<small>Renuka Oladri, Mohan Vamsi Varadaraju Priya, Jerry Wu</small>

### [From Direction to Magnitude: How Multimodal Instruction-Tuning Reorganizes the Geometric Encoding of Identity-Specifying Prompts in Transformer Hidden States](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09842v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-07-10 · `arXiv:2607.09842v1`

Multimodal instruction-tuning reorganizes identity encoding in transformer hidden states from direction-coded to magnitude-coded, a shift not observed in RL distillation or SFT models.

- Identity-specifying prompts produce statistically distinguishable geometric fingerprints in transformer hidden states, analyzed across four post-training regimes.
- The 1-Wasserstein distance between edge-wise distributions of Ollivier-Ricci curvature on k-NN trajectory graphs is used to compare three prompt conditions.
- In base models (Gemma-4-E4B base), identity encoding is direction-coded (separation 0.034, p=0.002 under angular k-NN).
- In multimodal instruction-tuned models (Gemma-4-E4B-it), identity encoding shifts to magnitude-coded (angular separation p=0.439, Euclidean p=0.042, mean norm of first generated state inverts length-ordering).
- This direction-to-magnitude reorganization is specific to multimodal instruction-tuning, not present in RL distillation (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) or SFT (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) models.

**Why it matters:** Understanding how different training regimes geometrically encode identity in hidden states can inform the development of more robust and controllable language models.

### [How LLM Task-Adaptation Reshapes Alignment: A Multi-dimensional Study of Behavioral and Representational Drift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22676v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-07-10 · `arXiv:2607.22676v1`

This study systematically evaluates how task-adaptation methods (SFT, KL-SFT, RLVR) reshape LLM alignment across 15 aspects in six domains, revealing non-uniform drift and method-specific impacts.

- Task adaptation methods (SFT, KL-SFT, RLVR) were evaluated across 15 alignment aspects in safety, factuality, stance stability, social harm, controllability, and instructability domains.
- RLVR improves task performance with comparatively small, non-zero, metric-specific alignment shifts.
- SFT leads to substantially larger alignment drift across domains compared to RLVR.
- KL regularization mitigates SFT's alignment drift, but KL-SFT still preserves alignment less effectively than RLVR.
- Representation-level analysis confirms that shifts in alignment-relevant representations track behavioral drift.

**Why it matters:** Task adaptation is an alignment intervention requiring multi-dimensional alignment evaluation as a standard component of post-training pipelines.

<small>James Elcock, William F. Shen, Xinchi Qiu, Nicholas D. Lane</small>

### [Super-Tuning: From Activation-Aware Pruning to Sparse Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09287v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-07-10 · `arXiv:2607.09287v1`

Super and Supra are sparse parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods that reuse pruning saliency signals to select trainable parameters, achieving high accuracy in arithmetic tasks on Llama models.

- Super is a sparse PEFT method that fixes a small trainable support using a Wanda-style activation-weighted magnitude score computed from a calibration pass.
- Supra is a hybrid adapter combining Super's sparse update with LoRA, maintaining a matched trainable-parameter budget via budget-splitting.
- Experiments on Llama-3.2-1B and Meta-Llama-3-8B for Math17K arithmetic tasks show Super/Supra variants achieve the highest average accuracy among tested adapter configurations.
- Low-score supports under both magnitude and Wanda-style orderings are effective, suggesting pruning-inspired orderings provide useful fixed sparse supports for PEFT, especially with low-rank adapters.

**Why it matters:** This approach reduces memory, compute, and storage costs for fine-tuning large language models by efficiently selecting which parameters to update.

<small>Ivan Ilin, Philip Zmushko, Peter Richtárik</small>

### [Complexity-Guided Component-wise Initialization for Language Model Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09204v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-07-10 · `arXiv:2607.09204v1`

This paper investigates reusing recurring spectral patterns from pretrained GPT-2-style models as an initialization signal, finding that while these patterns are diagnostic, coarse spectral matching alone does not yield performance advantages.

- Analyzed eleven pretrained GPT-2-style checkpoints for Frobenius norm and effective-rank entropy across layers and Transformer subcomponents.
- Observed shared depth trends in checkpoints, specifically increasing scale and stronger spectral concentration in residual-writing matrices.
- Constructed initialization schemes imitating component-wise magnitudes and spectral profiles of pretrained models.
- Found that these initializers visibly altered model structural spectral patterns, but did not provide a performance advantage over existing methods.
- Concluded that effective reuse of pretrained spectra likely requires preserving richer information than just component-wise scale and singular-value shape.

**Why it matters:** Understanding the limitations of spectral pattern reuse for initialization informs future research into more effective methods for leveraging pretrained model structures.

<small>Konstantin Garbers, Nicholas Oh</small>
