### [Inference-Time Consensus for Mitigating Hidden Behaviors from LLM Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23394v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-25 · `arXiv:2607.23394v1`

This paper introduces inference-time consensus decoding to mitigate targeted misbehavior and hidden preferences in LLMs fine-tuned on poisoned or ostensibly benign data by aggregating next-token distributions from multiple reference models.

- The method fine-tunes a separate reference model on each data source and aggregates their next-token distributions at decoding time.
- Two consensus decoders are introduced: a token-wise minimum, which caps each token at the lowest probability any source assigns, and a base-relative variant, which reverts to the base probability if sources move in opposing directions.
- The approach tolerates partial support across sources and different surface expressions of the same intention, relaxing exact agreement.
- Consensus decoding suppresses source-specific misbehavior while preserving shared desirable behavior, outperforming union training and weight averaging in controlled poisoning, subliminal learning, and emergent misalignment tasks.

**Why it matters:** This technique offers a robust defense against hidden behaviors and misalignments introduced during LLM fine-tuning, improving model trustworthiness and safety.

### [When Activation Oracles Learn Not to Read: Concept-Specific Blind Spots in Fine-Tuned Oracles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23379v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-25 · `arXiv:2607.23379v1`

Fine-tuned Activation Oracles (AOs) can develop concept-specific blind spots, failing to report hidden information persistently present during their own training, even when the concept is decodable within the oracle.

- Activation Oracles (AOs) are language models trained to answer natural-language questions about another model's internal activations.
- In a Taboo Word Guessing setting, fine-tuned AOs became "concept-specific anti-readers," failing to recover a hidden concept despite its persistent presence during training.
- This failure is not due to the concept's absence from subject or oracle representations; the target remains decodable inside the oracle.
- LogitLens and layer-ablation analyses indicate the failure originates in the AO readout pathway, not in the concept's internal representation.
- The study demonstrates that behavioral leakage, representation-level decodability, and AO-verbalizability can diverge.

**Why it matters:** This raises reliability concerns for learned interpretability interfaces, as AOs may not accurately report internal model states even when the information is present.

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-25 · `arXiv:2607.23271v1`

PeakPatch recovers negation sensitivity in CLIP by re-injecting lost syntax from intermediate layers, achieving significant performance gains on negation benchmarks with minimal added parameters.

- CLIP's insensitivity to negation is attributed to Representational Collapse, where compositional syntax in middle layers is lost in final layers as visual alignment increases.
- PeakPatch, a post-hoc correction system, intercepts the CLIP text encoder at its 'compositional peak' to recover negation signals without altering pretrained weights.
- An Embedding Correction Network (ECN) extracts a negation-specific signal and predicts a deviation vector to re-inject lost syntax into the final embedding space, while a Score Correction Network (SCN) predicts bounded scalar score offsets.
- PeakPatch adds 5.2M parameters (3.5% of backbone) and achieves 74.3% on COCO MCQ (+35.1 over CLIP) and 65.5% on VOC MCQ, outperforming fine-tuning baselines on out-of-distribution negation retrieval.
- The corrected embeddings improve text-to-image generation (+18.4 negation score) and generalize across ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14, and SigLIP backbones.

**Why it matters:** This method significantly enhances the understanding of negation in vision-language models, improving their utility in discriminative tasks and text-to-image generation without extensive retraining.

### [The Entropic Bound for Transformers: Why Static Rank Fails and Attention-Native Rank Recovers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23050v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-07-25 · `arXiv:2607.23050v1`

This paper introduces an attention-native intrinsic rank for Transformers, extending the Entropic Bound to accurately measure task-intrinsic capacity in both linear and softmax attention models.

- The intrinsic rank r* of the token-mixing operator is a tight lower bound for task-intrinsic capacity in a linear attention surrogate, with gradient descent recovering this rank under low-rank implicit-bias assumptions.
- A naive transfer of the static rank concept fails for real attention due to the input-conditioned nature of attention's mixing operator, not softmax or rank constraints.
- An attention-native intrinsic rank, defined as the minimum query-key kernel rank, restores the Entropic Bound structure (deficiency, achievability, recovery) for both linear and softmax attention.
- The energy effective rank serves as a robust estimator for the attention-native intrinsic rank, even with softmax distortion.
- The intrinsic rank is exactly recoverable from data for linear QK attention, but only partially recoverable for softmax attention due to nonlinear inversion and kernel-value identifiability effects.

**Why it matters:** This work provides a method to determine the minimum model capacity required for a given task, enabling more efficient Transformer design and training.
