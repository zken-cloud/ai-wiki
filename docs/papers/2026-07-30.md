### [DistillAlign: Coordinating Mode Covering and Mode Seeking in Autoregressive Video Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26811)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-28 · 👍 88 · ⭐ 82 · `arXiv:2607.26811`

DistillAlign introduces joint distillation for autoregressive video, combining mode-seeking Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) with a mode-covering Consistency Distillation constraint to improve generation quality, coverage, and diversity by addressing distributional misalignment.

- Existing autoregressive video distillation methods decouple initialization and DMD stages, pursuing different target distributions and often judging by visual scores like VBench.
- A distributional evaluation protocol measuring precision and coverage in a shared latent space reveals that high visual scores can mask low mode coverage, leading to suboptimal refinement.
- The mode-seeking nature of reverse-KL in DMD can reduce coverage and diversity by driving the student to high-probability teacher regions in late training.
- DistillAlign proposes joint distillation, integrating DMD's mode-seeking objective with a Consistency Distillation-based mode-covering constraint.
- The method improves generation quality, coverage, and diversity, outperforming baselines refined with a Wan-14B teacher even when using a Wan-1.3B DMD teacher.

**Why it matters:** This method significantly improves autoregressive video distillation efficiency and quality by optimizing distributional alignment, allowing smaller models to achieve performance comparable to or better than larger models.

<small>Jiaxing Li, Kai Zou, Cindy Zhou, Kaichen Huang, Junyao Gao, Zile Wang, Yang Liu, Bin Liu</small>

### [CoRT: Counterfactual Replay for Token-Level Rubric-Guided Policy Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25659)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-27 · 👍 80 · `arXiv:2607.25659`

CoRT introduces a token-level credit weighting method for rubric-conditioned GRPO using counterfactual replay to redistribute response-level advantages without an auxiliary scorer.

- CoRT addresses the uniform broadcasting of response-level rewards in GRPO by providing token-level credit allocation for rubric-based language model training.
- The method uses counterfactual replay to rescore sampled responses under both rubric-conditioned and criteria-free prompts, generating tokenwise log-likelihood contrasts.
- These contrasts are mapped to bounded, response-normalized weights, which redistribute the signed GRPO advantage across tokens.
- CoRT improves over response-level GRPO by an average of 4.4 percentage points and competes with learned token-level credit baselines without a separate relevance-learning stage.

**Why it matters:** This method enhances the precision of reinforcement learning for language models by enabling fine-grained credit assignment within responses, improving training efficiency and performance.

- Voice Memory employs a listener-thinker architecture where a frozen corrector reads a per-domain memory to decide on hypothesis correction or abstention.

This paper introduces "coherent overlap" in MoE routing, where selected experts from a shared geometric neighborhood provide useful multi-expert computation despite substantial subspace overlap, challenging geometric complementarity.

- Expert Subspace Separation Index (ESSI), matched-route residuals, and a prefix-controlled 2x2 factorial were used to distinguish route coherence, candidate quality, and candidate-by-context interaction.
- Across six MoE architectures, expert subspaces overlap substantially, yet actual routes explain token representations better than matched alternatives.

- The concept of "coherent overlap" describes routing selecting token-relevant experts from a shared geometric neighborhood where useful multi-expert computation persists without disjoint linear coverage.

**Why it matters:** Clarifying coherent overlap helps understand MoE functionality beyond geometric similarity, impacting redundancy assessment and pruning strategies.

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-07-30 · `arXiv:2607.28146v1`

ParliamentBench, an open-source benchmark based on Secret Hitler, evaluates 16 LLMs' deceptive capabilities, reasoning, and persuasion in social deduction scenarios, revealing frontier models' strong performance but general struggle with deceptive consistency.

- ParliamentBench evaluates 16 LLMs across 1,600 simulated matches in Secret Hitler, playing against each other and humans.
- Three novel metrics were introduced to isolate social deduction, reasoning, and deceptive consistency.
- Frontier models (GPT-5.4, Kimi K2.5, Grok 4.1 Fast, DeepSeek 3.1 Terminus) achieved strong performance in cooperative and deceptive roles.
- Most LLMs struggled with deceptive consistency, with deception retention dropping below 50% throughout a game.

**Why it matters:** Understanding LLM deceptive capabilities is crucial for safety when deploying them in high-stakes applications like medical and legal systems.

<small>Niklas Bauer, Lars Benedikt Kaesberg, Akiko Aizawa, Jan Philip Wahle, Bela Gipp, Terry Ruas</small>

### [PCAP-LM: An LLM-Native Text Representation for TLS Bulk Traffic Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28100v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-07-30 · `arXiv:2607.28100v1`

PCAP-LM is an LLM-native text representation for TLS bulk traffic analysis that uses PacketGlyphs and BPE to achieve significant size reduction and improve LLM accuracy for forensic question-answering.

- PCAP-LM transcodes raw network captures into semantic summaries using PacketGlyphs, an ASCII alphabet encoding packet direction, TCP/TLS state, log-scale size, and inter-packet delay.
- A constrained PMI-BPE tokenizer and motif run-length encoding are applied to aggressively collapse repetitive behavioral patterns.
- The method achieves an 812x size reduction over tshark -V, fitting entire captures within a single LLM context window, with a BPE vocabulary saturating at 159 tokens for 5G/4G TLS 1.3 bulk-download traffic.
- In forensic question-answering, a frontier LLM achieved 99.3% accuracy with PCAP-LM documents, compared to 51.0% with a token-budget-matched tshark -V prefix.
- The lossy design introduces a 24% false-negative rate for TCP retransmissions and requires vocabulary retraining for heterogeneous mixed-protocol environments.

**Why it matters:** This approach enables LLMs to effectively analyze large network traffic captures by reducing verbosity and improving accuracy in forensic tasks.

<small>Xavier Marjou, Lucas Tamic, Ilan Jaffeux-Cheniout</small>

### [RepBench: Compiling Benchmarks into Capability Representations for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28008v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-07-30 · `arXiv:2607.28008v1`

RepBench introduces a benchmark-grounded data layer for capability-aligned representation probing, compiling 13,427 benchmark papers and 353 datasets into 46,149 audited probe texts covering 94 capabilities.

- RepBench established a taxonomy of 182 capability clusters across 13 families by crawling 13,427 benchmark papers.
- The benchmark integrates 353 public datasets, yielding 46,149 audited probe texts for 94 capabilities, each supported by at least two independent benchmarks.
- Benchmark-pooled capability vectors show an interior clustering optimum at a small number of clusters on all 12 evaluated models, contrasting with raw per-text vectors lacking natural cluster granularity.
- Cross-benchmark transfer evaluation across twelve models revealed difference-in-means achieved the highest model-level mean on ten models, while logistic regression won the most capability-model cells, indicating readout method and aggregation criteria are significant evaluation dimensions.

**Why it matters:** This work provides a standardized, reusable framework for evaluating and comparing representation engineering methods in large language models, reducing reliance on paper-specific synthetic data.

<small>Yanshi Li, Xueru Bai, Shuman Liu, Long Zhang</small>

### [TriShield: Zero-Utility-Loss Defense Against Privacy Backdoors in Federated Language Model Fine-Tuning via Orthogonal Gradient Projection and Optimizer State Entanglement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27940v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-07-30 · `arXiv:2607.27940v1`

TriShield is a three-layer defense preventing privacy backdoors in federated LLM fine-tuning, achieving zero utility loss and 0% NeuroImprint reconstruction.

- TriShield employs a Parameter Artifact Detector to identify memory-neuron signatures before local training.
- A Stateful Virtual Iteration mechanism entangles Adam/AdamW's momentum state across virtual steps, invalidating NeuroImprint's inversion.
- A Zero-Utility Orthogonal Projection operator eliminates gradient components carrying private memorization by projecting updates onto the main-task semantic subspace via SVD.
- The defense theoretically proves zero mutual information between uploaded gradients and individual training samples after layers 2 and 3.
- Experiments on GPT-2 (117M) and Llama-Guard-3-1B show TriShield reduces NeuroImprint reconstruction to 0% with less than 5% additional GPU overhead.

**Why it matters:** This defense enables secure federated fine-tuning of LLMs by completely preventing privacy backdoor attacks like NeuroImprint without compromising model utility or increasing communication rounds.

<small>Cheng Wei</small>
