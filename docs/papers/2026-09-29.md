### [Beyond Teacher Assignment: Domain-Normalized Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35347)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-27 · 👍 105 · ⭐ 5 · `arXiv:2609.35347`

Domain-Normalized Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation (DN-MOPD) rescales domain feedback by its measured spread, preventing instruction-following signals from swamping mathematics signals when merging specialist language models into a single student.

- Standard MOPD fails to outperform the best single specialist on Qwen3.5 models because instruction-following feedback has a much larger spread than mathematics feedback, dominating student parameter updates.
- DN-MOPD retains prompt-based teacher routing but rescales token feedback per domain using the measured spread of each domain's signal.
- Across six public benchmarks, three Qwen3.5 model sizes, three random seeds, and two answer-length limits, DN-MOPD consistently outperforms standard MOPD and recovers lost math performance.
- Ablations with fixed domain weights show the primary gain comes from dampening instruction-following feedback strength rather than simply boosting math feedback.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that successfully consolidating domain-specialized models into a single multi-talented LLM requires balancing feedback magnitudes across domains, not just routing prompts to the correct teacher.

<small>Xin Li, Hao Jiang, Xin Gao, Annan Wang, Yuchen Xie, Jinghao Guo, Xingwei Qu, Yichi Zhang</small>

### [Post-Training Leaves Behavioral Shadows on Unrelated Decisions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29233)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-23 · 👍 72 · ⭐ 8 · `arXiv:2609.29233`

Active Taskless Distillation (ATD) enables language models to transfer downstream capabilities using only a single task-unrelated word output per prompt from a post-trained teacher.

- ATD probes the behavioral shadow of post-training by selecting prompts where a shared public ancestor model is nearly indifferent between two common words.
- Student models initialize from the shared ancestor and learn strictly from prompt-word pairs, without target-task examples, teacher logits, or teacher parameters.
- Applying ATD to Qwen2.5-1.5B using 5,664 prompt-word responses yields a 5.34 percentage point gain on HumanEval+ over a nuisance-matched control.
- Cross-model experiments demonstrate capability transfer across scientific knowledge, commonsense reasoning, and reading comprehension, with learned representations proving functional and composable.

**Why it matters:** The findings demonstrate that post-training updates leave systematic behavioral shadows in unrelated text, enabling silent capability transfer without exposing domain-specific training data or model parameters.

<small>Ziyang Zhang, Yubin Jing, Yuanhao Zeng, Yuyao Li, Haofan Wang, Yichen Gong</small>

### [How Far Are We from Removing the Visual Encoder? Scaling Laws for Encoder-Free Multimodal Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35457)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-27 · 👍 50 · `arXiv:2609.35457`

A systematic study demonstrates that encoder-free multimodal LLMs follow distinct compute-scaling laws, with visual losses predicted to match encoder-based MLLMs at approximately 10^22 FLOPs.

- Removing the visual encoder shifts compute-optimal allocations toward larger model sizes for the multimodal objective, while the text objective allocation remains unchanged.
- Loss-compute frontiers for text objectives overlap between architectures, but encoder-free models close their visual performance gap as pretraining compute scales toward 10^22 FLOPs.
- Language models adapt to raw pixels by shifting visual processing to earlier layers, relying on bidirectional visual token interactions, and concentrating expert routing for visual tokens.

**Why it matters:** The visual prior advantage of dedicated encoders diminishes with scale, making simpler, unified encoder-free MLLM architectures practical for large compute budgets.

<small>Lin Chen, Bolin Ni, Qi Yang, Lan Jiang, Kun Ding, Xiaoran Fan, Hower Yang, Ying Wang</small>

### [Groupwise Agentic Grading and Advantage Redistribution for Code Agent RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32577)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-25 · 👍 26 · `arXiv:2609.32577`

GAGAR improves code agent RL by using an SFT-trained agentic grader to rank test-passing trajectories in a shared workspace, redistributing advantages to favor higher-quality code while preserving the group's total advantage sum.

- GRPO assigns uniform advantages to all test-passing trajectories in a rollout group, ignoring code quality, implementation cleanliness, and out-of-scope changes.
- GAGAR retains rollout groups with mixed pass/fail outcomes and uses an SFT agentic grader to jointly inspect and rank passing trajectories in a shared workspace.
- The method downweights lower-ranked passing trajectories and proportionally rescales advantages to preserve the original total advantage sum, shifting credit to better solutions.
- Evaluated at industrial scale using pre-RL SFT checkpoints of MiMo-V2.6-Flash (310B parameters) and MiMo-V2.6-Pro (1.02T parameters).
- Experiments demonstrate higher task performance, reduced trajectory-length growth, and increased RL training stability in both code-only and mixed-task settings.

**Why it matters:** It enables large language model code agents to learn cleaner, higher-quality implementations without sacrificing the objective ground-truth feedback provided by executable test suites.

<small>Jinhao Dong, Liang Zhao, Zihao Yue, Wenhan Ma, Linghao Zhang, Lei Li, Shicheng Li, Yifan Song</small>

### [Less Sycophancy, Stronger Refusal? Lessons for AI Safety from Mechanistic Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35544v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-09-28 · `arXiv:2609.35544v1`

Researchers used compensatory feature injection during supervised fine-tuning to suppress sycophancy features in Qwen3.5 models, finding it restores refusal capabilities specifically under user pressure rather than enhancing direct refusal.

- Identified top sycophancy features in Qwen3.5 base models using sparse autoencoders (SAEs) and validated them via inference steering.
- Applied compensatory feature injection (CFI) during supervised fine-tuning, reducing learned sycophancy by 62.0% in the 35B-A3B model relative to ordinary fine-tuning.
- Demonstrated that reducing sycophancy does not consistently increase direct refusal of harmful requests.
- Recovered roughly 95% of lost refusal capabilities under active user pressure in the 35B-A3B model when trained with positive feature injection.

### [Spontaneous Context Restoration: How Language Models Recover from Corrupted Inputs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35475v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-09-28 · `arXiv:2609.35475v1`

Language models exhibit spontaneous context restoration from corrupted inputs via a two-phase internal process that can be predicted early using linear probes.

- Context restoration emerges spontaneously in attention-only transformers trained solely on clean sequences, without explicit denoising objectives.
- Internal processing follows a two-phase mechanism: early layers localize repair effects at corrupted positions, while later layers accumulate them at uncorrupted positions and aggregate them at the final output token via the residual stream.
- A linear probe trained on first-block hidden states predicts restoration failure with a mean ROC-AUC of 0.78, allowing early triage of corrupted prompts.
- Failed restorations display high nonlinearity along corruption directions, whereas moderate-corruption finetuning improves robustness and reduces displacement-normalized linearization error.

**Why it matters:** Early prediction of restoration failures enables efficient failure triage in deployment, saving compute by bypassing unnecessary downstream verification for corrupted inputs.

### [Why Deterministic PRM Guidance Underperforms in Discrete Diffusion Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35472v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-09-28 · `arXiv:2609.35472v1`

Deterministic Process Reward Model (PRM) guidance underperforms outcome-based reranking in discrete diffusion LLMs when evaluated at equal forward-pass budgets due to weak early-step reward signals and poor final-state scoring.

- Equal-budget testing on Dream-v0-Instruct-7B shows ORM reranking beats deterministic PRM guidance by 9.95 pp on GSM8K (8 candidates), 9.85 pp on MATH, and 12.16 pp on MBPP.
- PRM reward signals degrade significantly at high mask ratios; on GSM8K, PRM ROC-AUC drops from 0.77 to 0.54 as the mask ratio increases.
- Early pruning via PRM guidance reduces the maximum achievable accuracy in the candidate pool from 81.05% down to 67.30% on GSM8K.
- A Sequential Monte Carlo sampler restores candidate pool potential to 77.89%, but selecting the final output via early-state trained PRMs caps accuracy at 65.48%.

**Why it matters:** Effective test-time compute allocation in discrete diffusion models requires preserving early candidate diversity and using final-state verifiers rather than relying on deterministic mid-denoising PRM pruning.

<small>Yan Zhan, Shaobo Liu, Zhijun Gao</small>
