**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-07 · 👍 26 · `arXiv:2609.08572`

AgentGrad is a prompt optimization framework for multi-agent systems that improves prompt updates using sequential intervention for gradient extraction and semantic abstraction for gradient aggregation.

- Identifies flaws in existing textual gradient methods, such as unverified target prompt selection, lack of agent-level supervision, and random gradient aggregation that mixes unrelated failures.
- Uses sequential intervention to modify one agent's behavior at a time, isolating the target agent that resolves the failure and using its modified output as explicit agent-level supervision.

- Outperforms baselines across five multi-agent system (MAS) benchmarks while reducing wall-clock optimization time by an average of 2.5x compared to the next-fastest baseline.

**Why it matters:** It enables more accurate, scalable, and faster automatic prompt optimization for complex LLM-based multi-agent workflows without mixing unrelated failure modes.

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-06 · 👍 15 · `arXiv:2609.07139`

Transformers linearly encode inferred partner expertise in early layers, well before the information becomes causally active past the network's midpoint.

- Evaluated using ExpertCollab, a corpus of multi-turn research-planning dialogues across four partner expertise levels.
- Partner expertise decodability peaks in early layers and drops to near chance before the network's midpoint, whereas statically specified attributes remain decodable throughout.
- Counterfactual patching reveals an order-of-magnitude difference in causal impact: injecting expertise differences at peak decodability barely alters late-layer readouts, while injections past the midpoint propagate almost completely.
- The early-layer activation threshold was validated using a content-matched random control and a probe-free diagnostic.

### [Scores Alone Do Not Prove Discovery: The Discovery Certification Protocol for Auditing AI Research Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09219)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-06 · 👍 13 · `arXiv:2609.09219`

The Discovery Certification Protocol (DCP) introduces an executable three-gate audit framework using recovery and feedback tests to verify whether AI research agents generate genuine discoveries.

- Gate 1 verifies numerical improvement on sealed evaluations.
- Gate 2 isolates research history while providing agents with registered starting info and observed Web content; achieving the target generates a recovery witness and invokes the Core veto.
- DCP Core demands adequate controls, zero observed recoveries, and a finite-sample bound on recovery across a fresh registered episode.
- Gate 3 measures the average treatment effect of truthful feedback against a neutral policy from a shared checkpoint, evaluated with independent null calibration and a registered margin.
- Controlled audits in SQLite optimization and virtual catalyst control yielded zero recoveries across 96 episodes (upper bound 0.0468), while paired studies produced 30 truthful vs. zero neutral recoveries.

**Why it matters:** It establishes a rigorous, verifiable standard to prevent AI research agents from masking retrieval or shortcuts as novel scientific discoveries.

<small>Jingjie Ning, Shanshan Zhong, Xiaochuan Li, Ji Zeng</small>

### [A*-Thought-V2: Efficient Latent Reasoning via Geometric Dynamics of LLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07821)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-07 · 👍 11 · `arXiv:2609.07821`

A*-Thought-V2 optimizes LLM latent reasoning by compressing off-direction CoT steps into continuous latent tokens based on geometric trajectory alignment in a 3D PCA space.

- Models CoT as a hidden-state trajectory in 3D PCA space, keeping aligned steps as explicit text and compressing deviating steps (like checking or correction) into single latent tokens.
- Uses stepwise embedding forcing to pool redundant steps into a single latent embedding and label forcing to supervise latent tokens via soft multi-modal vocabulary distributions.
- Temporal variations in directional angles between local transitions and the global question-to-solution vector are used to identify reasoning phases like exploration and refinement.
- Evaluated on Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.6-27B, achieving up to a 2.6% accuracy gain, 50% length reduction, 2.29x Accuracy per Computation Unit, and 94.6% faster preprocessing.

**Why it matters:** It significantly reduces LLM context size and computational costs while improving reasoning accuracy by efficiently interleave explicit text with compressed latent representations.

### [Forward-Free LLM Depth Pruning via Weight Redundancy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09883v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-09-09 · `arXiv:2609.09883v1`

Weight-Redundancy Pruning (WRP) is a forward-free depth-pruning method for LLMs that uses checkpoint weights alone to measure inter-layer similarity and select Transformer blocks without calibration data.

- Eliminates calibration data and model forward passes by estimating layer redundancy directly from static checkpoint weights.
- Computes pairwise layer similarities by comparing attention output and MLP down-projection weights combined with relative projection-scale metrics.
- Constructs an all-pairs similarity matrix across layers to guide block selection and layer grouping.
- Outperforms standard forward-free magnitude pruning and achieves performance approaching activation-based depth-pruning methods.

**Why it matters:** WRP enables fast, compute-efficient LLM compression and block removal without the memory overhead, datasets, or execution time required for activation-based calibration.

### [Through the Looking Glass: Directly Reading and Writing Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10210v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-09-09 · `arXiv:2609.10210v1`

A non-parametric interpretability framework reads and writes directly to transformer components, revealing that predictions depend on a net of only dozens of components and enabling precise, single-unit knowledge edits at a fraction of rank-one update costs.

- Opposing component contributions are large: across 18 models, negative logit contribution mass is a median 7 times the positive mass, reducing the net drivers of a prediction to dozens of components (e.g., 53 carry 90% of a prediction, 13 are necessary, and 8 are sufficient).
- Across 12 external models (124M to 7B parameters), the complete upstream dependency graph accounts for only 1% to 3% of the total model, a fraction that does not scale with model size.
- Three-quarters of a layer's update is a fixed linear map of its input state, allowing direct reading of component outputs via downstream predictions and inputs via internal weights (58.9% above chance accuracy for the 8 strongest inputs).
- New associations can be installed into a single spare unit by reading keys and values directly from weights, reducing held-out loss by 0.25% at 1/40th the cost of a rank-one update.

**Why it matters:** Directly reading and editing transformer weights without training enables fine-grained mechanistic interpretability and extremely low-cost targeted model updates.

<small>Mark Oskin</small>

### [Deep and shallow biases in language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09901v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-09-09 · `arXiv:2609.09901v1`

A new bias depth score distinguishes stable, persistent model preferences (Deep biases) from prompt-dependent wording artifacts (Shallow biases) across language model training and debiasing workflows.

- Evaluated 4,442 opinion prompts across four LLMs, finding that only roughly 25% of concentrated preferences persist when scenarios are reframed.
- Categorizes persistent preferences as 'Deep biases' and prompt-dependent preferences as 'Shallow biases'.
- Deep biases are primarily inherited from pretraining, preserved through Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), and significantly harder to eliminate using fine-tuning or prompt-based debiasing.
- Demonstrates that single-prompt evaluation metrics conflate stable model beliefs with prompt-wording artifacts.

**Why it matters:** Evaluating bias depth allows developers to target truly ingrained model preferences rather than wasting debiasing efforts on transient, prompt-specific artifacts.

### [How Fragile Is Safety Alignment at Frontier Scale? A Single-Direction Attack on a 320B MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09793v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-09-09 · `arXiv:2609.09793v1`

Researchers successfully adapted single-direction ablation to a 320B quantized MoE model, demonstrating that safety alignment remains fragile at frontier scales.

- Tested directional ablation on GLM-5.3-Flash, a 320B parameter MoE model featuring 288 routed experts, a four-wide hyper-connection residual, and block-FP8 quantization.
- Isolated single-module interventions showed low refusal reductions (attention: 0.039, dense: 0.016, routed-expert: 0.148), whereas a joint intervention across all three achieved a 0.776 refusal reduction.
- Intervening strictly on components matched by the conventional module-naming recipe accounts for only 0.066 of the 0.776 refusal reduction, causing traditional single-direction attacks to fail silently.
- The adapted joint attack yielded 41 to 89 percentage-point refusal reductions across seven harmful benchmarks with no detected drop in general model capabilities.
- A category-concentrated refusal residue persisted across every attempted edit rank (1 through 12) for subspaces fitted on violence, sexual content, and hate speech.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that white-box refusal ablation techniques remain highly effective on frontier-scale MoE architectures when applied jointly across all residual-writing modules.

<small>Yi Shi, Tanyu Chen, Kai Shen</small>
