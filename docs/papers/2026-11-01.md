### [On the Fundamental Limits of Prefill-Based Decoding Length Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40541v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-10-29 · `arXiv:2610.40541v1`

Researchers derive fundamental theoretical bounds on prefill-based LLM decode length prediction, demonstrating that decoding stochasticity creates a hard performance ceiling where system error dominates model error.

- Autoregressive decoding is modeled as a stopped stochastic process where per-step stopping probabilities ramp up over hundreds of steps, yielding per-prompt normal length distributions with an explicit affine variance law: σ = αμ + b.
- The theoretical normal law with sampling-noise corrections accurately predicts per-prompt Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and central distribution mass within a few percent, accounting for observed mild right skew and excess kurtosis.
- Multiple predictor architectures (MLP, EGTP, FiLM, soft-label, and MoE variants) converge to near-identical performance close to the theoretical bound under fixed conditions, proving predictor differences are negligible compared to inherent sampling noise.
- Benchmark accuracy collapses across datasets, models, or sampling setups because existing evaluations conflate predictor quality with inherent task hardness.

**Why it matters:** Inference schedulers cannot overcome decode-length prediction errors caused by sampling noise, necessitating standardized benchmarks that evaluate predictors relative to theoretical limits rather than absolute metrics.

<small>Haowen Shi, Xuzhe Yan, Fahao Chen, Peng Li</small>

### [Rethinking Pre-pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40051v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-10-29 · `arXiv:2610.40051v1`

Compute-matched synthetic pre-pretraining fails to beat pure natural-language training across data regimes, with prior reported gains largely driven by suboptimal weight initialization.

- Evaluated four synthetic pre-pretraining (PPT) curricula—k-Dyck, neural cellular automata (NCA), declarative reference corpora, and code—across data-constrained, data-unconstrained, and high token-to-parameter regimes (881M parameters on 50B tokens).
- Under a data-constrained regime with a 100M-token corpus, repeating epochs of natural language achieved lower validation loss than any synthetic PPT curriculum, providing no downstream accuracy advantage.
- Prior reported PPT benefits are sensitive to weight initialization; correcting the initialization scheme in an NCA reproduction closed the 5.4% perplexity gap between PPT and the no-PPT baseline.
- Non-compute-matched PPT provides gains only at small natural-language token budgets (<1B tokens), and models completely forget PPT learned skills during subsequent natural-language training unless data is interleaved.

**Why it matters:** Allocating dedicated compute budgets to synthetic pre-pretraining stages is ineffective compared to directly training or repeating epochs on natural language.

<small>Alexander Stern, Tim Rocktäschel, Acyr Locatelli, Laura Ruis</small>

### [Attention, Dynamic Wave and Hybrid Sequence Mixers in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39854v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-10-29 · `arXiv:2610.39854v1`

Researchers formalize a Dynamic Wave recurrent layer and show a Hybrid Wave-Attention architecture achieves superior perplexity (5.95) over Pure Transformer (6.93) and Pure Wave (7.26) models at 10.94M parameters.

- The Dynamic Wave layer utilizes input-dependent exponential damping and cosine phase modulation to operate as a sub-quadratic recurrent alternative to Multi-Head Attention.
- Tested on 10.94M-parameter models up to 4096 tokens, Pure Wave saves 78.7% peak VRAM over unfused attention, but shows no VRAM advantage over fused scaled-dot-product attention (1404.3 MB vs. 1403.6 MB) and incurs 8.3% higher training cost.

### [KV Cache Quantization for Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39853v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-10-29 · `arXiv:2610.39853v1`

Researchers introduce TRACE and dLLM-KVQ to address KV cache quantization in diffusion language models, accounting for cache-reuse errors caused by changing bidirectional token representations across denoising steps.

- Diffusion language models (dLLMs) experience cache-reuse error prior to quantization because bidirectional token representations evolve as masked tokens are revealed.
- TRACE reconstructs feasible dLLM reveal trajectories from prompt-answer pairs to generate calibration states reflecting iterative masked decoding.
- dLLM-KVQ utilizes TRACE trajectories alongside output- and attention-level metrics to select per-layer key-cache entries for high-precision retention while applying low-bit value quantization.
- Evaluations on LLaDA and Dream models across GSM8K, HumanEval, MATH, and MBPP show dLLM-KVQ achieves the highest average score among quantized methods at 4-bit and 2-bit prefix/dual cache settings.

**Why it matters:** This work enables low-bit KV cache quantization for diffusion language models without suffering severe performance degraded by dynamic, non-causal token state updates.

A benchmark of speculative decoding under production conditions reveals that energy savings depend heavily on acceptance rates, shrinking as batch size increases and turning into energy losses at large batches or low acceptance.
