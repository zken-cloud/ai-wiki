**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-11-04 · 👍 27 · ⭐ 2 · `arXiv:2611.06483`

A study evaluates multi-turn agentic RL using Pass@(K,T), revealing performance depends on turn budgets, primarily aids error recovery rather than single-step decisions, and introduces BudgetMix RL to improve resilience across execution settings.

### [Model Merging Does Not Require Uniform LoRA Ranks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06486v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-11-04 · `arXiv:2611.06486v1`

A 36-expert study shows that model merging does not require uniform LoRA ranks across experts, provided the merge method avoids selecting spectral components purely by magnitude.

- Evaluated 36 experts across three domains (math, instruction following, multilingual), four ranks, and three model scales using six merging methods.
- Averaging and utility-based selection methods handle mixed LoRA ranks without loss, gaining up to 1.8 percentage points in multi-domain accuracy.
- Magnitude-based conflict-resolution methods like TIES discard smaller singular values, disproportionately dropping instruction adapters and causing IFEval accuracy to drop by 16 to 25 percentage points.
- Selecting spectral components by their effect on loss maintains balanced representation from all experts and keeps accuracy variance within 0.7 percentage points across an 8x rank range at 4B scale.

**Why it matters:** Practitioners can independently optimize the LoRA rank for each domain expert without degrading model performance during post-hoc merging.

### [Where Should a KV-Cache Repair Budget Go? Distractor Structure and Contextual Compatibility in Non-Prefix Reuse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06397v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-11-04 · `arXiv:2611.06397v1`

A study on non-prefix KV-cache reuse demonstrates that allocating a 5% repair budget to recomputing target chunks against reused context yields higher accuracy than re-inserting original prefill KV tensors or using InfoFlow reordering.

- Non-prefix KV-cache reuse loses 19% to 100% of context-dependent accuracy across three 7B-8B models when compared to full prefill baseline performance.
- Recomputing the answer-bearing target chunk against the reused cache outperforms inserting its full-prefill KV tensors across all seven tested experimental settings.

- Recomputing the retriever's top-ranked chunk adjacent to the question beats InfoFlow-style reordering at a 5% budget in six of nine open-domain QA and API-documentation settings, though token-level selection remains superior for dialogue memory.

**Why it matters:** It establishes that limited computational repair budgets are best spent recomputing target chunks in place rather than restoring original prefill KV states or reordering context.

### [Does Context Compression Preserve Refusal Alignment?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06380v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-11-04 · `arXiv:2611.06380v1`

Encoder-based context compression systematically weakens refusal alignment in instruction-tuned language models by attenuating activations along the decoder's refusal direction, despite high semantic reconstruction fidelity.

- Context compression attenuates model activation along the decoder's learned refusal direction, proving that semantic preservation does not ensure behavioral preservation.
- The refusal alignment degradation persists across multiple language model families and context compression architectures.
- Memory Steering, a lightweight inference-time intervention operating in the compressed representation space, restores refusal rates to near-baseline levels without retraining.

**Why it matters:** Systems using compressed context representations to save inference costs risk exposing safety vulnerabilities unless alignment-relevant features are explicitly preserved.

<small>Anthony Nguyen, Wenjun Lin</small>

### [Mass Producing Training Data with Surprising Side Effects](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06324v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-11-04 · `arXiv:2611.06324v1`

Researchers developed an automated pipeline that combined LLM judges with evolutionary, RL, and subliminal learning methods to generate a dataset of 6,575 training datasets with surprising side effects across eight model behaviors.

- DSSE objectives require data to elicit a target behavior, resemble ordinary post-training data, and be surprising to human auditors.
- The search pipeline generates 6,575 DSSEs across 8 behaviors, using held-out metrics for re-scoring and filtering.
- Discovered side effects generalize across different model sizes and model families.
- Frontier models outperformed human researchers at predicting the off-target side effects of the generated training datasets.

**Why it matters:** Automating the discovery of unexpected training side effects provides a benchmark to study out-of-domain generalization and predict model misbehaviors prior to deployment.

<small>Yaowen Ye, Jacob Steinhardt, Sarah Schwettmann, Cassidy Laidlaw</small>

### [FrontierPhysics: Evaluating Agents for End-to-End Physics Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06910v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-11-04 · `arXiv:2611.06910v1`

FrontierPhysics is an end-to-end benchmark comprising 56 expert-authored tasks designed to evaluate AI agents across complete physics research workflows.

- Covers 56 research tasks created by 42 physicists across nine theoretical, computational, and experimental physics domains.
- Requires agents to perform literature reviews, formulate research plans, conduct experiments, and write scientific manuscripts.
- Uses deterministic verifiers and expert rubrics to score both the correctness and publishability of research outputs.
- Evaluated 22 models across 26 configurations, with the highest-performing configuration succeeding in at most 46 out of 168 rollouts.
- Trajectory analysis indicates agents fail primarily due to insufficiently rigorous reasoning and limited expert intuition rather than execution errors or knowledge gaps.

**Why it matters:** It establishes a rigorous evaluation standard that shifts AI physics benchmarks from solving isolated static problems to assessing full-lifecycle research competence.

<small>Bingran You, Wenjun Ke, Ria Rosenauer, Bo Liu, Wenbo Chen, Min Chen, Shuqi Xu, Yue Ma</small>
