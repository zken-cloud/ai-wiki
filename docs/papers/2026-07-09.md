### [The Illusion of Equivalency: Statistical Characterization of Quantization Effects in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08734v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-09 · `arXiv:2607.08734v1`

This paper introduces correctness agreement to measure behavioral divergence in quantized LLMs, revealing significant changes even when accuracy and perplexity are preserved, and identifies sensitive attention projections.

- Accuracy and perplexity metrics fail to capture behavioral changes in quantized LLMs.
- Correctness agreement, a decision-level metric, measures overlap in correct predictions between base and quantized models.
- Behavioral divergence emerges under moderate quantization (8-bit to 2-bit) despite preserved task performance.
- Quantization acts as a structural operator on attention weights, with non-linear breakpoints at low bit-widths.
- Query and key projections are consistently more sensitive to quantization than value and output projections.

**Why it matters:** Relying solely on accuracy and perplexity for quantized LLMs can mask significant behavioral changes, necessitating new evaluation metrics for reliable deployment.

### [Two Axes of LLM Abstention: Answer Correctness and Question Answerability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08456v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-09 · `arXiv:2607.08456v1`

LLMs exhibit distinct axes for answer correctness and question answerability; a two-axis abstention policy, using hidden state probes for answerability, significantly improves abstention performance over single-score methods.

- Answer correctness and question answerability are separate axes for LLM abstention, not unified by a single confidence score.
- Ordinary answer-confidence tracks correctness but is nearly blind to question answerability, especially for false-premise questions (CREPE dataset).
- A linear probe on hidden states effectively detects question unanswerability, achieving 0.69 to 0.77 AUROC on false-premise questions, where other methods fail.
- Instructing models to check premises directly backfires, leading to 57% false challenges; routing this instruction with the hidden-state probe roughly triples challenge precision.
- A calibrated two-axis policy, using separate scores for answerability and correctness, certifies both unanswerable-answer and wrong-answer rates, achieving 0.75 coverage of correct answers compared to 0.31 for single-threshold policies.

**Why it matters:** This method enables more reliable LLM deployment by allowing precise control over abstention from incorrect answers and unanswerable questions, improving safety and trustworthiness.

<small>Benedikt J. Wagner</small>

### [How are linear representations learned? Exact solutions to the dynamics of abstraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08843v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-07-09 · `arXiv:2607.08843v1`

This paper provides exact solutions for the dynamics of linear representation alignment (abstraction) during neural network training, revealing how data, target geometry, network depth, and initialization scale influence its emergence.

- Exact solutions for abstraction trajectories in minimal linear networks show end-of-learning abstraction is determined by data and target geometry, improves with network depth, and its maximum is controlled by initialization scale.
- In nonlinear networks, erf networks approximate linear theory, while ReLU networks' abstraction depends more on input geometry than target geometry.
- A proven attenuation law states that both erf and ReLU nonlinearities weaken abstraction in activations compared to preactivations.
- Evidence for the attenuation law is found in open models like DINOv3 and Gemma 4.
- The theory is applied to improve linear probe generalization in LLMs.

**Why it matters:** Understanding abstraction dynamics can improve interpretability and control methods in deep learning, such as linear probes and activation steering.
