### [A Coulomb Particle Model for Learning Kernel Attention in Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23869v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-26 · `arXiv:2607.23869v1`

A particle-based method learns feature distributions for kernel machines by optimizing kernel-target alignment with Riesz/Coulomb repulsive potential, yielding task-adaptive random features for linearized Transformer attention.

- The method learns feature distributions by optimizing kernel-target alignment while regularizing particles with a Riesz/Coulomb repulsive potential.
- The resulting Hamiltonian provides diverse, task-adaptive random features and has a mean-field description via a McKean--Vlasov equation.
- The approach is instantiated in linearized Transformer attention by learning positive random-feature maps in a first alignment phase.
- After freezing the kernel, the remaining network parameters are trained with cross-entropy.
- Experiments show improved accuracy, calibration, and robustness for learned kernelized attention on synthetic classification and sentence-level benchmarks, maintaining linear-attention inference complexity.

**Why it matters:** This method offers a way to improve the performance of linearized Transformer attention by learning task-adaptive random features, potentially enhancing accuracy and robustness without increasing inference complexity.

<small>Masoud Badiei Khuzani, Sharath Honnaiah, Atiq Islam, Alex Cozzi, Abraham Bagherjeiran</small>

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-26 · `arXiv:2607.23838v1`

- TriShieldRAG implements three defense rings: Ingest Guard (screens documents for poisoning signatures), Retrieval Scorer (re-ranks retrieved documents by provenance and consistency-weighted trust), and Cross-LLM Consensus (polls Claude, Mistral Small, Llama 3.2, allows bounded re-retrieval on disagreement).

**Why it matters:** This framework significantly improves the trustworthiness of RAG systems by mitigating knowledge corruption attacks, making them more reliable for sensitive or dynamic information.

ObsDriveBench is a real-world multimodal benchmark evaluating vision-language models under adverse weather using camera, LiDAR, and radar across three observability-focused capability dimensions.

- Evaluates models on three capability dimensions: observability awareness, spatial reliability, and risk-aware decision-making.
- Contains over 14,000 training and 13,000 test multiple-choice questions constructed via observability meta-annotation and scene descriptions.
- Integrates synchronized camera, LiDAR, and radar sensor inputs captured under real-world fog, rain, snow, and low illumination.
- Introduces the ObsDrive model, which combines normal-weather supervised fine-tuning with adverse-weather reinforcement learning to improve model robustness.

**Why it matters:** It establishes a fine-grained, real-world evaluation and training methodology to address cross-modal inconsistencies and degraded environmental observability in autonomous driving systems.

<small>Qiao Yan, Yihan Wang, Zhenghao Xing, Jiaqi Xu, Pheng-Ann Heng</small>

### [Mission-Level Runtime Assurance for LLM-Assisted ISR Swarms over a Verification-Aware Fabric](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23532v2)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-26 · `arXiv:2607.23532v2`

A three-tier compositional runtime-verification framework and verification-aware messaging fabric detect cross-agent ISR swarm violations that bypass individual per-platform LLM guardrails.

- Decomposes mission policies across a three-tier (platform/squad/mission) architecture using an evidence-aware algebra evaluated on two axes: security and completeness.
- Downgrades unsupported negative verdicts to an explicit unknown state during communication loss, preventing silent false all-clears.
- Tracks provenance to identify the specific group of platforms that jointly trigger a compositional violation.
- Successfully detected an indirect prompt injection attack that split a prohibited collection task across four LLM-planner platforms, which per-platform monitors missed.

**Why it matters:** It prevents LLM-assisted autonomous swarms from executing dangerous split-objective attacks or exceeding collective budgets when individual platforms appear fully compliant.

<small>Nikolaos Kekatos, Panagiotis Katsaros, Alexios Lekidis, Theodoros Nestoridis, Tom Nianios</small>

### [Do Diagrams Help Large Language Models Reason? Evidence from Syllogistic Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23513v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-26 · `arXiv:2607.23513v1`

A study evaluating Claude 3.5 Sonnet and GPT-4o-mini on 285 syllogistic problems reveals that visual and linear diagrammatic representations do not consistently improve large language model logical reasoning over natural language or logical notation.

- Evaluated two contemporary LLMs (Claude 3.5 Sonnet and GPT-4o-mini) across four representational formats: natural language, logical notation, linear diagrams, and Euler diagrams.
- Benchmark consisted of 285 syllogistic reasoning problems sourced from Ando et al. (2024).
- Models performed well on entailment and contradiction problems but struggled with neutral problems and exhibited systematic conversion errors.
- Diagrammatic representations provided limited overall benefit to LLM logical reasoning performance compared to text-based formats.

**Why it matters:** The findings demonstrate that visual reasoning formats like Euler diagrams, which aid human logic, do not currently yield parallel performance gains in multimodal large language models.

<small>Risako Ando, Koji Mineshima</small>
