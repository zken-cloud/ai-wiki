### [What Does Privileged Information Add to On-Policy Self-Distillation?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20612)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-16 · 👍 16 · ⭐ 4 · `arXiv:2609.20612`

A systematic study using the new AMPLE-Math benchmark reveals that reference-free distillation accounts for most gains in on-policy self-distillation, with privileged references providing only modest additional improvements via cross-mode transfer.

- Introduced AMPLE-Math, a benchmark of 5,319 math problems across six distinct reasoning views sharing identical answers.
- Reference-free distillation drives most accuracy improvements in Qwen3-1.7B under thinking-enabled evaluation, whereas complete trace references add a 2 percentage point increase in SmolLM3-3B at step 50.
- Replacing short direct-response rollouts with long thinking-enabled rollouts converts performance gains into losses in both Qwen and SmolLM3 families while holding problems, references, and evaluations constant.
- Teacher profile and matched loss interventions show that altering token-level supervision often leaves overall student model behavior largely unchanged.

**Why it matters:** The findings show that the primary benefit of privileged references in on-policy self-distillation comes from enhancing cross-mode transfer between shared parameters rather than the depth of the revealed solution.

### [Don't Mask the Environment: Observation Supervision Changes How Agents Explore Under RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20715)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-16 · 👍 15 · `arXiv:2609.20715`

ActObs adds token loss supervision to environment observations during SFT, improving downstream RL exploration and model performance without increasing parameter count, data, or sequence length.

- Standard SFT masks observation tokens, whereas ActObs supervises both action and observation tokens already present in agent trajectories.
- During SFT, action and observation gradients rapidly become orthogonal; masking observations leaves a large residual observation gradient and degrades environment prediction below base model levels.
- Post-GRPO evaluation shows ActObs achieves superior pass@k across all evaluated sampling budgets on Terminal-Bench 2.0 (Qwen3-4B) and gains +3.4 pp at pass@16 (Qwen3-8B).
- On unseen cross-domain code editing tasks (aider-polyglot), ActObs combined with GRPO yields a +4.2 pp pass@1 improvement over action-only supervision at 4B parameters.
- ActObs retains higher policy entropy during RL and minimizes KL divergence from its SFT initialization, preserving environmental consequence prediction.

**Why it matters:** Supervising observation tokens during SFT prevents policy specialization degradation, offering a compute-neutral optimization that significantly enhances agent exploration and generalizability in downstream RL.

<small>Juzheng Zhang, Disha Makhija, Manoj Ghuhan Arivazhagan, Vinayshekhar Bannihatti Kumar, Rashmi Gangadharaiah</small>

### [Sample Count Is Not Enough: Candidate-Generation Strategy Shapes the Energy and Performance of LLM Test-Time Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19499)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-15 · 👍 14 · `arXiv:2609.19499`

Evaluating test-time scaling by candidate count alone hides systems overhead, as batching candidates into fewer sequential calls dramatically reduces energy and latency.

- Increasing candidate count N from 1 to 8 improved accuracy on 500 GSM8K prompts by 8.4 percentage points for Phi-3-mini and 18.4 percentage points for Qwen2.5-1.5B.
- Fixing N=8 and comparing schedules (1x8, 2x4, 4x2, 8x1) on A100 GPUs showed that 8 serial calls (8x1) consume 4.64-4.86x more gross GPU-device energy than a single batched call (1x8).
- Executing 8 serial calls yields 5.77-6.12x higher P95 latency compared to a single batched call of 8 candidates.
- The energy and latency inefficiency of serial generation schedules was consistent across multiple A100 nodes and SciQ/V100 setups.

**Why it matters:** LLM benchmarking and deployment must report generation schedules alongside GPU energy and latency, as larger batch sizes with fewer calls optimize systems cost without sacrificing reasoning budget.

<small>Mobina Kashaniyan, Ali Jannesari</small>

### [Cross-Modal Attention Acts as a Frequency Filter: Why Verbose Prompts Improve Robustness in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20139v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-09-17 · `arXiv:2609.20139v1`

Verbose prompts improve vision-language model (VLM) robustness against image corruption because question-conditioned cross-modal attention acts as a frequency filter over image patches.

- Verbose questions (e.g., 'Please look carefully and answer: is there a cat?') broaden the frequency support of cross-modal attention, acting as a spatial frequency filter.
- Fine-grained or semantically complex questions concentrate attention onto fewer visual scales, increasing fragility under image corruption.
- Model answer drift peaks when the spatial frequencies of the prompt's attention filter overlap with the image corruption frequencies.
- Evaluated on Qwen3-VL and LLaVA-OneVision (8B models) across GQA and CLEVR benchmarks, verbose paraphrasing reduced answer drift variance by 70% to 81%.

**Why it matters:** Padding prompts with verbose phrasing provides a simple, practical technique to significantly enhance VLM accuracy and robustness under visual corruption without modifying model weights.

### [Don't Mask the Environment: Observation Supervision Changes How Agents Explore Under RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20715v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-09-17 · `arXiv:2609.20715v1`

ActObs adds token loss supervision to environment observations during SFT, improving downstream exploration efficiency and pass@k performance during GRPO reinforcement learning without extra parameters or sequence length.

- Standard SFT applies loss solely to action tokens, causing action and observation gradients to become orthogonal and degrading the model's environment consequence prediction below the base model baseline.
- ActObs supervises existing observation tokens in trajectory data without increasing sequence length, data requirements, parameters, or forward pass count.
- GRPO fine-tuning on Qwen3-4B with ActObs achieves higher pass@k across all evaluated sampling budgets on Terminal-Bench 2.0 and improves cross-domain aider-polyglot pass@1 by +4.2 percentage points.

- ActObs maintains higher policy entropy during RL while requiring less total policy movement, keeping the policy closer to the SFT initialization.

**Why it matters:** Supervising observation tokens during SFT prevents loss of world-modeling capability, yielding policies that explore more effectively and solve more unseen tasks during downstream RL.

### [Limits of Confidence in Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20581v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-09-17 · `arXiv:2609.20581v1`

Theoretical analysis proves that discrete diffusion step distributions fail to match the training distribution due to conditional dependencies among newly generated token positions, causing elevated sampling noise.

- Discrete diffusion samplers (remasking and uniform-state) only match the target distribution if the written positions are conditionally independent given fixed tokens.
- Per-position marginal distributions cannot capture dependencies within token groups, nor can they determine if a group of positions is conditionally dependent.
- Evaluations on the synthetic ScanAndAdd task show that all multi-position writes chosen by confidence rankings involve dependent tokens.
- Conditional dependencies caused the generated distribution error on ScanAndAdd to reach 29 times the sampling-noise floor total variation, despite per-sample metrics scoring 1.0.

**Why it matters:** Multi-token sampling in discrete diffusion introduces distribution shift by relying on per-position confidence metrics that inherently miss token dependencies.
