### [Wave Forcing: Towards Speed-of-Light Streaming Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00859)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-10-30 · 👍 93 · ⭐ 68 · `arXiv:2611.00859`

Wave Forcing co-designs model architecture, training, and execution to enable continuous, multi-step video generation at 134.8 end-to-end FPS using a 1F1C pipeline.

- Uses chunk-wise causal attention to access earlier, cleaner chunks before denoising completes, achieving a 1F1C schedule (one chunk output per forward round) via staggered denoising across model replicas.
- Applies a three-stage training procedure introducing mixed-noise denoising, motion supervision, and causal adaptation via self-generated history to prevent long-rollout degradation and camera drift.
- Includes Wave Runtime (WaveRT) with Wave Parallelism (WP) for early K/V delivery, communication-computation overlap, custom kernels, and pipelined VAE decoding.
- Achieves 147.6 DiT FPS and 134.8 end-to-end FPS on eight H200 GPUs using a 4-step Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B model, matching Rolling Forcing quality with a higher Dynamic Degree.
- Outperforms Ulysses and Ring context-parallel baselines in DiT-only throughput by up to 2.53x across 1.3B to 14B model scales.

**Why it matters:** It eliminates the serial bottleneck of autoregressive video generation, enabling real-time, low-latency streaming of high-quality long videos.

### [When Does Latent Prediction Error Support Physical Surprise Claims in Video World Models?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01815)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-11-01 · 👍 18 · `arXiv:2611.01815`

A new framework evaluates video world models, demonstrating that latent prediction error fails to reliably measure physical surprise despite representations encoding some physical information.

- Evaluations on the IntPhys2 benchmark show V-JEPA2 latent representations retain modest information regarding physical event possibility, but appearance changes become less aligned with physical differences in deeper layers.
- Tests using the Kubric simulation framework reveal that physically impossible events can yield lower prediction error than possible ones, and static error thresholds cause high false-positive rates for possible events.
- On Physion++ clips, non-physical disruptions like static frame repetition can trigger higher prediction error than the original video, whereas full-clip color changes can paradoxically decrease prediction error.
- Across action-conditioned models, native prediction errors react substantially to pure appearance changes alongside incorrect action inputs.

**Why it matters:** Researchers cannot rely on raw prediction error from video world models as a surrogate for physical surprise or causal understanding without confounding visual and static artifacts.

### [Sonar-MoE: In-Backward Expert Updates for Memory-Efficient Mixture-of-Experts Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03577v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-11-02 · `arXiv:2611.03577v1`

Sonar-MoE applies optimizer-in-backward updates to batched MoE expert tensors, freeing expert gradients immediately to reduce peak GPU memory without sacrificing step time or model accuracy.

- Updates and frees batched expert tensor gradients during the backward pass so the GPU only holds a single layer of expert gradients at once.
- Reduces peak memory by 19% to 22% and step execution time by 17% to 23% compared to standard training with global-norm clipping.
- Produces bitwise identical updates to baseline training without expert gradient clipping, maintaining held-out loss within 0.003 across OLMoE-1B-7B and Granite-3.1-3B-A800M.
- Avoids a 20% to 72% runtime slowdown by executing updates on whole batched tensors rather than individual experts.
- Supports dense parameter global-norm clipping while running optimizer-in-backward across expert parameters.

**Why it matters:** It enables memory-efficient fine-tuning and scaling of large MoE models without incurring the severe execution speed penalties typical of per-expert updates.

### [Written Once, Read Differently How Language Models Own Answers Steer Their Next Ones](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03198v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-11-02 · `arXiv:2611.03198v1`

A study of 22 open-weight LLMs reveals that self-commitment across sequential questions is mediated by a shared linear residual stream direction, but how models read this direction varies significantly across scale, instruction tuning, and prompts.

- Evaluated 22 open-weight models (0.5B–72B parameters) across 3,600 question pairs and 120 entities to measure how a fixed initial yes/no answer shifts subsequent answers.
- A single shared commitment direction in the residual stream drives the effect; directional similarity predicts cross-question coupling on held-out entities (Spearman's ρ = 0.43–0.85), and ablating it leaves at most 17% of the effect.
- Reading circuits are unstable: larger models disperse reading across more attention heads, instruction tuning causes more questions to read the direction in reverse, and altering the system prompt can invert the circuit's sign in certain models.
- Ablating the commitment direction while sparing the attention sink removes most coupling at a <1.3% perplexity cost in 18 of 19 instruction-tuned models, though un-spared ablation induces a high perplexity cost predicted by the direction's alignment with text's leading principal component.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrating that models write consistent vector representations but read them unpredictably highlights a key mechanism and failure mode of internal consistency during multi-turn LLM reasoning.

<small>Marcus Armstrong</small>

### [Pretraining Data Scale Reverses the Muon-AdamW Fine-Tuning Gap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03633v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-11-02 · `arXiv:2611.03633v1`

Researchers discovered that while Muon beats AdamW during pretraining, AdamW yields superior post-fine-tuning performance when pretraining data budgets are sufficiently large or as model size increases.

- AdamW outperforms Muon after fine-tuning once pretraining exceeds a specific data budget threshold.
- As model size increases, AdamW overtakes Muon at progressively smaller token-to-parameter ratios.
- The authors introduce the margin-to-spread ratio (measuring correct logit distance over wrong logit spread) to explain fine-tuning output perturbations.
- Muon maintains a larger margin-to-spread ratio at small data budgets, whereas AdamW achieves a larger ratio at high pretraining token counts.
- In a linear associative memory model, the margin-to-spread ratio of gradient descent is mathematically proven and simulated to gain on Muon as data scale grows.

**Why it matters:** Choosing an optimizer purely based on pretraining loss efficiency can lead to suboptimal downstream performance after fine-tuning at large data and model scales.

<small>Catherine Li, Jingchu Gai, Andrej Risteski, Jacob Mitchell Springer, Aditi Raghunathan</small>

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-11-02 · `arXiv:2611.03627v1`

RLVR can cause models to switch from structured derivations to ineffective brute-force enumeration under out-of-distribution shifts, a failure mitigated by co-training on bijective inversion tasks without requiring process supervision.

- Evaluated RLVR using an equation-solving framework (f(x)=y for non-bijective string functions) to observe reasoning strategies under easy-to-hard OOD shifts like longer output strings or more solutions.
- Identified a specific RLVR failure mode where models achieve high in-distribution accuracy using step-by-step derivation, but abandon derivation for brute-force enumeration on OOD tasks.
- Demonstrated that co-training with bijective inversion tasks substantially restores the correct derivation strategy and OOD accuracy, despite these tasks being structurally different from non-bijective OOD evaluations.
- Provided theoretical analysis showing that outcome-only RLVR can implicitly assign higher credit to effective reasoning strategies without requiring explicit process supervision.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that high in-distribution accuracy in RLVR does not guarantee strategy generalization, but simple co-training interventions can implicitly enforce robust reasoning under OOD shifts without process-level rewards.

<small>Ziqi Pang, Yu-Xiong Wang</small>

### [FADE: Factored Ability--Difficulty Filtering for Online RLVR Scheduling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03625v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-11-02 · `arXiv:2611.03625v1`

FADE improves RLVR efficiency via a bilinear state-space filter that separates fast-changing policy ability from slow-changing prompt difficulty to target informative rollouts.

- Factored Ability--Difficulty Estimation (FADE) models prompt success logits as the inner product of a K-dimensional skill loading and a policy ability vector, minus a scalar item intercept.
- Uses Pólya--Gamma augmentation to enable closed-form posterior updates of the K-dimensional ability vector per minibatch using rank-K Woodbury steps.
- Instantly propagates policy skill updates to unobserved prompts sharing the same skill loadings while keeping item difficulty intercepts stable.
- Derives a closed-form utility function from the posterior mean and variance to estimate the probability of generating non-degenerate (mixed success) rollout groups.
- Consistently enhances downstream accuracy and calibration across five reasoning benchmarks and two base models while adding under 0.6 ms per step.

**Why it matters:** FADE optimizes the allocation of generation budgets in RLVR by preventing zero-advantage rollouts without relying on stale or noisy prompt pass-rate trackers.

<small>Tomas Duncun, Jonah Ellison, Mateo Ruiz, Hanae Fujita, Ingrid Solberg</small>

### [A Scaling Theory of Multi-Epoch Pretraining: Late-Time Overfitting and Ensembling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03590v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-11-02 · `arXiv:2611.03590v1`

Researchers established a scaling theory for multi-epoch pretraining using logit-averaged ensembles, using dynamical mean-field theory to prove ensembling mitigates late-time overfitting and shifts optimal compute-allocation laws.

- Logit averaging on FineWeb language-model experiments reduces late-training deterioration, delays optimal stopping, and outperforms equal-budget wider or deeper single models.
- Derives a dynamical mean-field theory (DMFT) for multi-epoch SGD in power-law random-feature ensembles to track error correlations across epochs and models.
- Shows ensembling reduces prediction variance that drives late-time test error growth, enabling compute-allocation where ensemble size grows at least as fast as training time per member.
- Demonstrates that excess test loss above the limiting loss decreases as a power of compute under the derived optimal stopping allocation.

**Why it matters:** As pretraining becomes data-constrained rather than compute-constrained, training multi-model ensembles over multiple epochs offers a compute-efficient path to lower test loss without requiring new tokens.

<small>Zhen Yang, Yidi Miao, Blake Bordelon</small>

### [FLAMIA: FLAsh MIcroscaling Attention for Fast and Causally Safe Training on Blackwell](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03344v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-11-02 · `arXiv:2611.03344v1`

FLAMIA introduces attention kernels optimized for NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs that use MXFP8 and mixed NVFP4/FP8 precision to achieve safe, consistent, and up to 1.42x faster training.

- Employs token-block scaling to reuse quantized operands across matrix orientations, alongside fused quantization to minimize overhead.
- Implements document-local scaling, workload-aware scheduling, and targeted safeguards to process packed variable-length documents without causal leakage or repacking.
- Maintains numerical consistency between forward and backward passes to preserve training quality and stability on Llama-style models.
- Delivers 1.10–1.40x speedups on B200 and 1.06–1.42x speedups on B300 for combined forward/backward attention calls compared to fast baselines.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates how to effectively translate low-precision hardware capabilities on Blackwell GPUs into end-to-end training acceleration for long-context Transformer models without compromising quality.

<small>Fedor Velikonivtsev, Mikhail Koltakov, Denis Kuznedelev</small>
