### [Training Object Permanence in World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28654)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-22 · 👍 116 · ⭐ 6 · `arXiv:2609.28654`

Researchers introduced WROP, a core-cognition inspired data infrastructure and 1.5M-sample training corpus designed to evaluate and train video world models on object permanence and solidity across six cognitive categories.

- WROP comprises 150 hand-designed cognitive tasks, using Blender generators to synthesize 10,000+ samples per task with randomized nuisance parameters like speed, lighting, and camera angles.
- The benchmark includes a 300-question exam used to evaluate 14 video models across reference-to-video, edit, and continuation architectures.
- The authors trained PWM-WROP, a 16B parameter world model, using PWM, a native-PyTorch training stack on AWS Trainium2.

**Why it matters:** Training video world models on targeted core-cognition datasets allows them to better acquire fundamental physical priors like object permanence, bringing artificial physical intelligence closer to human-like spatial reasoning.

### [Agent-Editing World Model: Rethinking World Modeling for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28416)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-22 · 👍 13 · ⭐ 3 · `arXiv:2609.28416`

The Agent-Editing World Model (AEWM) replaces environment observation prediction by evaluating action criticality and actively editing noisy reasoning-action history to reduce state contamination in LLM agents.

- AEWM uses an Action Judge to classify decisions into Critical, Exploratory, and Noisy categories, alongside a State Revision module that edits noisy continuations.
- The integrated system, EditAct, directly modifies the underlying trajectory state during real execution instead of generating simple natural language critiques.
- AEWM achieved 70.5% macro-F1 on the Action Judge benchmark, outperforming the top frontier model baseline by 10.6 percentage points.
- Rejection sampling fine-tuning on verified EditAct trajectories (AEWM-RFT) outperformed Self-RFT by 2.2–2.6 points across three domains without needing runtime AEWM guidance.
- Across six benchmarks and three LLM backbones, EditAct increased average task performance scores by 3.2–6.7 points over the strongest baseline.

**Why it matters:** It mitigates task-state contamination in long-horizon agent trajectories, allowing LLMs to fix outdated plans and unsupported assumptions without wasting compute on predicting complex tool outputs.

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-23 · 👍 9 · `arXiv:2609.29362`

Sparse AutoEncoders localise part-of-speech information in compact, category-dependent groups of latents rather than through one-to-one monosemantic mappings or atomic grammatical features.

- Part-of-speech (PoS) distinctions are highly recoverable from SAE activations, outperforming simple lexical memorization.
- Morpho-syntactic categories do not map one-to-one with individual latents; instead, they are represented by compact groups of sparse latents.

- Latent feature groups remain stable on held-out data and display feature overlap between related grammatical categories.

**Why it matters:** Understanding that SAEs represent syntax through distributed latent groups rather than single features informs how researchers decode and steer linguistic properties in language models.

### [On the Diffusibility of High-Dimensional Latents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28473)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-09-22 · 👍 4 · `arXiv:2609.28473`

- Finetuning Representation Autoencoders (RAEs) for pixel reconstruction recovers fine-grained visual details but alters geometry and reduces effective latent dimensionality.

### [PoEM: Predicting RL Outcomes from Existing Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30226v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-09-24 · `arXiv:2609.30226v1`

PoEM predicts the post-training RL policy for a new reward function by linearly combining log-policies of previously trained models without running additional RL.

- Log-space policies can be represented as linear combinations of existing log-policies when a new reward function is a linear combination of prior rewards.
- Log-policies from RL training span an approximately low-rank subspace across rewards, enabling approximation even when rewards are not linearly connected.
- Weighting coefficients for combining log-policies are estimated directly using reward or basis policy outputs on samples.
- The framework eliminates the need for computational RL post-training on new or combined reward functions.
- Methodology was experimentally validated across synthetic and real rewards in both text and image modalities.

**Why it matters:** It enables rapid deployment of updated or multi-reward foundation models while bypassing computationally expensive and unstable RL training.

<small>Kimia Hamidieh, Giannis Daras, Antonio Torralba</small>

### [FlashLoop: Fast and Memory-Efficient Looped Transformers via Lazy Updates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29812v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-09-24 · `arXiv:2609.29812v1`

FlashLoop is a training-free inference framework for Looped Transformers that leverages state update sparsity, sparse attention, and low-bit KV-residual quantization to reduce cross-loop computational and memory overhead.

- Identifies cross-loop redundancy: state updates concentrate on a small token subset, attention-output differences are dominated by sparse key columns, and KV residuals compress well via low-bit quantization.

- Achieves up to 1.64x end-to-end speedup and up to 6x reduction in KV-cache memory without accuracy loss across multiple Looped Transformer models.

**Why it matters:** It enables parameter-efficient Looped Transformers to scale to greater depths and longer contexts with significantly higher practical inference speed and reduced memory usage.

### [Encoded but Not Decoded: Layer-Localized Evidence for a Three-Level Gap in LLM Syntax](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29848v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-09-24 · `arXiv:2609.29848v1`

A three-level framework shows LLMs encode syntax better than they deploy it, with instruction tuning shifting the LM-head-decoded layer about ten layers later than the probe-decoded layer and favoring surface shortcuts.

- Evaluated seven models across English, Chinese, and German using a three-level framework: behavioral deployment, LM-head readout, and probe recoverability.
- Across all 14 (model, task) conditions, probe recoverability consistently equaled or exceeded LM-head readout, which equaled or exceeded behavioral deployment.
- The disconnect was strongest in subject-control tasks with nearest-noun heuristics, reaching a maximum gap of 0.653 on Qwen3-0.6B Instruct in QA and persisting at Qwen3-14B Instruct.
- Instruction tuning degraded behavioral deployment more than structural encoding in percentage terms.
- Activation patching confirmed the gap is layer-localized, with instruction tuning moving the LM-head-decoded layer approximately ten layers later than the probe-decoded layer.

**Why it matters:** Standard behavioral tests understate what structural information LLMs actually encode, while probing overstates what models can successfully deploy at the output layer.

### [Your Transformer Can Hold Two Thoughts at Once: Evidence of Linear Superposition in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29845v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-09-24 · `arXiv:2609.29845v1`

Researchers introduce the Superposition Linearity Hypothesis, demonstrating that Transformers process linearly combined text inputs to output a superposition of individual next-token distributions.

- Superposition linearity is an intrinsic architectural property of Transformers that naturally diminishes as pretraining progresses.
- Lightweight fine-tuning can substantially restore linearity, minimizing the divergence between superposed predictions and the average of individual next-token distributions.
- A novel guided decoding procedure successfully disentangles superposed outputs to generate two coherent text continuations simultaneously from a single forward pass.

**Why it matters:** Restoring and leveraging superposition linearity enables the simultaneous generation of multiple coherent text streams within a single model forward pass.

ExplorationBench evaluates AI systems' scientific exploration abilities using verifiable Alien Worlds with executable, counter-intuitive rules that prevent systems from relying on pre-training memory retrieval.
