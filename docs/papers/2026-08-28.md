### [PAWBench: How Far Are We from Probabilistically Aligned World Modeling?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27345)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-26 · 👍 73 · ⭐ 6 · `arXiv:2608.27345`

PAWBench and its evaluation protocol PAWEval benchmark video generation models as stochastic world models to measure how accurately repeated rollouts capture the real-world probability distribution of multi-outcome physical behaviors.

- Formalizes probabilistic alignment as a distributional criterion requiring world models to generate the correct distribution of valid outcomes under identical initial conditions.
- Introduces PAWBench, comprising 50 distinct physical scenarios, to evaluate whether video models act as accurate stochastic samplers.
- Presents PAWEval, an outcome-level protocol that translates multiple generated video rollouts into empirical outcome distributions.
- Evaluates eleven current video generation systems and finds that none consistently match reference probabilities while maintaining the full range of valid physical behaviors.

**Why it matters:** Determining whether video models achieve probabilistic alignment reveals whether they function as true physical world models rather than just generators of single plausible visual trajectories.

### [TTPO: Test-Time Policy Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27448)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-26 · 👍 61 · ⭐ 18 · `arXiv:2608.27448`

Test-Time Policy Optimization (TTPO) introduces an asymmetric objective combining On-Policy Self-Distillation and Grouped RL with token-level selection, enabling label-free test-time training for mathematical reasoning in large language models.

- TTPO addresses majority-vote pseudo-label fragility by treating agreeing and disagreeing rollouts asymmetrically.
- Agreeing rollouts are distilled via On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) while down-weighting already-converged token positions.
- Disagreeing rollouts are penalized using Grouped RL, restricting penalties to confident token-level errors.
- Majority-vote routing strengthens self-supervision as model quality improves, matching label-supervised OPSD across five competition-level benchmarks.
- Evaluations show Qwen3-1.7B performance increased from 38.0% to 45.2% during test-time training, with non-thinking gains between +25.2% and +36.4%.

**Why it matters:** TTPO enables large language models to continuously improve their mathematical reasoning performance at test time without relying on ground-truth labels.

<small>Aozhe Wang, Zhengxi Lu, Jianze Wang, Shangke Lv, Ying Liu, Weiming Lu, Jun Xiao, Yueting Zhuang</small>

### [Self-OPD: On-Policy Distillation for Flow Matching Models without Teacher](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26872)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-26 · 👍 55 · `arXiv:2608.26872`

Self-OPD is a teacher-free on-policy distillation framework for flow matching models that replaces external teachers with self-exploration, SDE trajectory branching, and advantage-weighted pull-push optimization.

- Branches deterministic next-state predictions into K stochastic SDE candidates at each timestep, rolling them out via ODE samplers.
- Calculates normalized advantages by comparing candidate rewards against a deterministic self-reference baseline.
- Optimizes the velocity field using an all-branch pull-push objective featuring direction-aware attenuation and SDE-variance normalization.
- Handles multi-objective alignment by fusing normalized scores directly at the reward level to prevent gradient conflicts.

**Why it matters:** It removes the high computational cost of training task-specific teacher models and mitigates compounding trajectory errors in flow matching distillation.

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-26 · 👍 55 · `arXiv:2608.27260`

### [The Handoff Tax: Continuing Non-Native Trajectories in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24358)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-24 · 👍 12 · `arXiv:2608.24358`

Studying trajectory handoffs between low-capability and high-capability LLM coding agents reveals a 'handoff tax' where full-trajectory model escalation recovers less than half the potential quality gain while driving up costs.

- Model escalation (low-to-high capability) recovers under 50% of the quality gap while incurring significant cost penalties when transferring full trajectories.
- Model downshifting (high-to-low capability) provides a favorable cost-quality trade-off for long-running agent tasks.
- Interface preferences reverse based on handoff direction: stripping trajectory context improves escalation quality, whereas removing context degrades downshift quality.
- Experiments evaluated Claude and GPT model families across different handoff timings, directions, and interfaces (full-trajectory transfer, compaction, and trajectory removal).

**Why it matters:** Optimizing how context is passed between cheap and expensive models allows developers to reduce LLM agent execution costs without unnecessarily sacrificing coding accuracy.

### [Disentangling Optimization Scale from Preference Scale in DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27032v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-08-27 · `arXiv:2608.27032v1`

A study reveals DPO's beta parameter entangles preference-noise scaling with optimization step size, causing non-monotone policy shifts, and introduces a centered-softplus reformulation to decouple these effects.

- DPO's beta simultaneously dictates the effective inverse preference-noise scale and rescales optimization dynamics, creating a coupling with the effective step size.
- At a fixed learning rate, policy deviation from the reference model is non-monotone in beta: it creates a dead zone at small beta, peaks at an intermediate value, and declines at large beta.
- Standard DPO loss values are incomparable across different beta parameters, as identical loss curves can exhibit multi-fold differences in KL divergence.
- The proposed centered-softplus reformulation is argmin-equivalent to DPO for beta > 0, decouples hyperparameter effects, and yields a linear preference-margin objective as beta approaches 0.

**Why it matters:** Decoupling preference scale from optimization scale reduces hyperparameter sensitivity and simplifies learning-rate tuning during language model alignment.

<small>Ivan Kruzhilov</small>

### [SWE-Prime: Fewer Trajectories, Better Performance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27449v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-27 · `arXiv:2608.27449v1`

SWE-Prime is a two-stage data selection method that filters agent trajectories at the trajectory and segment levels, enabling SFT on just 10% of data while outperforming full-dataset training on SWE-Bench benchmarks.

- Stage one performs trajectory-level screening based on process quality, result quality, and data representativeness to select high-quality successful trajectories.
- Stage two conducts segment-level selection by grouping consecutive steps into semantic segments and evaluating them for contribution to the solution, learnability, and risk.
- Unselected segments are retained during SFT to preserve contextual continuity, but loss computation is restricted strictly to the selected segments.
- Training on a 10% trajectory subset selected by SWE-Prime yields relative performance gains of up to 12.2% on SWE-Bench Pro and 24.2% on SWE-Bench Verified compared to training on the entire resolved dataset.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that masking noisy or redundant steps during loss computation significantly improves software engineering agent performance while reducing SFT data requirements.

### [RedEvoAgent: Automatic Red-Teaming Agent with Experience-Driven Skill Evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27439v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-27 · `arXiv:2608.27439v1`

RedEvoAgent is a black-box red-teaming agent that distills cross-case attack trajectories into evolving, human-readable attack skills to jailbreak LLM agents in execution harnesses.

- Targets risks in product-level LLM execution harnesses where jailbreaks cause harmful tool execution and state changes.
- Replaces full-trajectory retrieval with distilled, concise attack skills to minimize context overhead and mitigate retrieval bias.
- Evolves attack skills adaptively using tool-effectiveness profiling combined with Deciding-Tool Attribution for targeted skill updates.
- Incorporates a validation ratchet mechanism that exclusively retains skill modifications that demonstrate performance improvements.

**Why it matters:** It improves red-teaming tool efficiency and attack success across different target models and execution harnesses while maintaining context-efficient, interpretable attack strategies.
