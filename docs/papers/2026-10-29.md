**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-10-25 · 👍 256 · ⭐ 3 · `arXiv:2610.35201`

Denoising Token Credit Assignment (DTCA) improves reinforcement learning for diffusion language models by assigning token-level rewards based on prediction confidence gains, POS grouping, and entropy changes.

- DTCA calculates token contributions by tracking improvements in prediction confidence at remaining masked positions after a token is committed.
- Contribution weights are aggregated by part-of-speech (POS) categories to identify informative denoising timesteps and provide targeted token-level learning signals.

- Evaluated on LLaDA-8B-Instruct, DTCA achieved gains of up to 9.0 points on MATH500 and 5.3 points on GSM8K over the base model across various generation budgets.

**Why it matters:** DTCA solves the credit assignment problem in iterative diffusion language models, significantly boosting mathematical reasoning performance without relying solely on sparse outcome rewards.

### [Your Policy Is Secretly a Harness: The Decision Geometry of Agent Interfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37255)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-10-26 · 👍 7 · `arXiv:2610.37255`

A theoretical and empirical study revealing that post-trained agent policies implicitly encode harness-specific preferences, causing performance to drop significantly when harnesses change even if task-optimal decisions remain identical.

- Under KL-regularized control, log odds of an optimal policy reflect harness preferences, framing deployed return as harness ceiling minus policy mismatch.
- Evaluating harnesses using a fixed model conflates harness capacity with model alignment, as training updates only mismatch while altering harnesses changes both terms.
- On MiniWoB++, simply reversing observation field order reduced a trained policy's success rate from 85.0% to 19.2% despite optimal decisions remaining unchanged.
- On Terminal-Bench Lite coding benchmarks, the difference between ceiling and mismatch (estimated via a stronger model proxy) accurately predicts harness performance rankings.

**Why it matters:** Evaluating or changing agent interfaces with fixed models is misleading; maximizing performance requires re-training policies under the new harness to leverage higher harness ceilings.

<small>Jian Mu, Ruibo Shen, Yao Shu</small>

### [LinMix: Data Mixing as Linear Domain Attribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37390v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-10-27 · `arXiv:2610.37390v1`

LinMix frames data mixing as a linear domain attribution problem, replacing Dirichlet sampling with vertex sampling on the capped simplex to enable scalable, linear domain selection.

- Replaces Dirichlet proxy mix sampling with hyperparameter-free sampling at the vertices of the capped simplex (domains are either at cap or absent) to decorrelate domain effects.
- Derives an interpretable linear model where coefficients represent domain attributions, automatically isolating optimal mixes by retaining only domains with positive attribution values.
- Proves that Dirichlet sampling inflates attribution error as mixes concentrate around the prior mix, whereas vertex sampling is mathematically optimal and uniquely enables linear fitting at scale (e.g., 288 domains).
- Demonstrated empirical performance using 1B OLMo2 and 4B Qwen3.5 pretraining runs, achieving relative gains of 46% on MBPP and 14% on GSM8K.
- Outperformed the best nonlinear baseline by an average of 11% benchmark accuracy across 288 fine-grained domains.

**Why it matters:** LinMix eliminates the need for expensive proxy model search heuristics, enabling efficient and mathematically optimal pretraining data mixture optimization across hundreds of fine-grained domains.

<small>Hubert Baniecki, Przemyslaw Biecek, Martin Pawelczyk</small>

### [Mechanistic Failure Modes of Weight Pruning in Transformer Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37126v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-10-27 · `arXiv:2610.37126v1`

Mechanistic evaluation of pruned GPT-2 models reveals that magnitude neuron pruning causes circuit inversion, where output accuracy drops dramatically despite low perplexity and intact attention heads.

- Factorial pruning of GPT-2 across criteria (magnitude, random), components (heads, neurons), and sparsity levels (30%, 50%, 70%) shows output metrics miss internal mechanistic damage.
- Magnitude neuron pruning at 30% inverts the Indirect Object Identification (IOI) circuit: all 26 circuit heads remain intact, but Name Mover contributions flip sign, causing 17.7% accuracy despite lower perplexity than random head pruning (74.6 vs 140.1).
- Magnitude head pruning at 30% or higher zeroes the IOI circuit by removing output-facing heads, dropping model performance to near-chance.
- Replication using the Greater-Than circuit demonstrates that whether heads or neurons act as the critical failure point depends on the target circuit's specific architecture.
