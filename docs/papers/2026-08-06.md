### [ABSeeker: Training Long-Horizon Search Agents via Answer-Backtracked Credit Assignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05102)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-04 · 👍 37 · `arXiv:2608.05102`

ABSeeker introduces Answer-Backtracked Credit Assignment (ABC), a framework that converts sparse trajectory-level outcomes into dense, step-level supervision for training long-horizon search agents via SFT and RL.

- ABC uses Answer-Backtracked Clue Recovery to trace ground-truth answers back to intermediate required clues.
- Clue-Anchored Step Scoring evaluates individual search steps against recovered clues to convert sparse outcomes into dense rewards.
- The framework integrates into training via ABC-SFT (loss reweighting per turn) and ABC-GRPO (step-level rewards in GRPO).
- Trained on Qwen3.5-4B with 8.5k examples, ABSeeker achieves 37.3% on BrowseComp and 39.1% on BrowseComp-ZH, increasing to 55.3% and 52.9% with context management.

**Why it matters:** Dense step-level credit assignment allows small 4B-parameter models to match the search performance of 30B-scale agents while utilizing low data volumes.

<small>Yijun Lu, Rui Ye, Jiajun Wang, Yuwen Du, Tian Jin, Songhua Liu, Siheng Chen</small>

### [The Personalization Mirage: How LLMs Fabricate User Profiles, and Why Self-Monitoring Misleads](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04570)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-04 · 👍 35 · `arXiv:2608.04570`

A new benchmark, MirageBench, reveals that LLMs fabricate unsupported user attributes 35%–49% of the time, and that cross-model self-reported confidence negatively correlates with actual measured faithfulness.

- MirageBench evaluates 12 LLMs across 6 tasks, 150 personas, and 143,616 claims using an independent judge that achieved high human agreement (Cohen's kappa = 0.863).
- Over-inference (OI) is pervasive across all 12 evaluated models, with a cross-model mean of 41.6% of claims containing fabricated user attributes.
- A Self-Monitoring Inversion occurs at the model-selection level, where self-assessed OI negatively rank-correlates with judge-measured OI (rho = -0.60, p = 0.044).
- Within individual models, self-auditing ranks claim accuracy moderately well (AUROC 0.58–0.83), but OI rates vary significantly by task (27%–59%) and accumulate linearly in multi-turn interactions without revision.

**Why it matters:** Developers cannot rely on an LLM's self-reported confidence to select reliable personalization models, making external verification essential for persistent memory systems.

<small>Yushi Sun, Yanjie Zhang, Rui Sheng</small>

### [Distill Where You Fail: Recovering Learning Signals of Negative RL-Groups from Adaptive Teacher Guidance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00782)

RSTG enhances GRPO post-training by adaptively applying on-policy teacher distillation to negative zero-variance prompts and high-entropy/high-divergence tokens, combined with SFT on teacher-generated correct trajectories.

- GRPO suffers from zero gradient signals when all responses in a sampled group receive identical rewards (zero variance).
- Naively combining GRPO with on-policy distillation (OPD) degrades performance by restricting exploration, applying symmetric token suppression, and distilling unhelpful samples.
- RSTG restricts OPD at the sample level to negative zero-variance prompts weighted by teacher confidence scores.

- RSTG incorporates SFT on correct teacher trajectories to provide positive gradients, outperforming naive GRPO+OPD by +4.02% on math and +3.05% on code.

**Why it matters:** It resolves gradient sparsity and exploratory degradation in GRPO by extracting dense, selective learning signals from teacher models when student reinforcement learning fails.

OPD-V is a visual on-policy self-distillation paradigm that uses Positive and Negative Teachers to enforce Modality Balance, improving MLLM reasoning performance while lowering training costs.

- Identifies Modality Imbalance as a key bottleneck where dominant textual information prevents MLLMs from utilizing privileged visual inputs during OPSD.

- Validated across 6 benchmarks, 4 MLLM backbones, and 5 post-training baselines.

### [Chained Recursive Language Models for Multi-Iteration Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05124v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-05 · `arXiv:2608.05124v1`

Chained RLM is an inference-time architecture that repeatedly calls an LLM as fresh reasoning roots using plain-text summaries, blackboards, and durable artifacts instead of inheriting full conversational history.

- Divides complex long-context reasoning into staged partial tasks executed by the same underlying model without inheriting full context history.
- Passes state between fresh reasoning roots via a compact plain-text summary, a plain-text blackboard, and task-specific artifacts.
- Allows predecessor artifacts to be inspected, corrected, and extended in subsequent iterations to prevent early mistake propagation.
- Evaluates performance gains over direct LLM answering and recursive tool-calling on extraction, counting, ordering, and multi-hop reasoning tasks.

**Why it matters:** It improves reasoning accuracy on complex multi-hop tasks by preventing context clutter and early error propagation through structured, artifact-based context management.
