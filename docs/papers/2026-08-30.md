### [When Tool Outputs Become Commands: Separating Action Induction from Runtime Authorization in Tool-Augmented LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27146v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-27 · `arXiv:2608.27146v1`

SARA mitigates tool-output command injection by decoupling action induction from execution authorization, keeping Attack Success Rate below 0.63% on benchmarks.

- Identifies that untrusted tool outputs become risky 'commands' when action induction is conflated with execution authorization.
- Employs a context-isolated Action Probe to expose action-inducing semantics and log origin provenance without granting execution rights.
- Authorizes actual tool calls using strict criteria: matching user objectives, verified execution history, and goal-, chain-, and argument-level support.
- Uses a No-History-Promotion policy to prevent multi-step historical recurrence from converting untrusted action origins into execution authority.
- Limits Attack Success Rate (ASR) to <= 0.63% across AgentDojo and AgentDyn benchmarks while preserving core task utility.

**Why it matters:** SARA provides a practical, architecture-level defense to prevent indirect prompt injection and unauthorized execution in multi-step LLM tool agents without sacrificing utility.

### [Equal Ranking Quality, Different Decisions: Training Order-Consistent LLM Scorers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26762v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-08-27 · `arXiv:2608.26762v1`

Researchers introduce Order-Consistency SFT (OC-SFT) to train LLM scorers whose passage or response scores remain stable regardless of input candidate ordering.

- Published rerankers with near-identical nDCG@10 scores exhibit low decision stability, yielding retained-set overlaps of only 0.66 to 0.84 when document input order is permuted.
- Prompt-time modifications fail to eliminate order dependence without compromising ranking quality.
- OC-SFT trains model weights directly to decouple scores from input order, outperforming order-averaged distillation across 12 base models.
- On downstream QA tasks, OC-SFT reduces reader answer flips across permutation pairs to 0.125, compared to 0.149-0.164 for alternative order-focused objectives.
- A single OC-SFT permutation achieves higher retained-set overlap than averaging ten permutations of off-the-shelf models.

**Why it matters:** Ranking metrics like nDCG@10 mask severe decision instability in LLM scorers, meaning evaluations must measure threshold retention and downstream reader stability alongside ranking quality.

<small>Markus Frohmann, Mahdiyar Alavi, Elizabeth Lingg, Navid Rekabsaz</small>

### [Calibrated Enough to Know, Not Calibrated to Act: Fabricated Evidence Makes LLM Agents Commit to the Unknowable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27167v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-27 · `arXiv:2608.27167v1`

Escalating visual/evidential packaging raises frontier LLM agent commitment on unpredictable questions from 6.5% to 54.0%, even when evidence is completely fabricated, revealing a critical failure in the act/don't-act gate.

- Commitment to unpredictable questions increases from 6.5% under bare questions to 54.0% under maximal evidence packaging across 12 frontier models.
- Fabricating all data in a market panel yields a 36.8% commitment rate, which is statistically indistinguishable from the 37.6% commitment rate generated by genuine market data.
- Models demonstrate intact meta-cognition by classifying questions as irreducible 90% of the time (committing on only 0.4% of those), yet stated probabilities remain uncalibrated and decoupled from actions.
- Supervised fine-tuning of a 3B model on 540 synthetic probability cases (e.g., dice, coins) reduced commitment to 0.0% on target cases and generalized to three unseen domains.
- The trained action-gating mechanism is context-fragile: it requires response formats that allow room to reason and fails under rigid formatting constraints.

**Why it matters:** LLM agents deployed in financial or decision-making roles can be induced into confident, unauthorized actions simply by presenting them with authoritative-looking formatting, regardless of actual data validity.

<small>Pranav Aggarwal</small>

### [TTPO: Test-Time Policy Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27448v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-08-27 · `arXiv:2608.27448v1`

Test-Time Policy Optimization (TTPO) is an unlabelled test-time training method that combines token-filtered On-Policy Self-Distillation for agreeing rollouts with Grouped RL penalties for disagreeing rollouts using majority-vote pseudo-labels.

- Leverages an empirical asymmetry: rollouts disagreeing with majority-vote pseudo-labels are typically wrong regardless of pseudo-label accuracy.
- Applies OPSD to agreement rollouts while down-weighting already-converged token positions.
- Applies Grouped RL to penalize disagreement rollouts, targeting only confident token-level errors.
- Improves Qwen3-1.7B mathematical reasoning accuracy from 38.0% to 45.2% in test-time training without ground-truth labels, matching fully supervised OPSD.

**Why it matters:** TTPO enables continuous, label-free model optimization during inference on complex mathematical reasoning tasks without the risk of corruption from incorrect majority-vote pseudo-labels.

A systematic comparison of three fusion paradigms—Merge, Mix RL, and Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation—reveals key performance trade-offs, constraints, and guidelines for consolidating multi-domain RLVR capabilities in LLMs.

- Average performance across paradigms varies by at most 1.4 points, but domain-level gaps reach up to 8.6 points on individual benchmarks based on task-vector geometry.

- Selection criteria: Merge for existing experts needing low-cost fusion; Mix RL for training unified models from scratch; MOPD for retaining domain-specific gains where teacher bounds are acceptable.

### [Mechanistic Reaction Prediction via Discrete Flow Matching on Graph-Structured Electron Occupation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27429v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-27 · `arXiv:2608.27429v1`

MAELLE models chemical reactions via discrete flow matching over electron occupation vectors using Optimal Transport Continuous-time Markov Chains instead of traditional molecular topology graph edits.

- Formulates reactant-to-product mappings as a Continuous-time Markov Chain (CTMC) over graph-structured, integer-valued electron occupation vectors on bonding, non-bonding, and hydrogen sites.
- Employs Optimal Transport to generalize the discrete flow matching mixture path, generating mechanistically interpretable edit moves without needing elementary step annotations.
- Achieves competitive accuracy on USPTO-480K while outperforming existing models on structural complexity and reaction type out-of-distribution benchmarks.
- Predicts full electron redistributions, allowing the model to naturally recover chemistry-aligned mechanistic trajectories and identify side products.

**Why it matters:** By modeling fundamental electron rearrangements rather than ad-hoc topological graph edits, the approach improves out-of-distribution robustness and enables mechanistically interpretable side-product predictions.
