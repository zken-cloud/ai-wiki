### [GRACE: Generation-aware latent compression for efficient video generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10524)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-10-06 · 👍 57 · ⭐ 12 · `arXiv:2610.10524`

GRACE is a two-stage framework that compresses pretrained video autoencoders while maintaining compatibility with pretrained Diffusion Transformers through residual latent learning, DiT feature alignment, and asymmetric denoising.

- Retains a frozen base latent from the pretrained encoder while learning a residual latent to capture lost information under higher compression.
- Aligns compressed latents within the feature space of the frozen DiT to explicitly optimize the autoencoder for generation quality rather than just reconstruction.
- Adapts the DiT via lightweight fine-tuning and asymmetric denoising, processing the base latent ahead of the residual latent.
- Reduces token count by 8x and inference latency by 11.1x on Wan2.1-I2V-14B at 480x832x81 resolution while matching original VBench generation quality.

**Why it matters:** It enables order-of-magnitude inference speedups for large video diffusion models without requiring expensive retrain-from-scratch cycles or sacrificing generation quality.

### [Gains and Collapse in On-Policy Distillation:A Reinforcement Learning Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03185)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-10-01 · 👍 24 · ⭐ 2 · `arXiv:2610.03185`

This paper analyzes On-Policy Distillation (OPD) through a reinforcement learning perspective, revealing that OPD works by amplifying student behaviors favored by the teacher's implicit rewards rather than expanding student capabilities.

- OPD performance gains occur because the teacher's implicit reward model makes correct responses easier to sample from the student.
- OPD collapse into overlong, repetitive text is caused by reward hacking when the teacher's implicit preference misaligns with output quality.
- Masking unhealthy responses during training and using SFT initialization effectively mitigate model collapse.

**Why it matters:** Understanding OPD as implicit reward feedback shifts distillation focus toward the teacher's evaluation reliability rather than its generation quality, offering concrete fixes for generation collapse.

<small>Han Cui, Jianhao Yan, Yun Luo, Hongbo Zhang, Zhizhang Fu, Yue Zhang</small>

### [Minimal Witness Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07226)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-10-04 · 👍 16 · `arXiv:2610.07226`

Minimal-Witness Reinforcement Learning (MWRL) is a new RL framework that identifies complete families of irreducible, sufficient conditions by crediting proposed sets based on the coverage lost without them.

- Formalizes the search for irreducible sufficient conditions as a minimal-witness identification problem using a single black-box verifier bit.
- Assigns credit to each sampled proposal based on the marginal loss in total set-union coverage that occurs if that proposal is excluded.
- Derives a value iteration planner to systematically recover entire families of witnesses.
- Provides a policy gradient method designed to scale MWRL to large language models.

**Why it matters:** MWRL enables reinforcement learning models to discover diverse, non-redundant alternative explanations and mechanisms rather than converging on a single optimal solution.

- Addresses limited distributional overlap between strong teacher models and weaker student models during standard On-Policy Distillation (OPD).

### [SemanticFold: Latent Sequence Compression SeparatesLanguage Modeling, Decodability, and Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10304v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-10-07 · `arXiv:2610.10304v1`

SemanticFold introduces a latent prefix compression scheme to evaluate how sequence folding impacts language modeling likelihood, probe decodability, reasoning accuracy, and latency across five model scales using a fixed-target protocol.

- Evaluated across five models: Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-8B, SmolLM2-1.7B, Pythia-1.4B, and Pythia-6.9B under a fixed-target protocol to isolate compression effects.
- On Qwen3-1.7B at compression ratio R=1.7, compressed-minus-native mean NLL decreased by 0.135, driven primarily by residual adaptation rather than sequence shortening alone (MLP-only achieved 0.082 lower NLL than Full SemanticFold).
- On SmolLM2 at R=1.2, mean NLL increased by 0.013 over native, while both Pythia checkpoints showed effectively unchanged NLL.
- Linear probe accuracy and macro AUC varied by less than 0.03 in absolute value across conditions, demonstrating that likelihood, decodability, and reasoning dissociate under compression.

**Why it matters:** The findings demonstrate that sequence compression impacts language model fit, representation decodability, and downstream reasoning non-monotonically, proving that no single scalar metric can validate compressed latent states.

<small>Mingyan Liu, Min Huang</small>

### [Which Rollout Taught It That? BehaviorTrace and the Limits of Training-Data Attribution in Online RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10422v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-10-07 · `arXiv:2610.10422v1`

Researchers introduce BehaviorTrace, an evaluation harness showing that existing training-data attribution methods in online RL with GRPO are heavily biased by confounds like gradient magnitude and model fluency.

- Evaluated attribution methods (including GAS/renormalized TracInCP and TRAK-style estimators) on Qwen2.5-1.5B using GRPO online RL fine-tuning with a planted behavior.
- A baseline control ranking steps by gradient magnitude alone, without a target behavior, achieves 4.2x to 4.5x chance accuracy, matching or beating targeted estimators on two of three seeds.
- Model fluency predicts planted behavior labels at saturated checkpoints as effectively as all evaluated gradient-based attribution methods.
- Controlling for fluency reveals high instability across seeds and generation draws, though trigger-token gradients consistently align with targets where the behavior occurs across all three seeds.

**Why it matters:** The findings demonstrate that current RL attribution methods largely reflect uninformative confounds rather than true causality, necessitating rigorous controls for gradient size and fluency when evaluating training-data attribution.

<small>Amit Nautiyal</small>
