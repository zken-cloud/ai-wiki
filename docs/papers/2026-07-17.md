### [LongStraw: Long-Context RL Beyond 2M Tokens under a Fixed GPU Budget](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14952)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-15 · 👍 205 · ⭐ 41 · `arXiv:2607.14952`

LongStraw enables million-token RL post-training under fixed GPU budgets by optimizing execution for long contexts, addressing the gap between inference and training context lengths.

- LongStraw evaluates shared prompts without autograd and retains only model-specific state for later tokens.
- It replays short response branches one at a time, reducing the live training graph at the cost of additional replay time.
- LongStraw completes grouped Qwen scoring and response backward at 2.1M positions for groups of 2 and 8 on eight H20 GPUs, with a stress test reaching 4.46M positions.
- It validates end-to-end execution for a 2.1M-token prompt across 78 layers of GLM-5.2 on 32 H20 GPUs.

**Why it matters:** This allows AI agents to process and learn from significantly longer trajectories, improving their ability to handle complex, multi-step tasks.

### [BadWAM: When World-Action Models Dream Right but Act Wrong](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15207)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-15 · 👍 53 · ⭐ 44 · `arXiv:2607.15207`

BadWAM introduces World-Action Drift Attacks, a new class of adversarial attacks that use small visual perturbations to desynchronize a WAM's imagined future from its executed actions.

- BadWAM defines two attack types: action-only adversarial attacks for maximum disruption and imagination-preserving adversarial attacks for stealth.
- Action-only attacks directly drive the WAM toward task-failing actions, reducing success rates from 96.5% to 43.1%.
- Imagination-preserving attacks induce harmful action shifts while maintaining the model's predicted future close to its clean imagination.
- Future-preserving regularization can maintain strong attack performance in imagination-preserving attacks while reducing future imagination drift.

**Why it matters:** This research exposes a critical vulnerability in World-Action Models, demonstrating that their perceived robustness and safety through coupled action generation and world prediction can be fragile under adversarial conditions.

### [Demystifying On-Policy Distillation: Roles, Pathologies, and Regulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13399)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-14 · 👍 20 · `arXiv:2607.13399`

On-policy distillation (OPD) acts as an exploration catalyst, guiding students via token-level signals, but is susceptible to Student-Teacher Mismatch and Length Exploitation pathologies, which can be mitigated by advantage clipping and log-scale compression.

- OPD's primary role is an exploration catalyst, steering students with dense token-level guidance without expanding capability ceiling.
- OPD effectiveness is entirely dependent on the quality of its guiding signal, with prompt diversity being more critical than per-problem sampling numbers.
- Two pathologies are identified: Student-Teacher Mismatch (large teacher-student distributional gap misaligns guiding signal) and Length Exploitation (aggregated token-level objective creates length-dependent shortcuts).
- Lightweight signal regulations, advantage clipping and log-scale compression, are proposed to alleviate length exploitation and ensure faithful signal guidance.
- Experiments across seven benchmarks show these regulations enable effective distillation, outperforming OPD variants and RLVR baselines.

**Why it matters:** Understanding and regulating OPD signal quality is crucial for effective LLM post-training, preventing exploration pathologies and improving distillation outcomes.

<small>Rui Wang, Hongru Wang, Yi Chen, Boyang Xue, Tianqing Fang, Wenhao Yu, Kam-Fai Wong</small>

### [Bayesian Repetition Penalty: A Principled Adjacent-Conditional Framework for Reversing Attention Collapse in Autoregressive Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22694v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-17 · `arXiv:2607.22694v1`

A new Bayesian repetition penalty framework uses adjacent-conditional probability to correct anomalous confidence in autoregressive language models, reversing attention collapse without modifying training pipelines.

- The framework penalizes or compensates anomalous confidence by comparing a token's observed frequency against its corpus prior using an adjacent-conditional probability construction.
- The penalty ratio $R=f(m,n,p)/f(np,n,p)$ is self-normalizing and provides a closed-form logit offset with zero approximation error.
- The correction is applied as a frozen output-layer bias via exponential moving average, isolated from the loss gradient.
- Experimental validation on a 1.5B-parameter model reduced 2-gram repetition from 0.073 to near 0 while preserving generation quality.

**Why it matters:** This method offers a non-intrusive way to repair language models suffering from repetitive generation patterns, even after they have collapsed.

### [BayesPO: Bayesian Prompt Optimization via Parallel-Tempered Gradient-Guided Discrete MCMC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16001v1)

BayesPO introduces Bayesian posterior sampling for prompt optimization, using gradient-guided discrete MCMC with parallel tempering to explore prompt token space.

- Prompt optimization is framed as Bayesian posterior sampling, combining a task likelihood term (explaining input-output examples) and an LLM prior (favoring fluent instructions).
- The framework uses an energy-based posterior sampling problem, where gradients guide discrete Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) proposals over vocabulary tokens.
- BayesPO employs a Metropolis-Hastings corrected Gibbs-with-Langevin (GwL) proposal and integrates parallel tempering for global exploration of LLM-induced energy landscapes.
- Experiments with Qwen2.5 models show post-optimizing APE prompts on 24 instruction-induction subtasks improved average accuracy from 60.04% to 63.23%.
