**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-07-02 · 👍 83 · ⭐ 133 · `arXiv:2607.02980`

HiLS Attention is a chunk-wise sparse attention mechanism that learns chunk selection end-to-end under the language-modeling loss, enabling efficient and effective long-context LLMs.

- HiLS Attention factorizes attention hierarchically, with each query attending independently to retrieved chunks and fusing outputs based on retrieval scores.
- Retrieval scores are incorporated into forward attention computation, allowing direct optimization with the LM loss for end-to-end retrieval learning and native sparse training.

- Existing full-attention models can be converted to HiLS-Attention with lightweight continued pretraining to acquire ultra-long-context extrapolation while preserving in-domain performance.

**Why it matters:** This method enables the development of long-context LLMs that are both more efficient and more effective on general long-context tasks than full-attention models.

### [When Implausible Tokens Get Reinforced: Tail-Aware Credit Calibration for LLM Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07976v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-08 · `arXiv:2607.07976v1`

TACO calibrates uniform credit assignment in LLM reinforcement learning to mitigate "Positive-Credit Contamination" by suppressing positive updates for low-probability, contextually erroneous tail tokens.

- Identifies "Positive-Credit Contamination" where uniform credit assignment reinforces implausible tail tokens alongside plausible ones.
- Proposes Tail-Aware Credit calibratiOn (TACO) to adjust positive credit for tokens based on their risk of being unreliable.
- TACO computes a tail-risk score incorporating local generation context to differentiate unexpected rarity from uncertainty-driven exploration.
- TACO tunes positive credit for risky tokens without eliminating gradients, allowing useful rare patterns to be reinforced while dampening noise.
- Outperforms GRPO-style baselines across three LLMs and eight benchmarks, improving training stability and sustained performance in long-horizon RL.

**Why it matters:** This method enhances LLM reasoning capabilities by preventing the reinforcement of flawed reasoning behaviors during reinforcement learning.

### [Future Confidence Distillation in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07626v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-07-08 · `arXiv:2607.07626v1`

This work introduces future confidence distillation, a method to predict post-solution confidence from pre-solution hidden representations in LLMs, improving reliability and calibration.

- Post-solution confidence estimates (Judgement-of-Learning) are consistently better calibrated and more discriminative than pre-solution estimates (Feeling-of-Knowing) in LLMs.
- Linear probes on hidden representations recover more confidence-related information than explicit verbalizations from LLMs.
- Future confidence distillation trains predictors on pre-solution hidden representations using post-solution correctness probes as teacher confidence estimates.
- Distilled predictors achieve calibration improvements comparable to post-solution confidence, are sample efficient, and transfer across datasets within the same domain.

**Why it matters:** This enables more reliable and low-cost confidence estimation in LLMs by anticipating confidence before answer generation is complete, supporting confidence-aware systems.

### [KronQ: LLM Quantization via Kronecker-Factored Hessian](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07964v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-07-08 · `arXiv:2607.07964v1`

KronQ is a post-training quantization framework for LLMs that incorporates gradient covariance into the quantization objective, improving 2-bit weight-only quantization performance over existing methods.

- KronQ integrates gradient covariance into the quantization pipeline, contrasting with methods like GPTQ that use only input activation statistics.
- The quantization loss in KronQ, based on a Kronecker-factored Hessian approximation, depends on both activation and gradient covariances.
- KronQ employs bidirectional incoherence processing, extending random rotation to the output dimension using gradient covariance to reduce weight magnitude variance.
- KronQ derives a new sensitivity metric for inter-layer mixed-precision allocation, driven by gradient and activation Hessian traces.
- KronQ achieves 7.93 perplexity for 2-bit weight-only quantization on LLaMA-3-70B, while GPTQ and GPTAQ result in >2000 perplexity.

**Why it matters:** This method enables effective 2-bit weight-only quantization for large language models, significantly reducing their memory footprint and computational requirements.

### [The Key to Going Linear: Analysis-Driven Transformer Linearization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07706v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-07-08 · `arXiv:2607.07706v1`

This work linearizes transformer inference by analyzing state update design, showing softmax relies on key-dependent rank-1 orthogonal projections, and introducing structural interventions like sink tokens to improve performance.

- Softmax relies on key-dependent, rank-1 orthogonal projections, explaining why delta-style networks outperform purely gated accumulation in linearization.
- Structural interventions, including sink tokens, short convolutions, and fixed-budget cache routing, reduce approximation errors in linearized transformers.
- The linearization approach was scaled across LLaMA and Qwen models up to 32B parameters.
- The method outperforms prior post hoc baselines on MMLU and matches long-context retrieval of complex adaptive-caching frameworks.

**Why it matters:** This research provides a method to reduce the quadratic cost of causal self-attention, enabling more efficient long-context transformer inference.

### [PALS: Percentile-Aware Layerwise Sparsity for LLM Pruning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07557v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-07-08 · `arXiv:2607.07557v1`

PALS introduces percentile-aware layerwise sparsity for LLM pruning, adjusting per-layer ratios based on activation magnitudes to improve perplexity over uniform methods.

- PALS adjusts per-layer sparsity based on the 99th percentile of activation magnitudes, bounded to ±5% of the target ratio.
- On LLaMA-2-7B at 50% sparsity, PALS achieved 10.96 WikiText-2 perplexity, outperforming uniform Wanda's 12.92 (mean over 9 runs, p < 0.001).
- The benefits of PALS are architecture-dependent, showing marginal gains on LLaMA-3-8B and no gains on Mistral-7B.
- Gradient-based allocation for sparsity performed worse than random, indicating gradient magnitude is not a reliable predictor for discrete weight removal impact.

**Why it matters:** PALS offers a computationally inexpensive method to improve LLM pruning performance for specific architectures without fine-tuning.

<small>Yazdan Jamshidi, Alexey Shvets</small>
