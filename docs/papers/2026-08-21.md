**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-19 · 👍 133 · ⭐ 8 · `arXiv:2608.19880`

4DAnyone reconstructs 4D humans from monocular video by using 4D Gaussian Splatting combined with a video diffusion model that scales to tens of consistent novel views.

- Reference Context Packing (RCP) compresses growing reference views into a fixed-length mixed-resolution context to achieve O(1) reference-context complexity.

- Outperforms prior approaches on DNA-Rendering and DyMVHumans benchmarks for novel-view video synthesis and downstream 4DGS reconstruction.

### [SkillGate: Training In-Policy Skill Selection in Long-Horizon Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18852)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-18 · 👍 3 · `arXiv:2608.18852`

SkillGate resolves selector credit starvation in long-horizon agents by splitting credit into distinct outcome-reward channels for execution and local advantage channels for skill selection.

- Identifies 'selector credit starvation' where sequence-level rewards penalize correct skill selections if subsequent execution fails, worsening as trajectories lengthen.
- Partitions token support into two disjoint credit channels: outcome credit for execution tokens and action-local advantage for skill-naming tokens.
- Assigns a positive local advantage to skill-naming tokens only when the selected skill is correct for that trajectory.
- Improves trial success of a 9B model from 40.8% to 53.2% across five agentic benchmarks using a 16-candidate skill slate.
- Reduces exposure to misleading skill candidates by two-thirds while lowering total skill reads.

**Why it matters:** It enables long-horizon agents to accurately select required skills from large libraries without being degraded by noisy sequence-level reinforcement learning rewards.

### [Credit Without Ground Truth: Auditing Step-Level Credit Assignment in LLM Agents Against Executed Replay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19760v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-08-20 · `arXiv:2608.19760v1`

A study auditing LLM agent step-level credit signals against causal ground-truth from executed replays in ALFWorld shows that common credit rules perform no better than chance and primarily echo policy fluency rather than causal step contribution.

- Audited against re-sampled executed replays, LLM-judge scores, logprob ratios, and policy confidence fail to identify causally pivotal steps better than chance.
- Causal contribution is sparse, with only 30.5% of defined decision points showing measurable effect, while implicit credit strongly correlates with policy fluency (median rank correlation +0.75).
- Conditioning credit on final outcomes adds zero causal information (partial correlation -0.004 for Qwen).
- In training experiments across seven arms, variations in checkpoint performance were driven by training dose (effective sample size/optimizer steps) rather than the content of the credit signal.

**Why it matters:** Reinforcement learning and fine-tuning methods for LLM agents currently rely on step-level credit signals that fail to reflect actual causal impact, meaning training efficiency is dictated by sample volume rather than true credit assignment.

<small>Haiyue Zhang</small>

### [FlashPrefill V2: Block-Sparse Prefill Attention for Long-Context LLM Serving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19758v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-08-20 · `arXiv:2608.19758v1`

FlashPrefill V2 upgrades block-sparse prefill attention for production LLM serving by adding a mean correction term for low FP8/BF16 approximation error and optimizing memory access for engines like SGLang.

- Introduces a mean correction term to suppress approximation errors during extreme attention sparsity.
- Redesigns the sparse attention operator using PackGQA memory access, warp specialization, pingpong pipelining, and FP8 quantization support aligned with FlashAttention-3/4.
- Natively supports paged KV cache and continuous batching to enable direct integration into production inference engines like SGLang.
- Achieves up to 47.26x (FP8) and 27.19x (BF16) speedups over FlashAttention-2, and 30.49x over an FA3/4-aligned dense baseline at 128K context length on NVIDIA H20 GPUs.

**Why it matters:** It bridges the gap between theoretical sparse attention research and production LLM serving by drastically reducing long-context prefill latency in modern inference frameworks.

<small>Qihang Fan, Huaibo Huang, Zhiying Wu, Bingning Wang, Ran He</small>

### [Truncate Bad, Upweight Good: BoN-Style Distillation via Rank-Based Classification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19748v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-08-20 · `arXiv:2608.19748v1`

TUP is a Best-of-N distillation method that truncates low-ranked candidate completions from the target support and reweights the retained upper tail using a tunable sharpness parameter.

- Removes low-ranked candidates from the policy target support instead of using smooth full-support reweighting.
- Trains offline using binary cross-entropy with shifted-truncated win-rates as soft labels and distilled-to-reference log-likelihood ratios as logits.
- Features a closed-form, prompt-independent normalization.
- Provides theoretical proof that a lower-tail truncation rule can match the best monotone rank-reweighting for any unknown oracle reward.

**Why it matters:** It enables efficient offline policy distillation from Best-of-N sampling without relying on brittle top-rank reward model distinctions or retaining low-quality completions in the target support.

<small>Yarin Bar, Yaniv Romano</small>
