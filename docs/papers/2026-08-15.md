### [How Can Rhetoric Reward-Hack AI Reviewers? Dissecting Rhetorical Sensitivity in AI-Based Peer Review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08975)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-09 · 👍 39 · `arXiv:2608.08975`

Researchers created a controlled dataset of 4,200 rewritten ICLR manuscripts to demonstrate how content-preserving rhetorical changes systematically reward-hack AI reviewers' scoring behaviors across various evaluation conditions.

- Evaluated 4,200 full-paper variants from 120 anonymized ICLR 2026 submissions across six rhetorical dimensions using two LLM rewriters and five LLM reviewers.
- Evidence framing and novelty stance produced the largest positive-negative contrasts in overall assessment scores, followed by scope framing as a weaker second tier.
- Score shifts depended heavily on original ratings: lower scores tended to rise, higher scores fell, and directional impacts were most distinct in middle score ranges.
- Strict review protocols reduced mean overall assessment by 1.36 points without consistently altering rhetorical sensitivity, while recursive or reviewer-guided rewriting showed diminishing returns.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrating that LLM peer reviewers are susceptible to rhetorical reward-hacking highlights the need for evaluation systems that remain robust against stylistic manipulation of scientific papers.

<small>Ming Li, Chenguang Wang, Xirui Li, Xinyue Zeng, Dianqi Li, Peng Shi, Dawei Zhou, Tianyi Zhou</small>

### [Full-bandwidth transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08888)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-08 · 👍 9 · `arXiv:2608.08888`

The full-bandwidth transformer introduces latent feedback by fusing the previous top-layer hidden state with the next token embedding via a gated linear unit, bypassing the narrow single-token vertical bottleneck in standard autoregressive decoding.

- Fuses top-layer hidden states with sampled token embeddings using a gated linear unit to allow non-verbalized computation to re-enter the transformer stack.
- Preserves the standard transformer architecture, KV cache structure, and language-modeling training objective.
- Uses a scheduled multi-pass training objective late in pretraining with a small fraction of deeper feedback passes to enable parallel teacher forcing and ensure stability.
- Evaluated on 1B-parameter models trained on up to 400B tokens, matching or approaching standard transformers trained on 1.5x more tokens with negligible per-token overhead.

**Why it matters:** It improves pretraining efficiency, downstream task performance, and produces shorter reasoning traces without requiring architecture changes, custom KV caches, or significant decoding latency.

### [The data geometry of masking diffusion: Certified-optimal schedules via unmasking growth complexity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13520v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-13 · `arXiv:2608.13520v1`

Researchers introduced Unmasking Growth Complexity (UGC), a data geometry measure that bounds discrete masking diffusion KL error to enable certified-optimal unmasking schedules.

- Unmasking Growth Complexity (UGC) local increments directly control KL discretization error across Bernoulli-subset and fixed-cardinality schemes.
- UGC increments can be estimated directly from data samples via KL increments along coupled reveal trajectories.
- The framework provides certified-optimal samplers that achieve target KL errors with high probability and iteration complexity within a constant factor of oracle procedures.
- In the fine-partition limit, the squared integral of the square-root UGC density sets the leading-order optimal Euler discretization error.
- Adaptive multi-block schedules utilizing UGC achieve up to \tilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{d}) dimension-dependent efficiency gains over non-adaptive schedules using a constant number of blocks.

**Why it matters:** It enables sample-based optimization of discrete diffusion sampling schedules, achieving guaranteed accuracy bounds while reducing required computational steps by up to a factor of \tilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{d}).
