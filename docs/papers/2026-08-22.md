### [Thinking in a Low-Resource Language: What SFT Builds, What RL Fixes, What Accuracy Cannot See](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17744)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-18 · 👍 13 · `arXiv:2608.17744`

Fine-tuning 3.6-4.0B active parameter MoE models shows accuracy benchmarks miss key updates, while SFT establishes target-language reasoning and RLVR resolves formatting, leakage, and instruction-following defects.

- Accuracy benchmarks are noisy at this scale, with random seed changes causing a 7.7-point shift that exceeds all measured data and recipe effects.
- Base models produce zero Greek reasoning traces out of 1,000, but SFT increases target-language reasoning to ~98% of items while improving judged grammaticality without degrading general capability.
- SFT fails to fix formatting skips (24%), leakage into the reasoning channel (3.5%), and poor obedience to explicit language instructions (<50%).
- Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) reduces formatting fallbacks to 2.5%, reduces channel leakage to 0.0%, and increases instruction adherence by 9.1 percentage points.

**Why it matters:** Evaluating reasoning models solely on accuracy masks crucial behavioural shifts and defects, requiring specialized length-independent dimensions and RLVR to build auditable, language-aligned LLMs.

### [EXIMO: VLM Guided Exploration of VLA Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19891)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-19 · 👍 10 · `arXiv:2608.19891`

EXIMO is an efficient algorithm for finetuning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) robot policies using a three-stage pipeline consisting of VLM-guided exploration, imitation learning, and residual off-policy reinforcement learning.

- Operates through three sequential stages: explore, imitate, and optimize.
- Employs a Vision Language Model (VLM) as a planner in the explore phase to break long-horizon problems into sub-tasks and collect an orchestrated dataset with the VLA.
- Finetunes the VLA policy on the collected dataset during the imitate stage.
- Applies residual off-policy reinforcement learning in the optimize stage to refine the final policy.
- Improves sample efficiency and final task performance compared to existing VLA finetuning methods.

**Why it matters:** It reduces the need for costly human teleoperation data and overcomes RL sample inefficiency when adapting large VLA models to new long-horizon manipulation tasks.

### [Phantom Gains: Auditing Self-Improvement Against a Measured Null](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20290v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-20 · `arXiv:2608.20290v1`

A study shows language model self-improvement metrics often report false gains caused by measurement artifacts unless evaluated against a separately measured null control from baseline replicates.

- Auditing 3 rounds of rank-32 LoRA self-training on Qwen3-8B revealed seven measurement failures—such as inference batching artifacts from single greedy decodes—that invert findings when a frozen control is absent.
- The expansion statistic separating acquisition from sharpening assigned an untrained control model a false acquisition rate of 0.280, and standard threshold repairs failed to eliminate non-zero nulls.
- Replacing thresholds with a per-problem exact test against a pooled baseline under false-discovery-rate control yielded zero detected improvements across all held-out replicates.
- Comparing pipeline arms showed external distillation improves problems the base model rarely reaches, whereas three self-training variants failed to do so and corrupted baseline-solved problems above the measured floor (regression p < 10^-8).

**Why it matters:** Transition-level self-improvement claims require a measured null baseline generated from existing study replicates to prevent mistaking evaluation artifacts for true capability gains.

### [Inject, Align, Recover: Staged Post-Training for Retrieval-Free Document Knowledge Internalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20281v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-20 · `arXiv:2608.20281v1`

Inject, Align, and Recover (IAR) is a three-stage post-training framework that internalizes fixed document corpora into parametric model knowledge for retrieval-free question answering while preserving general capabilities.

- Inject stage converts source documents into continuation, rewrite, and instruction-conditioned reconstruction objectives rather than using standard continued pretraining.
- Align stage adapts the model using answer-only QA supervision.
- Recover stage merges the domain-adapted model with the base instruction model to restore general capabilities.
- Outperforms Vanilla SFT in 7 of 8 dataset-model evaluation settings across Llama, Phi, Qwen, and SmolLM families on Common Corpus (CC) and CCI benchmarks.
- Yields average gains of 3.6 percentage points in domain QA accuracy and 12.1 percentage points in mean general performance across IFEval, MMLU, and MSBench.

**Why it matters:** IAR enables language models to accurately answer domain-specific questions without requiring a retrieval pipeline at inference time, avoiding the general capability degradation typical of standard fine-tuning.

<small>Qian Kou, Xiaofeng Shi, Xiaosong Qiu, Hua Zhou</small>
