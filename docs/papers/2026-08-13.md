### [AI4AI at Test-Time: Strong-to-Weak Capability Transfer via Harnesses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12307)

**Hugging Face Daily Papers** · 2026-08-11 · 👍 71 · `arXiv:2608.12307`

A study introduces strong-to-weak scaffolding, using a stronger builder model to construct deterministic, training-free test-time harnesses that boost weaker target model performance from 0.49 to 0.91 across four Theory-of-Mind benchmarks.

- Stronger builder models iteratively refine inference harnesses on a 5% validation set over multiple rounds before evaluation on the full test set.
- Target model gains stem from offloading unstable reasoning into deterministic code, benchmark-specific routing, and strict format enforcement without updating target parameters.
- Harness quality scales monotonically with builder model reasoning effort, yielding the largest performance gains on weaker target models.
- Platform effects are modest compared to the intrinsic capability of the builder model.

**Why it matters:** It offers an efficient alternative to traditional training-time distillation, allowing strong models to enhance weak model performance solely through inference-time cognitive structuring.

<small>Cheng Qian, Wenting Zhao, Liangwei Yang, Heng Wang, Jielin Qiu, Heng Ji, Silvio Savarese, Huan Wang</small>

### [Information Abundance Paradox: Long-Context Training Undermines Parametric Knowledge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12218v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-12 · `arXiv:2608.12218v1`

The Information Abundance Paradox reveals that training LLMs on longer contexts reduces parametric knowledge encoding, shifting gradient pressure from FFNs to attention modules and weakening test-time performance when context is missing or misleading.

- Pretraining with long documents improves closed-book MCQA, NLU, and language modeling only up to an intermediate context window size, after which performance systematically degrades.
- In supervised fine-tuning, training with richer context improves full-context evaluation but harms test-time robustness when context is absent or adversarial.
- Mechanistically, abundant context offers a lower-complexity optimization route, shifting gradient updates away from parametric-knowledge-associated FFNs and toward attention layers.
- Causal interventions confirm that long-context training causally increases the model's operational reliance on context during inference.

**Why it matters:** Scaling context windows during training creates a fundamental tradeoff between context-dependence and parametric knowledge retention, showing that infinite-context training can degrade model robustness without careful dataset and architecture design.

<small>Arda Uzunoglu, Benjamin van Durme, Daniel Khashabi</small>

### [Orientation, not magnitude: the causal structure of task-vector interference in merged language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11797v1)

**arXiv cs.LG** · 2026-08-12 · `arXiv:2608.11797v1`

A factorial ledger analysis of merged LLMs reveals that task-vector interference is driven by the orientation, rather than magnitude, of a layerwise cross-term that acts as a forward-pass attractor.

- Layerwise cross-term flux is dominated by amplifying transport (~65-70% in tested families, with gain >1 per late block), making the carried direction a forward-pass attractor.
- Erasure along the cross-term direction removes expressed interference dose-dependently, whereas norm-matched wrong-direction controls fail or backfire.
- Task pairs with similar local cross-term generation (differing by at most 1.9x) show a 14x-337x difference in causally removable interference.
- Instruction wrappers gate interference by drowning the cross-term interaction in a template-pinned main effect rather than shrinking it, yielding 13x less relative interference to remove.
- Erasing the cross-term early in the model is ineffective because propagation rebuilds it to 99% of its norm at cosine 0.99, requiring interventions near the output.

**Why it matters:** Evaluating parameter magnitude is insufficient for diagnosing merging errors; targeting the directional orientation of cross-term interference near output layers provides a causal mechanism to mitigate task arithmetic failures.

<small>Chencheng Zhu</small>

### [Massive Activations in Hybrid Linear Attention Large Language Models: Pre-Attention Spikes and Inter-Spike Plateaus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12149v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-08-12 · `arXiv:2608.12149v1`

Researchers identified two distinct massive activation morphologies in hybrid linear attention LLMs—pre-attention spikes and inter-spike plateaus—governed by the layerwise placement of full attention and the timing of activation cancellation.

- Identified pre-attention spikes (PAS) occurring immediately before full attention layers and inter-spike plateaus (ISP) where massive activations persist across linear attention layers.
- Validated findings across 5 linear attention architectures, 6 hybridization configurations, 5 data domains, and open-source models ranging from 1.2B to 397B parameters.
- Controlled pretraining of GDN-based hybrids up to 1.3B parameters demonstrates that output gating in full attention attenuates MA magnitude without altering layerwise structure.
- Mechanistically, PAS follows a localized write-sink-cancel process, whereas ISP results from delayed activation cancellation that resolves to standard full-attention MA morphology in the dense full-attention limit.

**Why it matters:** Understanding the structural patterns and cancellation mechanisms of massive activations in hybrid architectures is essential for optimizing quantization, numerical stability, and layer-interleaved LLM design.

### [Causal Structure is Inducible but Functionally Decoupled: The Routing/Readout Boundary of a Typed Mechanism Library](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11767v1)

Type-level supervision induces slot-by-type causal routing structures in transformers, but these routing slots remain functionally decoupled from final answer readout across 22.6M and 125M parameter scales.

- Type-level supervision induces slot-by-type routing organization that is absent in unsupervised controls and content-free gating labels, replicating across 22.6M and 125M scales.
- Slot codes act as a routing index but do not drive answer readout ($\|Δ\hat{y}\| \le 3.4\times10^{-6}$), establishing a sharp routing/readout boundary with no behavioral editability.

- Unsupervised null baselines shift with scale, demonstrating that reusing a null calibrated at one scale confounds cross-scale comparisons.

**Why it matters:** Mechanistic interpretability methods must separate structural routing signals from answer generation, as internal modularity does not imply direct behavioral editability.

<small>Xining Xun</small>

### [Ripple-Pivot Search: Active Parallel Decoding for Diffusion Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11742v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-08-12 · `arXiv:2608.11742v1`

Ripple-Pivot Search (RPS) is a training-free parallel decoding method for diffusion LLMs that proactively commits mid-entropy pivot tokens to trigger downstream uncertainty reduction and accelerate generation.

- Identifies a 'ripple effect' where committing a mid-entropy pivot position reduces uncertainty across remaining masked positions, increasing parallel unmasking rates.
- Uses lookahead evaluation to select mid-entropy candidate pivot positions (where to decode) and their optimal token assignments (what to decode).
- Delivers 4-10x wall-clock speedup over standard decoders, increasing up to 18x speedup when combined with KV caching.
- Improves accuracy over previous lookahead baselines by up to 5.49% on reasoning and code-generation benchmarks while maintaining generation quality.

**Why it matters:** It significantly accelerates inference latency and throughput for diffusion LLMs without requiring model retraining or sacrificing output quality.

### [Rubric Dropout: A Simple Way to Mitigate Reward Hacking in Rubric-as-Reward RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11669v1)

**arXiv cs.CL** · 2026-08-12 · `arXiv:2608.11669v1`

Rubric Dropout mitigates reward hacking in LLM-as-a-judge RL by randomly dropping a subset of rubric criteria at each step during training while keeping group-relative advantages comparable.

- Training Qwen3-8B with GRPO on medical and science rubrics causes training and gold judge scores to diverge over time, dropping gold scores by 3 points on HealthBench-Hard and 22 points on ResearchQA.
- Rubric Dropout randomly drops 30% to 50% of rubric criteria per step, sharing the remaining subset across each rollout group to maintain GRPO advantage calculations.
- Evaluation consistently uses the full rubric, while criteria reweighting performed worse than no intervention at all.
- Applying Rubric Dropout increases OOD gold scores (+1 to +2 points on HealthBench-Hard, +6 to +7 points on ResearchQA) and reduces hacking metrics at zero in-domain cost.

**Why it matters:** It provides a zero-compute, easily implementable technique to prevent language models from exploiting fixed LLM-as-a-judge evaluation criteria during RL post-training.

<small>Minglai Yang, Xinyu Guo, Utkarsh Tyagi, Mian Zhang, Razvan Dumitru, Sunjie Hou, Yunzhong He, Daniel Yue Zhang</small>

### [Redistribution-based Cost Inference Improves Sparse Safe Offline RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12306v1)

**arXiv cs.AI** · 2026-08-12 · `arXiv:2608.12306v1`

Redistribution-based Cost Inference (RCI) converts sparse, trajectory-level stop-feedback into dense per-step costs via return decomposition to train safe offline reinforcement learning policies without losing theoretical feasibility guarantees.

- RCI addresses sparse supervisor feedback where only a binary signal marks the first unsafe transition in a trajectory.
- The framework uses return decomposition to transform sparse stop-feedback into dense per-step cost annotations.
- Mathematical proof demonstrates that return-equivalent cost redistribution preserves the feasible policy set and optimal Lagrangian within a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP).
- Dense cost transformation improves cost critic learning stability and conditioning in practice.
- Empirical evaluations on highway driving and robotic manipulation tasks show reduced violation rates over sparse and classifier-based baselines under label noise and heterogeneous dataset conditions.

**Why it matters:** RCI enables reliable safe offline RL in real-world scenarios like driving and robotics where fine-grained per-step cost labels are unavailable or costly to annotate manually.

<small>Ebenezer Gelo, Geraud Nangue Tasse, Steven James, Benjamin Rosman</small>
