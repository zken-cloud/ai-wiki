### [Calibration-Family Overfit: Why Trusted Sabotage Monitors Don't Transfer Across Lineages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06596v2)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-06 · `arXiv:2607.06596v2`

Trusted sabotage monitors suffer from calibration-family overfit, exhibiting a positive residual interaction (+0.172 AUROC) that causes a 21.7-point drop in sabotage detection when applied across different model lineages.

- Cross-family evaluation reveals a +0.172 (95% CI (+0.158, +0.185)) own-family AUROC advantage across four open-weight families after accounting for sabotage blatancy and baseline monitor capability.
- Geometric analysis shows each model lineage's sabotage occupies a distinct scoring direction; rotating the monitor's scoring direction toward the target lineage restores most lost detection performance.
- Under a defer-to-trusted policy with a 1% audit budget, off-lineage monitors catch only 19% of attack attempts compared to 41% caught by in-lineage monitors at the same cost.
- The overfit effect holds across an eight-family matrix spanning four frontier vendors, controls matched for blatancy, transcript-reading monitors up to 34B parameters, and multiple code benchmarks.

**Why it matters:** Single-pairing evaluations significantly overstate AI safety monitoring efficacy against uncalibrated model lineages, necessitating cross-family transfer matrices in AI control evaluations.

### [aiAuthZ: Off-Host, Identity-Bound Authorization for AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05518v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-06 · `arXiv:2607.05518v1`

aiAuthZ is an off-host authorization gateway that uses identity-bound HMAC-SHA256 signatures, single-use nonces, and argument-level policies to reduce AI agent attack success to 0% with 0.03 ms added decision latency.

- Evaluated 15 LLMs against eight real-world attack scenarios, finding refusal rates ranged from 100% down to 38%, with the most expensive model refusing only 50% of attacks.
- Enforces security off-host via per-message HMAC-SHA256 signatures, timestamps, single-use nonces, and unmodifiable role/argument policies.
- Logs all decisions in a SHA-256 hash-chained audit trail and issues HMAC-authenticated QR receipts achieving 94% mean verification accuracy across eight transmission channels.
- Blocked 100% of attacker-directed tool calls in the AgentDojo banking suite and 9 of 9 incident corpus case studies, compared to 4 of 9 for a non-identity-bound baseline.

**Why it matters:** It prevents compromised or deceived AI agents from executing unauthorized actions by enforcing strict, identity-verified authorization policy bounds outside of the model's runtime environment.

### [Learning Only What Valid Adapters Can Express: Subspace-Constrained Adaptation Against Fine-Tuning Poisoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05300v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-06 · `arXiv:2607.05300v1`

Subspace-Constrained Adaptation restricts fine-tuning to a low-dimensional subspace derived from trusted LoRA adapters, preserving clean task performance while blocking poisoning and backdoor attacks.

- Analysis of 196 public LoRA adapters on flan-t5-large reveals 30% to 38% of weight norm is redundant, with functional content lying in a shared low-dimensional subspace.
- Restricting gradient adaptation to 128 coordinates in this subspace matches full LoRA accuracy on clean data while maintaining 62-96% exact match under label inversion, compared to LoRA collapsing to 3-26%.
- Adaptation loss on corrupted data is two orders of magnitude higher (120x) than on clean data, providing an inherent out-of-distribution detection signal without additional modules.
- Against adaptive backdoor attacks within the subspace, attack success drops to 8% (vs. 100% for LoRA) when the target behavior is absent from the pool, but reaches 85% if the target matches existing pool behaviors.

**Why it matters:** It offers a built-in defense mechanism that prevents malicious or corrupted fine-tuning data from altering model behavior without requiring external anomaly detectors, provided a trusted adapter pool exists.

### [Agent Data Injection Attacks are Realistic Threats to AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05120v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-06 · `arXiv:2607.05120v1`

Researchers introduced Agent Data Injection (ADI), a new category of indirect prompt injection that bypasses current defenses by injecting malicious data disguised as trusted metadata or context formats to hijack AI agent execution.

- ADI targets trusted metadata (such as resource identifiers and data origins) and agent context data (such as tool call and response formats) rather than standard instructions.
- The attack exploits a fundamental failure in current AI agents to isolate trusted context data from untrusted external data.
- Demonstrated ADI vulnerabilities enable arbitrary click attacks on web agents (Claude in Chrome, Antigravity, Nanobrowser) and remote code execution or supply-chain attacks on coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI).
- ADI proved effective across both standalone LLMs and off-the-shelf AI agent configurations, successfully bypassing existing indirect prompt injection defenses.

**Why it matters:** Existing indirect prompt injection defenses are insufficient because they fail to prevent attackers from manipulating agent control flow through untrusted context and metadata injection.

Researchers introduced FARMA, an attack that poisons an LLM agent's persistent reasoning history to bypass defenses, and SENTINEL, a layered defense pipeline that mitigates it.

- FARMA inserts forged reasoning traces using evasive language to bypass keyword defenses, then uses self-referential reinforcement to defeat consensus-based defenses like A-MemGuard.

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-06 · `arXiv:2607.04645v1`

Retroactive Chain-of-Thought (RetroCoT) bypasses LLM safety alignment by framing harmful requests as forensic causal reconstructions of already-occurred events, achieving up to 58% attack success rates on gpt-4o.

- RetroCoT operates as a single-turn attack that asks the model to act as a forensic analyst and reverse-engineer the causal chain of a presupposed harmful outcome.
- On AdvBench (n=50), RetroCoT raised Attack Success Rates (ASR) from 0% to 58% on gpt-4o and from 4% to 52% on gpt-4o-mini compared to direct requests.
- GPT-5-family models initially achieved 0% ASR against RetroCoT by explicitly identifying the reconstruction premise in their refusal rationales.
- Adding a single adversarial feedback turn with evaluator critique bypassed GPT-5.4-mini protections, increasing its ASR from 0% to 48% (and to 85% in a control condition without fake low scores).

**Why it matters:** The findings demonstrate that safety alignment in frontier language models relies on specific pragmatic framing rather than a true semantic understanding of harmful intent.
