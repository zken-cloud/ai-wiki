### [Catastrophic Learning: A New Attack Vector on Continual Learning Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18976v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-19 · `arXiv:2608.18976v1`

Researchers identified a novel Continual Learning attack vector called catastrophic learning, where adversarial learning blockers target network plasticity to impede future task acquisition while simultaneously triggering catastrophic forgetting.

- Introduces learning blockers, an adversarial data manipulation that targets algorithm plasticity to reduce learnability of current or future unseen iterations.
- Proposes six specific attack strategies: Label-Exchange, Tensor-Exchange, Attraction-Coincident, Attraction-Preceding, Repulsion-Coincident, and Repulsion-Preceding.
- Attraction variants pull feature space representations together by minimizing loss between poisoned and victim iteration labels, while Repulsion variants maximize this loss so stability mechanisms resist parameter shifts.
- Evaluated across more than 4,480 simulations on MNIST and Split-CIFAR10 against three standard Continual Learning defense strategies: DER, ER-ACE, and iCaRL.

**Why it matters:** This attack vector exposes a critical security flaw in continual learning models where attackers can silently compromise a model's ability to learn future data streams before those data streams are even encountered.

<small>Benedikt Kluss, Niklas Bunzel</small>

### [FedLNS: Leverage LayerNorm Signature Modeling to Mitigate Adversarial Manipulation in Federated LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18736v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-19 · `arXiv:2608.18736v1`

FedLNS is a server-side framework that detects malicious updates in federated LLMs by modeling changes in LayerNorm parameters against a robust, history-aware reference without requiring extra client metadata or raw data.

- Extracts client signatures purely from returned model weights by tracking changes in trainable normalization-layer (LayerNorm) parameters.
- Operates entirely on the server side without needing trusted datasets, labeled attack examples, auxiliary detectors, or added communication overhead.
- Screens and filters out suspicious updates prior to standard or robust federated aggregation.
- Evaluated on GPT, BERT, and LLaMA architectures with 200 clients, achieving lower test perplexity than six baselines under 40% target manipulation across both IID and non-IID data distributions.

**Why it matters:** It enables federated LLM training to withstand heavy data manipulation attacks without compromising client privacy or requiring additional network bandwidth.

<small>Kai Li, Jong-Ik Park, Carlee Joe-Wong, Wei Ni, Falko Dressler</small>

### [Task-Conditioned Least-Privilege Learning for Executable Terminal and MCP Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18351v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-18 · `arXiv:2608.18351v1`

A post-training framework trains a 4B parameter model to minimize excess authority by auditing task actions against deterministic verifiers and task-specific authority envelopes.

- Evaluates agent actions pre-execution and post-effect using deterministic verifiers across six risk dimensions (completion, evidence, exact state, prohibited attempts, and safe success).
- Post-training Qwen3.5-4B over 1,500 tasks increased safe success from 64.36% to 98.48% across 2,896 evaluation episodes.
- Reduces excess-authority error events from 4.56% to 0.79% in main evaluations, and by 6.99 percentage points in a 400-task continuation study without losing capabilities.

**Why it matters:** Learned restraint via post-training provides a effective defense-in-depth layer to prevent excess-privilege actions in terminal and Model Context Protocol (MCP) environments, though traditional permission gating and sandboxing remain necessary.

<small>Alexander Tu, Michael Tu</small>

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-18 · `arXiv:2608.17722v1`

MemCatalyst uses Poisoning Text and Poisoning Image strategies to force Vision-Language Models to over-learn image-text inconsistencies, significantly enhancing membership inference data auditing performance with a minimal sample budget.

- Introduces MemCatalyst, a data poisoning framework comprising Poisoning Text (PT) and Poisoning Image (PI) strategies.
- Forces VLMs to memorize specific inconsistencies between textual semantics and image features during training to amplify membership information leakage.
- Demonstrates effective transferability of poisoned samples across different VLM architectures in black-box auditing scenarios.
- Validated against five state-of-the-art data auditing techniques on two prominent VLMs, increasing MI AUC scores while maintaining negligible impact on model utility.

**Why it matters:** It enables content creators and data holders to reliably detect unauthorized use of their intellectual property in Vision-Language Model training sets without degrading model performance.

### [Improving LLM-Based SSH Honeypots Through Prompting and Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18686v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-19 · `arXiv:2608.18686v1`

Researchers evaluated prompt design and supervised fine-tuning across eight models to improve shell emulation realism in local open-weight LLM-based SSH honeypots.

- Local open-weight LLMs prevent cost exploitation, provider changes, and versioning issues seen in cloud models, but suffer from command echoing, malformed outputs, and inconsistent filesystem states.
- Evaluation used 34 automated unit tests assessing single-session and fresh-session shell emulation accuracy across seven local open-weight models and a fine-tuned GPT-3.5 model.
- Fine-tuning performance depended strictly on dataset coverage: the original 112-conversation dataset yielded no aggregate pass rate improvement, whereas an expanded dataset derived from real honeypot logs significantly improved local models.
- Combining strong rule-based prompt engineering with supervised fine-tuning showed conflicting interactions due to redundant enforcement of shell-behavior constraints.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates that local open-weight LLMs can effectively emulate SSH shells without cloud dependencies if fine-tuned on real honeypot log datasets and properly prompted.

<small>Muris Sladić, Veronica Valeros, Eman Alibalić, Sebastian Garcia</small>

### [CTIFoundry: An Agent-Native Corpus Scaffold for Cyber Threat Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18613v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-19 · `arXiv:2608.18613v1`

CTIFoundry is an agent-native corpus scaffold that transforms unstructured threat intelligence into a deterministic ontology graph and typed tool surface, significantly improving agentic investigation performance.

- Materializes a deterministic ontology graph using official cross-references across CVE, CWE, CAPEC, and ATT&CK as typed, traversable edges.
- Features a span-grounded report layer with canonical, alias-resolved cross-vendor entities indexing provenance-carrying chunks and hybrid dense+lexical retrieval.
- Exposes the corpus structure at query time via seven typed tools and three procedural skills on a standard open-source agent harness.
- Improves overall F1 score by +0.19 to +0.28 on the CTIConnect benchmark across four models, enabling smaller models to outperform flagship models on flat substrates.
- Reduces tool call volume by approximately half on Claude models while increasing accuracy, with ablations showing super-additive composition between typed structure and procedural skills.

**Why it matters:** Replacing flat vector databases with structured, agent-native corpora allows LLM security agents to conduct faster, more accurate threat investigations using fewer compute resources.

### [Denoising-Aware Inversion: Revealing Privacy Risks in Noise-Protected Text Embeddings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18610v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-19 · `arXiv:2608.18610v1`

Researchers introduced DAEI, a denoising-aware embedding inversion pipeline using a residual autoencoder and Stein's unbiased risk estimate, breaking standard Gaussian noise defenses on text embeddings.

- Identifies the Double Noise Trap that causes standard generative embedding inversion models to fail on perturbed outputs.
- Introduces DAEI, combining a residual denoising autoencoder with generative text inversion to reconstruct text without clean target access.
- Trains the denoiser in an unsupervised manner using Stein's Unbiased Risk Estimate (SURE) on noisy observations alone.
- Achieves a ~154% relative BLEU improvement and a 32-60% increase in token-level F1 and ROUGE-L over generative baselines.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that adding Gaussian noise to text embeddings is insufficient to prevent sensitive privacy leakage against adaptive attackers.

### [FESC: Remodeling Long-Context Private Inference with Encrypted State-Space Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17442v2)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-18 · `arXiv:2608.17442v2`

FESC is a hybrid FHE-MPC system for private long-context selective State-Space Model (SSM) inference that achieves linear-time performance without quadratic Transformer bottlenecks.

- Uses a factorized scan-contract algorithm to stream state chunks on demand, compress input-dependent transitions, and prevent dense FHE-MPC conversions.
- Combines GPU-optimized CKKS encryption kernels for linear operations with MPC protocols for non-linear functions including SiLU, softplus, exponential, and RMSNorm.
- Employs approximation-aware fine-tuning to preserve near-plaintext model accuracy.
- Executes a 12-layer Mamba-base model at sequence length L=2,048 on a single A100 GPU in 77.3 minutes with a 32.7 GB peak memory footprint.

**Why it matters:** It enables private end-to-end processing of long-context documents using linear-time SSM architectures like Mamba-2 on single-GPU infrastructure.

### [Geometric Data Perturbation with Noisy-Anchor Alignment for Privacy-Preserving Collaborative Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18749v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-19 · `arXiv:2608.18749v1`

A novel Geometric Data Perturbation scheme perturbs shared anchor representations rather than private-data representations to maintain downstream model accuracy while preventing data recovery under analyst-participant collusion.

- Analyst-participant collusion can compromise non-colluding private data when using shared-anchor alignment from Data Collaboration (DC).
- Adding noise directly to private-data representations mitigates leakage but degrades utility, whereas adding noise to anchor representations preserves a better privacy-utility trade-off.
- Participants independently transform private data and a shared anchor matrix, perturbing only the anchor representation before a single-round upload.
- The central analyst uses the noisy anchor representations to align private-data representations by solving a Generalized Orthogonal Procrustes Problem.
- Empirical validation on MNIST and CelebA demonstrates higher learning accuracy than private-data noise under equivalent measured leakage.

**Why it matters:** It enables accurate one-shot collaborative learning across multiple parties without exposing private raw data to colluding participants and analysts.

<small>Keiyu Nosaka, Yamato Suetake, Yuichi Takano, Yukihiko Okada, Akiko Yoshise</small>

### [Diff-DDoS: Realistic Cyber-Physical Attack Synthesis and Robust Detection for 5G-Enabled CPS Using Tabular Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17796v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-18 · `arXiv:2608.17796v1`

Diff-DDoS uses a tabular denoising diffusion probabilistic model (TabDDPM) with inverse classifier guidance to generate distribution-preserving DDoS attacks and harden 5G cyber-physical intrusion detectors via adversarial training.

- Baseline detectors trained on hand-crafted attacks suffer severe performance degradation, with F1-score drops ranging from 47% to 100% when exposed to realistic attacks.
- The three-phase Diff-DDoS framework trains a baseline CNN detector on CDR spatiotemporal grids, synthesizes realistic attacks using TabDDPM, and iteratively hardens models via Adversarial Diffusion Training (ADT).
- Evaluated on a Milano CDR dataset, ADT paired with ResNet50 recovers F1-scores to 79.62% for silent-call, 100% for Internet-signaling, and 92.79% for blended attack scenarios.
- Following validation-based threshold calibration, ADT achieves a 100% F1-score on SMS-flooding, outperforming CTGAN (47.3%) and matching top gradient-based adversarial training baselines on silent-call attacks.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that tabular diffusion models can effectively synthesize realistic attack traffic to stress-test and defensively harden intrusion detection systems in data-scarce 5G deployments.

<small>Bilal Hussain, Xiao Tang, Qinghe Du, Tan Li, Muhammad Azhar, Danista Khan</small>

### [The Last Mile of Deepfake Speech Detection: An Industry-Academia Experience Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17585v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-18 · `arXiv:2608.17585v1`

A three-year industry-academia collaboration with Phonexia identifies critical barriers in deploying deepfake speech detection, including restrictive dataset licensing, benchmark audio mismatches, and uninterpretable log-likelihood ratio scores.

- Commercial deployment faces licensing restrictions on public benchmarks, limiting their use in model development.
- Real-world inputs deviate from public benchmarks, consisting of long, codec-degraded, and partially synthetic recordings rather than short, clean clips.
- Calibrated system outputs, such as a log-likelihood ratio of 2.5, lack actionable interpretability for non-expert customers.
- The authors advocate for commercially usable datasets, realistic deployment benchmarks, and standardized, interpretable scoring.

**Why it matters:** Solving these deployment barriers is necessary to translate near-perfect academic deepfake detection metrics into reliable, real-world commercial security systems.

<small>Anton Firc, Kamil Malinka, Vojtěch Staněk, Miroslav Hlaváček, Marek Bartoň</small>
