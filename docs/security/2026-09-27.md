### [AERIAL: Adversarial Evaluation of Robustness in Accuracy-Preserving Low-Precision EEG Decoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30037v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-09-24 · `arXiv:2609.30037v1`

AERIAL evaluates EEG decoders on BCI Competition IV-2a, demonstrating that accuracy-preserving compression (INT8 PTQ/QAT, 50% pruning) does not improve direct PGD adversarial robustness despite pruning reducing bidirectional transfer efficiency.

- Evaluated FP32, 50% pruned (P50), INT8 PTQ, and INT8 QAT versions of EEGNet and ShallowConvNet across 9 subjects and 3 seeds.
- At perturbation epsilon=0.005, EEGNet PGD accuracy stays at 22-24% across all FP32, P50, PTQ, and QAT models, showing no gain in direct robustness.
- P50 pruning reduces bidirectional transfer efficiency to 0.963/0.928 (FP32 to P50 / P50 to FP32), whereas INT8 PTQ maintains high transfer efficiency at 0.994/0.997.
- Simulated quantization aligns with native TensorRT hardware deployment, showing 95-98% agreement in clean and adversarial predictions.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that direct adversarial robustness, transferability, and hardware deployment efficiency are decoupled properties in compressed BCI models, meaning compression cannot be relied upon to mitigate direct attacks.

<small>Saim Rehman, Muhammad Shafique</small>

### [When Temporal Perturbations Act Like Sensor Biases: Label-Free Auditing of Wearable Activity Recognizers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29937v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-09-24 · `arXiv:2609.29937v1`

SpectrumAudit is a label-free auditing method that uses phase-randomized stimuli on held-out calibration windows to isolate and evaluate whether wearable HAR model vulnerabilities stem from persistent DC sensor offsets or AC variation.

- Evaluates frozen victim models by selecting a phase-randomized full-window stimulus and replaying its exact DC projection and budget-constrained zero-mean residual without refitting.
- Tested across 27 victim models spanning three datasets and three backbones, achieving three-phase robust accuracy drops ranging from 2.87 to 40.83 percentage points.
- Under a fixed replay budget, DC offsets proved more damaging than AC signals on 24 of 27 victims and accounted for at least 90% of the total accuracy drop on 22 of 27 victims.
- In a held-out UTD-MHAD evaluation, the selected waveform degraded accuracy by 13.49 pp and macro-F1 by 11.68 pp, compared to a -0.66 pp change for matched random baseline perturbations.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that apparent temporal vulnerabilities in wearable HAR models are often driven primarily by static sensor biases, allowing developers to audit model robustness without labeled data.

<small>Qingyu Wu, Yuan Wei, Renju Liu, Hua Cheng</small>

### [Unmasking Shortcut Learning in IoT Intrusion Detection: A Forensic, Multi-Paradigm Evaluation of Feature Dependence and Data Leakage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28725v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-09-23 · `arXiv:2609.28725v1`

A forensic evaluation of the CyberFlowIoT-GICAP benchmark reveals that high IoT intrusion detection performance often stems from shortcut learning, such as raw timestamp artifacts and leaky random-flow data splits.

- Using purely statistical flow behavior (Fbehav), LightGBM, Random Forest, and Deep MLP achieved identical Macro-F1 (~92.58%), showing performance is bound by feature representation rather than model complexity.
- Adding raw timestamps (Ftstamp) boosted nonlinear tree-based models to 99.28% Macro-F1 while linear models remained at 90.62%, demonstrating that decision trees exploit temporal dataset shortcuts.
- Attack detectability is asymmetric: high-rate attacks maintain >99.8% recall on behavioral features alone, whereas DNS Beaconing recall drops to 0.00% without contextual features.
- Conventional random-flow splitting artificially inflates attack recall by up to 14.00% compared to PCAP-disjoint splits due to session-level data leakage.

**Why it matters:** Reported near-perfect IoT intrusion detection metrics may fail in real-world deployments if models rely on temporal shortcuts and cross-session data leakage rather than true attack behaviors.

<small>Uday Shankar Roy, Mahbuba Jahan Minu</small>

### ["What I See is What I Hear": Deepfake Detection Across Diverse Hearing Abilities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28659v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-09-23 · `arXiv:2609.28659v1`

A study of 80 participants reveals that d/Deaf and hard-of-hearing populations are less accurate at detecting audiovisual deepfakes than hearing individuals, driven by higher false-positive rates on authentic media.

- In an 80-participant study, d/Deaf and hard-of-hearing (DHH) individuals achieved lower overall detection accuracy (76.4%) compared to hearing persons (88.0%, p<.001).
- DHH participants exhibited a significantly higher false positive rate (29.7%) on authentic media compared to hearing participants (11.2%).
- Audio-only manipulation detection varied widely by group: hearing persons scored 90.3%, hard-of-hearing scored 90.0%, cochlear implant users scored 79.4%, and d/Deaf participants scored 41.2%.
- Audiovisual manipulation detection accuracy clustered between 84% and 87% across all hearing ability groups.

**Why it matters:** Audiovisual deepfakes pose asymmetrical security and misinformation risks to d/Deaf and hard-of-hearing users, underscoring the necessity for accessible defense mechanisms tailored to diverse perceptual abilities.

<small>Magdalena Pasternak, Malvika Jadhav, Palavi V. Bhole, Aviva Smith, Elaina Trapatsos, Vincent Bindschaedler, Roshan Peiris, Ersin Uzun</small>
