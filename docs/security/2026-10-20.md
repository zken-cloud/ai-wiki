### [Accountable partial understanding: securing the epistemic attack surface of agentic systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26799v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-19 · `arXiv:2610.26799v1`

This paper characterizes the epistemic attack surface of agentic AI systems and proposes Accountable Partial Understanding (APU), a framework comprising six necessary conditions, five design principles, and two measurement dimensions for securing delegated software authority.

- Identifies a taxonomy of four epistemic attack categories targeting intent interpretation, evidence handling, delegated authority scope, and human supervisor comprehension.
- Defines six necessary APU conditions: bounded scope with explicit assumptions, inspectable evidence, assigned responsibility, visible residual unknowns, actionability, and temporal maintenance.
- Establishes five design principles, including bounded delegation, decoupled evidence, assumption tracking with invalidation, role-specific accountability views, and intervention-preserving design with residual unknown registers.
- Introduces two measurable probe dimensions—evidence linkage and invalidation latency—to evaluate accountability mechanisms under adversarial pressure.
- Grounds theoretical analysis using three agentic use cases: an AI coding agent, an autonomous cyber-defense agent, and an AI-mediated insulin delivery update system.

**Why it matters:** It offers a structured architecture and concrete metrics to safely grant autonomous authority to AI agents even when human supervisors cannot achieve full operational comprehension.

<small>Josiah Dykstra</small>

### [Game of Phones: Harnessing Game Theory and LLMs in 'Bot Wars' to Counteract Phone Scams](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26781v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-19 · `arXiv:2610.26781v1`

A game-theoretic framework enables LLMs to adapt counter-strategies during automated scam-baiting, achieving closer alignment to Nash equilibrium without model fine-tuning.

- Models scammer-baiter interactions as a two-player game with empirically grounded strategies, utility functions, and a Nash equilibrium benchmark.
- Uses in-context learning to dynamically adapt strategies without requiring fine-tuning.
- Evaluated across 300 simulated dialogues and three LLM architectures, achieving 28.9% to 79.7% closer alignment to Nash equilibrium compared to non-adaptive baselines.
- Releases an open-source codebase and a dataset of 300 dialogues annotated with turn-level strategy labels and utility scores.

**Why it matters:** Automating adaptive scam-baiting increases resource costs for scammers, offering a proactive approach to deplete their operational capacity.

<small>Nardine Basta, Robin Carpentier, Benjamin Zi Hao Zhao, Dali Kaafar</small>

### [Beyond Attack Success Rate: Cost-Aware Metric for Evaluating Iterative LLM Jailbreaks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26589v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-19 · `arXiv:2610.26589v1`

A new evaluation metric, ASR_STEP, penalizes iterative LLM jailbreak attacks based on the number of calls made to the victim model.

- ASR_STEP modifies traditional Attack Success Rate by discounting scores based on repeated victim-model interactions.
- GCG scores drop by 7.0 to 8.7 percentage points under ASR_STEP discounting on JailBreakBench.
- PAIR scores drop by 3.4 to 12.0 percentage points depending on the iterative strategy, while single-step PAP scores remain unchanged.

**Why it matters:** Evaluating jailbreaks with ASR_STEP provides a realistic security assessment by accounting for the increased risk of attack detection during multi-step model queries.

### [Connection Escrow: Holding AI-Agent Pod Egress in the Kernel Until an Isolated Verifier Rules](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26501v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-19 · `arXiv:2610.26501v1`

Connection escrow uses a 98-instruction eBPF program to hold outbound TCP connections in the kernel's connect() path until an isolated container verifier issues a signed, single-use verdict tied to the socket cookie.

- Uses a 98-instruction eBPF program on the pod's cgroup to park outbound TCP connections before packets are sent, attributing sockets to process, executable digest, and SPIFFE identity.
- Requires verifiers in separate containers to issue signed, unexpired, single-use verdicts referencing the specific socket cookie; late or forged verdicts cause connection resets.
- Captures request content without terminating end-to-end TLS by placing eBPF uprobes at the agent's TLS library boundary (OpenSSL or Go crypto/tls) for model scoring.
- Adds a 4.5 ms median latency per released connection (24 us policy overhead) and successfully gated 600 connections across six Kubernetes pods without modifying agents.
- Blocks bypass attempts including child processes, renamed binaries, raw sockets bypassing sidecar proxies, and direct attempts to reach the verifier.

**Why it matters:** It prevents compromised or malicious AI agents from bypassing network security controls via direct sockets or child processes without requiring proxy TLS termination or application modifications.

<small>Ashok Subbabhatta Gopalakrishna</small>

### [Fake News, Real Crashes: Information Injection Attacks on LLM Financial Trading Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26438v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-19 · `arXiv:2610.26438v1`

A new benchmark demonstrates that LLM financial trading agents are vulnerable to information injection, where fabricated news and specific 30-word templates reach up to 100% attack success rates by manipulating evidence rather than instructions.

- Information injection via fabricated news achieves a 62% attack success rate (ASR) compared to 14% for prompt injection, with two 30-word templates reaching 100% ASR across 15 U.S. stocks.
- Fact-poisoning effectiveness relies heavily on surface-level credibility cues (e.g., source names, 'BREAKING' tags), as removing them drops ASR from 100% to 0% despite identical underlying claims.
- Attack success is state-dependent; for example, a fake indicator panel scores 91% ASR against an agent inclined to hold, but only 12% against one inclined to buy.
- Model scaling, peer debate, and standard defenses fail against fact poisoning, with only hierarchical aggregation successfully reducing the ASR.

**Why it matters:** Automated LLM trading agents sharing similar news feeds and policies are highly susceptible to coordinated market manipulation and synchronized crashes triggered by simple, crafted fake news.

<small>Mengxiao Wang, Nitesh Saxena</small>

### [SpanGuard: Decoupling Instruction Localization from Authorization for Lightweight IPI Defense](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26013v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-19 · `arXiv:2610.26013v1`

SpanGuard decouples Indirect Prompt Injection (IPI) defense into unsupervised instruction localization and small-LLM authorization, reducing required model capability while maintaining a 0.32% attack success rate.

- Identifies that small LLMs fail at IPI defense primarily due to jointly performing instruction localization and authorization judgment.
- Uses lightweight unsupervised segmentation to partition heterogeneous tool outputs into localized regions based on semantic discontinuity.
- Employs a small language model to perform block-level authorization checks on localized regions against the user request.
- Achieves a 0.32% attack success rate with negligible loss in benign utility.

**Why it matters:** It enables lightweight, computationally cheap language models to provide robust IPI defense without relying on large, resource-intensive guardrail models.

<small>Yinxiang Wang, Zehua Ma, Kejiang Chen, Weiming Zhang, Han Fang</small>

### [SPLASH: Contrastive Audit Pressure for Detecting and Localizing Security Hallucinations in Generated Code](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26001v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-19 · `arXiv:2610.26001v1`

SPLASH introduces audit pressure, contrasting teacher-forced log-probabilities under security versus style reviews to detect and localize security hallucinations in LLM-generated code without model training or static rules.

- Measures audit pressure by evaluating teacher-forced log-probabilities of a generated program under a security review prompt versus a style review prompt.
- Aggregates token-level scores to statements and programs using a probabilistic model of competing edits to locate vulnerable statements and pick secure candidates.
- Evaluated on 63,684 programs generated by 12 models across 899 tasks, achieving 74% accuracy in selecting secure code and placing the vulnerable statement first in 58% of insecure samples.
- Requires no internal access to the generating model, model training, or CWE-specific rules, outperforming frontier LLM judges.

**Why it matters:** It enables black-box selection of secure code variants and precise line-level vulnerability localization in LLM outputs without relying on brittle static analysis or retraining the underlying models.

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-19 · `arXiv:2610.25937v1`

RAGLeak is a supply-chain privacy backdoor attack that forces LLMs to reversibly obfuscate retrieved private documents for later high-fidelity extraction by an auxiliary model.

- Decouples privacy leakage from direct textual exposure by reversibly obfuscating private retrieved documents upon trigger activation.
- Employs an auxiliary LLM to decode and recover the obfuscated private data with high fidelity.
- Achieves up to 100% Attack Success Rate (ASR) across three model families and three application domains.
- Demonstrates resistance to defenses, experiencing at most a 10-percentage-point drop in ASR under representative detection methods.
- Persists after clean fine-tuning and generalizes across different model scales and versions.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that compromised RAG models in the software supply chain can stealthily exfiltrate sensitive retrieved data without relying on obvious prompt injections or explicit text duplication.

### [Towards Architectural-Level Safety Control: Relocating Hazardous Knowledge into Removable Experts in Mixture-of-Experts Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25791v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-19 · `arXiv:2610.25791v1`

REX achieves architectural-level unlearning in Mixture-of-Experts models by relocating hazardous knowledge into newly added, physically removable experts using selective gradient masking and routing differentiation.

- Expands MoE architectures with dedicated experts to isolate hazardous knowledge from general capabilities.
- Employs selective gradient masking and routing differentiation to move risk-related knowledge out of original parameters into the new experts.
- Excision of the designated experts on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B drops WMDP-Bio accuracy by 48.4 points while keeping MMLU performance within 3 points.
- Prevents post-release knowledge recovery under subsequent fine-tuning; after 250M tokens of fine-tuning, WMDP-Bio accuracy only reaches 42.8% compared to a baseline rebound of 78.3%.

**Why it matters:** Physically removing dedicated experts prevents malicious actors from recovering unsafe knowledge through post-deployment fine-tuning, outperforming traditional weight-suppression unlearning methods.

### [How Effectively Do Security Skills Guide Coding Agents in Vulnerability Detection in Linux Kernel?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25705v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-19 · `arXiv:2610.25705v1`

An empirical study of 37 security skills across 14,800 runs shows LLM coding agents struggle with Linux kernel vulnerability detection, prompting SKILL-SIF, a self-improving framework that evolves skills based on failure patterns.

- In a benchmark of 100 Linux kernel vulnerabilities across 10 classes, Claude Code with four LLMs achieved 24.4% accuracy at the function level, 18.3% at the statement level, and 15.7% for vulnerability classification.
- Identified failure modes include limitations in evidence acquisition, state-relation reasoning, code interpretation, candidate verification, investigation coverage, and reporting.
- The SKILL-SIF framework iteratively improves security skills by learning from missed vulnerabilities, boosting fully correct vulnerability detection on 100 unseen cases from 2% to 16% (and aggregate LLM accuracy from 6.05% to 11.20%).

**Why it matters:** Existing static security skills offer limited efficacy for LLM-based kernel auditing, but failure-driven skill evolution can significantly enhance automated vulnerability detection in complex software.

<small>Haoran Xue, Nikta Akbarpour, Gias Uddin, Song Wang</small>

### [SEAL: Towards Persistent Tool Poisoning Attack against LLM Agents via Prefix-Eliciting Trigger](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25372v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-18 · `arXiv:2610.25372v1`

SEAL is a two-stage persistent tool poisoning method that uses prefix-eliciting triggers to maintain attack effectiveness across multi-round LLM agent interactions.

- Identifies that existing single-round tool poisoning attacks degrade in multi-round settings because agents fail to consistently surface attack-relevant signals as context evolves.
- Uses Prefix-Eliciting Trigger Optimization (PETO) to optimize context-specific triggers that force attack-relevant reasoning prefixes in individual contexts.
- Applies Local-to-Universal Trigger Aggregation (L2U) to combine local triggers into a universal trigger that generalizes across contexts and execution rounds.
- Demonstrates attack effectiveness, cross-model transferability, and defense resistance across seven multi-round scenarios.

**Why it matters:** It reveals that tool poisoning remains a practical security threat in realistic, multi-round LLM agent deployments where previous single-round attack methods fail.

<small>Nanjun Zhou, Yiming Li, Yukun Chen, Ting Qiao, Enhao Huang, Zhan Qin</small>

### [Policy Is Not Capability: Some Safety Benchmarks Measure Which Definition of "Unsafe" You Trained On](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25060v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-18 · `arXiv:2610.25060v1`

A study shows safety benchmarks often measure alignment with specific annotation policy definitions rather than actual model capability, with benchmark scores swinging dramatically based on policy labels or minor parameter tweaks.

- Significant benchmark contamination was found in training pools, including 88% of JailbreakBench-benign and 82% of OR-Bench-hard.
- Benchmark score variance across ten identical-architecture guardrail arms ranged from 0.043 on WildGuard-benign to 0.99 on HarmBench-copyright depending on the label definition used.
- Modifying just 0.03% of model parameters or altering the teacher model's soft labels during distillation can shift benchmark scores across their entire range (e.g., HarmBench-copyright from 0 to 100).
- Validation accuracy correlated strongly with annotator policy agreement (rho=+0.74) rather than actual detection capability (rho=+0.34).

**Why it matters:** Standard safety benchmarks can be heavily gaming-susceptible and confounded by hidden label definitions or teacher provenance, making reported safety capability claims misleading without strict policy controls.

<small>Vahid Jebraeeli, Jonathon Romero, Yijin Wang, Adrian Chan, Jianbang Zhang</small>

### [Don't Judge an Agent by Its Messages: Private-State Leakage under Passive Monitoring of Agent Communication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25032v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-18 · `arXiv:2610.25032v1`

This paper proves that passive auditing of agent communications cannot rule out private-state leakage even when task performance and message distributions are preserved, but a canonical task interface can mathematically bound this leakage.

- Fixing the task-relevant message distribution and declared utilities can still leave a full private bit exposed to public decoders.
- Using Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct on a synthetic routing task, selecting among 16 responses achieved 98.60% routing accuracy while allowing a public decoder to recover a private bit in all 8,192 held-out rounds.
- A law-preserving sender constructed from response samples allowed private bit recovery in 97.7% of rounds while keeping generic monitors without the true bit near chance accuracy.
- A canonical task interface bounds remaining information about a balanced attribute by twice the routing-error rate, even if the receiver shares a key with the sender.

**Why it matters:** Passive monitoring of agent communication is insufficient to guarantee privacy, highlighting the need to enforce communication constraints via task interfaces rather than relying solely on message audits.

<small>Daksh Pandey, Sayon Duttagupta, Xinhai Zou, Amit Singh Bhati</small>
