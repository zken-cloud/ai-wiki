### [Stealing Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09867v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-10 · `arXiv:2608.09867v1`

Researchers exposed a vulnerability in LLM providers where encrypted client-side reasoning traces are interchangeable across sessions, allowing adversaries to decrypt them using weaker models.

- Encrypted chain-of-thought blocks are fully compatible across different sessions, users, and models within Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google ecosystems.
- Injecting a high-capability model's encrypted trace into a weaker, less-safeguarded model forces the weaker model to output the reasoning verbatim in plaintext.
- Analysis of 315,320 public reasoning blocks yielded 367 PII artifacts and 182 credentials leaked via developer session logs.
- The vulnerability enables four attack vectors: anti-distillation circumvention, private data extraction, leakage of hazardous hidden reasoning, and invisible prompt injection.

**Why it matters:** This architectural flaw allows attackers to steal proprietary IP, extract sensitive user credentials, and bypass safety guardrails without directly jailbreaking primary models.

### [ColluSkill: Adversarial Cross-Skill Composition for Evading Agent Skill Scanners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09732v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-10 · `arXiv:2608.09732v1`

ColluSkill is a framework that evades LLM agent skill scanners by splitting malicious payloads across interdependent skills, alongside ChainGuard, a defensive scanner that detects workflow-level risks.

- Existing skill scanners inspect skills individually, creating a blind spot for risks that emerge only through cross-skill composition.
- ColluSkill decomposes malicious intents into sub-payloads spread across separate skills using LLM-based chain planning and scanner-feedback refinement.
- ColluSkill achieved a 96.0% average attack success rate against six existing skill scanners.
- ChainGuard evaluates candidate skills against installed skills by analyzing dependencies, artifact flows, and capability compositions, reducing ColluSkill's success rate to 22.5% with a 99.5% benign pass rate.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that single-skill security checks are insufficient for LLM agents, necessitating workflow-level analysis to prevent multi-skill collusion.

<small>Puyu Zeng, Simeng Qin, Jingzhi Li, Ju Jia, Zheli Liu, Xiaojun Jia</small>

### [Activation Probes Surface Code-Security Signals that the Model's Output Misses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09643v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-10 · `arXiv:2608.09643v1`

Linear probes trained on open-weight model activations detect software vulnerabilities that the same model fails to surface through direct text prompting or chain-of-thought reasoning.

- A single linear probe was trained per model on paired vulnerable-and-fixed Python functions and evaluated on unseen weakness types from real disclosed vulnerabilities.
- Across five open-weight models, the probe identified vulnerable functions over their fixes in 61-67% of single-function cases, significantly outperforming chance.
- Probing internal activations outperformed prompted YES/NO logit win-rates across all tested prompt variations.
- Written model verdicts, including those using chain-of-thought, frequently returned identical answers for both vulnerable and fixed functions, failing to distinguish between them.

**Why it matters:** Open-weight models can act as internal-activation security reviewers for code generated by closed-weight AI agents, extracting safety signals that surface-level text outputs omit.

### [Measuring the Wrong Thing: Internal Harmfulness Scores Anti-Rank Successful Jailbreaks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09624v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-10 · `arXiv:2608.09624v1`

A study reveals internal harmfulness scores anti-rank successful jailbreaks, rating effective wrapped attacks as safer than failed ones across multiple models, attack families, and judges.

- Wrapping harmful prompts raised Llama harmful generation from 0.05 to 0.27 while harmful intent AUROC dropped from 0.936 to 0.803.
- Among wrapped harmful prompts, the outcome AUROC fell to 0.220, placing successful jailbreaks below failed ones in internal safety score rankings.
- Active Attention Probing was introduced to provide a fixed, content-independent measurement coordinate for attention-based safety evaluation.
- The score reversal persisted across three target models, seven attack families, two judges, and occurred in rare token, passive, and detector-derived channels.

**Why it matters:** Filters relying on internal harmfulness scores waste their false-positive budgets on failed attacks while letting high-risk, successful jailbreaks pass undetected.

### [From Runnable to Verifiable: An Independent Reproducibility Study of LLM/Agent-Driven Vulnerability Validation Artifacts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09567v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-10 · `arXiv:2608.09567v1`

A pre-registered study of LLM-driven vulnerability validation artifacts reveals that only 56.7% are public, 61.1% run successfully after repairs, and internal verification oracles suffer from poor specificity (45%) and sensitivity (60%).

- 58 out of 102 (56.9%) anchor benchmark cases contained an internal script CVE identifier that diverged from the declared directory CVE.
- Only 10 of 18 (55.6%) sampled paper artifacts completed their workflow out-of-the-box (R0), rising to 11 of 18 (61.1%) after environment-only (R1) repairs.
- Artifact-embedded oracles were unreliable: 20 of 30 patched-counterfactual audits produced signal on patched builds, and 7 of 19 matched negative controls triggered on benign inputs.

**Why it matters:** Vulnerability validation tools triggering on a vulnerable build cannot be trusted as evidence of a specific CVE reproduction without strict patched-counterfactual and negative-control testing.

### [Dual-Adversarial Safety Alignment: Cultivating Intrinsic Threat Comprehension in LRMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09542v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-10 · `arXiv:2608.09542v1`

AdvSafe uses a dual-adversarial framework to train LRMs on 1K synthesized samples, teaching models intrinsic threat comprehension to improve jailbreak robustness without degrading reasoning utility.

- Phase 1 (Adversarial Synthesis): An autonomous agent dynamically generates deceptive jailbreak prompts that adaptively breach a strong teacher model.
- Phase 2 (Adversarial Extraction): The breached teacher executes a counter-attack by unmasking prompt camouflage, explaining the attack mechanism, and identifying mitigation steps.
- Student models undergo safety alignment by fine-tuning on a compact 1K reasoning dataset containing these extracted unsafety explanations.
- The approach improves out-of-distribution jailbreak robustness and maintains a superior robustness-utility trade-off compared to pattern-centric baselines.

**Why it matters:** It enables large reasoning models to generalize defenses against unseen attack patterns using minimal synthetic data while avoiding performance drops on benign reasoning tasks.

### [RangeFactory: Scalable Construction of Multi-Hop Cyber Ranges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09526v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-10 · `arXiv:2608.09526v1`

RangeFactory is an automated orchestration framework that constructs and validates multi-hop cyber ranges at scale by formulating range generation as a dependency resolution problem based on real attack trajectories.

- Formulates range construction via dependency resolution: extracts dependency data from real attacks, resolves them with template-guided orchestration, and validates runtime dependencies via end-to-end execution.
- Used to build RangeBench, which comprises 1,148 validated range instances across 287 unique attack chains.
- Generates a corpus of 5,541 outcome-annotated multi-hop attack trajectories for process analysis and agent training.
- Evaluation showed that 24.5-47.0% of agent runs compromising the entry vulnerability failed on subsequent steps, highlighting a sustained-compromise gap.

**Why it matters:** It enables scalable, automated benchmark creation and dataset generation to evaluate and train LLM agents on complex, multi-stage cyberattack chains.

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-10 · `arXiv:2608.09476v1`

ActBench is a self-evolving benchmark evaluating cowork agent behavioral safety from execution trajectories using adversarial task-reachable payloads optimized via reward-guided beam search.

- Evaluates execution trajectories rather than final responses across 600 cases, 213 scenarios, 15 risk behaviors, 6 execution spaces, and 48 web-service APIs.
- Adversarial cases pair benign tasks with payload injections while holding instructions, configurations, initial states, rating models, and trusted records constant.
- Payload generation uses reward-guided beam search optimizing attack effectiveness and task utility, alongside reflection-based payload revision from failed checkpoints.

### [DiffSafeMerge: Mitigating Backdoor Inheritance in Diffusion Model Merging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09445v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-10 · `arXiv:2608.09445v1`

DiffSafeMerge (DSM) mitigates backdoor inheritance during diffusion checkpoint merging by using a clean dataset and attack-agnostic stress probes to attenuate suspicious model blocks under a denoising-loss budget.

- Uses a small unlabeled clean reference set and fixed, attack-agnostic stress probes to evaluate and score individual source blocks.
- Shrinks suspicious block contributions toward a trusted reference checkpoint and selects attenuation levels constrained by a clean denoising-loss budget.
- Eliminated backdoor success in four high-risk merge cases (baseline ASR 48–100%) down to zero target matches across three seeds evaluated.
- Achieved the lowest case-averaged FID among all evaluated defense methods that successfully reached zero worst-target ASR across two datasets.

**Why it matters:** DSM allows developers to safely merge public diffusion model checkpoints without risking backdoor vulnerability inheritance or sacrificing clean image generation quality.

### [Governing the KV Cache: Preventing Timing Side-Channel Leakage in Multi-Tenant LLM Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09225v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-10 · `arXiv:2608.09225v1`

KVGov introduces cryptographic per-principal salting and a water-filling audit scheduler to eliminate timing side-channel prompt-extraction attacks in shared multi-tenant LLM key-value caches without sacrificing efficiency.

- Eliminates PROMPTPEEK, EarlyBird, and InputSnatch timing side-channel attacks by seeding the block-hash chain with a per-principal salt (sigma_p = HMAC_K(secret, principal_id)) to make cache keys cryptographically disjoint.
- Injects the salt at the prompt divergence boundary rather than the chain root, retaining an estimated 93% of prefix-cache efficiency while blocking cross-principal leakage.
- Includes ORIGAMI, a Stackelberg water-filling audit scheduler that decreases expected adversary utility by 12.6% under realistic tenant heterogeneity (Gini 0.63).
- Empirically verifies the exploitable timing channel on production hardware, measuring cold/cached TTFT ratios of 0.22 on vLLM/NVIDIA A100 (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) and 0.093 on llama.cpp/Apple Metal.

**Why it matters:** Multi-tenant LLM inference providers can secure shared prefix-caching architectures against full prompt reconstruction attacks with minimal loss in throughput.

### [Repeated-Game Security for Restaking-Based Verifiable Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09055v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-10 · `arXiv:2608.09055v1`

Researchers revealed that standard one-round slashing in restaking-based LLM inference fails under repeated games, proposing a history-dependent, reputation-weighted mechanism that restores subgame-perfect incentive compatibility.

- Proportional slashing creates a repeated-game gap where detected deviations lower future penalty exposure while dishonest cost savings accumulate across queries.
- Surveyed restaking protocols pass one-round security checks but allow profitable repeated-game deviations (1.5%–8% profit fractions) at discount factors between 0.92 and 0.98.
- The proposed defense integrates history-dependent challenges, reputation-weighted slashing, and stake vesting to secure infinite-horizon subgame-perfect incentives without per-query cryptographic verification.
- Testing across nine open-weight model pairs (0.5B to 14B parameters) showed that improved audit signal responsiveness reduces baseline audit rates by 2.6x at a 0.95 discount factor.
- The new mechanism cuts deviation profits by 31% to 54% while preserving low-latency economic verification.

**Why it matters:** It fixes a critical game-theoretic vulnerability in deployed restaking protocols, ensuring long-term cryptographic-free economic security for verifiable LLM inference.

<small>Zhenhang Shang, Yingzhe Yu, Kani Chen</small>
