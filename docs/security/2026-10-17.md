### [Managing open-weight model risk in enterprise deployments](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/10/16/managing-open-weight-model-risk-in-enterprise-deployments/)

**Microsoft Security Blog** · 2026-10-16

Microsoft and NVIDIA jointly published a whitepaper establishing a shared risk taxonomy and framework for managing open-weight AI model risks in enterprise deployments.

- Introduces a risk taxonomy mapped to NIST AI RMF and ISO standards across five categories: Cybersecurity, Data Protection, Content, Human-AI Interaction, and Bias.
- Emphasizes that weight openness does not solely determine risk, which instead depends on model provenance, architecture, permissions, tools, runtime, and deployment conditions.
- Recommends placing controls across the inference environment, application, and operational processes rather than relying exclusively on model-layer evaluations.
- Leverages Microsoft Foundry for automated red teaming, content-safety controls, and custom evaluations to generate empirical risk evidence.

**Why it matters:** It provides enterprise security and engineering teams with a standardized vocabulary and operational framework to evaluate and mitigate risks when deploying open-weight models.

### [Private Retrieval Augmented Generation via Random Projection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22297v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-15 · `arXiv:2610.22297v1`

A new privacy-preserving RAG method applies random projection to both query and datastore embeddings to prevent data extraction while maintaining semantic similarity.

- Applies identical random projection transformations to both datastore and query embeddings.
- Provably preserves semantic similarity between queries and retrieved items to maintain retrieval accuracy.
- Outperforms differential privacy (DP) baselines in retrieval and generation performance across multiple RAG architectures.
- Provides empirical privacy guarantees against strong data extraction attack models without requiring formal DP noise insertion.

**Why it matters:** Random projection offers a lightweight, high-utility alternative to differential privacy for securing sensitive knowledge bases in RAG deployments.

### [Federated Art Chase: Privacy Leakage and Data Poisoning in Federated Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21547v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-15 · `arXiv:2610.21547v1`

A study evaluates privacy and integrity risks in federated fine-tuning of MobileNetV4 on non-IID Quick, Draw! data, demonstrating gradient leakage limits, backdoor vulnerabilities, and FLTrust defense effectiveness.

- Centrally fine-tuned MobileNetV4-Conv-Small (97.50% test accuracy) adapted via head-only FedAvg across 10 non-IID clients improved client training accuracy from 90.34% to 91.49%.
- Gradient inversion by an honest-but-curious server extracted complete private label sets from single updates, but image reconstructions matched class prototypes rather than private drawings (mean SSIM 0.130).
- FedAvg backdoor attacks by 30%, 50%, and 100% malicious clients achieved 21.9%, 31.3%, and 54.7% attack success rates (ASR) with minimal clean accuracy loss (<=0.8 percentage points).
- Deploying FLTrust using a root set from centralized fine-tuning reduced backdoor ASR to 1.8% (30% malicious) and 4.0% (50% malicious), and label-flipping ASR from 12.6% to 0.8%, though it fails when 100% of clients are malicious.

**Why it matters:** It establishes empirical bounds on privacy leakage and defense mechanisms when adapting centrally pretrained models to decentralized, non-IID edge data.

<small>Vasileios Perifanis, Nikolaos Pavlidis</small>

`federated-learning` `model-privacy` `data-poisoning` `backdoor-attacks` `computer-vision`

### [QuasiLeak-Bench: Evaluating LLM-Based Privacy Detectors on Policy-Conditioned Quasi-Evidence Group Recovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21333v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-15 · `arXiv:2610.21333v1`

QuasiLeak-Bench is a new benchmark for evaluating LLM-based privacy detectors on policy-conditioned recovery of quasi-evidence groups—sets of weak quasi-identifiers that jointly expose private attributes.

- Addresses the gap in measuring joint-inference privacy risks by focusing on quasi-evidence groups alongside sender-specified disclosure policies.
- Uses a reverse-construction pipeline that selects weak quasi-identifiers, verifies their joint revelation of a target attribute, and generates documents around them with automated quality control.
- Reveals that existing privacy detectors transfer poorly and locally deployable LLM detectors lag behind proprietary models due to missing quasi-evidence groups.
- Demonstrates task learnability: fine-tuning a 4B parameter model on the benchmark's training split allowed it to outperform the strongest prompted model on held-out test documents.

**Why it matters:** It establishes a standardized framework to train and evaluate open-weights models for detecting complex, non-obvious privacy leaks that depend on contextual user policies.

<small>Yufan Zhu, Yangfan Jiang, Yang Fei, Shizheng Hou, Shubhankar Mohapatra, Xiaokui Xiao</small>

### [From Detection to Decision: Calibration and Deferral for Prompt-Injection Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20907v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-14 · `arXiv:2610.20907v1`

A benchmark of prompt-injection detectors under distribution shift reveals that native probabilistic outputs do not guarantee reliable security decisions and require high escalation rates to maintain low false-negative targets.

- Evaluated an LLM judge, the native probabilistic model Jev, and Llama Prompt Guard 2 (22M and 86M) across 47,611 held-out examples.
- The LLM judge achieved the highest AUROC (0.897), outperforming Jev (0.871), Prompt Guard 2 86M (0.757), and Prompt Guard 2 22M (0.714).
- Temperature scaling improved probability calibration but failed to improve overall discrimination performance.
- Targeting a 1% empirical false negative rate forced all gate-reviewer configurations to escalate over 91.8% of inputs while incurring high false-positive rates.

**Why it matters:** Relying solely on probabilistic security scores is insufficient under distribution shift, forcing deployed LLM applications to balance automated classification with costly human or secondary reviewer escalation.

<small>Qianlong Lan, Anuj Kaul, Vinothini Pandurangan, Indranil Sanyal</small>

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-14 · `arXiv:2610.20902v1`

ChainGraph externalizes LLM agent penetration-testing reasoning into a Neo4j property graph via Model Context Protocol, enabling graph-based exploit chaining and token-efficient decision-making.

- Integrates Neo4j with off-the-shelf agents like Claude Code and OpenAI Codex using Model Context Protocol (MCP) without a custom harness.
- Models findings, attack steps, credentials, assets, and evidence as typed nodes with pre/post-condition edges, separating document memory from reasoning memory.
- Formalizes five core operator queries in Cypher and Graph Data Science (GDS), introducing a chain-severity score distinct from local finding severities.
- Promoted three Medium-severity findings to Critical via path materialization to a forest root on a GOAD-modeled Active Directory environment.
- Reduced the agent reasoning payload by approximately 98% on a token proxy by scoping decisions to graph query results instead of full context history.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that AI agents can overcome context-window limitations to perform multi-step exploit chaining by offloading topological reasoning to a graph database.

### [INFUSE: A Unified Surface Benchmark for Indirect Prompt Injection in Agentic Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20776v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-14 · `arXiv:2610.20776v1`

INFUSE is a new unified benchmark evaluating indirect prompt injection attacks across realistic, long-horizon LLM agent workflows using 40 tasks, 56 attack scenarios, and five threat categories.

- Evaluated 19 LLMs across GPT, Claude, Gemini, Llama, Gemma, and Qwen families, generating 41,824 adversarial and 720 benign trajectories.
- Mean attack success rate (ASR-L) reached 19.6% via LLM-based evaluation and 15.4% via heuristic verification, with individual model ASR-L spanning 3.2% to 41.9%.
- Injections were delivered through realistic execution interaction surfaces rather than just initial prompts or isolated tool calls.
- Prompt-level instructional hardening reduced mean attack success from 58.2% to 14.3%, though it introduced model-dependent security-utility trade-offs, while existing guard models showed limited efficacy.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that long-horizon autonomous agents remain highly vulnerable to indirect prompt injection via operational interaction surfaces, where existing guard models fail to provide adequate defense.

<small>Thuy Dung Nguyen, Jingyi Guo, Rana Seda Dubauskas, Sreepriya Damuluru, Taylor T. Johnson, Kevin Leach</small>

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-14 · `arXiv:2610.20679v1`

CNRF is a selective safety framework for VLMs that uses context-matched counterfactual pairs and neighborhood support estimation to isolate risk directions from input unfamiliarity.

- Constructs context-matched benign-harmful counterfactual pairs and leverages their representation differences as capability-related risk directions.
- Decouples neighborhood support estimation from risk scoring to prevent unfamiliar inputs from being misclassified as harmful.
- Features a victim-adaptive interface that routes selected requests to a same-victim response review, releasing safe outputs or generating constrained alternatives.
- Achieves an 86.06-89.30% macro defense success rate across three VLM architectures and five multimodal jailbreak families while preserving benign utility.

**Why it matters:** It improves low-false-positive safety interventions by providing grounded evidence for risk decisions rather than relying on blunt representation boundary separation.

### [Encoded but Exposed: Language Embeddings can Reveal More Than They Should in Recommender Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21713v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-15 · `arXiv:2610.21713v1`

Item language embeddings in recommender systems can be reverse-engineered to reveal item identities and re-identify users via a simple cosine-similarity attack using external auxiliary text.

- A simple cosine-similarity attack recovers item identities with high mean reciprocal rank (MRR) across various language models and candidate-set sizes on two real-world datasets.
- Information leakage is strongest when auxiliary text overlaps with the victim representation, but it remains effective through semantic alignment of independently sourced descriptions.
- Item identity leakage propagates to enable user re-identification even from sparse recommendation rating data.
- Evaluating obfuscated item descriptions as a defense demonstrates a clear trade-off between privacy leakage and recommender utility.

**Why it matters:** Using standard language embeddings for item representations in recommender systems poses severe privacy risks by enabling unauthorized item recovery and subsequent user re-identification.

LLM4SAST is a universal framework that combines large language models, metamorphic relations, and dynamic feedback to automatically detect, filter, and cluster software faults in static application security testing tools.

A privacy-preserving text inference framework uses CKKS homomorphic encryption for health-misinformation classification and semantic retrieval without sacrificing plaintext predictive accuracy.

- The classification pipeline fuses BioBERT embeddings, PCA-reduced MPNet embeddings, and SVD-reduced TF-IDF features, achieving 0.971 accuracy and 0.950 macro-F1 in both plaintext and ciphertext execution.

**Why it matters:** It enables public health organizations to analyze misleading health claims and retrieve semantic matches over sensitive user queries without compromising confidential user data or exposing proprietary server-side models.

### [Phantomware: a Data Augmentation Framework for the Detection of Emerging Malware Campaigns](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20427v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-14 · `arXiv:2610.20427v1`

Phantomware is a data augmentation framework that generates diverse synthetic malware representations directly in a pretrained detector's latent space to detect emerging, data-scarce malware campaigns.

- Operates entirely within the latent representation space of a pretrained malware detector, bypassing the generation and costly validation of functional binary executables.
- Exploits latent-space geometry to provide constructive guarantees for generating controlled, diverse target-family synthetic representations.
- Eliminates the dependency on a well-trained detector or adversarial manipulation objectives that suffer from unpredictable sample generation in data-scarce settings.
- Demonstrates improved detection of unseen real malware variants and increases robustness against adversarially generated malware samples.

**Why it matters:** It enables effective malware detector training for early-stage, data-scarce campaigns without requiring functional binary creation or costly dynamic execution validation.
