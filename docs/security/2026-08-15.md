### [Understanding Backdoor Vulnerabilities in Vertical Federated Learning: The Gap Between Research and Practice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12962v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-13 · `arXiv:2608.12962v1`

A new paper identifies unrealistic assumptions in current Vertical Federated Learning (VFL) backdoor attack and defense research and introduces BVBench, a benchmark designed to evaluate vulnerabilities under practical constraints.

- Identifies a gap between VFL research and real-world practice caused by attacks relying on unrealistic prior knowledge and flawed evaluation methods.
- Redefines VFL threat models to account for practical operational constraints and asymmetric information structures where the initiator withholds task details.
- Proposes realistic backdoor workflows for training-time poisoning and inference-time activation by malicious contributors.
- Introduces BVBench, a comprehensive backdoor-centric benchmark preloaded with state-of-the-art baselines for realistic VFL security evaluation.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that existing VFL backdoor defenses and attack models are fragile under realistic conditions, requiring a shift toward practical vulnerability assessments.

<small>Ziqi Zhao, Jialin Lu, Junjie Shan, Junyuan Zhang, Shuya Yang, Ka-Ho Chow</small>

### [Beyond Visual Evidence: Revealing and Mitigating Relational Privacy Leakage in Document MLLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12911v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-13 · `arXiv:2608.12911v1`

Researchers identified relational privacy leakage in document MLLMs when visual evidence is scarce and introduced the Dynamic Relational Unlearning Framework (DRUF) alongside the DocPrivacyBench benchmark to mitigate it.

- Document MLLMs performing Key Information Extraction (KIE) infer missing sensitive fields using memorized field relations when visual evidence is absent or minimal.
- The Dynamic Relational Unlearning Framework (DRUF) combines a Relational Decoupling Unlearning (RDU) module with a dynamic set update mechanism to suppress high-risk field pair leakage.
- DocPrivacyBench was created to evaluate privacy leakage susceptibility and utility preservation across three MLLMs and six unlearning baselines.
- DRUF improved privacy leakage suppression by 4.8 percentage points over the strongest baseline while preserving document extraction utility.

**Why it matters:** This work enables identity document processing MLLMs to reduce sensitive personal data exposure caused by training data memorization without degrading key extraction accuracy.

<small>Beining Xu, Hairui Wang, Jiaxin Wang, Changsheng Chen, Anirban Chakraborty</small>

### [Adversarial Robustness in Smishing Detection: A Comparative Analysis of Adversarial Fragility in Classical vs. Transformer-Based Detection Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12889v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-13 · `arXiv:2608.12889v1`

A comparative study shows transformer-based smishing detectors resist adversarial attacks far better than classical models, though high clean-text accuracy does not guarantee adversarial robustness.

- Evaluated 27,037 messages across classical models (Random Forest, XGBoost, CNN+BiLSTM) and multilingual transformers (mBERT, XLM-RoBERTa) using three attack types and intensity levels.
- Classical models faced near-catastrophic failure under character obfuscation and structural perturbation with a Robustness Degradation Ratio (RDR) up to 0.988.
- Multilingual transformers exhibited higher resilience with RDR capped at 0.351, identifying structural perturbation as their primary vulnerability.
- XLM-RoBERTa achieved higher clean-text baseline accuracy than mBERT but suffered greater overall performance degradation under adversarial testing.
- Statistical significance of architectural impacts was validated using Mann-Whitney U, Friedman tests, and Cliff's d effect-size analysis.

**Why it matters:** Security teams cannot rely on clean-text evaluation metrics alone, requiring architecture-specific adversarial defenses for real-world smishing detection.

<small>Denzel Chiuseni, Athanase Bahizire, Silva Hama, Jema David Ndibwile</small>

### [How China-Origin Vision-Language Models Move from Refusal to Reframing in State Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11816v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-12 · `arXiv:2608.11816v1`

A 21,708-trial audit of nine vision-language models reveals that censorship in China-origin VLMs is migrating from explicit refusals to state-aligned reframing, driven by subject recognition rather than visual pixel detail.

- Evaluated nine VLMs (seven China-origin, two non-China) across 200 core entries spanning ten sensitive topics and a seven-variant visual-abstraction probe across two prompt languages.
- Across four generations of Qwen models, explicit refusal rates decreased while state-aligned framing increased, shifting state-aligned distortion into fluent reframing.
- Chinese-language prompting roughly tripled the odds of state-aligned framing within every model tested, with China-origin models reframing 1.6x to 3.2x more than non-China models.
- State-aligned framing peaked in text-only political commentary (36.5%) and was gated by high-level recognition of the depicted subject, persisting even when images were reduced to silhouettes.
- Audit dimensions included explicit refusal, information integrity, visual grounding, state-aligned framing, language consistency, and response length, scored independently by two LLM judges and validated by human experts.

**Why it matters:** The transition from explicit refusals to implicit reframing removes the primary signal users rely on to detect that AI-generated information has been intentionally altered or withheld.

<small>Guang Yang, Fengchen Liu, Alex Wang, Homa Hosseinmardi, Amir Ghasemian</small>

### [Operationalizing Cyber Threat Intelligence with GraphRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13050v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-13 · `arXiv:2608.13050v1`

Replacing naive vector retrieval with Microsoft GraphRAG enables automated cyber threat intelligence systems to generate durable detection plans focused on resilient behaviors rather than volatile indicators.

- Evaluates Microsoft GraphRAG against Naive RAG using identical language models and generation prompts across nine CTI reports from four vendors.
- In an APT28 case study, the GraphRAG-generated plan maintained 100% detection effectiveness after IP, domain, and hash rotation, whereas the Naive RAG plan dropped to 29% effectiveness.
- GraphRAG consistently extracts higher-level, harder-to-evade behaviors from the Pyramid of Pain compared to vector-similarity retrieval.
- Prompt wording during the generation stage was identified as nearly equal in importance to the choice of retrieval backend.

**Why it matters:** Using knowledge-graph retrieval allows security operations centers to automatically create threat hunting plans that remain effective even after attackers change basic infrastructure and file hashes.

<small>Atul Kabra, Prakhar Paliwal, Manjesh K. Hanawal</small>

### [A Comparison of Malware Image Transformations Using Grad-CAM and Hybrid Learning Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12077v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-12 · `arXiv:2608.12077v1`

Researchers evaluated Grad-CAM and HiResCAM on eight binary-to-image malware transformations, discovering that model accuracy and XAI explanation faithfulness do not align.

- Evaluated eight distinct binary-to-image transformation methods using quantitative faithfulness and stability metrics for Grad-CAM and HiResCAM heatmaps.
- Extracted features from Grad-CAM heatmaps using a MobileNetV2 CNN and trained a Random Forest model, achieving 0.777 test accuracy across 17 malware families (outperforming the 0.750 benchmark).
- Demonstrated an inverse trade-off where image transformation techniques yielding the highest explanation faithfulness provided only mid-tier classification accuracy.

**Why it matters:** High-performing image-based malware classifiers may rely on unfaithful visual features, requiring security analysts to carefully weigh classification accuracy against explanation reliability.

<small>Vibha Bhavikatti, Mark Stamp</small>

### [Instruction Alignment for Binary Code Representation Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11766v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-08-12 · `arXiv:2608.11766v1`

A new training approach incorporates fine-grained, debug-info-derived instruction alignment as an auxiliary objective to improve function-level binary code representation learning and retrieval accuracy.

- Existing binary code representation methods focus on coarse function-level embeddings while neglecting fine-grained instruction-level correspondences.
- Preliminary findings show that models finetuned for function-level similarity naturally improve instruction alignment over base pre-trained models.
- The proposed method explicitly leverages compiler debug information to introduce instruction alignment as an auxiliary training objective.
- Experimental results demonstrate improved retrieval accuracy and enhanced discriminative capability in similarity judgments.

**Why it matters:** Integrating instruction-level alignment into binary representation models yields more accurate and interpretable code embeddings for reverse engineering and security analysis tasks.

<small>Huaijin Wang, Shuai Wang</small>
