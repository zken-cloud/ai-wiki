### [SAGE: Similarity-Based Cleaning of Poisoned Training Data from Verified Examples](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01788v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-01 · `arXiv:2610.01788v1`

SAGE defends against clean-label data poisoning by using a generic feature extractor and non-parametric, similarity-weighted predictions built from a small set of verified clean and poisoned examples.

- Relies on a very small set of expert-verified clean and poisoned examples to prevent model overfitting.
- Employs a generic feature extractor pre-trained on a separate dataset to process inputs.
- Detects poisoned training data using a non-parametric, similarity-weighted prediction algorithm against the verified set.
- Evaluated against seven clean-label poisoning attack methods on standard benchmarks.
- Demonstrates that the class distribution of verified clean examples impacts defense performance more than the raw count of verified examples.

**Why it matters:** SAGE drastically reduces the labor and cost of data verification by requiring only a handful of verified examples to successfully clean poisoned training sets.

### [Backdoor Purification for LoRA-Tuned LLMs via Null-Space Projection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00685v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-09-30 · `arXiv:2610.00685v1`

A novel backdoor purification method for LoRA-tuned LLMs uses null-space projection to eliminate backdoor triggers without prior trigger knowledge, clean references, or post-hoc retraining.

- Removes the need for strong traditional assumptions like trigger prior knowledge, clean reference datasets, or aggressive retraining.
- Extracts backdoor directions via data curation and feature approximation to construct orthogonal null spaces for input and output channels per layer or head.
- Projects LoRA updates onto the constructed null spaces without requiring post-hoc retraining of suspect parameters.
- Reduces the attack success rate (ASR) from nearly 100% to under 10% in empirical evaluations.

**Why it matters:** It allows practitioners to securely deploy compromised LoRA adapters without sacrificing general model capabilities, downstream skills, or spending compute on retraining.

### [Backdoor Containment via Expert Quarantine and Shutdown in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00663v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-09-30 · `arXiv:2610.00663v1`

Researchers introduce Quarantined Expert Shutdown (QES), a defense that channels LLM backdoor triggers into a designated MoE-like expert during training, enabling complete backdoor mitigation at deployment by zeroing that expert's routing weight.

- Presents a 'learn, but channel' strategy that allows backdoor formation during training but isolates trigger behavior into a dedicated, quarantined component.
- Implements QES by augmenting Transformer models with routed expert-specific LoRA branches, lightweight routers, and auxiliary routing objectives.
- Enables deployment-time mitigation via a single constant-time operation that zeros the quarantined expert's routing weight without requiring trigger screening or weight updates.
- Reduces the attack success rate (ASR) from 100% to 0–10% across most tested settings spanning two tasks, three attacks, and four LLM families while maintaining utility.

**Why it matters:** It establishes a new paradigm for LLM security that neutralizes hidden backdoors at deployment with minimal computational overhead and without modifying core weights or screening inputs.

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-01 · `arXiv:2610.02074v1`

The Homomorphic Advantage Operator (HAO) stabilizes FHE-based reinforcement learning by applying a zero-mean centering projection to temporal-difference targets, preventing Bellman drift without adding multiplicative depth or ciphertext bootstrapping.

### [Can AI Oversight Be Zero Knowledge?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01995v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-01 · `arXiv:2610.01995v1`

Researchers prove that zero-knowledge proofs and debate for general oracle-aided AI oversight are impossible in the random oracle model, but fully achievable if the oracle cryptographically signs its answers.

- In the random oracle model, zero-knowledge proofs for general oracle-aided computations do not exist, even if prover and verifier run significantly longer than the computation itself.
- This impossibility result extends to debate models used for scalable AI oversight.
- By requiring the oracle to attach a cryptographic signature to its answers, every oracle-aided computation can be verified in zero knowledge with an efficient prover and verifier under collision-resistant hash function assumptions.
- The signed-oracle approach enables scalable oversight without assuming an honest opponent in debate or computation robustness in single-prover protocols.

**Why it matters:** It establishes the fundamental theoretical limits of private AI oversight and offers a practical cryptographic path to verify confidential model outputs without exposing underlying sensitive data.

<small>Alessandro Chiesa, Ziyi Guan, Burcu Yildiz</small>

### [SafeDepth: Safety-Aware Token-Level Adaptive Computation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00815v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-09-30 · `arXiv:2610.00815v1`

SafeDepth is a lightweight, plug-in framework that uses a router and adapter to dynamically select Transformer layer execution, reducing computation and harmful responses while keeping the backbone model frozen.

- Existing token-level adaptive models show higher harmful-response rates than full-depth backbones, as safety features depend on layer execution, layer position, and inference phase (prompt vs. generation).
- SafeDepth trains a lightweight router and adapter to bypass computations that lead to unsafe outputs while retaining those necessary for harmful request recognition and safe refusals.
- The framework keeps the pretrained backbone frozen, avoiding additional pretraining.
- Evaluated on Llama-3-8B-Instruct, SafeDepth dropped HarmBench-HJ unsafe responses from 55.44% to 25.25% compared to FlexiDepth.
- SafeDepth reduced the XSTest false-refusal rate from 12.0% to 4.0% while maintaining performance on benign tasks.

**Why it matters:** It enables token-level early exiting and layer skipping in LLMs to improve compute efficiency without suffering the safety degradation common in existing adaptive execution frameworks.

- Formulates the Accountability Proof Block (APB) combining system-constructed evidence, human decision blocks, and Ed25519 cryptographic signatures bound to registered principals.
