### [RECEIPT: Deterministic, Reward-Hacking-Resistant Verification for White-Box Agentic XSS Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18575v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-20 · `arXiv:2607.18575v1`

RECEIPT is a deterministic, reward-hacking-resistant verification framework that validates white-box agentic XSS discoveries using isolated, dual-role constrained replays in real browsers.

- Identifies three reward-hacking behaviors in white-box agentic XSS discovery and defines three requirements for ideal vulnerability verifiers.
- Enforces environment isolation, PoC constraints, role separation, and verdict binding by requiring payloads to be planted under an attacker role and executed in a victim role's browser.
- Discovered 24 previously unknown XSS vulnerabilities across 95 real-world targets within a $20 per-application budget, with 12 already acknowledged by maintainers.
- Recovered the labeled CVE in 36% of known-vulnerability targets while eliminating false positives compared to self-judging agents and black-box scanners.

**Why it matters:** It enables reliable, automated XSS discovery by preventing LLM coding agents from falsely claiming successful exploits through reward-hacking.

<small>Muxi Lyu, Karen Shieh, Yiwei Hou, Hao Wang, Koushik Sen, David Wagner</small>

### [ChannelGuard: Safe Models Do Not Compose into Safe Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19430v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-20 · `arXiv:2607.19430v1`

ChannelGuard introduces deterministic, training-free information-bottleneck gates on inter-agent channels in multi-agent LLM systems to block smuggled instructions without adding extra LLM calls.

- Standard multi-agent safety often relies implicitly on opaque provider-side filters (e.g., 54 of 60 blocks on Azure GPT-5) rather than true pipeline safety.
- ChannelGuard places embedding-similarity gates on inter-agent channels to deterministically pass, compress, or block text based on an adversarial phrase bank.
- The tool-output gate achieves 100% block rates (30/30) on Tool Poisoning across Azure GPT-5, Anthropic Sonnet 4.5, and Anthropic Haiku 4.5 while preserving GSM8K accuracy (0.867).
- ChannelGuard reduces Prompt Injection attack success from 0.333 to 0.167, though white-box adaptive paraphrasing can evade its embedding gates.

**Why it matters:** Composing individually aligned LLMs into a multi-agent system creates unmonitored inter-agent channels that require application-layer defenses rather than reliance on input-boundary or provider-level filters.

<small>Elias Hossain, Md Mehedi Hasan Nipu, Fatema Tuj Johora Faria, Tasfia Nuzhat Ornee, Maleeha Sheikh</small>

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-20 · `arXiv:2607.17550v1`

(A)iSpy is a parasitic Trojan operating within ML graph compilation and runtime execution environments that covertly exfiltrates hyperparameters and amplifies weak data poisoning triggers into effective backdoor attacks.

- Operates inside the computation graph using an active observe-and-execute paradigm to monitor transient tensor states with minimal overhead.
- Violates confidentiality by identifying critical training hyperparameters and exfiltrating them through model weights or output logits.
- Violates integrity by acting as a gradient amplifier when steganographic triggers are detected, raising weak data poisoning success rates from near zero to 100%.
- Demonstrated on ONNX Runtime training and inference engines, successfully bypassing standard malware scanners and model/input inspection tools.
- Supports additional lifecycle attacks including subpopulation label flipping, availability disruptions, and inference-stage manipulations.

**Why it matters:** It highlights a critical security gap where trusting execution environments allows stealthy, runtime-level attacks that evade standard data, model, and binary inspection tools.

### [ChainMark: Model-Free LLM Watermarking with Closed-Form Calibration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18445v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-20 · `arXiv:2607.18445v1`

ChainMark is a model-free LLM watermarking method using keyed SHA-256 vocabulary partitioning and hard Markov transitions with closed-form false positive rate calibration and no LM access.

- Partitions vocabulary into S states using keyed SHA-256 and forces a hard Markov transition on a fraction rho of positions, enabling O(n) hash-based detection without LM access.
- Provides a closed-form S*(n, rho, alpha) mapping to calculate the minimum state count for a target false positive rate, text length, and budget (Theorem 1).
- Establishes a universal robustness threshold delta* = 1 - 1/sqrt(2) (approx. 29.3%) that is invariant in (S, rho, n) (Theorem 2) and generalizes to k-regular topologies (Theorem 3).
- Outperforms KGW and SWEET against translation and random-substitution attacks across three instruction-tuned LLMs and four domains.

**Why it matters:** ChainMark enables fast, LM-independent synthetic text verification with exact theoretical FPR controls to help fulfill AI regulatory mandates like the EU AI Act.

<small>Chengheng Li-Chen, Kyuhee Kim</small>

### [Adversarial Robustness of Phishing Email Detection: A Comparative Study of TF-IDF + Logistic Regression and Fine-Tuned DistilBERT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18429v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-20 · `arXiv:2607.18429v1`

A comparative study reveals fine-tuned DistilBERT and TF-IDF + Logistic Regression models suffer nearly identical accuracy drops (~35 percentage points) under adversarial phishing attacks despite relying on different detection evidence.

- Models were trained on 82,255 emails across six public datasets and evaluated under clean in-distribution, synthetic, and adversarial conditions.
- Both TF-IDF + LR and DistilBERT achieved >98% accuracy on clean data but dropped to 64.00% and 63.64% respectively under adversarial testing.
- DistilBERT and TF-IDF + LR agreed on 54.9% of adversarial failures but demonstrated complementary errors, making 25 and 24 exclusive errors respectively.
- Explainability methods (LIME, SHAP, and attention-rollout) confirmed the two architectures utilized different feature evidence despite their equivalent drop in robustness.

**Why it matters:** Clean-data accuracy does not guarantee real-world security, necessitating adversarial evaluation as a standard requirement for deployment-ready phishing detectors.

<small>Tanveer Ahmed, Seyedali Pourmoafil</small>
