### [Location Matters: Sparse Latent-Variance Attacks for Face Privacy against Diffusion Personalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14254v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-10 · `arXiv:2611.14254v1`

SEAL is a sparse adversarial defense modifying only 5% of facial image pixels to prevent unauthorized diffusion model personalization while maintaining high visual quality.

- Uses a differentiable top-k masking scheme that jointly optimizes both the location and magnitude of spatial perturbations.
- Applies a variance-oriented objective over the frozen VAE encoder's latent distribution combined with a saliency-guided dispersion penalty to avoid visual artifacts on key facial features.
- Achieves visual quality metrics exceeding 41 dB PSNR, 0.97 SSIM, and at most 0.07 LPIPS.
- Reduces post-personalization identity similarity to a maximum of 0.15 on CelebA-HQ and VGGFace2 datasets.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that targeted, sparse pixel modifications can effectively protect personal facial identity against unauthorized AI generation without degrading image quality.

### [Data-Efficient Unlearning-Based Backdoor Detection in Federated Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13491v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-10 · `arXiv:2611.13491v1`

Screening via Unlearning Response Analysis (SURA) detects federated learning backdoor attacks by repeatedly probing client models with a 10-image clean proxy while managing batch normalization state drift to ensure precise readout.

- Reuses a minimal clean proxy (10 images) across multiple train-mode loss-ascent steps to amplify malicious unlearning responses without requiring additional data.
- Restores the shared pre-probe Batch Normalization (BN) state exclusively at readout to eliminate BN drift and isolate parameter changes caused by probing.
- Evaluated across six backdoor attack types, achieving detection performance that matches or approaches systems relying on significantly larger proxy datasets.

**Why it matters:** It enables federated learning servers to reliably identify backdoored client models using an extremely small, low-overhead set of clean proxy data.

### [Pulse or Provenance? What rPPG-Based Deepfake Detectors Learn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13992v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-10 · `arXiv:2611.13992v1`

Researchers demonstrate that rPPG-based deepfake detectors learn non-physiological high-frequency synthesis artifacts and dataset provenance rather than genuine cardiac signals.

- Simple chrominance traces achieve 0.998 AUC on Celeb-DF++ talking-face forgeries and remain discriminative when extracted from backgrounds, full frames, or frequencies above the 0.7-4 Hz heart-rate band.
- Injecting synthetic physiological pulses has minimal impact, while removing high-frequency content causes synthesized videos to be classified as real and self-reenactment frame animation introduces the spurious signal.
- Face-based chrominance and CLIP-based detectors trained on Celeb-DF++ classify >99% of real FaceForensics++ videos as fake, demonstrating cross-dataset failure due to learning video source identity over manipulation.
- Celeb-DF and Celeb-DF++ official splits share identities and source content between train and test sets, where reusing test data in training inflates weaker detector performance by up to 0.30 AUC.

**Why it matters:** The findings invalidate claims that rPPG detectors rely on physiological cardiac cues, exposing severe cross-dataset generalization failures and dataset leakage vulnerabilities in existing benchmarks.

<small>Othmane Harraq, Tamer Aldwairi</small>

### [What a CAGE 4 Score Measures: An Audit of a Learned Cyber-Defense Benchmark, Its Reference Defenders, and the Tables Built on Them](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14068v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-10 · `arXiv:2611.14068v1`

An audit of the CAGE Challenge 4 cyber-defense benchmark reveals silent action dropping, unreported state leakage in reference wrappers, unseeded policy variance, and undocumented scoring conditions across published comparison tables.

- The CAGE 4 simulator silently drops actions for busy agents while charging points for discarded Restore actions, which reference defenders KEEP and H-MARL bypassed using undocumented wrapper flags.
- Removing the busy-flag check drops the evaluation scores of KEEP and H-MARL by 99.58 and 145.98 points per episode, respectively.
- Training without the leak alters trajectory progression, causing KEEP's performance to drop by up to 125 points at 2,000 updates, exceeding the published reproduction interval.
- Evaluation seeds the environment but not the policy, leaving 88.9% of per-episode outcome variance to the policy's random sampling draw.
- None of the five published comparison tables specify the artifact, sampling, criterion, and adversary required for reproducible comparisons.

**Why it matters:** The findings demonstrate that published CAGE 4 benchmark leaderboards rely on baseline state leakage and unseeded variance, invalidating reported performance comparisons for learned cyber defenders.

### [MOAI: Module-Optimizing Architecture for non-interactive secure transformer Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13641v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-10 · `arXiv:2611.13641v1`

MOAI is a non-interactive, homomorphic encryption-based secure transformer inference framework that uses consistent packing strategies and rotation-free algorithms to significantly reduce computational overhead.

- Combines column and diagonal packing strategies consistently across all layers to eliminate format conversion overhead.
- Introduces rotation-free algorithms for Softmax and LayerNorm, removing 2,448 HE rotations in BERT-base inference.
- Uses column packing to eliminate rotations in plaintext-ciphertext matrix multiplications and interleaved batching to reduce rotations in ciphertext-ciphertext matrix multiplications.
- Achieves a 52.8% reduction in evaluation time over THOR (CCS '25) and a 55.7% reduction over Powerformer (ACL '25), reaching an amortized time of 2.36 minutes per input on a GPU.
- Demonstrates extendibility by scaling the framework to LLaMA-3-8B.

**Why it matters:** MOAI drastically cuts the computational latency of non-interactive homomorphic encryption, bringing private, secure LLM inference on untrusted cloud providers closer to practical deployment.

### [Latent Barrier Steering: Hierarchical Safety for Generative Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13227v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-09 · `arXiv:2611.13227v1`

Latent Barrier Steering (LBS) combines latent-space steering with path-space Control Barrier Function (CBF) quadratic programs to ensure safety in flow-matching generative planners.

- Decouples safety into behavior selection via latent steering and path certification via a path-space CBF Quadratic Program (QP).
- Steers the generator's behavior latent toward rollouts with higher safety margins before decoding.
- Applies path-space CBF QP correction strictly to residual violations.
- Provides finite-time recovery guarantees while increasing the safety margin available to the path corrector.

**Why it matters:** LBS prevents planners from failing when entire behavior modes are blocked by constraints, improving goal-reaching success while reducing post-hoc correction effort.
