### [Protecting CPU AI On Edge TEEs: WebAssembly's Promise and Practical Challenges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12050v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-08 · `arXiv:2610.12050v1`

Researchers developed a system to protect AI intellectual property by executing unaltered WebAssembly-compiled models within Arm TrustZone using OP-TEE and the WebAssembly Micro Runtime (WAMR).

- Uses WebAssembly Micro Runtime (WAMR) inside an OP-TEE Trusted Application to execute unaltered AI models on Arm TrustZone.
- Implements an AI model distributor that encrypts WebAssembly binaries using encryption keys stored in device hardware fuses.
- Incurs a 22% overhead compared to manually ported OP-TEE applications.
- Adds 6% inference latency compared to native execution while eliminating significant porting effort.

**Why it matters:** It enables secure edge deployment of proprietary AI models against root-level OS attacks without requiring manual, complex model refactoring for Trusted Execution Environments.

### [From Public Posts to AI-Search Citations: Measuring the Fragility of AI Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11932v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-08 · `arXiv:2610.11932v1`

Researchers evaluated 10 AI-search platforms, demonstrating that low-barrier public posts can be systematically injected into citations and generated outputs within hours, including via cheap commercial generative engine optimization (GEO) services.

- Evaluated 17,211 citation instances across 6,356 unique domains on 10 AI-search platforms, finding high citation concentration where the top-20 domains account for 20.5% to 70.8% of citations per platform.
- Identified low or medium account setup and posting barriers in 15 of 22 tested publication platforms tied to frequently cited domains.
- Fabricated concepts published on preferred low-barrier platforms were cited by 8 of 10 AI-search platforms within seven days, with one high-preference post outperforming over 20 matched low-preference posts.
- Demonstrated commercial exploitation using a $14 GEO purchase to create 13 posts, causing one AI-search platform to cite marker-controlled content within one hour.

**Why it matters:** Low publication barriers on platform-preferred domains allow unverified public content and paid GEO campaigns to rapidly manipulate AI-search citations and generated answers.

<small>Qi Liu, Geng Hong, Xinyang Zhang, Pei Chen, Yutong Li, Min Yang</small>

### [SemField: A Simple, Linear, Continuous, yet Robust Semantic Watermark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11848v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-08 · `arXiv:2610.11848v1`

SemField is a simple, training-free semantic watermark that embeds a continuous, linear signal into the sentence embedding space to resist paraphrasing and structural document tampering.

- Uses a shared secret key to define a Gaussian direction, selecting candidate sentences that maximize cumulative document alignment with this vector.
- Introduces SemField-PL, a polarity-locked variant that sets an initial orientation from the first sentence and reinforces it across text generation.
- Aggregated document-level formulation provides exact invariance to sentence reordering and theoretical robustness against sentence insertion and deletion.
- Outperformed 12 baselines across three models, achieving a mean TPR of 88.2% to 90.4% at a 1% FPR under clean detection and four paraphrasing attacks.

**Why it matters:** It offers an open-source, robust solution for identifying LLM-generated text that survives paraphrasing attacks and structural edits without degrading text quality.

<small>Varun Gumma, Navonil Majumdar, Soujanya Poria</small>

### [Detection-Guided Adaptive Purification with Diffusion Models for Robust Audio Deepfake Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10752v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-07 · `arXiv:2610.10752v1`

Detection-Guided Adaptive Purification (DGAP) uses score shifts from light diffusion purification to dynamically adjust purification strength, successfully neutralizing audio deepfake adversarial attacks without retraining detectors or degrading benign inputs.

- Identifies adversarial manipulation by measuring detector score shifts caused by an initial light diffusion purification step.
- Bypasses additional processing for benign inputs with low score shifts, preserving subtle spoofing cues and audio fidelity.
- Applies stronger diffusion-based purification only to inputs flagged as adversarial before passing them to the final pretrained detector.
- Outperforms nine existing defense methods across three deepfake detectors and three adversarial attack settings, remaining effective against defense-aware adaptive attacks.

**Why it matters:** It enables robust, model-agnostic protection for existing audio deepfake detectors against adversarial attacks without requiring costly detector retraining or degrading performance on clean audio.

### [Applying Security by Design at the Point of Execution: How Governed Security Requirements Affect the Security of AI-Generated Code](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10659v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-07 · `arXiv:2610.10659v1`

Delivering governed security-by-design requirements via a Model Context Protocol (MCP) server at execution time significantly reduces security vulnerabilities in AI-generated Python and backend code.

- Integrating SbD-ToE security requirements via an MCP server increased DualGauge Python task pass rates from 44.1% to 78.0% and test pass rates from 77.4% to 93.0%.
- On BaxBench containerized backend scenarios, non-exploitable functionally correct code increased from 65% to 86%, matching the authors' upper-bound Oracle Security Reminder (85%) without prior knowledge of tests.
- The pre-specified joint secure-and-functional metric did not show significant improvement on either benchmark due to stricter generated code failing rigid, unstated functional test assumptions.
- The study highlights benchmark limitations, noting that current tools measure weakness detection rather than actual conformance with applicable security requirements.

**Why it matters:** Providing governed security requirements directly to AI agents during generation matches unrealistic oracle baselines, proving that automated context delivery can effectively secure AI-generated software.

### [Receiver-Domain Behavioral Probing for Backdoor-Resilient Federated GPS Spoofing Detection in UAV Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10360v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-07 · `arXiv:2610.10360v1`

A new receiver-domain behavioral probing method mitigates accuracy-inflation backdoor attacks in federated UAV GPS spoofing detection by evaluating client models on counterfactual features instead of relying on self-reported validation metrics.

- Demonstrates that self-reported accuracy in UAV federated learning allows two compromised clients out of ten to achieve a backdoor lift of +0.3036 via data poisoning, update scaling, and inflated reports.
- Introduces receiver-domain behavioral probing, which tests submitted models against counterfactual spoofed samples created by setting discriminative GPS features to benign values to determine true client weight.
- Reduces attacker-induced lift to -0.0265 under independent and identically distributed (IID) client conditions while identifying compromised aircraft.
- Outperforms Byzantine-robust aggregation like Multi-Krum when attacker counts are misconfigured, preventing Multi-Krum's performance degradation from +0.0061 to +0.2837.
- Acknowledges performance limitations and failure cases when applied under environments with strong client heterogeneity.

**Why it matters:** It prevents malicious UAVs from manipulating federated learning weights through self-reported metrics, securing collaborative GPS spoofing detection without needing prior knowledge of the exact number of attackers.

<small>Will Jedrzejczak, Cole Walther, Dilpreet Gill, Moh Khalid Hasan</small>

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-07 · `arXiv:2610.10655v1`

Nullify is a training-free, non-destructive LLM unlearning method that uses inference-time activation steering vectors constrained to a null space, effectively erasing private knowledge while retaining overall model utility.

- Applies steering vectors during inference to redirect privacy-related activations away from memorized answers.
- Utilizes a null-space constraint on steering vectors to ensure activations for retained queries remain virtually unaffected.

- Matches or outperforms existing unlearning baselines in forget quality on TOFU and MUSE benchmarks while preserving utility.

**Why it matters:** It offers an efficient, computationally inexpensive way to remove sensitive data from LLMs at inference time without risking collateral damage to general model capability.

### [Power Side-Channel Membership Inference Attack on Embedded Machine Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10909v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-07 · `arXiv:2610.10909v1`

Researchers introduced PSCMIA, a power side-channel membership inference attack that determines if a sample was in an embedded ML model's training set using power traces without needing prediction probabilities or labels.

- PSCMIA targets embedded systems where standard model outputs are suppressed or restricted, exploiting data-dependent computational power leakage.
- Evaluations covered fully connected (FC) and convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures deployed on STM32F3 and XMEGA hardware across MNIST, FMNIST, CIFAR10, and CINIC10 datasets.
- The attack achieved ROC-AUC scores up to 0.907 on FC models.
- For CNN models, the ROC-AUC gap between PSCMIA and standard probability vector-based shadow MIA ranged from 0.006 to 0.116.
- PSCMIA outperformed traditional label-only MIAs in 11 out of 16 tested model-dataset-hardware configurations.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that suppressing software outputs on embedded ML hardware is insufficient to protect training data privacy against attackers with physical access.

A study reveals that adding a single colon to a candidate answer causes significant false acceptance spikes in the Jev decision model depending on how structured judging requests are presented.

### [ProxyEraseAgent: Blind Watermark Removal in the Wild](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11290v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-08 · `arXiv:2610.11290v1`

ProxyEraseAgent achieves a 94.8% success rate in blind watermark removal by using candidate decoders as proxy boundary estimators to guide sequential, quality-constrained image transformations.

- Addresses the open-loop limitation of single-image attacks by obtaining removal directions without accessing the target hidden decoder.
- Ranks publicly available candidate decoders by calibrated response strength to select top performers as proxy boundary estimators.
- Uses proxy feedback to execute a progressive search across heterogeneous operations, including geometric distortion, JPEG compression, image reconstruction, and gradient perturbation.
- Demonstrates a 94.8% attack success rate across evaluations on 11 distinct watermarking systems.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that blind, single-image watermark removal can achieve high specificity and success rates without direct target decoder access, exposing vulnerabilities in practical watermarking deployments.
