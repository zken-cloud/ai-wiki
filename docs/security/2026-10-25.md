### [On the Adversarial Vulnerability of Neural Receivers with Unrolled LDPC Decoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30345v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-21 · `arXiv:2610.30345v1`

End-to-end gradient attacks on neural receivers fail due to gradient masking from unrolled LDPC decoder saturation, but new losses bypassing or supervising early iterations restore attack efficacy.

- Identified gradient masking caused by tanh saturation in the belief-propagation check-node boxplus operation, decaying gradient norms by over four orders of magnitude across 10 unrolled iterations.
- Proposed L_BP, which applies per-iteration supervision to route gradients through unsaturated early iterations of the unrolled LDPC decoder.
- Proposed L_LLR, a margin loss on pre-decoder log-likelihood ratios that completely bypasses the decoder's computational graph.
- Demonstrated that both loss functions increase block-error rate (BLER) by 1 to 4 orders of magnitude at matched perturbation power across Sionna NeuralReceiver and DeepRx architectures.
- Observed that L_LLR-optimized perturbations achieve up to 3 orders of magnitude higher BLER on unseen channel realizations compared to L_BP at low perturbation-to-signal ratios (PSR).

**Why it matters:** The findings reveal critical security vulnerabilities in end-to-end 5G NR neural receivers, demonstrating that safety from gradient attacks was an artifact of gradient masking rather than actual physical robustness.

<small>Aakash Saini, Jafar Aco Mohammadi, Wolfgang Gerstacker</small>

### [VX-One: Typed, Calibrated Decision Heads for Real-Time Perimeter Triage - An empirical study of unknown-attack honesty, cascade gray zones, and calibration under drift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30090v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-21 · `arXiv:2610.30090v1`

VX-One is a small, multi-task decision network (100K-10M parameters) that delivers typed, calibrated outputs for real-time perimeter triage in ~0.7-1.7 ms p50 CPU latency.

- Achieves sub-2ms CPU p50 latency and outperforms baselines in binary detection under temporal drift on CIC-IDS2017 (AUROC 0.711 vs 0.637), though XGBoost still wins on multi-class attribution.
- Tested as a Phase 2 model following Suricata (52,674 rules), resolving the ~99.9% gray-zone traffic left by signatures on payload-less flows to achieve combined recall >=0.978.
- Exhibits limitations under unseen attack families and drift: abstention thresholds transfer poorly across datasets, and calibration collapses under temporal drift across all evaluated models (ECE rises from 0.04 to 0.40).

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that lightweight, typed multi-task networks can efficiently resolve signature-engine gray zones at the network edge, while highlighting critical calibration failures under temporal drift.

<small>Juan S. Santillana</small>

### [Parameters efficient language model with latent cross-attention for IoT intrusion detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30222v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-21 · `arXiv:2610.30222v1`

A compact Transformer architecture uses latent cross-attention compression to achieve high-accuracy IoT intrusion detection with only 306k non-embedding parameters.

- Compresses input into a small latent space prior to processing with a minimal Transformer core, totaling 1.6M parameters (6MB) and 306k non-embedding parameters.
- Outperforms SecurityBERT by ~2 points in macro F1-score on Edge-IIoTset and performs within 0.7 points on CICIoT2023, while being 7x smaller and ~89x faster on simulated edge hardware.
- Demonstrates that a single Transformer layer is sufficient and that parameter sharing matches layer stacking performance while reducing parameters by up to 66%.

**Why it matters:** It proves that Transformer-based intrusion detection can be deployed efficiently on memory-constrained IoT edge devices without sacrificing classification accuracy.

<small>Frederic Adjewa, Moez Esseghir, Leila Merghem-Boulahia, Cheikh Kacfah</small>
