### [Look Here, Then Decide: Small Decision Models in a Harness Find Memory-Safety Vulnerabilities at Scale on Commodity GPUs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16565v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-12 · `arXiv:2611.16565v1`

Researchers built a harness using Intern-Decision-4B, a 4-billion parameter open-weight decision model, to prioritize vulnerability searches without frontier LLMs, confirming 187 memory-safety vulnerabilities across 4,000 C/C++ projects.

- Uses Intern-Decision-4B to score candidate functions in one forward pass, serving strictly as a queue ranker rather than a binary classifier.
- Combines the 4B decision model with deterministic incomplete-fix miners and shape localizers to target analysis without using a large frontier model.
- Achieved processing throughput of 900-1,100 repositories per hour on three GB10 systems, running 4.4 decisions/sec on a GB10 and 9.8 decisions/sec on an RTX 4090.
- Confirmed 187 memory-safety vulnerabilities (12 critical, 148 high, 23 medium, 4 low), placing confirmed findings in the top 10 candidates 7.5 times more effectively than random ordering.
- Evaluated raw model performance at 0.73 AUC for separating vulnerable from benign functions, but only 0.56 AUC for separating a vulnerable function from its own patch.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that small, open-weight models paired with deterministic analysis can scale automated vulnerability discovery across thousands of C/C++ codebases at low cost on commodity GPU hardware.

<small>Tim De Wachter, Kyryl Volkov, Myrto Eleftheria Gkogkou, Mathieu Huysman</small>

### [Privacy Leakage from Recurrent State in LLM Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16048v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-11 · `arXiv:2611.16048v1`

A new attack methodology reveals that private facts and system prompt rules stored in an LLM's recurrent state leak through state-probing, persistence across public dialogue, causal decision shifts, and side-channel power analysis.

- Recurrent state probing successfully extracts private author facts, personal attributes, and system instructions, with attribute extraction generalizing to unseen authors.
- Retained information remains inferable after subsequent public dialogue and continues to causally alter future model decision margins.
- Complete-read power traces recover private text under fixed execution schedules, while unprivileged accounts on partitioned GPUs can infer user attributes (e.g., age) from whole-device power during public requests.

**Why it matters:** Serving platforms and hardware architects must implement strict state access controls, GPU telemetry restrictions, and power-side-channel mitigations to prevent context leaks beyond immediate LLM outputs.

<small>Weimin Fu, Shikun Liu, Qiying Li, Yuntao Liu, Pan Li, Xiaolong Guo</small>

`privacy` `llm-inference` `side-channel-attacks` `recurrent-state` `gpu-security`

### [Speech Tokenizers are Vulnerable: Transferable Semantic Attack and Robust Tokenizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14929v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-11 · `arXiv:2611.14929v1`

Researchers developed T-SemAttack, a transferable semantic attack targeting speech tokenizers, and ROSETok, a robust speech tokenizer trained via noisy training and semantic distillation to withstand perturbations.

- T-SemAttack uses a semantic encoder ensemble to drift deep representation spaces of speech tokenizers while preserving perceptual quality, degrading downstream ASR and LALM performance.
- Analysis reveals that cross-model attack transferability stems from layer-wise representation drift and directional alignment of input gradients across different surrogate encoders.
- ROSETok combines noisy training with robust semantic distillation to maintain high-fidelity speech reconstruction while improving downstream model robustness.
- Evaluation was conducted across multiple datasets, 9 open-source and black-box ASR systems, and 4 LALMs.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrating transferable semantic attacks highlights critical security vulnerabilities in speech front-ends that threaten downstream automatic speech recognition and large audio-language models.

<small>Zhisheng Zhang, Yifan Mi, Yixuan Zhou, Haiyun Li, Shengbo Cai, Dongfu Song, Ziang Xu, Jie Hao</small>

### [Infrastructure for Verifiable AI: Where Proof Costs Go and How Hardware Can Help](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17042v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-12 · `arXiv:2611.17042v1`

A four-layer framework and empirical measurements across six provers and seven kernels identify cryptographic overheads, VRAM limits, and hardware bottlenecks in zero-knowledge verifiable AI workloads.

- In low-rank fine-tuning, the cost of opening fixed model weight commitments stays nearly constant from 64 to 2048 input lengths, whereas intermediate value costs scale up.
- Hardware capacity dictates execution feasibility; a 13B-parameter fine-tuning workload fails on a 40GB GPU due to memory constraints but succeeds on an 80GB GPU.
- Cryptographic kernel profiling reveals that instruction issue and synchronization limits create bottlenecks that simple bandwidth-only memory models fail to predict.
- The dominant computational and memory costs shift based on the selected zero-knowledge proof system and the input sequence length.

**Why it matters:** Designing efficient hardware acceleration for verifiable AI requires targeting exact cryptographic and memory bottlenecks rather than relying solely on raw bandwidth or isolated cryptographic operation speedups.

<small>Yihan Xia, Wenxin Xu, Taotao Wang</small>

### [Prevalence Bounds for Edited Watermarked Text: Power Envelopes and Calibration Sensitivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17035v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-12 · `arXiv:2611.17035v1`

Researchers derived sharp power envelopes and finite-sample bounds to quantify the prevalence share of edited watermarked text in a corpus for Gumbel-max and green-list (KGW) watermarking schemes.

- Identifies theoretical upper bounds of 1 for unrestricted edits, leading to a power envelope indexed by three explicit budgets: retention floor, entropy, and in-place substitutions.
- Derives finite-sample bounds and streaming confidence sequences under exact nulls and nulls calibrated from independent human reference samples.
- Validated empirically on OPT-1.3B and Qwen3-1.7B outputs using 12,000 human documents; at a 0.2 true watermarked share, exact-null bounds yielded approximately (0.14, 0.38).
- Highlights sensitivity limits: LLM-paraphrased KGW text falls below the theoretical envelope, and minor null errors (0.01) break exact-null coverage at 100,000 documents unless repaired via calibrated nulls with large reference sets.

**Why it matters:** It enables auditors to reliably estimate the exact proportion of AI-generated content in a document corpus even after text has undergone unknown edits, substitutions, or paraphrasing.

<small>Kuancheng Ye</small>

### [Fault Injection Attacks on Q-Learning: Analysis, Vulnerabilities, and Countermeasures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16151v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-12 · `arXiv:2611.16151v1`

This paper provides a hardware fault injection analysis of five Q-Learning variants using ChipWhisperer voltage and clock glitching, demonstrating severe vulnerabilities in standard, robust, linear, and deep Q-learning implementations.

- Voltage and clock glitching attacks corrupt reward computations, action selection, and Q-value updates during learning, as well as inference actions across Standard, Robust, Linear, and Deep Q-Learning variants.
- Injected NaN and infinite values persist in Robust Q-Learning's Q-table because its greedy policy relies on the same vulnerable execution code as Standard Q-Learning.
- Resilient Q-Learning using Triple Modular Redundancy (TMR) with majority voting corrects single Q-table copy corruptions, but leaves shared inputs (like rewards) and the voting code unprotected.
- In a microscopic traffic simulator test, a single injected infinite value corrupted under 4% of the Q-table but increased accumulated vehicle waiting time by 148x.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrating that hardware fault injection can severely degrade reinforcement learning performance highlights critical security gaps in safety-critical edge deployments like intelligent traffic control systems.

<small>Tom Slooff, Anthony Etim, Jiaqi Yu, Francesco Regazzoni, Jakub Szefer</small>

`reinforcement-learning` `fault-injection` `hardware-security` `q-learning` `cyber-physical-systems`

### [SecDVA: A Maliciously Secure Multi-Party Framework for Data Value Assessment in Modern Machine Learning Data Markets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15373v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-11 · `arXiv:2611.15373v1`

SecDVA is the first maliciously secure multi-party computation framework for machine learning data value assessment, protecting seller data and buyer models without exposing intermediate information.

- Utilizes Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory to linearize model updates alongside mini-batch approximations to lower computational overhead for large models.
- Implements a (2+1)-party MPC protocol leveraging function secret sharing and custom non-linear primitives.
- Optimizes distributed point functions (DPF) and distributed comparison functions (DCF) to reduce AES calls in the underlying MPC backends.
- Prevents privacy leakage and strategic manipulation while enabling data evaluation prior to purchase.

**Why it matters:** It enables secure, privacy-preserving, and computationally feasible data valuations in machine learning data markets without requiring buyers or sellers to reveal proprietary models or datasets.

### [ReliaGuard: Reconstruction-Aware Watermarking for Source Attribution and Blind Edit Localization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16368v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-12 · `arXiv:2611.16368v1`

ReliaGuard is a reconstruction-aware watermarking framework that optimizes keyed VAE feature responses to enable joint source attribution and blind edit localization under generative edits and image processing.

- The writer optimizes keyed feature responses across both the protected image and its VAE reconstruction subject to image-distortion and source-preservation constraints.
- The reader uses residual evidence refinement and native-resolution-guided multiscale reading to predict edit masks from the received image and secret key alone.
- Achieves edit-region Intersection-over-Union (IoU) scores of 85.61% for complete generative editing outputs and 94.90% for locally spliced outputs at a nominal 1% background false-positive rate threshold.
- Outperforms the strongest baseline by 45.02 percentage points on complete generative outputs while maintaining 100% decoded identifier-bit accuracy under both full and spliced editing conditions.
- Maintains 81.01% (complete) and 86.49% (spliced) localization IoU with ~92.78% bit accuracy across seven families of subsequent image processing operations.

**Why it matters:** It prevents background VAE reconstructions in generative editing pipelines from degrading spatial watermarking evidence, allowing reliable tamper localization alongside robust copyright attribution.

<small>Hailong Wang, Wuzheng Tan, Zixin Zhang, Xujun Fang, Fengjuan Gong</small>

### [Protocol-Aware Auditing of Domain-Blind Feature Attribution in Network Intrusion Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16018v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-11 · `arXiv:2611.16018v1`

Researchers formalize domain blindness in NIDS explanations and introduce protocol-aware query controls for LIME, SHAP, Integrated Gradients, and DeepLIFT to prevent out-of-domain feature attribution errors.

- Formalized domain blindness in feature attribution methods and proved valid-query invariance, ensuring consistent explanations when models agree within a declared rule-valid set.
- Instantiated protocol-aware query controls across LIME, SHAP, Integrated Gradients (IG), and DeepLIFT, evaluating them on four NIDS datasets and three classifier families.
- Revealed that standard LIME's reported perfect top-feature repeatability masked high underlying variability, which PA-LIME measured at a mean of 0.575 across six settings.
- Demonstrated that PA-DeepLIFT and PA-IG achieved the highest attribution-similarity curve areas (0.452 and 0.413) under FGSM attacks with protocol repair, increasing mean within-PA rank agreement from 0.217 to 0.366.

**Why it matters:** Protocol-aware controls prevent explainability tools from querying invalid network states, providing more trustworthy and robust security audits for network intrusion detection systems.

<small>Emmanuelli El-Mahmoud, Abanisenioluwa Kolawole Orojo, Pablo Rivas, Erika Leal</small>

### [Your Secrets Are Showing: Deep Image Hiding Models Are Surprisingly Easy to Attack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15583v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-11-11 · `arXiv:2611.15583v1`

Deep image hiding models are vulnerable to cross-model secret extraction, allowing a single decoder like HiNet to reveal hidden image content produced by unknown, unseen steganography architectures.

- Tested cross-model secret extraction across 11 hiding models using invertible neural networks and autoencoders with CNN, Transformer, and Mamba backbones in spatial and frequency domains.
- Standard training of a HiNet decoder enables the extraction of recognizable secret image content from unseen target models without knowing their specific architecture.
- Frequency analysis revealed a shared bias toward mid- and high-frequency embedding across models, enabling enhanced attacks by suppressing secret textures and enhancing cover textures during training.
- The attack successfully transfers to out-of-distribution secrets, multi-image hiding schemes, and privacy-focused restoration methods like AIRPNet.

**Why it matters:** Deep steganography methods fail to provide confidentiality against adversarial extraction, rendering them unsafe for privacy-preserving image security applications.

<small>Hao Liu, Jing Yu, Weizhi Liu, Lingling Yuan, Fengyong Li, Keke Gai, Zhaoxia Yin, Xinpeng Zhang</small>

`steganography` `deep-learning` `image-privacy` `adversarial-attack` `computer-vision`
