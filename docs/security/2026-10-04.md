### [ReCast: Contract-Preserving Protection for Fixed-Interface Multimodal Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01184v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-01 · `arXiv:2610.01184v1`

ReCast is an agentic framework that protects private multimodal inputs by converting them to textual records, rewriting entities, and applying invertible numerical mapping before generating sanitized media for remote solvers.

- Converts private inputs locally into a shared textual evidence-query record, using a distilled 4B model to rewrite entities and topics.
- Applies a locally invertible, role-aware numerical map to substitute values and reverses protected program operands locally post-execution.
- Achieves 75.10% accuracy on 4,000 held-out ChartQA and NMSQA examples, retaining 92.43% of the unprotected remote accuracy.
- Reduces source-content leakage to 7.95% in solver-bound requests based on a model-based audit, outperforming evaluated local baselines.

**Why it matters:** It enables secure utilization of powerful remote multimodal solvers without altering their fixed media interfaces or exposing sensitive source content.

<small>Bingchen Pei, Lichong Chen, Bingxi Zhao, Ziang Wu, Sirui Wang, Min Zhang, Yanhao Chen, Qingxu Liu</small>

### [Crossing the Cyber Divide: Sim-to-Sim and Sim-to-Real Transfer for RL Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00759v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-09-30 · `arXiv:2610.00759v1`

A new framework decouples state alignment from action translation to enable zero-shot policy transfer for reinforcement learning cyber attack agents across different simulators and emulated environments without retraining.

- Decouples state alignment and action translation to solve zero-shot policy transfer across heterogeneous cyber environments.
- Evaluated across four distinct cyber platforms: CyberBattleSim, NetSecGame, CyberWheel, and NASim, including emulated virtual machine deployments.
- Achieved a 45.2% win rate during simulator transfer for policies with a 60.5% source performance, fully preserving performance in closely aligned setups.
- Demonstrated high behavioral similarity in emulated virtual machine environments with a Jensen-Shannon divergence of 0.085 relative to native policies.

**Why it matters:** This framework allows reinforcement learning security agents trained in abstract simulators to be directly deployed to different simulators or real-world emulations without costly retraining.

<small>Sabrina Saika, Yinuo Du, Aritran Piplai</small>

### [Progressive-Resolution Secure Aggregation for Federated Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00695v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-09-30 · `arXiv:2610.00695v1`

Progressive-resolution secure aggregation (PSA) enables a server to authorize successively finer resolutions of an aggregated update from a single client upload without requiring renewed client participation.

- Formulates a progressive-resolution secure-aggregation functionality that uncouples aggregate precision authorization from the initial client upload phase.
- Represents clipped and dithered client updates using compatible nested-lattice digits.
- Protects separately releasable layers through a combination of secure aggregation and an additional aggregate pad.
- Uses a non-colluding release controller to hold and grant aggregate pads, preventing the server from unlocking finer resolution layers without authorization.

**Why it matters:** It allows federated learning servers to dynamically adjust aggregate precision post-upload without burdening client devices with re-transmission or additional computation.

<small>Seyed Mohammad Azimi-Abarghouyi</small>

### [Combining Homomorphic Encryption and Differential Privacy in Federated Learning for Model Inspection and Availability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01650v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-01 · `arXiv:2610.01650v1`

A federated learning framework combines homomorphic encryption for model training with differential privacy for model inspection and release, improving both model utility and estimated privacy over differential-privacy-only baselines.

- Combines homomorphic encryption (HE) during training with differential privacy (DP) applied specifically for model inspection and final release.
- Uses a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC)-based Bayesian privacy estimation method to evaluate the framework's overall privacy guarantees.
- Achieves a test loss of 1.09 on the FEMNIST dataset compared to 2.37 for a DP-only training baseline.
- Improves estimated privacy, yielding a posterior mean epsilon (ε) of 4.32 compared to 7.26 for the DP-only approach.
- Demonstrates that intermittent model monitoring preserves the encrypted training trajectory while maintaining privacy bounds comparable to or stronger than DP-only training.

**Why it matters:** By restricting differential privacy to periodic inspection rather than applying it to every training step, federated systems can achieve significantly higher model accuracy without sacrificing privacy.

<small>Ceren Yıldırım, Kamer Kaya, Sinan Yıldırım, Erkay Savaş</small>

### [Made to Measure: Designing Image Watermarks to Specification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00780v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-09-30 · `arXiv:2610.00780v1`

TAILOR is a three-stage request-conditioned image watermarking framework that dynamically composes multiple watermark fragments using SMT optimization to satisfy specific robustness, false-positive rate, latency, and quality specifications.

- Offline characterization maps individual fragment recovery, image distortion, and runtime across embedding strengths as response curves.
- Joint configuration selection translates multi-criteria requests into an SMT model to solve for fragment choice, strengths, order, and geometric recovery.
- Live calibration tests selected configurations on target images and dynamically refines non-transferable performance predictions.
- In evaluations across 7,321 requests, TAILOR achieved a 96.21% request satisfaction rate with a mean PSNR of 41.02 dB.

**Why it matters:** It enables automated, custom watermark generation that optimizes the trade-offs between image quality, attack robustness, runtime, and false-positive rates for specific deployment needs.

<small>Mingzhe Li, Yuefeng Peng, Kejing Xia, Pranav Jeyakumar, Ruolan Leslie Famularo, Shiqing Ma</small>
