### [GROB: A Multi-Agent Architecture for Public-Trace Investigation of Candidate Agentic Activity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11467v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-08 · `arXiv:2610.11467v1`

GROB is a multi-agent architecture that conducts read-only collection of public Internet traces to preserve evidence for later resolution when privileged telemetry is unavailable.

- Operates via controlled, read-only collection and preservation of public traces without requiring privileged telemetry.
- Demonstrated trace resolution in a frozen September 2026 corpus where Census-labelled identifiers captured on September 9 were resolved via public revision records to Census requests from June 16–17.
- Highlights limitations of public traces, noting that while sparse evidence supports later reconstruction, it cannot establish organizational attribution.
- Identifies agent identity continuity as an unresolved challenge for autonomous language-model agents.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates that sparse, unprivileged public traces can be collected proactively to successfully reconstruct past autonomous agent activity once supplementary public evidence emerges.

<small>Chiara Bonfanti, Cataldo Basile</small>

### [Characterizing Statistical Separability in TP-CRIV for Probabilistic AI Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11163v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-08 · `arXiv:2610.11163v1`

A theoretical characterization of statistical separability in third-party challenge-response identity verification (TP-CRIV) for probabilistic AI models that quantifies verification performance based on challenge and response counts.

- Formulates a statistical framework connecting challenge-wise probabilistic model behavior to system-level verification separability between matching and non-matching provers.
- Derives theoretical relationships showing how the number of independent challenges and repeated responses per challenge impact the target Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC).
- Provides a method to estimate the minimum required verification budget (challenge and response repetitions) needed to reach a specific target AUC.
- Validated empirically on Large Language Models (LLMs) using open-ended challenges, showing close alignment between theoretical and observed AUC metrics.

**Why it matters:** It provides a rigorous mathematical foundation to calculate the exact testing budget required to audit and verify remotely deployed probabilistic AI models without direct access to the reference model.

<small>Teruki Sano, Minoru Kuribayashi, Masao Sakai, Shuji Isobe, Eisuke Koizumi, Zhang Zhang, Satoru Matsumoto</small>

### [BRACE: Differential Privacy for Dense Associative Memory with LSR Energy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11218v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-08 · `arXiv:2610.11218v1`

The BRACE algorithm introduces a differentially private retrieval mechanism for log-sum-ReLU dense associative memory that adaptively corrects boundary-sensitive perturbations across state trajectories.

- Addresses privacy challenges caused by the finite-support retrieval dynamics of log-sum-ReLU (LSR) dense associative memory (DAM).
- Introduces Boundary-Responsive Adaptive Correction Evolution (BRACE) to control cumulative noise over the retrieval trajectory.
- Proves minimax optimality by establishing dimension-independent terminal and full-trajectory error rates with optimal scaling for inverse temperature and retrieval horizon.
- Provides central limit theorems to quantify privacy-induced variability and characterize the asymptotic distribution of private retrieval dynamics.

**Why it matters:** It establishes a theoretical baseline for dimension-independent, privacy-preserving state retrieval and uncertainty quantification in energy-based memory systems.

<small>Chang Qu, Zhaoyang Shi</small>

### [Speedbumps: Rejection Attacks on Speculative Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10929v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-07 · `arXiv:2610.10929v1`

Researchers introduced Speculative Rejection Attacks (SRAs), using adversarial suffixes to reduce draft-token acceptance rates in speculative decoding and inflate target LLM inference latency.

- Attacks append adversarial suffixes to degrade draft-target distribution agreement, forcing more target-model forward passes per generated token.
- Speedbump-P optimizes acceptance using the target's probability of draft proposals, while Speedbump-D optimizes based on the overlap between draft and target distributions.
- Attacks can reduce inference speed below standard autoregressive decoding, transfer across different draft/target model pairings, and remain effective under sampling.
- Applying regularization during suffix optimization restores output quality but reduces attack effectiveness, offering a trade-off between stealthiness and speed degradation.

**Why it matters:** SRAs reveal that the interaction between draft and target models is a viable attack surface, allowing malicious inputs to cause severe denial-of-service and cost inflation in speculative LLM serving systems.

<small>Adam Y. J. Jones, Yu Yuan, Sergio Maffeis</small>

### [PairAudit: Guiding Human Review with Graph Tokens under Distribution Shift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10260v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-10-07 · `arXiv:2610.10260v1`

PairAudit uses graph tokens to analyze relational prediction patterns and identify confident misclassifications under distribution shift, maximizing error correction within a fixed human review budget without retraining the detector.

- Leverages graph tokens to capture relational patterns across connected nodes rather than performing feature aggregation to build a new predictor.
- Identifies confident intrusion detection errors on unseen attacks where uncertainty-based and anomaly-score methods fail.
- Integrates human feedback to dynamically evaluate whether identified relational anomalies justify altering review priorities.
- Outperforms uncertainty-based review in average error corrections across security tasks while accounting for all review costs.

**Why it matters:** It enables security operations to catch novel, confident attack misclassifications under strict human auditing budgets without needing to retrain existing intrusion detection models.

<small>Jiran Tao, Binyan Jiang</small>
