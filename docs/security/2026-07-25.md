### [On AI Safety and Security Technical Debt in Engineering AI-Enabled Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23365v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-25 · `arXiv:2607.23365v1`

This study introduces AITD-MAP, an integrated framework mapping a taxonomy of 31 AI technical debt types across 7 root-cause classes to 18 safety and security risks, backed by 34 actionable mitigation guidelines.

- Systematic review of 60 primary studies categorizes 31 distinct AI Technical Debt (AITD) types into a root-cause taxonomy of 7 classes.
- Maps identified AITDs to 18 trust-related concerns, specifically 6 safety hazards and 12 security vulnerabilities.
- Synthesizes 34 lifecycle mitigation guidelines consisting of 8 safety-focused and 26 security-focused strategies.
- Introduces the AITD-MAP framework based on AI TRiSM principles to connect debts, risk impacts, and mitigation techniques for software engineers.

**Why it matters:** It provides AI software engineers with a structured framework to make latent safety and security technical debts visible and actionable across complex, tightly coupled AI pipelines.

<small>Muhammad Tukur, Hayatullahi B. Adeyemo, Tao Chen, Nour Ali, Anis Zarrad, Rick Kazman, Marco Agus, Rami Bahsoon</small>

### [Accountable yet Anonymous AI Agents - Split-Knowledge Binding in National Agent-Identity Layer in China](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23207v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-25 · `arXiv:2607.23207v1`

China is deploying a national agent-identity layer using split-knowledge binding to enable agent accountability while maintaining business-layer anonymity from commercial entities.

- Split-knowledge binding uses a structural and procedural separation across two separate government agencies rather than cryptographic mechanisms to hold identity escrow.
- Re-identification of an agent's verified legal principal requires legal due process to compel both holding agencies simultaneously, as neither agency can re-identify independently.
- The paper introduces the ex-post attribution thesis, claiming that only attribution-based accountability holds legal force for AI agent actions with legal consequences.
- The architecture defines an 'accountability surface' to map which specific agent actions generate identity-bearing traces.

**Why it matters:** It demonstrates a national-scale framework for balancing user privacy with regulatory accountability for autonomous AI agents without relying on cryptographic primitives.

<small>Yifan He, Zhiguang Shan, Le Luo, Wei Wang</small>

### [False Prophets: On the Security of World Models in Agentic Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23147v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-25 · `arXiv:2607.23147v1`

Researchers identified security vulnerabilities in text-based world models used by autonomous terminal agents, demonstrating that induced mispredictions can trigger unintended command execution, data extraction, and financial loss with up to a 95% success rate.

- Identified vulnerabilities unique to text-based world models in terminal agents that enable malicious code execution, denial of service, wallet drainage, and sensitive data extraction.
- Demonstrated that attackers can manipulate approximate world models to cause mispredictions in agentic pipelines with success rates up to 95%.
- Introduced a new security benchmark dataset specifically designed for evaluating vulnerabilities in text-based world models.
- Provided practical mitigation recommendations to harden agentic systems against world model exploitation.

**Why it matters:** Integrating unhardened world models into autonomous agents creates high-impact security risks, allowing attackers to manipulate an agent's predictive logic to execute arbitrary terminal commands or exfiltrate private data.

<small>Erik Imgrund, Anna Wimbauer, Klim Kireev, Konrad Rieck</small>

### [Hiding in Plain Sight: An Effective Physical Adversarial Patch Attack against Visual-Infrared Fused Face Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23292v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-25 · `arXiv:2607.23292v1`

VIPatch introduces a novel physical adversarial patch attack against visual-infrared fused face detection models using jointly optimized, natural-looking gradient-color masks and band-aid stickers.

- Targets visual-infrared fused face detection models in both digital and physical domains.
- Jointly optimizes a gradient-color mask and a band-aid sticker pattern across both visual and infrared modalities.
- Uses digital patch optimization to guide the physical fabrication of inconspicuous adversarial patches.
- Achieves attack success rates exceeding 90% in physical and digital evaluations while remaining unobtrusive to humans.

**Why it matters:** It reveals critical security vulnerabilities in multi-modal visual-infrared face detection systems using realistic physical artifacts that evade human detection.

### [GLST: Defending Confidence-Driven V2X Collaborative Perception Against Stealthy Multi-Attacker Feature Injection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23059v1)

**arXiv cs.CR** · 2026-07-25 · `arXiv:2607.23059v1`

GLST defends V2X collaborative perception against stealthy feature injection by combining global feature, multi-scale local residual, and ego-semantic topology consistency signals.

- Pretend Benign attacks exploit confidence-driven sparse V2X frameworks like Where2comm by injecting perturbations into uncertain yet perception-critical regions.
- Single-signal trust defenses fail under multi-attacker scenarios because compromised nodes can establish a pseudo-consensus that manipulates trust estimation.
- Global-Local Structural Trust (GLST) evaluates collaborators using three signals: global feature consistency, multi-scale local residual consistency, and structural consistency with ego-side semantic topology.
- On the OPV2V dataset under a four-attacker Pretend Benign attack, GLST retains an AP@0.3 of 0.69, outperforming single-signal defenses while resisting PGD gradient-based attacks.

**Why it matters:** It prevents malicious connected vehicles from degrading 3D object detection in V2X networks, ensuring robust autonomous driving perception even during coordinated multi-node attacks.

<small>Ji He, Ying Wang, Lijie Zheng, Xinghui Zhu, Yulong Shen, Xiaohong Jiang</small>
