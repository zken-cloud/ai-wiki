### Themes

Infrastructure efforts today highlight a shift away from pure token-generation speed toward tackling the operational friction of complex pipelines. As reinforcement learning post-training becomes standard, sync lag between disaggregated training and inference clusters has emerged as a major efficiency killer. Initiatives like NVIDIA's [Reshard Collective](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/accelerating-reinforcement-learning-weight-refit-with-the-reshard-collective/) and [REINFORCE Zero](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-11-06-zero-mismatch-async-rl) attempt to stabilize asynchronous RL under heavy weight lag, while architectural patches like the [Multi-layer Indexer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03768) seek to bypass indexer bottlenecks in sparse attention without forcing expensive model retraining.

Concurrently, the practical deployment of agentic systems is outstripping defenders' ability to safely evaluate or rely on them. Real-world threat actors are already deploying agentic frameworks like [ARTEX](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/06/artex-abuse-expands-as-cybercriminals-target-financial-sector/) in financial attacks. Meanwhile, defensive automation demonstrates clear brittle points—as shown by the failure of fast-tier triage models in [Escalate or Close](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08229v1)—prompting a surge in more rigorous, programmatic benchmarks like [TB-MathModel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08237) and [BioBasicsBench](https://openai.com/index/measuring-how-ai-agents-handle-wet-lab-science) to verify agent outputs beyond basic multiple-choice evaluations.
