### Themes

Agent security research today exposes a glaring rift between static benchmarks and deployment realities. Studies like [Passing the Test You Trained On](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03448v1) show that top-ranking prompt-injection detectors fail to maintain their effectiveness when handling agent tool inputs. Worse, [Threat-Preserving Representation Sensitivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03585v1) demonstrates that superficial changes—such as simply renaming a tool—can radically alter an agent's attack success rate. Current safety guarantees are largely superficial facades vulnerable to trivial environment shifts.

Concurrently, researchers are pushing for domain-specific evaluation metrics to replace generic LLM benchmarks. From verifying executable engineering specs in [SimuVerity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02304) to legal taxonomy rewards in [LexReward](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39071), the focus is shifting toward practical execution. However, fundamental biases persist: [Source Preference in the Wild](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03195) reveals that search agents routinely pick worse answers simply because they come from favored sources, proving that domain evaluation must account for hardcoded behavioral flaws before agents can be trusted with autonomous execution.

### Worth a closer look

- [Science or Slop?: Benchmarking and Mitigating Scientific Slop in AI-Generated Papers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00531) — A necessary attempt to formalize the detection of low-quality AI scientific papers using global reasoning metrics and grounded experimental tracking.
- [AgentTrap: Stateful Feedback Deception against Autonomous Penetration Testing Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02869) — An inventive shift toward active defense that turns dynamic agent planning against itself, using deceptive honeypot endpoints to waste agent cycles and capture credentials.
- [Source Preference in the Wild: How LLM Agents Favor Items by Source, and How to Reduce It](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03195) — A sobering look at 12 search agents across three domains showing how models consistently ignore better content in favor of preferred domain sources.
