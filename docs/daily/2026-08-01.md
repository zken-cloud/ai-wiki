# 📰 Daily Briefing — 2026-08-01

### The short version
*   A [Chinese-speaking threat actor](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/autonomous-ai-cyber-attack-campaign/) is reportedly using AI models to autonomously scan for vulnerabilities, marking a shift from theoretical to real-world AI-assisted attacks.
*   Microsoft is responding with [Project Perception](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/07/27/rethinking-security-for-the-age-of-ai/), an "agentic security system" designed to use AI to counter AI-driven threats, and is funding external research through a new [red teaming alliance](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/07/27/enhancing-ai-security-through-global-ai-red-teaming/).
*   NVIDIA argues that for AI agents, the surrounding "harness" architecture is as critical as the model itself, with [major impacts on performance and cost](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/six-agent-harness-capabilities-for-higher-model-performance/).

### Themes
Today's items paint a clear picture of an emerging AI security arms race. The use of AI for [autonomous vulnerability scanning](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/autonomous-ai-cyber-attack-campaign/) by attackers represents a significant offensive escalation. In response, defenders are moving beyond traditional security paradigms. Microsoft's announcement of [Project Perception](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/07/27/rethinking-security-for-the-age-of-ai/) outlines a vision for AI agents that autonomously defend networks, creating a direct agent-on-agent conflict scenario.

This focus on AI agents—autonomous systems that can perceive, reason, and act—is the central theme. Both attackers and defenders are leveraging them. Meanwhile, technical deep dives from NVIDIA highlight the practical challenges, noting that an agent's performance is heavily dependent on its [surrounding software harness](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/six-agent-harness-capabilities-for-higher-model-performance/). While some pieces, like another on [deploying secure agents](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/four-ways-to-deploy-more-secure-ai-agents/), remain high-level, the trend is clear: the industry is grappling with the security implications of moving from passive models to active agents.

*   [Rethinking security for the age of AI](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/07/27/rethinking-security-for-the-age-of-ai/): This is a strategic announcement from a market heavyweight. While it's a forward-looking project, it signals how enterprise security architecture is likely to evolve, with specialized AI agents taking over continuous monitoring and response.
