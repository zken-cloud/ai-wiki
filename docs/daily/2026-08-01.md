### Themes
Today's items show a clear focus on building, securing, and running more capable AI agents. Research papers like [Qwen-UI-Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28227) and [Beacon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28595) aim to create more effective agents, while security teams are both building [agentic defense systems](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/07/27/rethinking-security-for-the-age-of-ai/) and tracking the first signs of [autonomous agentic attacks](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/autonomous-ai-cyber-attack-campaign/) in the wild. This agent-centric push is supported by a parallel effort to solve fundamental limitations, particularly memory, with new architectures like [Metis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26760) and [Memory Decoder at Scale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27919) proposing native, persistent memory for models.

Underpinning these model-level advances is a fierce battle over infrastructure. The release of the massive [Kimi K3](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/deploying-kimi-k3-on-amazon-sagemaker-hyperpod-and-amazon-eks/) model immediately sparked a flurry of activity from infrastructure players. The vLLM project announced [day-0 support](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-07-27-k3) and is holding its [first conference](https://vllm.ai/events/vllm-conference/2026), highlighting the critical role of inference optimization engines. Meanwhile, NVIDIA's posts on [AI model co-design](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/co-designing-ai-model-attention-for-fast-interactive-long-context-inference/) and the [performance gaps in supposedly identical clusters](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-exemplar-cloud-lessons-for-unlocking-full-performance-on-ai-infrastructure/) underscore that raw compute is not enough; squeezing out performance is a complex, system-level challenge.

### Worth a closer look
*   [BM25 Wins at Scale: A Scaling Study of Retrieval-Augmented Generation Paradigms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26497): A must-read for anyone building RAG systems. This controlled study suggests that for large document corpora, the decades-old BM25 algorithm is more effective than fashionable and complex agent-based retrieval methods. It's a potent, evidence-based reality check on current engineering trends.
*   [Ten advances in mathematics and theoretical computer science](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics): The claims are extraordinary and, if verified, mark a new phase of AI capability. It's worth reading to scrutinize the specific problems OpenAI claims to have made progress on and to assess whether this is a true leap in abstract reasoning or a case of clever problem formulation.
*   [Chinese-Speaking Threat Actor Harnesses AI Models for Autonomous Cyberattacks](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/autonomous-ai-cyber-attack-campaign/): This report moves the threat of AI-powered attacks from the theoretical to the observed. The details are important: the AI is currently used for autonomous vulnerability scanning, not full exploitation. It provides a concrete, if early, look at how adversaries are beginning to operationalize AI.
*   [Metis: Memory Foundation Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26760): This paper proposes a fundamental architectural shift by integrating persistent memory and memory-access procedures directly into a foundation model's core computation. It's a departure from simply expanding context windows or bolting on external databases, and it's worth understanding the proposed mechanism as a potential path toward models with genuine long-term state.
