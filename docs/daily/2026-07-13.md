# 📰 Daily Briefing — 2026-07-13

*26 items across 2 categories.*

### The short version
*   Researchers claim that pre-trained video generation models can serve as [general-purpose vision learners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09024), achieving state-of-the-art results on understanding tasks and suggesting a path toward a unified vision foundation model.
*   A new paper challenges text-only pretraining, arguing that [visual pretraining on document images](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09657) consistently improves a model's language intelligence.
*   A mechanistic explanation has been proposed for why LLMs fail to generalize finetuned facts, attributing the problem to a ["Knowing--Using Gap"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08393) where new knowledge isn't correctly routed to reasoning circuits.
*   Meta reports a 28% reduction in ads retrieval tail latency by implementing a [custom, open-source kernel scheduler](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/07/13/ml-applications/modernizing-the-meta-ads-service-with-an-open-source-kernel-scheduler/), demonstrating significant gains from systems-level optimization.

### Themes
A recurring theme today is the repurposing of generative models for analytical tasks. Researchers are finding that the rich internal representations learned by models trained to generate content can be effectively adapted for understanding it. For instance, the backbone of a [video generation model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09024) is being used as a universal vision system, while a frozen [text-to-image model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06553) is being adapted for dense prediction tasks like depth estimation. This suggests a potential convergence, where the distinction between generative and discriminative models may be more about the final layers than the foundational architecture.

Meanwhile, a significant amount of work continues to focus on patching the known flaws of today's models. This is less about grand architectural shifts and more about the necessary, incremental engineering to make systems more reliable and efficient. Efforts range from diagnosing why LLMs [fail to generalize memorized facts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08393) to developing better methods for [long-context inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09415), more stable [policy distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04751), and more accurate [model quantization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07964). This highlights the ongoing gap between headline-grabbing capabilities and production-ready robustness.

### Worth a closer look
*   [Towards Mechanistically Understanding Why Memorized Knowledge Fails to Generalize in Large Language Model Finetuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08393): This paper provides a compelling, if preliminary, explanation for a common frustration with LLMs. The concept of a "Knowing--Using Gap" and "knowledge-circuit misalignment" offers a tangible mental model for why simply adding facts via finetuning often fails to improve reasoning. It's a key read for anyone working on model reliability and interpretability.
*   [Scalable Visual Pretraining for Language Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09657): The claim here is fundamental and counterintuitive: that training a model on images of text, not just the text itself, improves language understanding. If this holds up, it could force a rethink of the data pipelines for the next generation of foundation models, moving beyond pure text streams.
*   [Modernizing the Meta Ads Service With an Open-Source Kernel Scheduler](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/07/13/ml-applications/modernizing-the-meta-ads-service-with-an-open-source-kernel-scheduler/): This is a valuable look at AI infrastructure in the wild. It bypasses abstract claims and details a specific, systems-level engineering project that yielded concrete, significant performance improvements for a massive service. It’s a reminder that progress in AI isn't just about the models, but also the schedulers, kernels, and hardware they run on.
*   [Video Generation Models are General-Purpose Vision Learners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09024): The ambition here is notable. The paper posits that a single generative backbone could unify a wide array of computer vision tasks. It's worth reading to assess the evidence for this strong claim and to understand how the internal features of a video generator are being repurposed for classification, segmentation, and more.
