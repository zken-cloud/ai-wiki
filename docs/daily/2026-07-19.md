### The short version
*   AI systems are now generating novel ideas and proofs for unsolved problems in pure mathematics, though human verification remains essential.
*   A stark warning for global AI deployment: the clinical safety of medical LLMs can collapse when applied in languages other than English, with one model's correctness score dropping from positive to negative.
*   The promised speedups from speculative decoding are more modest than claimed, with a new study on consumer hardware finding a maximum 1.61x gain that is highly conditional.
*   A self-improving AI for theorem proving not only solves problems but co-evolves its own benchmarks and tools, achieving a state-of-the-art solve rate.

### Themes
A clear theme today is the growing use of AI in formal mathematics, suggesting a shift from tool to collaborator. One paper shows an AI generating key, non-obvious insights that led to [five new proofs in Banach space theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17388v1). Another details a [self-modifying proof agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17352v1) that improves its own performance by co-evolving its prompts, tools, and the very benchmark it's measured against. This points toward a future where AI not only solves problems we pose but helps discover new ones.

A second, more sobering theme is the difficulty of translating lab results into real-world value and safety. A study on [speculative decoding on consumer hardware](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17283v1) pours cold water on hype, showing that theoretical speedups don't always materialize under realistic conditions. More critically, research on [medical language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17270v1) reveals a catastrophic drop in clinical correctness when moving from English to Hausa, highlighting a massive, under-discussed safety risk. These papers serve as a reminder that progress in AI is often messy and fraught with practical and ethical hurdles that aren't solved by a better architecture alone.

### Worth a closer look
*   **[Mathematical Discovery in the Wild: AI-Guided Proofs in Banach Space Theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17388v1):** Read this for a glimpse of AI as a genuine research partner. The paper details how LLMs provided key suggestions that led to new theorems, moving beyond mere problem-solving to something resembling creative discovery. The process is as interesting as the result.
*   **[Safety That Does Not Transfer: Cross-Lingual Clinical Correctness Drift in Deployable Medical Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17270v1):** A critical and alarming paper. The precipitous drop in medical safety when a model is applied to a non-English language is a five-alarm fire for anyone building or deploying "global" AI systems. The data is stark and the implications for equity and safety are profound.
*   **[Lossless but Not Free: An Empirical Anatomy of Speculative Decoding on Consumer Hardware](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17283v1):** A necessary reality check for developers. If you're hoping speculative decoding will magically double your LLM's speed on a MacBook, read this first. It carefully dissects the conditions required to see any benefit and finds the real-world gains are often marginal, saving you from a potentially fruitless optimization effort.
