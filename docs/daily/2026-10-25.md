### Themes

Today’s papers highlight a growing push to challenge foundational assumptions across AI safety, training, and deployment. Rather than accepting theoretical risks or numerical defaults at face value, empirical studies are probing where standard implementations fail. For instance, [Questioning Instrumental Convergence in RLVR: Checkpoint Evidence from OLMo-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31624v1) questions theoretical claims about instrumental convergence by demonstrating that reinforcement learning doesn't consistently increase power-seeking trajectories. On the core math side, [A Fix for Softmax and Cross-Entropy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31642v1) shows that standard gradient instability may simply be an unaddressed floating-point bug in basic activation functions.

Concurrently, a sharp divide is forming between commercial deployment strategies and practical security requirements. While vendors focus on monetization through ad scaling ([ChatGPT Ads expands to 26 new countries as over 100,000 advertisers have used the platform](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-ads-expands-26-new-countries)) and enterprise consulting ([What FDE means at OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/forward-deployed-engineering-means)), security research is leaning heavily into micro-architectures. Work like [VX-One: Typed, Calibrated Decision Heads for Real-Time Perimeter Triage - An empirical study of unknown-attack honesty, cascade gray zones, and calibration under drift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30090v1) and [Parameters efficient language model with latent cross-attention for IoT intrusion detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30222v1) emphasizes small, sub-million-parameter, deterministic models optimized for sub-millisecond edge execution over broad general-intelligence APIs.

### Worth a closer look

- [Questioning Instrumental Convergence in RLVR: Checkpoint Evidence from OLMo-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31624v1): Interrogates a core theoretical risk in AI safety, providing empirical evidence that reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards does not cause monotonic increases in instrumental behaviors.
- [A Fix for Softmax and Cross-Entropy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31642v1): Identifies two distinct numerical failure modes in standard softmax implementations and presents a practical fix to prevent subtle gradient degradation during model training.
