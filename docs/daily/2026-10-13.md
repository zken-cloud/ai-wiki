### The short version

- **Instruction controls fail under open-ended tasks:** Research into [Thinking Inertia: LLMs Keep Thinking When Told Not To](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11765) shows that reasoning models persistently engage in internal inference even when explicitly instructed not to, especially as the answer space broadens.
- **Coding agent benchmarks remain disconnected from reality:** Analyzing nearly 5,000 real-world IDE sessions, [Coding-Agent Benchmarks Should Match Their Users' Task Flows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09633) exposes how standard issue-resolution evaluations fail to reflect actual human-agent interaction patterns.
- **Tackling agent latency via KV cache recycling:** To stop wasting compute on redundant prefills during multi-turn agent runs, [Improve TTFT for Multi-Turn Agentic Workloads with Bi-Directional KV Transfer in vLLM](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-10-12-bidirectional-kvxfer-multiturn-agentic-workload) introduces bidirectional cache transfers between decode and prefill nodes.
- **Infrastructure efficiency over raw compute purchases:** Meta highlights a pragmatism-first approach in [Turning Opportunistic AI Inference Capacity Into Better Ads Recommendations](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/10/12/ml-applications/turning-opportunistic-ai-inference-capacity-into-better-ads-recommendations/), harvesting idle, fragmented GPU cycles across its fleet to handle recommendation models without expanding hardware orders.

### Themes

Today’s research and infrastructure updates reflect a pragmatic pivot away from brute-force scaling toward operational efficiency and evaluation hygiene. On the engineering side, system designers are squeezing performance out of existing hardware rather than simply waiting for larger clusters. Meta’s opportunistic capacity harvesting ([Turning Opportunistic AI Inference Capacity Into Better Ads Recommendations](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/10/12/ml-applications/turning-opportunistic-ai-inference-capacity-into-better-ads-recommendations/)), vLLM's bidirectional KV cache movement ([Improve TTFT for Multi-Turn Agentic Workloads with Bi-Directional KV Transfer in vLLM](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-10-12-bidirectional-kvxfer-multiturn-agentic-workload)), and IBM's compiler stack simplification ([Extending Inductor for the IBM Spyre Accelerator](https://pytorch.org/blog/extending-inductor-for-the-ibm-spyre-accelerator/)) all focus on eliminating architectural overhead and wasted compute cycles.

Simultaneously, the benchmark landscape is facing a reality check. Standard evaluation setups increasingly fail to capture real-world usage or model failures. Papers like [Coding-Agent Benchmarks Should Match Their Users' Task Flows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09633) demonstrate that synthetic SWE benchmarks are poor proxies for developer IDE interactions, while [AgentSWE: Can Coding Agents Build the Agent You Actually Want?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13361) attempts to standardise evaluation for meta-agent construction. Compounding these evaluation issues, [Thinking Inertia: LLMs Keep Thinking When Told Not To](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11765) reveals that safety and prompt-level constraints are far less robust than system designers assume once task complexity scales up.

### Worth a closer look

- [Thinking Inertia: LLMs Keep Thinking When Told Not To](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11765) — Essential reading for system engineers relying on negative prompting or strict system instructions, demonstrating how reasoning models systematically bypass constraints under open-ended task conditions.

- [Improve TTFT for Multi-Turn Agentic Workloads with Bi-Directional KV Transfer in vLLM](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-10-12-bidirectional-kvxfer-multiturn-agentic-workload) — A practical technical breakdown for infrastructure teams attempting to optimize latency and compute costs in continuous multi-turn LLM agent loops.
