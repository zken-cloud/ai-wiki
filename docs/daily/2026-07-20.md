### Themes

The dominant story today is that agentic AI security is vastly lagging behind deployment. Abstract threat models are fast becoming real-world incidents, illustrated by [Hugging Face](https://embracethered.com/blog/posts/2026/ai-intrusion-are-now-real/) detailing an intrusion carried out by an autonomous AI agent. Academic research backs up the chaos: researchers demonstrated that stateful agents suffer from temporal persistence exploits ([The Chronos Vulnerability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19433v1)), that long-term memory can be poisoned to force vulnerable code generation despite system guardrails ([Insecure Coding Preferences in Long-Term Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17619v1)), and that individual models certified as "safe" routinely fail when connected into multi-agent systems ([ChannelGuard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19430v1)).

At the same time, labs and enterprises are aggressively pushing long-running, autonomous execution. OpenAI explicitly acknowledged the difficulty of aligning these systems in [Safety and alignment in an era of long-horizon models](https://openai.com/index/safety-alignment-long-horizon-models). Despite safety gaps, domain-specific implementations are accelerating anyway—spanning healthcare consultation models ([Cura 1T](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15314)), large-scale physical robotics ([Xiaomi-Robotics-1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15330)), and high-performance computing infrastructure ([Trusted Credentials, Untrusted Behavior](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18485v1)).

### Worth a closer look

- [Loop the Loopies!](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16051): A compelling look at whether architectural depth-recurrence (looping Transformer layers) can replace external reasoning tools and scratchpads while competing at the level of IMO/IPhO gold medalists.
- [ChannelGuard: Safe Models Do Not Compose into Safe Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19430v1): Important reading for anyone assembling multi-agent workflows, proving that prompt-level alignment completely breaks down when models communicate directly with each other over unmonitored channels.
- [Insecure Coding Preferences in Long-Term Memory: Security Risks for LLM-based Code Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17619v1): Demonstrates a critical flaw in agent personalization: once an LLM records an insecure coding preference to its long-term memory, it stubbornly ignores downstream safety instructions and reliably produces vulnerable code.
