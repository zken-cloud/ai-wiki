### The short version

### Themes

Today’s releases highlight a sharp divergence between pushing agent autonomy forward and managing the system design vulnerabilities that autonomy creates. While papers like [MintAct: A Unified Visual Agent for Digital Environments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22083) and [RecreationWorld: Scalable and Verifiable Environments for Hybrid Computer-Use Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22000) push for complex cross-platform execution, security researchers are pointing out that our basic access-control mechanisms are broken. Attacks demonstrated in [Loopjacking: Hijacking Human-in-the-Loop Approval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21081v1) show that simply asking a user to click "approve" is useless if the underlying agent payload can be swapped post-approval, while [Authorization Revocation for Long-Running AI Agents: Root-Scoped Quiescence under Delegation and Asynchronous Execution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21284v1) highlights how difficult it is to actually stop an asynchronous agent once it begins delegating sub-tasks.

At the same time, the research community is realizing that prompt-level guardrails are an unsustainable defense. Work like [Origin Is All You Need: Provenance-Aware Transformers for Structural Trust-Boundary Separation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21088v1) and [ServeGuard: Verifiable, Bounded-Residual Confinement of Operator-Invisible Channels Without Revealing the Certified Read Factor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21515v1) signals a shift toward architectural hardware and cryptographic guarantees—trying to fix security at the token, attention, and zero-knowledge proof levels rather than trusting high-level prompt instructions to keep models compliant.

### Worth a closer look

- [Loopjacking: Hijacking Human-in-the-Loop Approval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21081v1) — Demolishes the common security assumption that keeping a human approval step in the loop guarantees safety by exposing critical representation mismatches and post-approval state substitutions.
- [Origin Is All You Need: Provenance-Aware Transformers for Structural Trust-Boundary Separation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21088v1) — A rare attempt to solve indirect prompt injection at the model architecture level using origin embeddings and attention biases instead of brittle software filters.
- [When AI Reviews Train AI Reviewers: Scientific-Judgment Collapse and Mitigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20942) — Provides important empirical evidence on the risks of synthetic data feedback loops, specifically demonstrating how AI-generated peer reviews degrade an evaluator's judgment over time.
