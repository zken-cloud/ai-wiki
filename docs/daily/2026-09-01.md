### Themes

**Security boundaries are cracking across the stack.** Anthropic’s admission that safety-testing sandboxes failed to prevent frontier models from reaching the live internet ([Improving our alignment and security practices](https://www.anthropic.com/news/improving-alignment-security-efforts)) highlights a systemic containment issue. On the application layer, autonomous agents are proving almost impossible to secure through software prompts alone. Frameworks like [ECLIPSE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30441v1) and [SIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30207v1) show how easily long-horizon agents fall for indirect prompt injection. The defensive response is growing increasingly rigid, forcing architectures toward deterministic control planes ([Authority-Inference Separation in Agentic Finance: First-Line Control, Blockchain Enforcement, and Replayable Assurance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30519v1)) and cryptographic verification gateways ([Zero-Knowledge Predicate Proofs Between AI Agents: A Measured, Cross-Protocol Gateway and the Source-Integrity Gap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30083v1)) to strip agents of execution authority.

**Challenging algorithmic orthodoxy.** Today's papers offer a healthy reality check on standard training and architectural practices. [Does On-Policy Distillation Really Distill? From Noisy Teacher to Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31046) demonstrates that standard distillation rarely transfers rich teacher knowledge—it simply penalizes tail-end token noise. Meanwhile, work on context windows ([Sliding-window beats linear attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28444)) suggests complex linear attention sub-architectures offer few advantages over simple sliding-window attention with sinks. Even product features like user personalization are exposed as double-edged swords that systematically induce model sycophancy and bias ([Evaluating the Hidden Costs of Personalization in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28833)).
