### Themes
Today's research reveals a clear focus on refining AI agents beyond simply scaling them up. Instead of relying on brute-force computation, multiple papers propose more sophisticated training and evaluation methods. Techniques like token-level credit assignment in [CoRT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25659), co-evolving evaluators and solvers in [DecoEvo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25675), and using game solvers as teachers in [CAST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25308) all point toward a push for more efficient and nuanced learning. The goal is to build agents that learn more from less, a necessary step for tackling complex, long-horizon tasks.

This drive for sophistication extends to embodied AI, where progress appears to be fighting a war on two fronts. On one side, engineers are achieving impressive performance gains, with [TurboVLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27205) claiming real-time control on consumer-grade hardware. On the other, fundamental questions about capability remain. The [HumanCLAW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27180) framework finds that these models may lack the "embodied self-awareness" needed for physical interaction, suggesting that faster decision-making might not translate to smarter physical actions.

### Worth a closer look
*   [TurboVLA: Real-Time Vision-Language-Action Model at 32 Hz on an RTX 4090 with <1 GB VRAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27205): The performance claims are the main story. Achieving 32 Hz on a single consumer GPU with minimal VRAM would be a significant step toward deploying capable robots affordably. The key is its direct "Vision+Language to Action" mapping, which bypasses the typical, computationally heavy LLM reasoning step. Worth scrutinizing if this simplification sacrifices too much generalisation for speed.
*   [Can AI agents conduct open-ended AI research? Early evidence from two case studies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27191): This paper introduces a clever "shadow evaluation" methodology where agents try to solve research problems from unpublished papers. It's a provocative look at the future of scientific discovery. The "early evidence" framing is appropriate; the results are likely modest, but the approach itself is a valuable contribution to understanding and measuring progress toward AGI.
*   [HumanCLAW: Can Vision-Language Models Act Through a Body?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27180): This work is important for its sobering conclusion. By creating a framework that separates high-level decision-making from low-level motor control, the authors find that current VLMs struggle with a sense of their own physical form. It's a necessary reality check for the hype around embodied intelligence.
*   [DecoEvo: Score-Decoupled Co-Evolution of Solver and Rubric-Generator Skills in Text Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25675): This paper tackles a core problem in AI alignment and capability: how do you evaluate performance on open-ended tasks where a good metric doesn't exist? Their solution—to have an AI "solver" and an AI "evaluator" evolve together but with separate goals—is an elegant approach to self-improvement that could have broad applications beyond text generation.
