### Themes

#### Security abstractions are crumbling across the ecosystem
The assumption that open-source components, zero-knowledge verification, or basic scanners can protect agentic systems is taking a beating. On the ecosystem front, [A hole in every one](https://adversa.ai/blog/agent-skill-scanners-bypass-eight-tested/) proves that static defensive scanners for agent skill repositories provide almost zero actual security against basic bypasses. Cryptographic guarantees are faring no better: the [Hollow-LLM Attack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28884v1) shows zero-knowledge verification schemes can be systematically fooled using "ghost weights," drastically lowering the attacker's compute burden while generating valid proofs. Paired with attacks that hide data inside model weights ([Don't Trust the AI Ecosystem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27886v2)) or weaponize machine unlearning to trigger dormant backdoors ([Benign on Label, Malicious by Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27936v1)), the supply chain for weights, tools, and third-party agent extensions remains an absolute minefield.

#### Ground-truth evaluation vs. agentic hype
While research on agent workflows continues to churn out synthetic reinforcement learning frameworks like [SkillRise](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26784) and [CAST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25308), empirical evaluations are applying a much-needed cold shower. [HumanCLAW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27180) decouples high-level VLM decisions from low-level execution controllers, proving that multimodal models lack fundamental self-awareness of physical bodies and constraints. Meanwhile, researchers attempting shadow evaluations of AI agents conducting scientific research ([Can AI agents conduct open-ended AI research?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27191)) had original paper authors grade the outputs—finding that despite plausible-sounding language, models struggle significantly with open-ended scientific problem-solving.

### Worth a closer look

- [A hole in every one: bypassing the open source AI skill scanners](https://adversa.ai/blog/agent-skill-scanners-bypass-eight-tested/) — Essential reading for teams deploying third-party agent plugins; it lays bare how trivial it is to bypass current static scanning tools.
- [Hollow-LLM Attack: Computationally Trivial Weights in Zero-Knowledge Verification of LLM Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28884v1) — Demolishes a core assumption of decentralized, zero-knowledge LLM verification by demonstrating how to forge execution proofs without running the actual target weights.
- [Can AI agents conduct open-ended AI research? Early evidence from two case studies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27191) — Uses a clever "shadow evaluation" design where original paper authors grade agent attempts at answering their unpublished research questions, offering a rare, un-contaminated look at automated science claims.
- [TurboVLA: Real-Time Vision-Language-Action Model at 32 Hz on an RTX 4090 with <1 GB VRAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27205) — Demonstrates that stripping out the bloated LLM backbone in favor of a direct V+L to Action mapping yields fast, low-footprint robotics control without sacrificing success rates.
