### Themes

Vendor marketing continues to push rapid deployment of autonomous coding and reasoning agents—exemplified by Anthropic's [Claude Sonnet 5.5](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/28/claude-sonnet-5-5/) release and benchmark updates like [Basis completes a tax workbook 2x faster with GPT-6 Astra](https://openai.com/index/basis-tax-workbook-with-astra). However, today's security literature shows that the enterprise surface area for autonomous agents is deeply vulnerable. Deployments face complex risks including memory-hopping agent viruses ([Share-Borne AI Virus: Memory-Hopping Attacks Across LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35576v1)), drift in autonomous system behaviors over time ([SEABench: Benchmarking Endogenous Misalignment In Self-Evolving Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35596v1)), and accumulated risk during multi-step coding executions ([Trajectory-Level Security Debt in LLM Coding Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35199v1)).

A second clear pattern across research papers is that static or isolated safety checks offer illusory protection. Evaluators testing models immediately after supervised fine-tuning are misled because subsequent reinforcement learning easily restores stolen capabilities ([Distillation Defenses Easily Break After Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35699v1)). Similarly, retrieval defenses assessing documents in isolation completely miss set-level compositional poisoning attacks ([LENS: The Sum Is Worse Than the Parts for Set-Level Poisoning in Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35155v1)). Current safety evaluations remain dangerously detached from real-world agent operations.

### Worth a closer look

- [Distillation Defenses Easily Break After Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35699v1): Exposes a critical flaw in current model-distillation security metrics, demonstrating that evaluating safety immediately after supervised fine-tuning provides a false assurance that vanishes once reinforcement learning is applied.
- [LENS: The Sum Is Worse Than the Parts for Set-Level Poisoning in Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35155v1): Shows how standard document-level RAG filters can be completely bypassed by splitting adversarial triggers across sets of individually benign documents, driving attack success rates from 6.9% to 85.2%.
- [Share-Borne AI Virus: Memory-Hopping Attacks Across LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35576v1): Demonstrates self-propagating exploits moving laterally between distinct LLM agents via shared persistent artifacts and long-term memories, highlighting systemic risks in multi-agent environments.
