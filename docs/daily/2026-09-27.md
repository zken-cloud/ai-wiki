### The short version

- **Multi-agent consensus is fundamentally fragile**: A small proportion of deceptive agents can easily hijack LLM deliberation pipelines, revealing that automated multi-agent consensus lacks human resilience to peer pressure ([How does Adversarial Influence Scale in Multi-Agent Systems?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30028v1)).
- **Quantization masks visual hallucinations**: Lower precision weights (INT8, NF4) maintain baseline top-line accuracy in 8B vision-language models while quietly exacerbating visual grounding hallucinations ([GHOST-Q: Towards Studying Grounding Hallucinations Overlooked Under Same-score TradeOffs in Quantized VLMS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29999v1)).
- **IoT intrusion benchmarks are cheating**: High detection performance in network anomaly models continues to rely heavily on shortcut learning, such as raw timestamp artifacts and leaky dataset splits ([Unmasking Shortcut Learning in IoT Intrusion Detection: A Forensic, Multi-Paradigm Evaluation of Feature Dependence and Data Leakage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28725v1)).
- **Natural context breaks decision models**: Adding innocuous, realistic context to prompts can flip correct outputs in specialized decision models to high-confidence incorrect answers without altering the ground truth ([JevOut: Natural Context Can Flip Decision Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30243v1)).

### Themes

A recurring thread across today's papers is the widening gap between traditional benchmark metrics and operational reliability. Models routinely look fine on paper while breaking in production. Quantized vision-language models retain headline accuracy scores while hallucinating visual grounding ([GHOST-Q: Towards Studying Grounding Hallucinations Overlooked Under Same-score TradeOffs in Quantized VLMS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29999v1)), dedicated decision models flip under trivial context additions ([JevOut: Natural Context Can Flip Decision Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30243v1)), and IoT security benchmarks rely on temporal leakage to pretend they work ([Unmasking Shortcut Learning in IoT Intrusion Detection: A Forensic, Multi-Paradigm Evaluation of Feature Dependence and Data Leakage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28725v1)). High scores remain poor proxies for robustness.

At the same time, multi-agent and distributed architectures continue to trade away security for scale. Deliberative agent frameworks succumb linearly to minority bad actors, failing where human groups usually resist ([How does Adversarial Influence Scale in Multi-Agent Systems?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30028v1)). Meanwhile, distributed embodied reinforcement learning pipelines expose physical action-observation histories through basic gradient inversion ([Temporal Gradient Inversion for Private Trajectory Reconstruction in Embodied Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30258v1)). Splitting workloads across agents or nodes creates fresh attack surfaces faster than it solves compute constraints.

### Worth a closer look

- [How does Adversarial Influence Scale in Multi-Agent Systems?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30028v1): Essential reading for system designers building LLM ensembles, demonstrating how straightforward bad-faith agents ruin group deliberation.
- [Unmasking Shortcut Learning in IoT Intrusion Detection: A Forensic, Multi-Paradigm Evaluation of Feature Dependence and Data Leakage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28725v1): A sharp forensic breakdown of how popular cybersecurity ML benchmarks hide dataset contamination and timestamp reliance behind strong baseline metrics.
- [GHOST-Q: Towards Studying Grounding Hallucinations Overlooked Under Same-score TradeOffs in Quantized VLMS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29999v1): Exposes how post-training quantization degrades spatial anchoring in vision models without alerting standard validation checks.
