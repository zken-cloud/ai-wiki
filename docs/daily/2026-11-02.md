### The short version

### Themes

The dominant narrative across today's research is the massive gulf between synthetic safety benchmarks and real-world deployment risks. Researchers show that standard evaluations mask severe failures in detecting hijacked agents when faced with rare attacks, unseen tools, or benchmark transfers ([What Standard Evaluations Hide About Detectors of Hijacked LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41803v1)). Worse, even protocol-compliant Model Context Protocol (MCP) tool-call metadata leaks user behavioral profiles without ever inspecting prompt content ([How Much Can Tool Calls Reveal About You? Privacy Leakage from Tool-Call Traces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41885v1)). While researchers propose complex defenses like on-policy self-distillation ([Erase the Injected Region](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41307v1)), bad actors rely on standard social engineering, such as spoofing marketplace extensions to gain full developer access ([Inside a malicious Claude Code-branded extension](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/10/30/inside-malicious-claude-code-branded-cursor-extension-persistent-ssh-backdoor/)).

On the architecture front, labs are attempting to move past discrete token constraints toward continuous representations. Frameworks like [Latent Reasoning in Continuous Space for Unified Multimodal Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42149) attempt continuous-state reasoning for generation, while physical embodied agents rely on world action models to bridge raw video with continuous motor outputs ([WorldBridge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40748), [HiFOR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40373)). Yet, regardless of algorithmic tricks to optimize test-time compute scaling ([Does Scaling Toward AGI Depend on What System-1 Lets System-2 Execute?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39497)), vendors are increasingly constrained by real-world physical infrastructure, forcing initiatives like OpenAI's [Grid Protectors Program](https://openai.com/index/grid-protectors-program).

### Worth a closer look

- [Inside a malicious Claude Code-branded extension and its persistent SSH backdoor](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/10/30/inside-malicious-claude-code-branded-cursor-extension-persistent-ssh-backdoor/): A forensic breakdown of how simple typosquatting on code-editor extension registries remains a highly effective vector for supply-chain attacks targeting AI developers.
