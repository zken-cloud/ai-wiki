### Themes

The infrastructure layer is aggressively reorganizing around long-horizon, autonomous agents rather than static inference workloads. NVIDIA's open-source push—spanning [Agent Substrate](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/agent-substrate-advancing-kubernetes-for-agents-at-scale/), [OpenShell](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/open-source-openshell-infrastructure/), and [NVIDIA Snapshot](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/start-and-restore-gpu-workloads-in-seconds-with-nvidia-snapshot/)—aims to lock down the enterprise orchestration tier. Coupled with updates like [Encoder Disaggregation in vLLM](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-11-11-encoder-disaggregation-epd), the stack is optimizing for stateful, multi-turn workloads that require rapid context recovery and distributed execution.

Concurrently, the security narrative surrounding agentic systems is souring as guardrails fail to keep up with capabilities. While benchmarks like [BypassGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13684v1) track how easily agents evade endpoint defenses, foundational research shows structural limits to safety. A formal impossibility proof in [No finite rulebook can certify every safe program](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13367v1) demonstrates that deterministic rulebooks cannot guarantee safety or confidentiality in AI-generated code. Paired with Microsoft's finding on stealth sleeper agents, the current reliance on post-hoc evals and rule-based wrappers looks increasingly naive.

### Worth a closer look

- [No finite rulebook can certify every safe program: an impossibility theorem for gatekeeping AI agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13367v1) — Establishes a formal theoretical barrier showing that static, machine-checkable rules are fundamentally incapable of certifying complex temporal safety properties for agent-generated code.
- [Detecting sleeper agents hiding in AI model neurons](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/10/detecting-sleeper-agents-hiding-in-ai-model-neurons/) — Demonstrates that precise, minimal parameter edits (<10 neurons) can introduce latent backdoors that slip cleanly past standard safety benchmarks.
- [Tree-Ising: Repairing Language Models with Bit-Level Ising Edits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14210) — Proposes a clever token-free method for fixing model reasoning loops by formulating weight repairs as discrete optimization problems instead of relying on inefficient agentic retries.
- [Anthropic launches roadshow for proposed initial public offering](https://www.anthropic.com/news/roadshow-launch) — Sets up the first major public market stress-test for frontier AI lab valuations, providing concrete pricing metrics for the industry's capital burn.
