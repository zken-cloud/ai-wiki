# 📰 Daily Briefing — 2026-07-26

*26 items across 2 categories.*

### The short version
*   A new paper on [model merging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27240v1) reveals a critical flaw: combining a base model with a safety-tuned version can preserve refusal behaviour while catastrophically degrading core capabilities.
*   An investigation uncovers a thriving [black market for discounted LLM tokens](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/26/relay-market/#atom-everything), primarily in China, built on compromised API keys, posing a significant threat to application security and provider business models.
*   A proposed framework called [TriShieldRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23838v1) claims to reduce the success rate of knowledge corruption attacks on RAG systems from 91% down to 13% using a multi-layered defence.
*   Research into LLM political alignment finds that [prompt instructions account for 88-93% of the variance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23519v1) in a response's political slant, suggesting that alignment is more about prompt-following than a model's inherent identity.

### Themes
Today's items paint a picture of an increasingly complex and brittle AI ecosystem. Multiple papers highlight how popular techniques can fail in unexpected ways. [Model merging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27240v1) can lead to "asymmetric collapse," where safety overwrites competence. The widely used [LoRA fine-tuning method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23711v1) is shown to have a predictable threshold for catastrophic forgetting. Even seemingly simple systems like [LLM listwise rerankers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24869v1) are vulnerable to trivial position-based attacks.

This brittleness extends from model internals to deployed applications. The vulnerability of RAG systems to data poisoning is severe enough to warrant a [three-stage defence framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23838v1). Meanwhile, the entire economic layer built on APIs is being undermined by a sophisticated [token reselling market](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/26/relay-market/#atom-everything). While many papers propose solutions—from new serving architectures like [Kalypso](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23815v1) to better context management like [ACM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23809v1)—the dominant theme is the discovery of new and subtle ways these systems can break.

### Worth a closer look
*   [Asymmetric Collapse in Model Merging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27240v1): This is a must-read for practitioners. It provides a concrete, alarming example of how a popular, cost-saving technique can silently destroy model capabilities. The finding that safety fine-tuning can overwrite recognition is a crucial, non-obvious warning.
*   [An Inside Look at the Relay Market Powering Token Resellers and Fraud](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/26/relay-market/#atom-everything): A rare and valuable piece of investigative work into the shadow economy of AI. It moves beyond theoretical exploits to document a real, functioning black market, highlighting systemic vulnerabilities in how API access is managed and secured.
*   [Auditing Alignment Controllability in LLMs via Political Axes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23519v1): The paper's strength is its quantitative conclusion: prompt instructions are vastly more influential on a model's expressed politics than the base model itself. This challenges the narrative of deeply ingrained model biases, suggesting alignment is often a shallow, instruction-following phenomenon.
*   [The Intruder Threshold: A Spectral Law for LoRA Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23711v1): It’s a bold claim to have discovered a "spectral law" for anything in deep learning. If the authors' theory holds, it provides a fundamental, predictive understanding of why and when LoRA, a near-ubiquitous technique, causes catastrophic forgetting.
